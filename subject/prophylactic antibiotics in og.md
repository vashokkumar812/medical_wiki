### caesarean section

traditional timing
- antibiotics given after cord clamping

concerns with pre-incision antibiotics
1. masking neonatal bacterial culture results
2. increased colonisation with antibiotic resistant organisms
3. risk of fetal compromise if maternal anaphylaxis occurs

evidence
- pre-incision antibiotics ↓ post-operative endometritis and surgical site infection by ~50%
- no increase in neonatal sepsis observed
- benefit difference between elective vs emergency caesarean unclear

recommendations

| aspect | recommendation |
|---|---|
| prophylaxis | antibiotics for all caesarean sections |
| timing | ideally before skin incision |
| optimal timing | ≥30 min before surgery |
| antibiotic type | narrow spectrum covering gram positive, gram negative, some anaerobes |
| first line | cefazolin 2 g iv |
| weight >120 kg | cefazolin 3 g iv |
| prolonged surgery | repeat dose if >3 hours |

penicillin allergy

| allergy type | regimen |
|---|---|
| non-severe hypersensitivity | cefazolin as above |
| severe hypersensitivity | clindamycin 600 mg iv + gentamicin 2 mg/kg iv |

additional situations

| condition | antibiotic |
|---|---|
| mrsa colonisation or risk | add vancomycin 15 mg/kg iv |
| caesarean during labour or ≥4 h after rupture of membranes | consider azithromycin 500 mg |

note
- give prophylaxis even if patient already receiving antibiotics for prolonged rupture of membranes

### operative vaginal delivery

- operative delivery increases risk of maternal sepsis

evidence
- anode trial (2019)

finding
- prophylactic antibiotics significantly reduce maternal infection

regimen
- single dose iv
- amoxicillin 1 g + clavulanic acid 200 mg

- women with known carriage or risk factors
- give intrapartum prophylactic antibiotics
- follow gbs guidelines

### preterm prelabour rupture of membranes (pprom) ≤36+6 weeks

benefits
- prolongs pregnancy
- reduces maternal and neonatal infection

no proven effect
- perinatal mortality
- long term outcomes

antibiotics not recommended
- preterm labour without membrane rupture

indications for antibiotics
1. gbs prophylaxis
2. prolong latency period

recommended regimens

| regimen | dose |
|---|---|
| erythromycin | 250 mg orally 4 times daily for 10 days or until labour |
| ampicillin/amoxicillin regimen | ampicillin/amoxicillin 2 g iv q6h for 48 h → amoxicillin 250 mg orally q8h for 5 days + erythromycin 250 mg orally q6h for 10 days |

alternative

| drug | reason |
|---|---|
| azithromycin | easier dosing, better tolerance, similar outcomes |

note
- choice depends on presence of chorioamnionitis
- modify therapy based on investigations
- if penicillin allergy → consult guidelines/expert advice

### prophylactic antibiotics in obstetrics

areas with insufficient evidence

- manual removal of placenta
- elective or emergency cervical cerclage
- prevention of infection after 3rd or 4th degree perineal tears

approach
- decision should be individualised based on clinical situation

special situation
- women with heart disease may need prophylaxis for infective endocarditis

### major gynaecological surgery prophylaxis

indications
- major abdominal surgery
- laparoscopic surgery
- vaginal surgery
- mid-urethral sling procedures

recommended regimen

| regimen    | dose                                       |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| first line | cefazolin 2 g iv + metronidazole 500 mg iv |

penicillin allergy

| allergy type | regimen |
|---|---|
| non-severe | cefazolin + metronidazole |
| severe | clindamycin 600 mg iv + gentamicin 2 mg/kg iv |
