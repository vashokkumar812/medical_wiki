- PCOS

  - Rotterdam Consensus diagnostic criteria
    - Diagnosis requires ≥2 of 3 criteria
      - Oligo-ovulation and/or anovulation
      - Clinical and/or biochemical hyperandrogenism
        - Clinical
          - Hirsutism
          - Acne
          - Androgenic alopecia
        - Biochemical
          - Elevated total testosterone
          - Elevated free testosterone
          - Elevated free androgen index (FAI)
          - Elevated DHEAS
      - Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound
        - ≥12 follicles in either ovary
        - Follicles 2–9 mm diameter
        - And/or ovarian volume >10 mL
        - If follicle >10 mm present
          - Repeat scan during ovarian quiescence
          - Recalculate ovarian volume
    - Exclude alternative causes
      - Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
      - Cushing syndrome
      - Androgen-secreting tumours

  - Clinical features

    - Reproductive
      - Menstrual disturbance due to anovulation
        - Oligomenorrhoea
        - Amenorrhoea
        - Dysfunctional uterine bleeding
      - Obesity
      - Infertility
      - Hyperandrogenisation
        - Hirsutism
        - Acne
        - Androgen-dependent alopecia
      - Miscarriage
      - Endometrial hyperplasia
      - Endometrial cancer

    - Metabolic
      - Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)
      - Type 2 diabetes mellitus
      - Gestational diabetes mellitus
      - Dyslipidaemia
      - Cardiovascular disease

  - Suggested routine investigations

    - Pelvic ultrasound
      - Preferably transvaginal
      - Assess endometrium
        - Endometrial thickness
        - Exclude endometrial pathology
        - Exclude endometrial hyperplasia
      - Assess ovaries
        - Polycystic ovarian morphology
        - Exclude ovarian androgen-producing tumours

    - Hormonal investigations
      - Ideally days 2–5 of cycle if oligomenorrhoeic

      - β-hCG (if amenorrhoea)
        - Exclude pregnancy
        - Pregnancy = most common cause of amenorrhoea

      - FSH
      - LH
      - Oestradiol (E2)
        - LH elevated in up to 50% of women with PCOS
        - Helps exclude
          - Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism
          - Premature ovarian failure

      - Thyroid function tests
        - Exclude hypothyroidism

      - Prolactin
        - Exclude hyperprolactinaemia
        - Mild prolactin elevation may occur in PCOS

      - 17-hydroxyprogesterone
        - Exclude congenital adrenal hyperplasia

      - Androgen profile
        - DHEAS
        - Total testosterone
        - SHBG
        - Free androgen index (FAI)
        - Supports diagnosis
        - Helps exclude androgen-producing tumours

    - Metabolic screening

      - 75 g OGTT (2-hour)
        - Recommended if BMI ≥28 kg/m²
        - Exclude
          - Impaired glucose tolerance
          - Type 2 diabetes mellitus

      - Fasting lipid profile
        - Total cholesterol
        - LDL cholesterol
        - HDL cholesterol
        - Triglycerides
        - Exclude dyslipidaemia

  - Indications for specialist referral

    - Abnormal unexplained per-vaginal bleeding
    - Very heavy bleeding
    - Anovulatory dysfunctional uterine bleeding not responding to treatment
    - Infertility
      - Especially if additional infertility factors present
      - Example: abnormal semen analysis
    - High suspicion of alternative hyperandrogenic disorders
    - Diagnostic uncertainty regarding hyperandrogenic disorders
    - Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus
    - Severe hypercholesterolaemia
    - Recurrent miscarriage
    - Intolerance of standard treatments
    - Treatment failure



- Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)
  - Most common cause of
    - Chronic anovulation
    - Anovulatory infertility
  - Prevalence
    - 10–21% of reproductive-age women
    - Up to 70% undiagnosed
  - Aetiology
    - Multifactorial
    - Genetic predisposition
    - Possible in-utero androgen programming
    - Strong association with insulin resistance
  - Pathophysiology
    - Central abnormality: insulin resistance
      - Hyperinsulinaemia
        - Synergises with LH
        - ↑ ovarian androgen production
        - ↑ adrenal androgen production
        - ↓ hepatic SHBG production
        - ↑ free androgen index
        - ↑ bioavailable androgens
      - Consequences
        - Hyperandrogenism
        - Follicular arrest
        - Anovulation
        - Oligomenorrhoea/amenorrhoea
    - Obesity
      - Major exacerbating factor
      - Worsens metabolic and reproductive abnormalities

- Diagnosis

  - Rotterdam criteria (most widely accepted)
    - Requires ≥2 of 3 after excluding alternative causes
      - Oligo/anovulation
      - Hyperandrogenism
        - Clinical
          - Hirsutism
          - Male pattern alopecia
          - Acne
        - Biochemical
          - Elevated free testosterone
          - Elevated free androgen index (FAI)
          - Elevated bioavailable testosterone
      - Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound
        - Older criterion: ≥12 follicles
        - Guideline criterion: ≥10 small antral follicles in each ovary

  - Exclude other causes
    - Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
    - Androgen-secreting tumour
    - Cushing syndrome
    - Thyroid disease
    - Hyperprolactinaemia
    - Pituitary disorders
    - Adrenal disorders

  - Oligo/anovulation
    - Cycles >35 days
    - Cycles <21 days
    - Regular cycles do not exclude anovulation
    - Confirm ovulation
      - Mid-luteal progesterone (day 21 of 28-day cycle)

  - Hyperandrogenism assessment
    - Clinical
      - Hirsutism
      - Acne
      - Alopecia
    - Biochemical
      - Free testosterone preferred
      - FAI
      - Bioavailable testosterone
    - Hormonal contraception affects androgen testing
      - Ideally assess after 3 months cessation
    - Rapid virilisation or markedly elevated testosterone
      - Exclude androgen-secreting tumour
      - Exclude late-onset congenital adrenal hyperplasia

  - Adolescents
    - Menstrual irregularity may be physiological for first 2 years post-menarche
    - Investigate if persists into third year
    - Ultrasound unreliable
      - Up to 70% of normal adolescents have polycystic ovarian morphology

  - Suggests androgen-secreting tumour
  - Features

    - Deep voice

    - Severe hirsutism
    - Increased muscularity
    - Male body habitus
    - Increased libido

- Examination

  - BMI
    - Overweight = 25–29.9 kg/m²
    - Obesity = ≥30 kg/m²

  - Blood pressure

  - Ferriman–Gallwey score
    - Hirsutism assessment

  - Acanthosis nigricans
    - Marker of insulin resistance
    - Hyperpigmented velvety plaques
    - Sites
      - Neck
      - Axillae
      - Skin folds
      - Under breasts

  - Gynaecological examination
    - Usually low yield
    - Mainly to exclude alternative pathology

- Polycystic ovaries (PCO) without PCOS ("asymptomatic PCO")

  - Definition
    - Polycystic ovarian morphology
    - No ovulatory dysfunction
    - No hyperandrogenism

  - Important concept
    - Polycystic ovaries alone ≠ PCOS
    - Seen in ~20% of healthy women
    - Younger women naturally have more follicles
      - Risk of overdiagnosis

  - Rotterdam recommendation
    - Do not diagnose PCOS solely from ultrasound appearance
    - More evidence required

  - Fertility implications
    - Increased sensitivity to FSH stimulation
    - Increased risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS)
    - Higher IVF pregnancy rates than women with normal ovaries

  - Management
    - No treatment required if asymptomatic

- Long-term health risks

  - Reproductive

    - Endometrial hyperplasia
      - Chronic anovulation
      - Unopposed oestrogen exposure

    - Endometrial cancer
      - Increased risk likely
      - Exact magnitude uncertain

    - Ovarian cancer
      - Association unlikely

    - Breast cancer
      - Theoretical increased risk
        - Obesity
        - Hyperandrogenism
        - Infertility
      - Evidence inconclusive

    - Miscarriage
      - Reported in 30–50% of pregnancies
      - Approximately 3-fold increased risk
      - Evidence conflicting
      - Obesity likely major confounder

    - Gestational diabetes mellitus
      - Increased risk

  - Metabolic

    - Insulin resistance
      - Present even in many lean women

    - Impaired glucose tolerance
      - Prevalence 31–35%

    - Type 2 diabetes
      - Prevalence 7.5–10%
      - Lifetime risk increased 5–10-fold
      - >20% in some cohorts

    - Predictors of diabetes
      - Obesity
      - Family history
      - Insulin resistance
      - Beta-cell dysfunction

    - Progression
      - 10–30% with normal/impaired OGTT develop diabetes within 2–3 years

    - Metabolic syndrome
      - Common

  - Cardiovascular

    - Increased prevalence of risk factors
      - Obesity
      - Diabetes
      - Hypertension
      - Dyslipidaemia
      - Insulin resistance
      - Elevated PAI-1
      - Increased carotid intima-media thickness

    - Current evidence
      - Increased risk-factor burden established
      - Direct increase in coronary events not definitively proven

- Investigation strategy

  - Initial tests
    - TSH
    - Prolactin
    - FSH

  - Exclude mimics
    - Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
    - Cushing syndrome
    - Androgen-secreting tumour

  - Diabetes screening

    - OGTT preferred

    - Reason
      - Fasting glucose misses many cases

    - Rotterdam recommendation
      - 75 g OGTT in obese women
      - BMI ≥28 kg/m²

  - Insulin resistance testing

    - Not routinely recommended

    - Reasons
      - Not required for diagnosis
      - Not required for treatment selection
      - No validated test

- Lifestyle management (FIRST-LINE)

  - Indications
    - All women
    - Especially overweight/obese

  - Components
    - Calorie restriction
    - Exercise
    - Behaviour modification
    - Dietitian support
    - Treatment of eating disorders

  - Exercise
    - ≥150 min/week total
    - ≥90 min/week moderate-high intensity aerobic

  - Diets
    - Low GI
    - High protein
    - Metabolic superiority unproven

  - Benefits of ~5% weight loss
    - Improves insulin resistance
    - Restores ovulation
    - Restores menstrual regularity
    - Improves fertility
    - Improves mental wellbeing
    - Reduces diabetes risk
    - Reduces cardiometabolic risk

  - Weight gain worsens
    - Hyperinsulinaemia
    - Hyperandrogenism
    - Metabolic dysfunction
    - Reproductive dysfunction

- Menstrual symptoms

    - First-line
    - Contraception
    - Endometrial protection
    - Cycle regulation
    - Lighter menses
    - Improves acne
    - Improves hirsutism

  - Cyclical progestogens
    - Endometrial protection

- Hirsutism and acne

  - First-line
    - Cosmetic therapy
      - Laser preferred
      - Electrolysis

  - Eflornithine
    - Facial hair
    - Faster response

  - COCP
    - First-line medical therapy

  - Anti-androgens
    - Spironolactone
    - Cyproterone acetate
    - Must use reliable contraception

- Infertility

  - Most common cause
    - Chronic anovulation due to PCOS

  - General measures
    - Weight reduction
    - Smoking cessation
    - Exercise
    - Folate supplementation

  - First-line ovulation induction
    - Clomiphene citrate

  - Metformin
    - Improves ovulation
    - Not consistently shown to improve pregnancy rates alone
    - Helps insulin sensitivity
    - May assist weight reduction

  - Combination therapy
    - Metformin + clomiphene
    - Better than clomiphene alone
      - Ovulation
      - Pregnancy rates

  - Second-line
    - Gonadotrophins (FSH)
    - Laparoscopic ovarian drilling

  - Third-line
    - IVF
    - ICSI

  - Early referral
    - Age >35 years
    - Additional infertility factors

- Impaired glucose tolerance

  - Management
    - Weight loss
    - Exercise
    - Dietary modification

  - Metformin
    - Reduces progression to T2DM

  - Lifestyle modification
    - More effective than metformin

- Long-term treatment

  - COCP
    - Traditional therapy
    - Effective symptom control
    - Possible worsening of insulin sensitivity
    - Evidence conflicting

  - Metformin
    - Targets insulin resistance
    - Restores regular menses (~62%)
    - Theoretical reduction in T2DM
    - Theoretical reduction in CVD
    - Long-term superiority over COCP unproven

- Metformin and pregnancy

  - Observational evidence
    - Reduced miscarriage
    - Reduced GDM (31% → 3%)

  - AMC point
    - Evidence observational only
    - Routine use in pregnancy not recommended without stronger RCT evidence

- Long-term monitoring

  - Smoking assessment

  - Weight/BMI
    - At most visits

  - Waist circumference

  - Lipids
    - Every 2 years if normal
    - Annually if abnormal or overweight

  - Blood pressure
    - BMI <25 → annually
    - BMI >25 → every visit

  - OGTT
    - Every 2 years
    - Annually if additional diabetes risk factors

  - Women with IGT
    - Annual diabetes screening

  - Monitor for
    - Diabetes

- AMC exam pearls

  - PCOS = most common cause of anovulatory infertility
  - Diagnosis = Rotterdam criteria (≥2/3)
  - Insulin resistance is central pathology
  - Obesity worsens all manifestations
  - Polycystic ovaries alone ≠ PCOS
  - Acanthosis nigricans = insulin resistance marker
  - Virilisation → exclude androgen-secreting tumour
  - Endometrial cancer risk due to chronic anovulation + unopposed oestrogen
  - OGTT superior to fasting glucose
  - Weight loss (~5%) may restore ovulation and fertility
  - Clomiphene = first-line ovulation induction
  - Metformin improves ovulation but not clearly pregnancy rates as monotherapy
  - Metformin + clomiphene > clomiphene alone
  - Lifestyle modification is most effective intervention for preventing T2DM
  - Asymptomatic PCO
    - Increased OHSS risk during IVF
    - Higher IVF pregnancy rates
