# erectile dysfunction

- Definition
	- Inability to achieve or maintain erection sufficient for satisfactory sexual intercourse
- step wise approach
	- initiate discussion
	- define probnlem
	- history
		- assess SHIM score
	- examination
	- ix
	- rx
- Epidemiology
	- \> 70 years
- association
	- LUTS
	- BPH
- risk factors
	- Advanced age
	- Atherosclerosis-related risk factors (eg cardiovascular disease, cigarette smoking, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, diabetes mellitus)
	- Neurological conditions
		- Alzheimer’s disease, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson’s disease, stroke
		- Spinal cord disorders
		- Peripheral nerve disorder (eg diabetic neuropathy)
	- Pelvic surgery (eg radical prostatectomy), radiation, trauma
	- Endocrinological (eg hypogonadism, hyperprolactinaemia, thyroid disorder)
	- Obesity and metabolic syndrome
	- Penile abnormalities (eg Peyronie’s disease, venous leak)
	- Psychological and psychiatric conditions
		- Partner-related, stress, guilt, situational anxiety
		- Self-image problems, low self-esteem, history of sexual abuse, highly restricted sexual upbringing
		- Generalised anxiety disorder, depression, psychosis
		- Other disorders of arousal or orgasm: hypoactive sexual desire disorder, sexual aversion disorder, anorgasmia, postorgasmic illness syndrome
	- Medication
		- Antihypertensive (eg diuretics, alpha and ==beta blockers==)
		- Psychotrophic medications (eg selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and other antidepressants, antipsychotics, anxiolytics)
		- Anticonvulsants, anti-Parkinson’s agents
		- Hormone-affecting – anti-androgens, corticosteroids, chronic opioid use
	- Substance abuse
		- Alcohol
		- Illicit drugs (eg cannabis, barbiturates, cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine)
	- Erectile dysfunction associated with other sexual dysfunction(s) (eg rapid ejaculation)
- diagnosis
	- to avoid taboo
		- say that this is normal
	- examination
		- may find
			- penile plaques (Peyronie’s disease)
			- small testis size (measured with an orchidometer)
			- signs of lack of androgenisation
- ix
	- Most patients will not need specialised investigations.
	- Morning testosterone, glycated haemoglobin or fasting blood glucose, lipid panel
	- if first and second line treatment didnt work
		- do penile duplex Doppler ultrasound, using an intracavernosal vasoactive agent
	- Screen for
		- Hypertension
		- Diabetes
		- Dyslipidaemia
		- Smoking
		- CVD diseases
			- erectile dysfunction usually precedes coronary symptoms by three years
		- rationale
			- Erectile dysfunction may be an early marker of cardiovascular disease
			- Penile arteries narrow earlier than coronary/cerebral arteries
- Management
	- Optimise modifiable risk factors and related comorbidities (as appropriate)
	    - Lifestyle – smoking cessation, healthy diet, exercise, reduce alcohol intake, avoid recreational drugs
	    - Re-enforce blood pressure, dyslipidemia, diabetes control
	    - Assess for cardiovascular disease
	- Treat reversible causes
	    - Low testosterone
	    - Medication-induced erectile dysfunction – consider alternatives
	    - Psychogenic erectile dysfunction – consider referral to a therapist
	- First-line therapy (phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor)
		- drugs
			- Sildenafil
				- Headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, abnormal vision
			- Tadalafil
				- Headache, dyspepsia, back ache and myalgia, flushing, nasal congestion
				- uses
					- improve LUTS
			- Vardenafil
				- Headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, abnormal vision
		- contraindications
			- opical nitrates
			- severe congestive cardiac failure
			- unstable angina (or angina with sexual intercourse)
			- resting hypotension
			- recent stroke and myocardial infarction
		- before classifying non responsive verify that
			-  the medication was sourced from an accredited pharmacy (ie not online, overseas)
			- it was taken as directed (sildenafil and vardenafil are usually prescribed on an empty stomach as absorption is reduced after fatty meals) and was allowed adequate time to onset of action before sexual stimulation
			- sexual stimulation was attempted
			- the patient was prescribed the maximum dose allowed
			- the patient trialled at least two different types of PDE5 inhibitor and at least three times.
	- Referral to a urologist
	    - Second-line therapies
		    - penile injections
			    - drug
				    - Alprostadil
				- adverse effects
					- pain
					- prolonged erections
					- fibrosis
		    - vacuum erection devices
			    - mostly patient will stop themselves
			    - adverse effects
				    - pain
				    - inability to ejaculate
				    - bruising
				    - ‘hinging’
				    - paraesthesia.
		    - Low-intensity external shock wave lithotripsy
			    - indications
				    - arteriogenic erectile dysfunction
	    - Third-line therapies
		    - penile prosthesis
	-  If arteriogenic erectile dysfunction
		- refer to cardiologist

# Peyronie's Disease

- Clinical Features
	- Penile curvature
	- Penile deformity
	- Penile pain
	- Penile shortening
	- Erectile dysfunction (advanced disease)

- Management
	- Medical Therapy
		- Oral therapies
		  - Limited evidence
		  - Off-label use
	- Penile Traction Therapy
		- May improve deformity
		- Requires adherence
	- Surgery
		- Most effective treatment
		
		- Perform only when:
		  - Disease stable
		  - No penile pain
		- Surgical Risks
			- Recurrence
			- Penile shortening
			- Altered penile sensation
			- Erectile dysfunction 
	- Coexisting Erectile Dysfunction
		- Concurrent penile prosthesis recommended

# premature ejaculation
- treatment
	
	- First line
	
	  - SSRIs 
		  - Dapoxetine
	    - Delay ejaculation
	    - absorption affected by alcochol
	
	  - Topical anaesthetic
	    - Reduces penile sensitivity
	
	    - Lidocaine-prilocaine cream
	      - Apply 20–30 minutes before intercourse
	      - Use condom to prevent partner numbness
	
	- Second line
	
	  - Behavioural therapy
	
	    - Stop-start technique
	
	    - Less stimulating sexual positions
	
	    - Double condoms
	
	    - Cognitive distraction
	- Secondary PE often associated with **erectile dysfunction**
		→ Trial **PDE5 inhibitors**


# Delayed Ejaculation / Orgasmic Dysfunction

- Counselling
  - Sexual counselling
  - Arousal techniques
  - Genital stimulation
  - Role-playing

- Penile vibratory stimulation
  - Improves penile sensitivity
  - May improve delayed ejaculation
  - May improve orgasmic dysfunction

- Off-label pharmacotherapy
  - Bupropion
  - Cyproheptadine
  - Cabergoline

- Hypogonadism-associated orgasmic dysfunction
  - Testosterone replacement improves orgasm

# Retrograde Ejaculation

- Sympathomimetics
  - Imipramine
  - Pseudoephedrine

- Mechanism
  - Bladder neck contraction
  - Prevents semen entering bladder

- Adverse effects
  - Hypertension
  - Urinary symptoms

- Fertility management
  - Electroejaculation for sperm retrieval
