# Rosacea

- **Definition**
	- Chronic inflammatory facial dermatosis
	- Persistent central facial erythema

- **Risk factors**
	- Fair skin
	- Celtic/European origin
	- Age 30–50 years
	- Females more common
	- Rhinophyma more common in males

- **Triggers**
	- Sun exposure
	- Hot/cold weather
	- Alcohol
	- Spicy food
	- Hot drinks
	- Stress
	- Exercise
	- Topical steroids

- **Subtypes**
	- Erythrotelangiectatic
		- Flushing
		- Persistent erythema
		- Telangiectasia
	- Papulopustular
		- Papules
		- Pustules
		- No comedones
	- Phymatous
		- Skin thickening
		- Rhinophyma
	- Ocular
		- Blepharitis
		- Conjunctivitis
		- Corneal involvement possible

- **Pathophysiology**
	- Vascular hyperreactivity
	- Demodex mite association
	- Abnormal inflammatory response

- **Differential diagnosis**
	- Acne vulgaris
		- Comedones present
	- Seborrhoeic dermatitis
	- Perioral dermatitis
	- SLE
	- Keratosis pilaris

- **General management**
	- Trigger avoidance
	- Sunscreen SPF ≥30
	- Gentle skin care
	- Avoid topical corticosteroids

- **Topical treatment**
	- Metronidazole
	- Azelaic acid
	- Ivermectin
		- Demodex-associated disease
	- Brimonidine
		- Temporary reduction of erythema

- **Oral treatment**
	- Doxycycline
		- First-line oral therapy
	- Isotretinoin
		- Refractory papulopustular rosacea
		- Phymatous rosacea

- **Laser/IPL**
	- For persistent erythema/telangiectasia

- **Phymatous rosacea**
	- Rhinophyma
	- Surgical/laser treatment may be needed

- **Important distinguishing feature**
	- Rosacea lacks comedones
