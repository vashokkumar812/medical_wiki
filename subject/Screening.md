#### practice points
Those with at risk patterns of alcohol consumption should be offered brief advice on the risk of drinking. Risky patterns of drinking are more common in:
- young adults aged 18–25 years
- people aged >60 years
- people with mental or physical health conditions
- people with a family history of alcohol dependence
- people who use illicit drugs or take medications that interact with alcohol. Be alert to drinking in young people aged <18 years

- Key Exam Points
	- Melanoma risk highest after previous melanoma
	- UV radiation is major preventable cause
	- Routine screening not recommended for average-risk population
	- Very high-risk patients require:
		- Dermoscopy
		- Total body photography
		- 6-monthly review
	- Immunosuppression markedly increases skin cancer risk

- Epidemiology
	- Most common cause in Australia
		- Autoimmune thyroid disease
	- Thyroid antibodies
		- More common in women

- Screening Recommendations
	- Asymptomatic adults
		- Routine screening not recommended
	- Pregnant women
		- Routine screening generally not recommended
		- Thyroid function testing recommended in pregnant women with:
			- History of thyroid dysfunction
			- Symptoms/signs of thyroid dysfunction
			- Goitre
			- Known thyroid antibody positivity
			- Type 1 diabetes
		- Timing in pregnancy
			- As early as possible after 6 weeks gestation
	- Thyroid imaging
		- Only indicated if concern for structural abnormality
- Iodine Deficiency
	- very rare
- Congenital Hypothyroidism
	- Screened via:
		- Australian Newborn Bloodspot Screening program
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples
	- No additional screening recommendations
- Higher-Risk Populations for Case Finding
	- Type 1 diabetes
	- Coeliac disease
	- Down syndrome
	- Family history of thyroid disease
	- Past thyroid disease
	- Symptoms/signs of thyroid dysfunction

# Coeliac Disease Screening

- epidemiology
	- Self-reported non-coeliac wheat sensitivity / gluten avoidance
		- ~13.8% of Australians
	- Proven coeliac disease
		- Men
			- ~1.2%
		- Women
			- ~1.9%

- screening
	- General population screening
		- Not recommended
		- Insufficient evidence
	- Reasons
		- Uncertain benefit of population screening
		- May detect asymptomatic disease
		- Risk of unnecessary investigations
		- Risk of overtreatment

- indications for testing
	- Symptoms
		- Persistent unexplained gastrointestinal symptoms
		- Delayed growth
		- Weight loss
		- Prolonged fatigue
		- Persistent mouth ulcers
		- Iron deficiency
		- Vitamin B12 deficiency
		- Folate deficiency
	- Family history
		- First-degree relative with coeliac disease
	- Associated conditions
		- Type 1 diabetes
		- Autoimmune thyroid disease
		- Irritable bowel syndrome (adults)

- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples
	- No additional screening recommendations
