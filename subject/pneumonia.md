- Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)
	- Common causes
		- Typical
			- Streptococcus pneumoniae (most common)
		- Atypical pneumonia
			- Mycoplasma pneumoniae
				- Outbreaks common
				- Incubation 2–3 weeks
				- Fever
				- Headache
				- Cough
				- Chest signs may be minimal despite marked radiographic changes
			- Chlamydia pneumoniae
				- Similar to Mycoplasma
				- Does not occur in epidemics
			- Legionella
				- Spread via water droplets
					- Potable water
					- Cooling towers
				- Features
					- High fever
					- Diarrhoea
					- Hyponatraemia
					- Neutrophilia
					- No organisms seen on Gram stain

	- 

	- Diagnosis
		- Chest X-ray
			- Gold standard investigation
			- Consolidation or infiltrates
			- May be normal early in atypical pneumonia
	- Investigations
		- Chest X-ray
		- Full blood count
			- Often normal in viral infections
		- Blood cultures
		- Atypical serology
			- Mycoplasma
			- Chlamydia
			- Legionella
			- Viral infections
		- Pertussis serology
			- Persistent cough
			- Outbreak settings

	- Aspiration pneumonia
		- Suspect anaerobic infection if
			- Neurological disorders
				- Myasthenia gravis
			- Dysphagia
			- Oesophageal disease
			- Dental sepsis
			- Altered consciousness
			- Terminal illness

	- Severity assessment
		- Pneumonia Severity Index (PSI)
			- Class I–V
			- Predicts 30-day mortality

	- Hospital admission criteria
		- Age >50 years
		- Significant comorbidity
			- Neoplastic disease
			- Cerebrovascular disease
			- Renal disease
			- Liver disease
			- Congestive cardiac failure
		- Altered mental state
		- Pulse >125/min
		- Respiratory rate >30/min
		- Systolic BP <90 mmHg
		- Temperature <35°C or >40°C

	- Treatment
		- PSI Class I–II
			- Amoxycillin 1 g PO 8-hourly for 7 days
			- Plus one of
				- Doxycycline
					- 200 mg loading dose
					- Then 100 mg daily for 7 days
				- Roxithromycin 300 mg daily for 7 days
		- Penicillin allergy
			- Cefuroxime 500 mg PO 12-hourly for 7 days
		- Duration
			- Typical bacterial CAP
				- 5–10 days
			- Mycoplasma
				- 14 days
			- Chlamydia
				- 14 days
			- Legionella
				- 21 days


	- Follow-up
		- Repeat chest X-ray after 2–3 weeks


- Acute bronchitis
	- Usually viral
	- Antibiotics not routinely recommended
	- Common pathogens
		- Mycoplasma pneumoniae
		- Chlamydia pneumoniae
		- Bordetella pertussis
		- Streptococcus pneumoniae
		- Haemophilus influenzae
		- Moraxella catarrhalis
	- Sputum colour
		- Does not predict bacterial infection
		- Does not predict antibiotic benefit
	- Supportive management
		- Fluids
		- Rest
		- Paracetamol
	- Bronchodilators
		- Not routinely recommended
	- Cough suppressants
		- Insufficient evidence
	- Reduce antibiotic use
		- Delayed prescribing
		- Patient education

	- Consider antibiotics / full CAP assessment if chest signs plus
		- Very unwell
		- Age >55 years
		- Significant comorbidities
		- Smoker

- Psittacosis (Chlamydia psittaci)
	- bird/animal exposure + 
		- Headache
		- Myalgia
		- Diarrhoea



	- Diagnosis
		- Best test
			- PCR
		- Serological confirmation
			- Convalescent sample after 2–4 weeks
			- Fourfold rise in C. psittaci titre
		- Pitfalls
			- False negatives can occur
			- Cross-reactivity common
				- Especially with C. trachomatis antibodies

	- Treatment
		- First line
			- Doxycycline
		- Alternative
			- Macrolides if tetracyclines contraindicated
		- Nationally notifiable disease



- Prevention of CAP
	- Influenza vaccine
		- Annually
		- Age >65 years
		- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders >50 years
		- Chronic disease
	- Pneumococcal vaccine
		- Every 5 years
		- Age >65 years
		- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders >50 years
		- Chronic disease
		- Immunocompromised
		- Asplenia

- Case pearl (AMC style)
	- 36-year-old man
	- Semi-rural area
	- Pet bird owner
	- Fever + severe headache + dry cough
	- CAP not improving with amoxycillin-clavulanate
	- Develops
			- Haemoptysis
			- Myalgia
			- Diarrhoea
			- Night sweats
			- Rash
	- CXR
			- Peribronchial consolidation
			- Left perihilar collapse/consolidation
	- Viral PCR negative
	- Key clue
			- Bird exposure
	- Most likely diagnosis
			- Psittacosis (Chlamydia psittaci)
	- Diagnostic test
			- PCR throat swab/sputum
			- Paired serology showing fourfold titre rise
	- Treatment of choice
			- Doxycycline
	- Exam trigger
			- Severe atypical pneumonia + bird exposure = Psittacosis until proven otherwise

- Complicated pneumonia = pneumonia associated with:
	- Parapneumonic effusion
		- Reduced breath sounds with dull percussion note
	- Empyema
	- Lung abscess
	- Necrotising pneumonia



- Most common
	- Most common cause = viral infection
	- Most common bacterial cause = Streptococcus pneumoniae
	- Most common presenting symptoms:
		- Fever
		- Cough
		- Tachypnoea


- Clinical features
	- Fever
	- Cough
	- Tachypnoea at rest
	- Increased work of breathing
	- Respiratory distress
	- Chest wall retractions
	- Grunting
	- Nasal flaring
	- Hypoxaemia
	- Lethargy
	- Poor feeding
	- Abdominal pain
	- Apnoea (especially neonates)


- Diagnosis
	- Primarily clinical
	- Routine laboratory and radiological investigations are not required for uncomplicated CAP

- Investigations
	- Initial
		- No routine investigations required in mild uncomplicated CAP
	- Most appropriate
		- Clinical diagnosis
	- If severe or complicated pneumonia suspected:
		- do sepsis assessment
	- Not routinely recommended:
		- CRP
		- Procalcitonin
		- Viral panels
		- Testing for atypical pathogens

- Treatment
	- Supportive
		- Oxygen if SpO₂ <90%
		- Adequate hydration
		- Monitor respiratory status
		- If IV or NG fluids required:
			- Restrict to approximately 2/3 maintenance
			- Regular fluid status review
	- Admission indications:
		- Immediate or severe penicillin allergy:
			- Oral:
				- Doxycycline
				- Azithromycin
			- IV:
				- Ciprofloxacin + vancomycin
		- Atypical pneumonia:
			- macrolides in severe non-responding cases

- Prognosis
	- Follow-up chest X-ray not required for uncomplicated cases with complete recovery
	- Follow-up chest X-ray after 6 weeks for:
		- Complicated pneumonia
		- Recurrent pneumonia in the same lobe
		- Suspected anatomical abnormality
		- Suspected foreign body
	- Outpatients should be medically reviewed within 48 hours
