- Hyperemesis gravidarum (HG)
	- Usually peaks
		- By 12 weeks
	- Usually resolves
		- By 20 weeks


- causes
	- hCG elevation
	- Multiple pregnancy
	- Molar pregnancy

- Important complications of HG
	- Dehydration
	- Hypokalaemia
	- Hyponatraemia
	- Starvation ketosis
	- Malnutrition
	- Wernicke encephalopathy
		 - Due to thiamine deficiency
	- Acute kidney injury
	- Weight loss
	- Psychological distress



- Diagnosis
	- HG is diagnosis of exclusion
	- Must exclude other pathology
	- Assess severity using validated tool
		 - PUQE-24 preferred
			- Measures symptoms over last 24 hours
			- Assesses
				- Duration of nausea
				- Number of vomiting episodes
				- Number of retching episodes
			- Severity classification
				- Mild
					- Score 4–6
				- Moderate
					- Score 7–12
				- Severe
					- Score ≥13

- Investigations
	- Urine dipstick
		- Ketonuria ≥1+ significant


- Management overview

- Dietary management
	- Small frequent meals
	- High carbohydrate
	- Low fat
	- Bland foods
	- Salty/dry foods
	- High protein snacks
	- Avoid fatty/spicy foods
	- Avoid empty stomach
	- Eat before rising from bed
	- Dry crackers before getting out of bed
	- Cold/carbonated/sour fluids often better tolerated
		 - Ginger ale
		 - Lemonade

- Ginger
	- Can improve mild NVP
	- Dose
		- 250 mg PO up to QID
	- Maximum
		- 2 g/day
	- Side effect
		- Acid reflux

- Pregnancy vitamins pearl
	- Some women improve after stopping pregnancy multivitamins
	- Continue
		- Folic acid
		- Iodine

- Acupuncture/acupressure
	- Considered safe
	- P6 wrist acupressure may help symptoms

- Psychological/supportive management
	- Consider
		- Social work
		- Psychological support
		- Physiotherapy
	- Assess mental health antenatally and postnatally

- Intravenous fluid therapy
	- Used for
		- Dehydration
		- Electrolyte imbalance
	- First-line fluid
		- 0.9% sodium chloride
			- 1–2 L initially
			- Often 1 L over 1 hour
	- Further fluids
		- 1 L over 1–2 hours depending on status

- Dextrose fluids
	- ONLY after
		- Thiamine given
		- Hyponatraemia excluded
	- Reason
		- Prevent Wernicke encephalopathy

- Wernicke encephalopathy
	- Due to thiamine deficiency
	- Risk increased with prolonged vomiting >3 weeks
	- Prevent with
		- IV thiamine 100 mg before dextrose
	- Classic triad
		- Confusion
		- Ataxia
		- Ophthalmoplegia

- Electrolyte replacement
	- Potassium chloride
		- Usually 30–40 mmol/L
		- Maximum rate
			- 10 mmol/hour
	- Magnesium sulphate
		- 10–20 mmol/day

- Pharmacological management
	- Stepwise approach preferred
	- Use agents with best pregnancy safety data first

- First-line medications
	- Pyridoxine (vitamin B6)
		- 10–25 mg PO 3–4 times/day
		- Maximum
			- 200 mg/day
		- Better with doxylamine
		- High doses may cause peripheral neuropathy
	- Doxylamine
		- 6.25–25 mg PO up to TDS
		- Maximum
			- 50 mg/day
		- Side effect
			- Sedation
		- Often combined with pyridoxine
	- Ginger
		 - Useful in mild disease

- Antacid therapy
	- Consider if dyspepsia/reflux symptoms
		- Antacids
		- Ranitidine
		- Omeprazole

- Pharmacological treatment of NVP/HG
	- Stepwise approach preferred
	- Severe HG may require individualised therapy
	- Aim
		- Symptom control
		- Hydration
		- Nutritional support
		- Prevention of complications

- First-line agents
	- Pyridoxine (vitamin B6)
		- Common initial therapy
		- Better efficacy with doxylamine
		- High prolonged doses
			- Peripheral neuropathy risk
	- Doxylamine
		- Antihistamine
		- Commonly combined with pyridoxine
		- Major side effect
			- Sedation
	- Ginger
		- Useful in mild disease
		- May worsen reflux

- Second-line agents
	- Cyclizine
		- Antihistamine
		- Side effect
			- Sedation
	- Promethazine
		- Sedating antihistamine/antiemetic
		- Important adverse effects
			- Sedation
			- Hypotension with rapid IV administration
		- IV route not recommended
			- Highly caustic
		- High doses near delivery
			- Prolonged neonatal neurological disturbance
	- Prochlorperazine
		- Dopamine antagonist antiemetic
		- Side effects
			- Sedation
		- High doses near delivery
			- Prolonged neonatal neurological disturbance
		- IV administration
			- Give slowly
	- Metoclopramide
		- Dopamine antagonist/prokinetic
		- TGA recommendation
			- Limit duration to ≤5 days
	- Domperidone
		- Prokinetic antiemetic
		- Limited pregnancy safety data
		- Use only if clearly needed
		- Short treatment duration preferred
	- Ondansetron
		- Second-line agent
		- Important adverse effects
			- Constipation
			- Headache
			- Dizziness
		- Consider laxatives
		- Safety concern
			- Conflicting evidence regarding first trimester
				- Cardiac defects
				- Oral clefts

- Third-line agents
	- Corticosteroids
		- Reserved for severe/intractable HG
	- Hydrocortisone
		- IV steroid used in severe disease
		- Transition to oral prednisolone after improvement
	- Prednisolone
		- Used after hydrocortisone or severe refractory HG
		- Gradually taper to lowest effective dose
		- Important precautions
			- Monitor blood glucose
			- Avoid prolonged use
			- Requires regular follow-up
		- Possible fetal risk
			- Conflicting association with oral clefts in first trimester

- Acid suppression therapy
	- Useful because reflux/dyspepsia commonly coexist with NVP/HG
	- Important pearl
		- No increased congenital malformation risk with listed acid suppression agents

- Antacids
	- Containing
		- Magnesium
		- Calcium
		- Aluminium
	- Side effects
		- Constipation
		- Diarrhoea

- H2 receptor antagonists
	- Ranitidine
	- Famotidine
	- Generally well tolerated

- Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)
	- Omeprazole
		- Preferred PPI in pregnancy
			- Greatest pregnancy experience
	- Esomeprazole
	- Lansoprazole
	- Rabeprazole
	- Pantoprazole
	- Generally well tolerated

- Enteral/parenteral nutrition
	- Consider if severe HG refractory to medical therapy
	- Requires multidisciplinary management
		- Obstetrics
		- Gastroenterology
		- Nutrition team
	- Enteral feeding options
		- Nasogastric tube
		- Nasoduodenal tube
		- Nasojejunal tube
		- PEG feeding
		- Jejunostomy feeding
	- Parenteral nutrition
		- Via PICC line

- Important AMC pharmacology pearls
	- Pyridoxine + doxylamine
		 - Common first-line regimen
	- Doxylamine/promethazine/cyclizine
		 - Cause sedation
	- Ondansetron
		 - Second-line therapy
		 - Constipation common
		 - Safety concerns in first trimester debated
	- Metoclopramide
		 - Short-term use preferred
	- Steroids
		 - Reserved for refractory HG
	- Promethazine IV
		 - Avoid due to tissue injury/hypotension risk
	- Omeprazole
		 - Preferred PPI in pregnancy
	- Persistent severe HG despite therapy
		 - Consider enteral/parenteral feeding
