- Key points
	- Suspect paracetamol poisoning in all deliberate adolescent self-poisonings
	- Consider co-ingestants
	- Time to acetylcysteine is critical
		- Delays >8 hours increase risk of hepatotoxicity
	- Verify laboratory units on nomogram
		- mg/L
		- micromol/L
	- Management differs for
		- Immediate release ingestion
		- Modified release ingestion
		- Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion
	- Modified release and repeated supratherapeutic ingestions
		- Discuss with Poisons Information Centre or toxicologist

- Background
	- Common in
		- Accidental paediatric ingestion
		- Deliberate self-poisoning
	- Hepatic failure uncommon if treated early
	- Acetylcysteine
		- Safe and effective antidote

- Children requiring assessment
	- Any symptomatic child
	- Deliberate self-poisoning
	- Suspicion of non-accidental poisoning
	- Unknown ingestion quantity
	- Acute ingestion
		- >200 mg/kg
		- Or >10 g
			- Whichever is less
	- Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion
		- >200 mg/kg or >10 g over 24 hours
		- >300 mg/kg or >12 g over 48 hours
		- Therapeutic dose exceeded for >48 hours with symptoms

- Risk assessment
	- History
		- Intentional vs accidental
		- Time of ingestion
		- Dose ingested
		- Form
			- Liquid
			- Tablet
			- Suppository
		- Formulation
			- Immediate release
			- Modified release
		- Exact product name
		- Co-ingestants
		- Maximum possible mg/kg dose
	- Examination
		- May initially appear well
		- Hepatotoxicity develops after 48–72 hours
		- RUQ tenderness
		- Hypotension
		- Hypoglycaemia
		- Acute kidney injury
		- Encephalopathy
		- Coagulopathy
		- Fulminant hepatic failure
		- Check for MedicAlert bracelet

- Rumack-Matthew nomogram
	- Used for
		- Acute immediate release ingestion
		- Modified release ingestion
	- Not used for
		- Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion
	- Plot
		- Serum paracetamol concentration
		- Against time since ingestion
	- Below treatment line
		- No acetylcysteine required
	- Above treatment line
		- Acetylcysteine required

# Single immediate release ingestion

- Investigations
	- Serum paracetamol concentration
		- 4 hours post ingestion
	- Liquid formulation
		- 2 hours post ingestion
	- LFTs
	- INR
		- If >24 hours post ingestion
		- Or abnormal ALT

- Start acetylcysteine immediately if
	- Serum concentration unavailable within 8 hours
	- Symptoms of hepatic injury

- Decontamination
	- Activated charcoal
		- Limited role
		- Toxicologist consultation recommended
		- Consider within 2 hours if
			- >200 mg/kg
			- Or >10 g
		- Rarely required in children <6 years

- Liquid paracetamol ingestion
	- Children <6 years
		- Serum concentration at 2–4 hours
	- Concentration <150 mg/L
		- No acetylcysteine required
	- Concentration ≥150 mg/L
		- Repeat at 4 hours
		- If still ≥150 mg/L
			- Start acetylcysteine

# Modified release paracetamol ingestion

- Investigations
	- Initial serum concentration
			- >4 hours post ingestion
	- Repeat concentration
			- 4 hours later
	- Baseline LFTs
	- INR
		- If >24 hours post ingestion
		- Or abnormal ALT

- Decontamination
	- Activated charcoal
		- Discuss with toxicologist
		- Offer if
			- >200 mg/kg or >10 g within 4 hours
			- >500 mg/kg or >30 g within 24 hours
			- Rising serial concentrations

- Management
	- Discuss all modified release ingestions with toxicologist or Poisons Information Centre
	- Serial paracetamol concentrations required

# Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion

- Do not use nomogram

- Investigations
	- Serum paracetamol concentration
	- ALT
	- Required if
		- >200 mg/kg or >10 g over 24 hours
		- >300 mg/kg or >12 g over 48 hours
		- Excess dosing >48 hours with symptoms

- Start acetylcysteine if
	- ALT >50 U/L
	- Or serum paracetamol concentration >20 mg/L

- Management
	- Discuss with toxicologist or Poisons Information Centre

# Acetylcysteine

- Indications
	- Serum concentration above nomogram treatment line
	- Delayed presentation
	- Hepatic injury
	- Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion meeting treatment criteria

- Monitoring
	- LFTs
	- INR
	- Renal function
	- Serial paracetamol concentrations

- Complications of poisoning
	- Hepatotoxicity
	- Acute liver failure
	- Coagulopathy
	- Hypoglycaemia
	- Acute kidney injury
	- Encephalopathy


# Acetylcysteine Infusion

- Total dose
	- 300 mg/kg IV
	- Two-stage infusion

- Dose calculation
	- Based on actual body weight
	- If weight >110 kg
		- Use 110 kg for calculations

- Standard infusion regimen
	- First infusion
		- 200 mg/kg
		- Over 4 hours
		- In crystalloid solution
			- 7 mL/kg
			- Maximum 500 mL
	- Second infusion
		- 100 mg/kg
		- Over 16 hours
		- In crystalloid solution
			- 14 mL/kg
			- Maximum 1000 mL

- Massive paracetamol ingestion
	- If serum concentration >2× nomogram line
		- Double dose of second infusion

- Administration
	- Compatible with all IV fluids
	- Preferred dilution
		- Maintenance fluid
			- 0.9% sodium chloride in 5% glucose
	- Include acetylcysteine volume in total infusion volume
	- Additional maintenance fluids may be given
	- Larger infusion bags acceptable if required

- Adverse effects
	- Gastrointestinal
		- Nausea
		- Vomiting
		- Abdominal pain
		- Diarrhoea
	- Non-immunologic anaphylaxis
		- Flushing
		- Rash
		- Pruritus
		- Wheeze
		- Shortness of breath
		- Angioedema
		- Hypotension

- Management of anaphylactoid reaction
	- Stop infusion
	- Give promethazine
	- Restart after 30 minutes
		- At half previous rate
	- Gradually increase to full rate over 30 minutes
	- Previous reaction
		- Does not contraindicate future use

- Monitoring
	- At 18 hours after starting infusion
		- Paracetamol concentration
		- ALT
	- If presentation >24 hours post ingestion
		- INR
		- Venous blood gas
		- Urea
		- Electrolytes
		- Creatinine

- Renal complications
	- Acute kidney injury
		- Occurs in ~5%

- Discontinuation
	- Do not stop early without toxicology advice
	- Standard duration
		- 20 hours
	- Stop infusion only if all present
		- ALT normal
		- Paracetamol concentration <10 mg/L
		- Clinically well
	- All other cases
		- Discuss with toxicologist or Poisons Information Centre
