- diverticular disease
	- definition
		- diverticulosis
			- outpouchings of colonic wall
		- diverticular disease
			- symptomatic spectrum and complications of diverticulosis

	- epidemiology
		- >45 years
			- 10%
		- >70 years
			- 65%
		- sigmoid colon
			- most common site
		- Asian populations
			- more right-sided disease

	- pathogenesis
		- low-fibre diet
			- prolonged transit
			- increased water resorption
			- low stool bulk
			- increased intracolonic pressure
		- pressure-induced diverticula formation
			- weak points where vasa recta penetrate muscle layer
		- diverticulitis
			- obstruction of diverticular neck by faecolith
			- bacterial overgrowth + inflammation
			- oedema + microperforation

	- presentations
		- asymptomatic diverticulosis
		- symptomatic diverticular disease
			- abdominal pain
			- altered bowel habits
		- diverticulitis
			- LLQ pain
			- diarrhoea
			- fever
			- localised peritonitis
		- complications
			- perforation
			- fistula
			- obstruction
			- diverticular bleeding

	- investigations
		- preferred
			- CT abdomen with IV contrast
		- noncontrast CT
			- suboptimal

	- classify diverticulitis
		- uncomplicated diverticulitis
			- no:
				- perforation
				- peritonitis
				- sepsis
				- septic shock
				- abscess >5 cm

		- complicated diverticulitis
			- any:
				- perforation
				- peritonitis
				- sepsis
				- septic shock
				- positive blood cultures
				- abscess >5 cm
				- fistula
				- bowel obstruction

	- uncomplicated diverticulitis
		- assess severity
			- nonsevere
				- outpatient management often suitable

			- severe uncomplicated disease
				- systemic infection
				- peritonitis
				- unable to tolerate oral intake
				- failed outpatient treatment
				- persistent fever/leukocytosis
				- worsening pain after 2–3 days

		- management
			- conservative
				- bowel rest
				- observation

		- antibiotics
			- not routinely required if:
				- uncomplicated left-sided disease
				- immunocompetent

			- antibiotics indicated if:
				- immunocompromised
				- right-sided diverticulitis
				- no improvement after 72 h conservative treatment

		- oral antibiotic choices
			- no penicillin hypersensitivity
				- amoxicillin-clavulanate

			- nonsevere penicillin hypersensitivity
				- cefuroxime
				- metronidazole

			- severe penicillin hypersensitivity
				- trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
				- metronidazole

		- follow-up colonoscopy
			- not routinely required in all uncomplicated cases
			- selective approach recommended
				- complicated imaging features
				- family history
				- colorectal cancer risk factors
				- patient preference

	- complicated diverticulitis
		- management
			- admit
			- IV antibiotics
			- source control if required

		- indications for surgery
			- perforation with peritonitis
			- abscess not amenable to drainage
			- bowel obstruction

		- source control options
			- percutaneous drainage
			- peritoneal lavage
			- colonic resection

		- empirical IV antibiotic regimens
			- standard regimens
				- aminoglycoside-based
					- gentamicin or tobramycin
					- metronidazole
					- amoxicillin or ampicillin

				- non-aminoglycoside alternatives
					- ceftriaxone + metronidazole
					- piperacillin-tazobactam

			- nonsevere penicillin hypersensitivity
				- ceftriaxone
				- metronidazole

			- severe penicillin hypersensitivity
				- gentamicin or tobramycin
				- clindamycin
				- add metronidazole
					- if delayed source control expected

		- antibiotic modification
			- modify according to:
				- culture results
				- susceptibility testing

			- if aminoglycoside regimen used
				- and susceptibilities unavailable after 72 h
					- switch to non-aminoglycoside regimen

			- if yeast identified from deep surgical samples
				- consider antifungal therapy
				- seek expert advice

		- IV to oral switch
			- criteria
				- clinically improving
				- haemodynamically stable
				- tolerating oral intake

			- oral continuation therapy
				- no penicillin hypersensitivity
					- amoxicillin-clavulanate

				- nonsevere penicillin hypersensitivity
					- cefuroxime
					- metronidazole

				- severe immediate penicillin hypersensitivity
					- if ceftriaxone tolerated
						- cefuroxime
						- metronidazole

					- if ceftriaxone not used/tolerated
						- trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
						- metronidazole

				- severe delayed penicillin hypersensitivity
					- trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
					- metronidazole

		- duration of antibiotics
			- no surgery
				- total IV + oral duration
					- 7–10 days

			- surgery performed
				- 4 days after adequate source control

		- follow-up colonoscopy
			- recommended 6–8 weeks after recovery

	- colonoscopy after diverticulitis
		- rationale
			- exclude malignancy mimicking diverticulitis

		- malignancy risk
			- uncomplicated diverticulitis
				- low risk
				- ~0.7%
			- complicated diverticulitis
				- higher risk
				- ~10.8%

		- high-risk CT features for malignancy
			- abscess
			- localised lymphadenopathy
			- relative absence of diverticula
			- focal mass effect
			- multiple areas of fat stranding

	- dietary advice
		- fibre
			- high-fibre diet recommended
			- associated with:
				- fewer complications
				- lower hospitalisation
				- less surgery

		- nuts, seeds, corn
			- avoidance not recommended
			- no evidence they increase risk

	- penicillin hypersensitivity
		- severe immediate reactions
			- anaphylaxis
			- airway angioedema
			- hypotension
			- collapse

		- severe delayed reactions
			- DRESS
			- SJS/TEN
			- severe blistering/desquamative rash
			- acute interstitial nephritis

	- multidrug-resistant organism risk
		- consider:
			- resistant Enterobacterales
			- VRE
			- Candida
		- seek specialist advice
