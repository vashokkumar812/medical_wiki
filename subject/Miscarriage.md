## terms
### Early pregnancy 
- gestation up to 13+6
- if > 14
	- refer to the obstetrician
### Retained products of conception ( RPOC) 
- Retained products of conception
	- with colleagues 
- ‘pregnancy tissue’ 
	- families
	- refer to the AP plane

### Miscarriage
- pregnancy loss less than 20 weeks
- The term ‘abortion’ should not be used

### GS
- mean sac diameter

## info

### ASUM criteria for miscarriage
1. VAGINAL US showing
	1. no fetal heart activity with fetal pole >7mm  ==OR==
	2. a gestational sac>25mm without fetal pole ==OR==
	3. the lack of gestational sac/fetal growth over a defined time period, no less than 7 days.

### types
#### Missed miscarriage
- confirmed ultrasound diagnosis of miscarriage with no passage of pregnancy tissue, and an intact intrauterine gestational sac
- includes
	- ‘early fetal demise’ 
	- an anembryonic pregnancy

#### Incomplete miscarriage
- some pregnancy tissue passed out
- history of pregnancy symptoms followed by an episode of heavy bleeding with the passage of clots with or without recognized pregnancy tissue.

#### complete miscarRiage
- previously sited intrauterine pregnancy which is deemed to be completely evacuated
- USG
	- not always necessary
	- decided after viewed pregnancy tissue, clinical symptoms
	- send pregnancy tissue to histopathological lab

#### [[hydatidiform mole]]
- based on USG and increased HCG
- Histology of pregnancy tissue is required for diagnosis
- refer to gynaecology => will do suction curettage ==> histopathalogy ==> consult with oncologist

#### septic miscarriage
- any type + evidence of intrauterine infection

	- expectant > medical > surgery

#### Expectant management
##### timeline
- upto 3 weeks

##### treatment given
- analgesia, anti emetics, NSAIDS with or without codeine
- medical certificate
- allow to eat and drink

##### Follow-up:
- Review at 1 week
  - Face-to-face or telephone review as appropriate
	  - If pregnancy tissue not passed:
	    - Consider ultrasound confirmation
	    - Discuss medical or surgical management based on woman’s preference
	  - If pregnancy tissue has passed or is very likely based on history:
	    - Symptom review at 1–2 weeks

- Imaging and β-hCG surveillance:
  - If intrauterine pregnancy was previously confirmed on ultrasound and woman is asymptomatic:
    - No repeat ultrasound required
  - If intrauterine pregnancy was not previously confirmed:
    - Arrange serial serum β-hCG monitoring to ensure appropriate decline
    - Further management guided by:
      - Symptoms
      - β-hCG trend
      - Ultrasound findings
    - Arrange review if the woman has concerns

- Recommend follow-up with local medical practitioner:
  - Within 4–6 weeks
  - Earlier if clinically indicated

#### MEDICAL MANAGEMENT
- must discuss with gynecologist


##### Treatment regimen

Medical management options:
- if 
	- Missed miscarriage + Intact gestational sac + CRL ≤25 mm
	  - Pre-treatment with Mifepristone ==> Misoprostol within 48 hours
- if Other miscarriage diagnoses: Incomplete miscarriage, Retained pregnancy tissue
  - Misoprostol alone
- Miscarriage >13 weeks gestation:
  - Inpatient management required
  - Refer to inpatient treatment protocol

#### Outpatient Medical Management Protocol

##### For missed miscarriage
- if missed miscarriage
	- Prescribe (off label):
	  - MS2step product
	  - Mifepristone 200 mg orally on Day 1
	  - Followed by Misoprostol:
	    - 800 mcg (2 × 400 mcg tablets) buccal at 24–48 hours
	    - Then a further 400 mcg (2 × 200 mcg tablets) buccal 4 hours later
- else
	- - Misoprostol 800 mcg (4 × 200 mcg tablets) buccal
	  - Followed by repeat dose:
    - 400 mcg (2 × 200 mcg tablets) buccal 4 hours later 
- on discharge
  - NSAIDs
  - paracetamol ± codeine
  - metoclopramide
  - analgesics + anti emetics :  30 minutes prior to first dose of Misoprostol
- Anti-D as indicated  
  - Refer to Anti-D (RhD) Immunoglobulin Use in Maternity Patients guideline

##### FDIU 13–23+6 weeks gestation

- Manage as inpatient
- Discuss with:
  - Acute Gynaecology consultant on call
  - Liaise with Obstetrics team

### Surgical management – suction curettage (Day Surgery Unit)

#### What is it
- Planned day admission for suction curettage

#### Key procedural steps (exam-relevant)
- Pre-operative assessment:
  - Screen for chlamydia and bacterial vaginosis if symptomatic
  - Can use self-collected swabs
  - Order other investigations as indicated
  - Plan for comorbidities (e.g. diabetes, latex allergy)
  - Arrange pre-operative blood tests if required

- Counselling:
  - Discuss contraception and future pregnancy plans
  - LARC options (IUCD, Implanon NXT®) can be inserted at time of procedure

- Consent:
  - Obtain written informed consent

- Cervical priming:
  - Recommended for all patients
  - Especially:
    - Previous caesarean section
    - Gestation ≥10 weeks
  - Misoprostol 400 mcg buccal
    - 90 minutes pre-procedure

- Medications (if indicated):
  - Misoprostol 400 mcg PV or buccal pre-op
  - Paracetamol 1 g + ibuprofen 400 mg orally

- Anti-D:
  - Administer as indicated (RhD guideline)

- Infection follow-up:
  - Check results
  - Ensure treatment and follow-up

### decision

1. if active pain or bleeding
	1. try
		1. miscarriage is in progress and pregnancy tissue can be removed from cervix at speculum examination
		2. if symptoms didnt resolve
			1. surgery
2. if signs of infection : uterine tenderness or purulent discharge
		1. prompt evacuation of the uterus with surgery and antibiotics
3. if concern with medical management
	1. do surgery

### incomplete miscarriage
- RPOCs <15 mm (AP measurement)
-  High likelihood of spontaneous expulsion without intervention
  - Expectant management

- RPOCs 15–35 mm (AP measurement)
  - Medical or expectant management

- RPOCs >35–50 mm (AP measurement)
  - Increasing volume of retained pregnancy tissue
  - Usually managed surgically
  - If medical management is chosen:
    - Consider admission
      - Observe for a few hours until most pregnancy tissue has passed
      - Ensure bleeding has settled to an acceptable level
    - Remaining pregnancy tissue
      - Likely to pass within 24 hours
      - May take longer in some cases
    - If no resolution with misoprostol:
      - Surgery is indicated
      - Perform speculum examination before deciding on:
        - Surgical evacuation of the uterus, or Discharge
	      - because Pregnancy tissue may already be present in the vagina

### complete miscarriage
  - certain diagnosis
    - Surgical and medical uterine evacuation are contraindicated if diagnosis is certain

  - If diagnosis is uncertain
    - No previously documented intrauterine pregnancy on ultrasound, or
    - Doubt regarding the nature of passed tissue
    - Then:
      - Arrange serial β-hCG measurements
      - Gynaecology review required
      - Aim: exclude ectopic pregnancy and confirm pregnancy resolution

  - Management approach
    - Treat according to symptoms
    - Expectant management usually appropriate

  - Imaging
    - Routine ultrasound not required
    - Ultrasound indicated if:
      - Persistent bleeding and no further pregnancy tissue seen on speculum examination, or
      - Non-resolving or plateauing β-hCG levels

  - Histology considerations
    - Decidua or fibrous clot may mimic pregnancy tissue
    - If any doubt:
      - Send tissue for histology
      - Ensure follow-up of results

  - Follow-up
    - Serial β-hCG measurements recommended
    - Continue until complete biochemical resolution is confirmed
