- Definition
	- BGL <4 mmol/L
- Treatment
	- IV glucose
		- 10% glucose 2 mL/kg bolus
		- Change fluids to:
			- 10% glucose + KCl
	- Important
		- Do NOT stop insulin
- Persistent Hypoglycaemia
	- Reduce insulin infusion
- Oral Treatment (if safe)
	- Criteria
		- pH ≥7.3
		- Alert patient
	- High GI carbohydrates
		- <5 years / <25 kg
			- 5–10 g carbohydrate
			- Examples:
				- Glucose tablets/gel
				- 50 mL lemonade
				- 100 mL cordial
				- 1 tsp honey
				- 2 jellybeans
				- 60 mL juice
		- >5 years / >25 kg
			- Larger equivalent doses
	- Recheck BGL
		- After 15–20 min
		- Repeat if BGL still <4 mmol/L
- Treat Precipitating Cause
	- Common causes
		- Missed insulin
		- Intercurrent illness
	- Infection
		- Investigate
		- Antibiotics if indicated
- Persisting Acidosis consider
	- Inadequate insulin
	- Poor rehydration
	- Sepsis
	- Hyperchloraemic acidosis
	- Toxins
		- e.g. salicylates
- Bicarbonate
	- Avoid routinely
	- indications
		- Life-threatening hyperkalaemia
		- Inotropic support required
	- Action
		- Discuss with ICU before use
	- paediatric consultation indications
		- All DKA cases
		- All new diabetes diagnoses
- ICU / Transfer Indications
	- Age <2 years
	- Coma
	- Shock
	- Seizures
	- Cerebral oedema
	- Severe acidosis
		- pH <7.1
		- HCO₃ <5
