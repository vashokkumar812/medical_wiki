# Anogenital Warts
* Cause
  - Human papillomavirus (HPV)
  - Most commonly HPV types 6 and 11
* Transmission
  - Direct skin-to-skin contact
    - Apparent lesions
    - Subclinical lesions
  - Contact with genital secretions
  - Microabrasions in recipient's skin
    - Allow viral entry into basal epithelial cells
  - Most HPV infections are asymptomatic
  - Long latent period
    - Presence of warts in only one partner does not necessarily indicate recent infidelity
* Pathogenesis
  - HPV infection produces type-specific immunity
  - Most anogenital warts caused by HPV 6 and 11
  - Treatment is cosmetic rather than curative
* Clinical features
  - Warty growths
    - Anogenital skin
    - Mouth
  - Usually little discomfort
    - Sometimes itching
    - Significant psychological distress may occur
  - Urethral lesions
    - Distorted urinary stream
    - Bleeding
  - Perianal lesions
    - Perianal itching
  - Anal lesions
    - Rectal bleeding after passage of stools
  - Cervical lesions
    - Detected during vaginal examination
    - Perform cervical screening as per national guidelines
* Complications
  - Oncogenic HPV genotypes may cause malignancy
    - Penis
    - Vulva
    - Vagina
    - Cervix
    - Anus
    - Oropharynx
* Diagnosis
  - Usually clinical
    - Based on visual appearance
  - Atypical lesions
    - Consider biopsy to exclude malignancy
    - Features suggesting atypical lesions
      - Variable pigmentation
      - Raised plaque-like lesions
      - Cervical warts
  - HPV PCR
    - Not used to diagnose anogenital warts
* Investigations
  - Any STI presentation provides an opportunity for comprehensive STI testing
  - Cervical lesions
    - Cervical screening as per national guidelines
  - Atypical lesions
    - Biopsy to exclude malignancy or precancerous changes
* Management
  - Treatment options
    - Patient-applied podophyllotoxin paint
      - Apply twice daily for 3 days
      - Follow with 4 days off treatment
      - Repeat weekly
      - 4–6 cycles until resolution
    - Patient-applied imiquimod 5% cream
      - Apply 3 times per week at bedtime
      - Wash off after 6–10 hours
      - Continue until resolution
      - Maximum duration: 16 weeks
    - Clinician-applied cryotherapy
      - Weekly treatment
    - Rarely required
      - Excision under local anaesthetic
      - Ablative therapy under general anaesthetic
      - Seek specialist advice
  - Treatment advice
    - Treatment is cosmetic rather than curative
    - HIV infection
      - Poorer treatment response
      - May require longer treatment cycles
      - Increased recurrence
  - Other immediate management
    - Avoid shaving or waxing pubic lesions
      - May cause microtrauma
      - Facilitates local spread through autoinoculation
    - Provide patient factsheet
    - Offer HPV vaccination if not previously vaccinated
      - Not a therapeutic vaccine
      - May protect against future acquisition of other HPV types
  - Notifiable condition
    - Genital warts are not notifiable
* Special treatment situations
  - Complicated or disseminated infection
    - Consider referral for laser or diathermy
    - Persistent intra-anal lesions in people living with HIV
      - Consider surgical excision
      - Consider HPV DNA typing to guide follow-up
  - Pregnancy
    - Warts may grow rapidly
    - Cryotherapy or diathermy can be used
    - Cryotherapy may have a poor response
    - Lesions often resolve spontaneously after delivery
      - Immune function returns to normal postpartum
    - Normal vaginal delivery is permitted
      - Risk of transmission to baby is extremely low
  - Atypical lesions
    - Consider referral and biopsy
    - Particularly in elderly people with new lesions
    - Biopsy to exclude malignancy
    - Biopsy atypical lesions especially in
      - Immunosuppressed patients
      - People living with HIV
  - Meatal warts
    - Treat with cryotherapy
  - Intra-anal warts
    - Cryotherapy
    - Refer for surgical management if indicated
  - Cervical warts
    - Initial cervical cytology
    - Refer to gynaecologist
      - Consider colposcopy
      - Consider biopsy
      - Treatment as indicated
  - Differential diagnosis
    - Syphilis
      - Condylomata lata
* Contact tracing
  - Not recommended
  - Most sexual partners have probably acquired infection subclinically
* Follow-up
  - Not required if symptoms resolve
  - Review if
    - Patient is anxious
    - Warts are difficult for patient to visualise
* Test of cure
  - Not applicable
* Retesting
  - Not routinely required
  - Consider testing for other STIs
    - If not performed at first presentation
    - Repeat after relevant window period
* Auditable outcomes
  - 100% of patients diagnosed with genital warts are provided with information
