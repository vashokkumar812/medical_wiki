## Epidemiology
  - Most common serious neurological disorder in pregnancy

- increased risk
    - Hyperprolactinaemia
    - Premature menopause
    - Hypothalamic amenorrhoea
    - Increased anovulatory cycles
    - reduced fertility
    - Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)


## management
- Goals before conception
	- Seizure-free for ≥6 months if possible
	- Monotherapy preferred
	- Lowest effective AED dose
	- Avoid changing AEDs once pregnancy established
	- Use divided doses to avoid high peak drug levels
-  Epilepsy
	- Not a contraindication to pregnancy
- folic acid supplementation
	-  5 mg daily
	- Start ≥1 month before conception
	- Continue throughout first trimester
- Contraception

  - Enzyme-inducing AEDs (EIAEDs)
	  - drugs
	    - Phenobarbitone
	    - Primidone
	    - Phenytoin
	    - Carbamazepine
	    - Oxcarbazepine
	    - Felbamate
	    - Topiramate

    - Effects
      - Reduce hormonal contraceptive efficacy
	  - Preferred contraception with EIAEDs
	    - Levonorgestrel-releasing IUD (Mirena)
	      - First-line option
	    - Depot medroxyprogesterone acetate (DMPA)
	      - May require administration every 10 weeks instead of every 12 weeks
	    - Barrier contraception
	
	  - Combined oral contraceptive pill (COCP)
	    - If required
	      - Use higher-dose oestrogen preparation
	        - 50 mcg ethinylestradiol
	
	  - Avoid with EIAEDs
	    - Progestogen-only pill (mini-pill)
	    - Levonorgestrel implant
	    - Etonogestrel implant

  - Non-enzyme-inducing AEDs
    - Valproate
    - Zonisamide
    - Benzodiazepines
    - Gabapentin
    - Levetiracetam
    - Pregabalin
    - Tiagabine
    - Vigabatrin

    - Hormonal contraceptive efficacy unaffected





- Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs)

  - Congenital malformation risk
	  - higher in AED exposure
  - Lamotrigine and COCP
    - COCP increases lamotrigine metabolism
    - Lamotrigine levels decrease by approximately 50%
    - May worsen seizure control

    - Management
      - Increase lamotrigine dose when starting COCP
      - Reduce lamotrigine dose when stopping COCP

  - Valproate (VPA)

    - Highest teratogenic risk

    - Risk significantly increased
      - Dose >1100 mg/day


	
	- Recommendation
	  - Avoid during pregnancy whenever possible

  - Relative risk of major congenital malformations

    - Valproate
    - Phenobarbitone
    - Topiramate
    - Carbamazepine
    - Phenytoin
    - Levetiracetam
    - Lamotrigine

  - Specific AED associations

    - Valproate
			- Oral clefts
			- Cardiac defects
			- Neural tube defects
			- Hypospadias
			- Neurodevelopmental effects
				- Reduced cognitive ability
				- Autism spectrum disorder

    - Phenobarbitone
      - Cardiac defects
      - Oral clefts

    - Topiramate
      - Cleft lip

  - Lamotrigine
    - No increased risk of major congenital malformations compared with untreated epilepsy
    - Preferred option in women of reproductive age

- Pregnancy Care

  - Effect of pregnancy on epilepsy

    - Seizure frequency
      - Usually unchanged

    - Seizure-free during pregnancy
      - increased

    - Factors increasing seizure frequency
      - Sleep deprivation
      - Medication non-compliance
      - Altered AED pharmacokinetics

    - Seizure-free for 12 months before pregnancy
      - Reduces seizure risk

  - Effect of epilepsy on pregnancy

    - Possible associations
      - Low birth weight
      - Lower Apgar scores
      - Pre-eclampsia
      - Antepartum bleeding
      - Placental abruption
      - Prematurity

  - Effect of epilepsy on fetus

    - Maternal seizures may cause
      - Fetal hypoxia
      - Fetal injury
      - Placental abruption
      - Miscarriage
      - Approximately fourfold increased risk of epilepsy

- Monitoring During Pregnancy

  - AED levels
    - Monitor at least once each trimester
    - More frequent monitoring if poorly controlled epilepsy

  - AED concentrations decrease during pregnancy
    - Phenobarbitone
    - Primidone
    - Carbamazepine
    - Phenytoin
    - Valproate

- Fetal Surveillance

  - Ultrasound at 11–13 weeks

    - Assess for
      - Acrania/anencephaly
      - Increased nuchal translucency

  - Morphology ultrasound
    - 18–20 weeks

  - Targeted assessment
    - Neural tube
    - Heart
    - Face

- Vitamin K

  - Maternal antenatal vitamin K
    - Not routinely recommended

  - Neonate
    - Phytomenadione (vitamin K) after birth

- Intrapartum Care

  - Increased seizure risk during labour

  - Contributing factors
    - Sleep deprivation
    - Anxiety
    - Hyperventilation
    - Poor AED absorption
    - Non-compliance

  - Management
    - Continue AEDs
    - Secure intravenous access
    - Avoid maternal exhaustion
    - Avoid hyperventilation

  - Generalised tonic-clonic seizure
    - Continuous CTG monitoring

  - Acute seizure treatment
    - IV lorazepam
    - IV diazepam

  - Delivery
    - Vaginal delivery usually possible

- Postpartum Care

  - Neurology review

  - AED monitoring
    - Plasma levels may fluctuate up to 8 weeks postpartum

  - Dose reduction for
    - Lamotrigine
    - Oxcarbazepine

- Breastfeeding

  - All AEDs are excreted into breast milk

  - Newer AEDs
    - Higher breast milk concentrations

  - Use caution with
    - Lamotrigine
    - Levetiracetam

  - Balance
    - Benefits of breastfeeding
    - Potential neonatal adverse effects

- Postpartum Safety Advice

  - Ensure medication compliance

  - Ensure adequate sleep

  - Reduce infant injury risk
    - Feed baby while seated on floor or low surface
    - Bathe baby with another adult present
    - Minimise carrying baby
    - Use pram with automatic brake
