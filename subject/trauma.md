- Fracture Assessment
  - Examine
    - Injured area
    - Joint above
    - Joint below
    - Neurovascular status
      - Pulses
      - Capillary refill
      - Motor function
      - Sensation

  - Imaging
    - AP X-ray
    - Lateral X-ray
    - Additional views as indicated

- Neurovascular Compromise

  - Features
    - Absent distal pulses
    - Limb ischaemia
    - Pallor
    - Cold limb
    - Delayed capillary refill
    - Neurological deficit

  - Initial Management
    - Immediate fracture reduction/alignment
    - Splint immobilisation
    - Repeat neurovascular examination

  - Most Appropriate First Step
    - Fracture reduction

  - If pulses remain absent
    - Urgent orthopaedic review
    - Urgent vascular surgery review
    - CT angiography if appropriate and does not delay treatment

- Open Fracture

  - Definition
    - Fracture + skin breach in same limb segment
    - Assume communication with fracture until proven otherwise

  - Risks
    - Infection
    - Osteomyelitis
    - Non-union

  - Initial Management
    - IV antibiotics immediately
    - Tetanus prophylaxis if required
    - Remove gross contamination
    - Sterile saline-soaked dressing
    - Splint immobilisation

  - Most Appropriate Definitive Treatment
    - Urgent operative irrigation and surgical debridement

- Open Fracture + Neurovascular Compromise

  - Priorities
    - Immediate fracture reduction
    - Restore perfusion
    - Neurovascular reassessment

  - Then
    - IV antibiotics
    - Tetanus prophylaxis
    - Sterile dressing
    - Splinting

  - Definitive Management
    - Urgent operative debridement
    - Orthopaedic ± vascular surgery

- Antibiotics in Open Fracture

  - Give as early as possible
  - Ideally within 3 hours
  - Delayed administration increases infection risk

- Tetanus Prophylaxis

  - Give according to immunisation status and wound risk

- Fractures Requiring Urgent Orthopaedic Referral

  - Open fracture
  - Neurovascular compromise
  - Displaced fracture
  - Compartment syndrome
  - Significant associated pathology

- Fractures Requiring Early Orthopaedic Follow-up

  - Scaphoid proximal pole fracture
  - Talus fracture
  - Calcaneal fracture
  - Salter-Harris III
  - Salter-Harris IV
  - Salter-Harris V

- Follow-up

  - First review
    - 7–14 days

  - Assess
    - Position
    - Alignment
    - Healing
    - Neurovascular status

  - Repeat X-ray if indicated

- Fracture Healing Stages

  - Inflammatory phase
    - First week

  - Soft callus
    - Weeks 2–3

  - Hard callus
    - Weeks 4–12

  - Remodelling
    - Months to years
