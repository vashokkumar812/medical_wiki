- Etiology
	- Caused by Parvovirus B19 infection
	- Transmission:
		- Respiratory secretions
		- Hand-to-mouth contact
		- Vertical transmission (mother to fetus)
		- Blood transfusions and blood products

- Pathogenesis
	- suppression of erythropoiesis ==> Anaemia, Red cell aplasia

- Most common
	- Most common childhood manifestation = erythema infectiosum (slapped cheek syndrome)
	- Most common adult manifestation = asymptomatic infection
	- Most common fetal complication = fetal anaemia

- Epidemiology
	- Household exposure = highest risk
	- School/daycare exposure = intermediate risk
	- Community exposure = lower risk

- Clinical features
	- Maternal infection
		- Often asymptomatic
		- Mild fever
		- Malaise
		- Erythematous rash
		- Slapped cheek rash (less common in adults)
		- symmetrical Arthralgia or arthritis
			- Hands
			- Wrists
			- Knees
			- Ankles
	- Fetal effects
		- Fetal anaemia
		- Hydrops fetalis
		- Pregnancy loss
		- acities
		- pleural effusiin
		- skin edema
		- Intrauterine fetal death
		- Multisystem organ damage


- Investigations
	- Initial
		- Parvovirus B19 serology
			- IgM
			- IgG
	- Most appropriate
		- Maternal Parvovirus B19 serology
	- If recent maternal infection confirmed before 20 weeks:
		- Serial fetal ultrasound every 1–2 weeks for 12 weeks
		- Middle cerebral artery (MCA) peak systolic velocity Doppler
	- If fetal anaemia suspected:
		- Maternal-fetal medicine assessment

- Treatment
	- Initial
		- Reassurance and counselling
		- Maternal serological assessment
		- Fetal surveillance if infection confirmed
	- Most appropriate
		- Serial ultrasound monitoring
		- MCA Doppler surveillance
		- Maternal-fetal medicine referral if:
			- Fetal anaemia
			- Hydrops fetalis
		- Intrauterine transfusion may be required for severe fetal anaemia

- Complications
	- Maternal
		- Chronic anaemia in immunocompromised women
		- Persistent infection
		- Red cell aplasia
		- Aplastic crisis
			- Sickle cell disease
			- Thalassaemia
	- Fetal
		- Fetal anaemia
		- Hydrops fetalis
		- Miscarriage
		- Stillbirth
		- Multisystem organ damage

- Prognosis
	- Maternal prognosis generally excellent
	- Risk of pregnancy loss:
		- higher before 20 weeks
	- Prognosis worsens if fetal hydrops develops

- Prevention
	- Hand hygiene
	- Avoid exposure where possible
	- Pregnant healthcare workers who are seronegative (IgG negative) should avoid caring for patients with complicated or chronic parvovirus infection
