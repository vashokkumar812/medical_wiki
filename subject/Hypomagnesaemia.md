## Definition
- **Serum Mg²⁺ < 1.3 mEq/L**
- Normal range: **1.3 – 2.2 mEq/L**

---

## Causes

### 1. Decreased Intake
- Malnutrition, starvation  
- Alcoholism  
- Prolonged TPN without Mg²⁺  
- Premature infants (low body stores)  

### 2. GI Losses
- Diarrhoea, malabsorption, steatorrhoea  
- Bowel resection, ileostomy  
- Laxative abuse  
- Acute pancreatitis (fat saponification → Mg²⁺ binding)  

### 3. Renal Losses
- Loop & thiazide diuretics  
- Aminoglycosides, amphotericin, cisplatin, cyclosporine  
- Renal tubular disorders  
- Hyperaldosteronism  
- Hypercalcaemia (competes with Mg²⁺ reabsorption)  

### 4. Redistribution
- Refeeding syndrome  
- Hungry bone syndrome (post-parathyroidectomy)  
- Insulin therapy (drives Mg²⁺ into cells)  

---

## Clinical Features

### Neuromuscular
- Tremor, fasciculations  
- Tetany, seizures  
- Positive **Chvostek** & **Trousseau signs**  
- [[weakness]], [[ataxia]], vertigo  

### CNS
- Irritability, confusion  
- Psychosis, [[depression]]  

### Cardiovascular
- **Arrhythmias** (esp. with [[Hypokalaemia]] & [[Hypocalcaemia]])  
- Torsades de pointes  
- Prolonged QT, PR & widened QRS  

### Electrolyte Associations
- **↓ K⁺** ([[Hypokalaemia]] resistant to correction until Mg²⁺ replaced)  
- **↓ Ca²⁺** (due to impaired PTH release & resistance)  

---

## Investigations
- Serum Mg²⁺ (may not reflect total body deficit)  
- Associated: low K⁺, low Ca²⁺, low phosphate  
- ECG → prolonged PR, QT, QRS widening, Torsades  

---

## Management

### Mild / Asymptomatic
- Oral Mg²⁺ salts (Mg oxide, Mg hydroxide, Mg glycerophosphate)  
- Correct associated **[[Hypokalaemia]]** & **[[Hypocalcaemia]]**  

### Moderate–Severe / Symptomatic
- **IV Magnesium sulfate**
  - 1–2 g (4–8 mmol) over 10–15 min (if arrhythmias/seizures)  
  - Maintenance: 4–6 g over 12–24 hr infusion  
- Reduce dose in **renal failure**  

### Special
- Torsades de pointes → IV MgSO₄ (even if serum Mg normal)
