- Limping child

- Key points
    - Most limping children
        - Do not require investigations
    - Gait observation
        - Helps localise pathology
    - Acute inability to walk/weight bear
        - Red flag
    - Septic arthritis
        - Orthopaedic emergency
        - Limp + swelling + reduced ROM ± fever

- Background
    - Common causes
        - Transient synovitis
        - Acute myositis
        - Minor trauma

    - Always consider serious pathology

    - Pain may be referred
        - Examine
            - Joint above/below
            - Spine
            - Abdomen

- Causes by age

    - 0–4 years
        - Trauma/non-accidental injury
        - Toddler fracture
        - Bone/joint infection
        - Transient synovitis
        - Acute myositis
        - DDH

    - 5–10 years
        - Transient synovitis
        - Acute myositis
        - Perthes disease
        - Inflammatory arthritis

    - >10 years
        - Stress fractures/sprains
        - Traction apophysitis
            - Osgood–Schlatter
            - Sever disease
        - SUFE
        - Inflammatory arthritis

- Causes at all ages

    - Infection
        - Osteomyelitis
        - Septic arthritis
        - Sepsis
        - Bursitis
        - Discitis
        - Epidural collection

    - Trauma
        - Including inflicted injury

    - Malignancy
        - Haematological
        - Bone
        - Soft tissue

    - Rheumatological/immunological
        - Reactive arthritis
        - Autoimmune arthritis
        - HSP
        - Vasculitis
        - Serum sickness
        - Post-infectious arthritis
        - Guillain–Barré syndrome
        - Acute rheumatic fever

    - Intra-abdominal/GU
        - Appendicitis
        - Ovarian torsion
        - Testicular torsion

    - Haematological
        - Sickle cell crisis
        - Haemophilia

    - Functional limp

- Assessment

- History
    - Symptoms >7 days
    - Recurrent presentations
    - Trauma
    - Severe localised joint pain
    - Pain waking from sleep
    - Urinary/bowel changes
    - Inability to weight bear
    - Fever
    - Night sweats
    - Chills/rigors
    - Rash
    - Weight loss
    - Lethargy
    - Anorexia
    - Recent viral illness
        - Myositis
        - Transient synovitis
    - Recent streptococcal infection

- Examination

- General
    - Fever
        - Absence does not exclude infection
    - Pallor
    - Petechiae
    - Purpura
    - Ecchymosis

- Gait assessment
    - Antalgic gait
    - Waddling gait
    - Regression of mobility

- Localisation
    - Bone vs joint vs soft tissue
    - Hip pathology may present as knee pain

- Joint examination

    - Look
        - Resting position
        - Symmetry
        - Leg length
        - Swelling/deformity
        - Skin changes

    - Feel
        - Heat
        - Tenderness
            - Including calf
        - Crepitus
        - Fluctuance

    - Move
        - Active movement
        - Passive movement
        - Compare both sides
        - Marked reduction in ROM
            - Suggests significant pathology
                - Septic arthritis

- Additional examination
    - pGALS screen
    - Neurological examination
    - Neurovascular examination
    - Abdomen
    - Scrotum
    - Spine
    - Signs of systemic disease

- Management

- No investigations if ALL present
    - No red flags
    - Mild/no discomfort
    - Ambulating
    - Clear diagnosis
    - Review plan within <7 days

- Imaging

- X-ray
    - Area of suspicion

    - Pelvis AP/frog-leg views
        - SUFE
        - DDH (>6 months)
        - Perthes disease

    - Normal X-ray does not exclude
        - Septic arthritis
        - Early osteomyelitis

    - Inflicted injury signs may be subtle

- Ultrasound hip
    - Detects effusion
    - Cannot differentiate
        - Septic arthritis
        - Transient synovitis

- MRI/bone scan
    - Persistent symptoms ≥7 days
    - Suspected osteomyelitis

- Laboratory investigations
    - Elevated WCC/CRP/ESR/platelets
        - Suggest infection/inflammation
    - May be normal in chronic infection

    - Blood cultures
        - Before antibiotics if possible

    - FBE/blood film
        - If malignancy suspected

- Treatment
    - Treat according to working diagnosis
    - Paracetamol
    - NSAIDs

    - Escalating analgesia requirement
        - Reassess diagnosis

    - Suspected infection
        - Follow bone/joint infection protocol

    - Septic arthritis
        - Urgent orthopaedic review

    - Follow local antibiotic guidelines

- Consult paediatrics when
    - Symptoms >7 days
    - Unable to weight bear after analgesia
    - Systemically unwell
    - Malignancy suspected
    - Concern for abuse
    - Suspected septic arthritis
    - Suspected inflammatory arthritis

- Transfer indications
    - Requires higher-level care

- Discharge criteria
    - Cause identified and managed

    - OR

    - Comfortable ambulation
    - High-risk diagnoses unlikely
    - Follow-up arranged
