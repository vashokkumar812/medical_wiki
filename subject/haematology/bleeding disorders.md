# haemophilia 

- Haemophilia
	- X-linked bleeding disorder
- epidrmiology
	- males
- Types
	- Haemophilia A
		- Factor VIII deficiency
	- Haemophilia B
		- Factor IX deficiency
- Key management principles
	- Do not delay factor replacement for investigations
	- Treat severe/suspected major bleeding immediately
	- If bleeding persists despite adequate factor replacement
			- Suspect factor inhibitor
	- Discuss all bleeding episodes with haemophilia treatment centre
	- Consult haematology before invasive procedures
			- Lumbar puncture
			- Arterial puncture
	- Never give IM injections
	- Treat before complete assessment if severe bleeding suspected

- classification

| Severity | Clotting Factor Level | Typical Bleeding Pattern |
|----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| **Severe haemophilia** | **<1%** | Frequent spontaneous bleeds; joint bleeds; muscle bleeds; bleeding after minor trauma |
| **Moderate haemophilia** | **1–5%** | Bleeding after minor injury; joint bleeds possible; severe bleeding after moderate trauma, surgery, or invasive procedures |
| **Mild haemophilia** | **>5–40%** | Bleeding mainly after major trauma, surgery, or invasive procedures |

- Investigations
	- Important principle
		- Investigations must not delay factor replacement
	- Imaging
		- Depends on
			- Mechanism of injury
			- Suspected bleeding site
	- Routine coagulation studies
		- Usually not required
	- Emicizumab (Hemlibra)
		- Interferes with coagulation assays
		- Standard coagulation tests unreliable
			- APTT
			- Factor VIII levels
			- Factor VIII inhibitor assays
	- with recurrent mucosal bleeding
		- FBE
		- Ferritin
			- Iron deficiency common
- General treatment principles
	- Most bleeds require factor replacement
		- Exceptions
			- Minor bruising
			- Minor soft tissue injury without functional impairment
	- Prompt factor replacement
		- Reduces pain
		- Prevents long-term complications
	- Invasive procedures
		- Only after factor replacement

	- Avoid
		- IM injections

- Clotting factor replacement
	- Do not delay in severe/life-threatening bleeding

	- Preferred products
		- Recombinant clotting factor concentrates

	- Plasma-derived concentrates
		- Occasionally used
			- Especially with factor inhibitors


	- Factor inhibitor
		- Suspect if
			- Persistent bleeding despite adequate factor replacement
			- Previously responsive patient now non-responsive

	- If inhibitors present/suspected
		- Use bypassing agents
			- Bypass inhibitor to allow clotting

	- Important logistical issue
		- Factor concentrates not routinely available in all hospitals
			- Regional/rural patients require
				- Management plans
				- Access to blood products



- Joint and muscle bleeds
	- Use P.R.I.C.E immediately

	- P = Protection
		- Immobilise affected area
			- Splints
			- Slings
			- Crutches

	- R = Rest

	- I = Ice
		- Reduces pain and bleeding

	- C = Compression
		- Gentle compression bandage

	- E = Elevation

- Venous access
	- Important issue
		- Frequent venepuncture required
		- Major fear/stressor in children

	- Principles
		- Minimise unsuccessful attempts
		- Reduce distress

	- Helpful measures
		- Distraction techniques
		- Relaxation techniques
		- Nitrous oxide sedation
		- Parent-assisted comforting

	- Bleeding risk after venepuncture
		- Apply pressure ≥3 minutes

	- IV cannulas
		- Usually not discharged home in situ
			- Unless specialist-approved

- Analgesia
	- Avoid
		- Aspirin
		- NSAIDs
			- Ibuprofen
			- Diclofenac
		- Reason
			- Worsen bleeding

	- Preferred analgesics
		- Paracetamol
		- Opiates
			- Severe pain

	- Adjunctive pain control
		- Splinting
		- Immobilisation

- Consultation
	- Consult local paediatric team for
		- Any bleeding episode in child with haemophilia

	- Also consult
		- Haemophilia treatment centre

- Transfer indications
	- Care exceeds local capability
	- Suspected intracranial haemorrhage
	- Neck/throat bleeding
	- Forearm/calf bleed
			- Risk of compartment syndrome
	- Hip/inguinal bleed
			- Risk of iliopsoas haemorrhage
	- Undiagnosed abdominal pain
	- Persistent haematuria
	- Severe pain from bleeding

- Discharge criteria
	- No active bleeding
	- Appropriate follow-up arranged
	- Family provided with management plan
		- Haemophilia treatment card

# von Willebrand Disease (VWD)

- epidemiology
	- Affects males and females equally
	- Most common inherited bleeding disorder
- pathology
	- Deficiency or dysfunction of von Willebrand factor (VWF)
- clinical features
	- Typical features
		- Easy bruising
		- Mucosal bleeding
			- Epistaxis
			- Oral mucosal bleeding
			- Menorrhagia
		- Post-operative bleeding
- Types
	- Type 1 (most common)
		- ↓ VWF levels
		- Mild bleeding

	- Type 2
		- Qualitative (functional) VWF defect
		- Variable bleeding severity

	- Type 3 (rare)
		- Near absence of VWF
		- Resembles moderate–severe haemophilia
			- Joint bleeds
			- Muscle bleeds

- Red Flags
	- Major haemorrhage
	- Head injury
	- Suspected internal bleeding
	- Immediate factor replacement required
	- Do not delay treatment for complete assessment

- Investigations
	- Treatment should not be delayed
	- FBE
	- Ferritin
		- Iron deficiency common with recurrent mucosal bleeding
	- Imaging as clinically indicated
	- Routine coagulation studies not routinely required

- Management

	- Severe or Life-threatening Bleeding
		- Immediate VWF/FVIII replacement

	- Tranexamic Acid
		- Effective for
			- Epistaxis
			- Oral bleeding
			- Menorrhagia
		- Can be used alone or with
			- DDAVP
			- Factor concentrate
		- Contraindicated in haematuria

	- Desmopressin (DDAVP)
		- Releases endogenous VWF and FVIII
		- Effective in
			- Mild–moderate Type 1 VWD
			- Proven responders
		- Occasionally effective
			- Type 2
		- Ineffective
			- Type 3
		- Not sufficient alone for major bleeding
		- Avoid/caution
			- Age <3 years
			- Seizure disorders
		- Risks
			- Hyponatraemia
			- Seizures
		- Fluid restriction required for 24 hours after administration

	- VWF/FVIII Concentrate (Biostate®)
		- Indications
			- Severe bleeding
			- Type 1 unresponsive to DDAVP
			- Type 2 VWD
			- Type 3 VWD
		- Preferred for
			- CNS bleeding
			- Major trauma
			- Surgery
			- Joint/muscle bleeding
			- GI bleeding

- Analgesia
	- Use adequate analgesia
	- Avoid
		- Aspirin
		- NSAIDs

- Haematology Consultation
	- Any child with VWD and bleeding
	- Prior to any invasive procedure

- Urgent Transfer Indications
	- Suspected intracranial haemorrhage
	- Neck or throat bleeding
	- Forearm/calf bleed
			- Risk of compartment syndrome
	- Hip or inguinal bleed
			- Risk of iliopsoas haemorrhage
	- Undiagnosed abdominal pain
	- Persistent haematuria
	- Severe pain from bleeding
	- Ongoing mucosal bleeding

- Discharge Criteria
	- No active bleeding
	- Follow-up arranged
	- Written management plan provided

# Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)

- Definition
	- Autoimmune thrombocytopenia
	- Diagnosis of exclusion
	- Triad
		- Isolated thrombocytopenia (<100 × 10⁹/L)
		- Well child
		- Normal FBE and blood film (except platelets)

- Key Facts
	- Most common cause of symptomatic acquired thrombocytopenia in children
	- Treatment based on bleeding severity, not platelet count
	- Majority do not require treatment
	- Intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) rare (<1%)

- Classification
	- Newly diagnosed ITP
		- Usually age 2–6 years
		- Often follows viral infection
		- Sudden onset
		- Spontaneous recovery within 3–6 months

	- Persistent ITP
		- Thrombocytopenia for 3–12 months

	- Chronic ITP
		- Thrombocytopenia >12 months
		- More common in adolescents

- Clinical Features
	- Sudden petechial rash
	- Easy bruising
	- Excessive bruising after minor trauma
	- Mucosal bleeding
		- Epistaxis
		- Gingival bleeding
		- GI bleeding
		- Haematuria
		- Menorrhagia
	- Child otherwise appears well

- Intracranial Haemorrhage (Rare)
	- Headache
	- Nausea
	- Vomiting
	- Lethargy
	- Irritability
	- Reduced consciousness
	- Focal neurological deficits

- Associated Factors
	- Recent viral infection
			- CMV
			- EBV
			- VZV
	- Recent live vaccine
			- MMR
	- Recent medication changes
	- Family history of autoimmune disease

- Red Flags (Suggest Alternative Diagnosis)
	- Bone pain
	- Limp
	- Weight loss
	- Anorexia
	- Fever
	- Night sweats
	- Jaundice
	- Infective symptoms
	- Arthritis
	- Myalgia
	- Dry eyes
	- Mouth ulcers
	- Recurrent infections
	- Family/personal history of bleeding disorder

- Differential Diagnoses
	- Leukaemia
	- Bacterial infection
	- Viral infection
	- Aplastic anaemia
	- Other bone marrow failure disorders
	- Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)
	- Drug-induced thrombocytopenia
	- Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP)
	- Haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS)
	- Inflicted injury
	- Localised petechiae after vomiting/Valsalva
			- SVC distribution

| Modified Buchanan Bleeding Score | Bleeding Risk | Description |
|----------------------------------|--------------|-------------|
| **0** | None | No new haemorrhage |
| **1** | Minor | Few petechiae (≤100) and/or ≤5 small bruises (≤3 cm), no mucosal bleeding |
| **2** | Mild | Petechiae (>100) and/or >5 bruises (>3 cm) |
| **3a** | Moderate (Low Risk) | Blood in nares, painless oral purpura, oral/palatal petechiae, buccal purpura along molars only, epistaxis ≤5 min |
| **3b** | Moderate (High Risk) | Epistaxis >5 min, haematuria, haematochezia, painful oral purpura, significant menorrhagia |
| **4** | Severe | Mucosal bleeding requiring intervention or suspected internal haemorrhage (brain, lung, joint, muscle, etc.) |
| **5** | Life-threatening | Documented intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) or life-threatening bleeding at any site |

# ITP – Investigations

- Initial Investigation
	- FBE + blood film only
	- Blood film must be reviewed
		- Exclude alternative diagnoses
	- Further investigations
		- Only if suggested by history, examination or complications

# ITP – Management

- Principles
	- Treat based on bleeding severity, not platelet count
	- Goal
		- Stop active bleeding
		- Not to normalise platelet count
	- Consider
		- Active bleeding
		- Future bleeding risk (eg surgery)
		- Psychosocial factors

- Low-Risk ITP
	- Features
		- Petechiae
		- Bruising
		- Painless oral/palatal petechiae
		- Blood crusting in nares
	- Management
		- Outpatient
		- Usually no treatment
		- Repeat FBE in 1 week
		- Family education

- Moderate ITP
	- Features
		- Epistaxis >5 min
		- Haematuria
		- Haematochezia
		- Painful oral purpura
		- Significant menorrhagia
	- Management
		- Often admit
		- Haematology review of blood film before treatment
		- Increase platelet count enough to stop bleeding
		- First-line
			- Oral prednisolone
		- Second-line
			- IVIG
			- If poor response or rapid platelet rise needed
		- Adjuncts
			- Tranexamic acid for epistaxis
			- ENT intervention if needed
	- Important
		- Avoid tranexamic acid if haematuria present

- Severe ITP
	- Features
		- Suspected internal haemorrhage
			- Brain
			- Lung
			- Muscle
			- Joint
		- Severe mucosal bleeding requiring urgent intervention
	- Management
		- Urgent haematology consultation
		- IVIG + pulse IV methylprednisolone
		- Platelet transfusion
		- IV tranexamic acid
		- Urgent surgical intervention if required
		- Consider thrombopoietin receptor agonists
			- Romiplostim
			- Eltrombopag

- Life-Threatening ITP
	- Features
		- Intracranial haemorrhage
		- Life-threatening bleeding at any site
	- Management
		- Emergency haematology-led management

# Haematology Consultation

- Severe or life-threatening bleeding
- Age
	- <1 year
	- >14 years
- Abnormal blood film
- Concern for alternative diagnosis
- Chronic ITP
- Head injury
- Signs of intracranial haemorrhage

# Family Education

- Provide written diagnosis information
- Avoid head injury
- Avoid contact sports
	- Football
	- Rugby
	- Soccer
	- Hockey
	- Martial arts
- Attend ED after any head injury
- Avoid high-risk activities
	- Horse riding
	- Skiing
	- Cycling
	- Skateboarding
	- Scooters
	- Trampolines
	- Playground climbing
- Avoid
	- NSAIDs
	- Antiplatelet drugs
	- Anticoagulants
	- Intramuscular injections
- Seek urgent review for
	- Significant bleeding
	- Symptoms of ICH

# Transfer

- Severe or life-threatening haemorrhage
- Required care exceeds local capability

# Discharge Criteria

- Family understands
	- Diagnosis
	- Management plan
	- Activity restrictions
	- Follow-up
	- When to attend ED
- Clear follow-up arranged
- Repeat FBE timing documented
