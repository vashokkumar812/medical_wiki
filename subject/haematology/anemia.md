## practise tips
- Iron-deficiency anaemia is blood-loss anaemia until proved otherwise
- It is possible to be tired from iron deficiency without anaemia
- Blood-loss anaemia is usually due to menorrhagia or gastrointestinal loss until proved otherwise. 
- Investigations for suspected anaemia should include FBE, ESR and iron studies
	- Others to consider are Hb electrophoresis, vitamin B12 and folate levels, and kidney function tests. 
- Hypothyroidism can cause a normocytic or a macrocytic anaemia
- A therapeutic trial of iron (without investigations) is indefensible
- Intramuscular injections of iron can tattoo so use with care: an IM iron dose is not ‘stronger’ than an oral iron dose
- If microcytic anaemia is not responding to treatment, consider sideroblastic anaemia.

- Iron deficiency anaemia (IDA)
	- pathophysiology
		- Iron requirements
			- Total requirement during pregnancy: 1000–1200 mg
			- Recommended intake: 27 mg/day
			- Demand increases from 2nd trimester
			- Peaks in 3rd trimester
	- Diagnosis of iron deficiency
	  - Ferritin <30 ng/mL suggests iron deficiency
	  - Typical laboratory findings
	    - ↓ Hb
	    - ↓ MCV (microcytic)
	    - ↓ MCH (hypochromic)
	    - ↓ Ferritin
	    - ↓ Transferrin saturation
	- complications
		- Maternal complications of IDA
		  - Fatigue
		  - Increased risk of postpartum haemorrhage
		  - Maternal depression
		- Fetal and neonatal complications
		  - Low birth weight
		  - Prematurity
		  - Neonatal iron deficiency
		  - Cognitive impairment
		  - Behavioural disorders
		  - Retinopathy of prematurity
	
	- Management of iron deficiency
		- First-line: oral iron
			- Common side effects
			  - Nausea
			  - Constipation
			  - Abdominal discomfort
			  - Dark stools
			- Monitor response
			- Reticulocytosis within 1 week
			- Hb rise within 2–4 weeks
			- Continue treatment
			- For 3 months after Hb normalisation or until 6 weeks postpartum
		- Intravenous iron
		  - Indications
		    - Intolerance to oral iron
		    - Inadequate response to oral iron
		    - Severe iron deficiency
		    - Late pregnancy requiring rapid correction
	  - Preparations
	    - Ferric carboxymaltose
	    - Iron polymaltose
	  - Generally used in 2nd and 3rd trimesters
