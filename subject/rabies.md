- cause
	- organism
		- Rabies virus
			- monkeys
			- dogs
		- Australian Bat Lyssavirus (ABLV)
			- bats 
				- flying foxes, microbats 
- transmission 
	- saliva of animals : animal bites, scratches, mucosal exposure
	- Human-to-human transmission not proven

- pathogenesis
	- infects nervous system → encephalitis & brainstem dysfunction → death

- epidemiology
	- **Not endemic in Australia** (see global distribution for endemic regions)

- clinical features
	-  Incubation
		- 5 days to many years
	- Clinical disease caused by rabies virus & other lyssaviruses is indistinguishable → termed **“rabies”**  
- if bat involved 
	- → keep bat (if safe) for ABLV testing  
- Investigations
	- **Not required** for asymptomatic children  
- Treatment
	- First aid
		- soap/detergent wash for 15 min 
		- local iodine-containing solution  
		- If wound open: clean ± debride, **do not suture**  
		- First aid substantially reduces infection risk  

- prophylaxis
	- general measures
		- avoid wildlife contact
		- vaccination prior to travel to endemic areas  
	- Post-exposure prophylaxis  
		- From mammals overseas **or** bats in Australia  
		- Category 2 bat exposures are higher risk than Category 2 mammal exposures

**Important notes:**  
- If unsure → discuss with Public Health Unit / Infectious Diseases team  
- First vaccine dose = **ASAP after exposure**  
- Check allergies: *Rabipur® contains egg protein*  
- Administer vaccine **away from HRIG site** (eg contralateral deltoid or anterolateral thigh)  
- HRIG
	- dose
		- 20 IU/kg
	- site
		- Infiltrate as much as possible into/around wound  
		- if remaining give on other limbs except vaccine site
- If painful
	- ensure analgesia ± sedation  
  - Do NOT use topical or local anaesthetic infiltration in wound  
- Consult **Immunisation Handbook** + Infectious Diseases team for immunocompromised children  
- Post-exposure prophylaxis started overseas
	- If documented appropriate vaccine course
		- continue with standard PEP regimen  
	- If unclear documentation
		- may need to restart at **Day 0** (consult Immunisation Handbook)  
- Notifications
	- Any **confirmed rabies/ABLV case** → urgent notification to Dept of Health  

- Discharge
	- When vaccination schedule planned  
	- Wounds reviewed
