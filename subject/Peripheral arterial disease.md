  
| PAD Category                              | Key Symptoms / History                                                                                           | Peripheral Pulses   | Important Differentials / Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Asymptomatic or ‘masked’ PAD              | • Asymptomatic but has PAD risk factors • Reduced physical activity masking symptoms                             | • Reduced or absent | • Mobility limitation due to other causes: – Sedentary lifestyle – Musculoskeletal disease – Comorbid illness (heart failure, COPD)                                                                                                                            |
| Intermittent claudication                 | • Reproducible pain, cramping or fatigue in calf, thigh or buttock • Occurs during exercise and relieved by rest | • Reduced or absent | • Neurogenic claudication (nerve root entrapment, spinal canal stenosis) • Venous claudication (post-DVT, venous insufficiency) • Musculoskeletal causes (hip or knee osteoarthritis)                                                                          |
| Chronic limb-threatening ischaemia (CLTI) | • Rest pain (worse on limb elevation) • Nocturnal pain • Tissue loss: ulceration, gangrene, necrosis             | • Absent            | • Trophic changes: hair loss, muscle wasting, clawed toes • Ulceration: punched-out, sloughy, necrotic • Gangrene: infection, blistering, abscess, skin breakdown • Must differentiate from: – Venous ulceration – Malignant skin ulceration – Neurogenic pain |

- Pathogenesis
	- Endothelial injury
		→ lipid deposition
		→ atherosclerotic plaque formation
		→ reduced limb blood flow
		→ ischaemia
		→ claudication/rest pain/tissue loss

- Risk factors
	- Smoking
	- [[DM]]
	- [[SHTN]]
	- Hypercholesterolaemia
	- Family history
	- AF
			- Embolism risk
	- Beta blockers
	- Anaemia

- Clinical features
	- Most common symptom
		- Intermittent claudication
			- Usually calf muscle pain
		- Present in only ~10% of patients

- Diagnosis
	- Based on
		- History
		- Examination
		- Ankle-brachial index (ABI)

	- If atypical exertional leg pain
		- Exercise testing
			- Active pedal plantar flexion/treadmill
			- Measure postexercise ABI

- Imaging
	- Indications
		- Considering surgical/endovascular intervention
		- Exclude aneurysm
			- Abdominal aortic aneurysm
			- Popliteal aneurysm

	- Imaging of choice
		- Duplex ultrasound (DUS)
			- Preferred radiological investigation

- Treatment

	- Nonpharmacological
		- Low-salt diet
		- Low-fat diet
		- Moderate sugar intake
		- Smoking cessation
		- Exercise program
			- Walk until pain occurs
			- Rest until pain subsides
			- Start
				- 30 min/session
			- Progress to
				- 60 min/session
				- 5 times/week
		- Weight reduction
		- Vitamin E
		- Garlic

	- Medical management

		- Antiplatelets
			- Clopidogrel
				- 75 mg/day
				- Superior to aspirin 325 mg/day

			- Notes
				- Not PBS-listed for primary prevention of PAD
				- Aspirin does not reduce vascular events in asymptomatic low ABI patients

		- Statins
			- Strongest evidence base
			- Not PBS-listed for asymptomatic PAD

		- Antihypertensives
			- Ramipril
				- 10 mg/day
				- Increases pain-free walking distance

		- Agents improving walking distance

			- Cilostazol
				- Improves walking distance in intermittent claudication
				- No evidence reducing cardiovascular events

			- Pentoxifylline
				- Marginal benefit only

	- No significant evidence
		- Beta blockers
		- Calcium channel blockers

- Referral to vascular surgeon
	- Diagnostic uncertainty
	- Critical limb ischaemia (CLI)
			- Rest pain
			- Ischaemic ulceration
			- Gangrene
	- Lifestyle/work-limiting claudication
			- No improvement after 4–6 months of
				- Exercise program
				- Risk-factor modification
				- Medical therapy
	- Patient/GP considering intervention

- Indications for surgery
	- Lifestyle-limiting intermittent claudication refractory to conservative therapy
	- Critical limb ischaemia

- Surgical options
	- Endovascular angioplasty
	- Stenting
	- Peripheral bypass surgery
	- Endarterectomy

- Choice of procedure
	- Endovascular angioplasty/stenting
	- Open surgical reconstruction
			- Peripheral bypass
			- Endarterectomy

- Prognosis
	- Symptomatic PAD
			- Worse prognosis than
				- Coronary artery disease
				- Cerebrovascular disease

- Screening
	- Routine PAD screening
			- Not currently recommended in Australia
