- Definition
	- Temporary arrest of intestinal peristalsis
	- Functional bowel obstruction without mechanical blockage

- Overview
	- Common after abdominal surgery
	- Causes diffuse bowel inactivity

- Causes

	- Postoperative State
		- Most common cause

	- Drugs
		- Opioids
		- Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)

	- Other Possible Causes
		- Peritonitis
		- Electrolyte imbalance
			- Especially hypokalaemia
		- Sepsis
		- Severe illness

- Symptoms
	- Nausea
	- Vomiting
	- Vague abdominal discomfort
	- No colicky pain
			- Helps differentiate from mechanical obstruction
	- Abdominal distension
	- Constipation
	- Obstipation

- Signs
	- Distended abdomen
	- Silent abdomen
	- Reduced or absent bowel sounds

- Investigations

	- Abdominal X-ray
		- Air present in:
			- Small bowel
			- Colon
		- Generalized gaseous distension

- Management

	- Supportive Treatment
		- IV fluids
			- “Drip”
		- Nasogastric decompression
			- “Suction”

	- Additional Measures
		- Correct electrolyte abnormalities
		- Stop precipitating drugs if possible
		- Early mobilization postoperatively

- Key Exam Points
	- Paralytic ileus is functional, not mechanical obstruction
	- Common after surgery
	- No colicky pain
	- Silent abdomen is characteristic
	- X-ray shows air in both small and large bowel
