- Silicosis
  - Occupational pneumoconiosis
  - Caused by inhalation of crystalline silica
    - Quartz
    - Cristobalite
    - Tridymite
  - Causes progressive pulmonary fibrosis
  - Disease may progress despite cessation of exposure

- Risk Factors
  - Stonemasonry
  - Engineered stone benchtop workers
  - Mining
  - Quarrying
  - Sandblasting
  - Construction work
  - Inadequate respiratory protection

- Clinical Features
  - Progressive exertional dyspnoea
  - Chronic cough
  - Reduced exercise tolerance
  - Finger clubbing
  - Fine inspiratory crackles
  - Advanced disease
    - Respiratory failure

- Examination
  - Finger clubbing
  - Bilateral crackles
    - Often upper zones

- Differential Diagnoses
  - Sarcoidosis
  - Tuberculosis
  - Hypersensitivity pneumonitis
  - Coal workers' pneumoconiosis
  - Asbestosis
  - Connective tissue disease-associated ILD
  - Lung malignancy
  - Berylliosis

- Initial Investigation
  - Chest X-ray

- Chest X-ray Findings
  - Upper and mid-zone nodules
  - Fibrocystic upper lobe changes
  - Upper lobe fibrosis
  - Hilar elevation
  - Hilar lymphadenopathy
  - Pneumothorax may occur

- Most Appropriate Investigation
  - High-resolution CT (HRCT) chest

- HRCT Findings
  - Micronodularity
  - Upper lobe fibrosis
  - Upper lobe volume loss
  - Hilar lymphadenopathy
  - Mediastinal lymphadenopathy
  - Confluent fibrotic masses
  - Bullae
  - Emphysema
  - Pneumothorax

- Additional Investigations
  - Pulmonary function tests
  - FBE
  - UEC
  - LFTs
  - Autoimmune screen
    - ANA
    - ENA
    - RF
    - Anti-CCP
    - ANCA
    - CK

- Pulmonary Function Tests
  - Restrictive pattern
  - Obstructive pattern
  - Mixed pattern
  - Reduced DLCO

- Progressive Massive Fibrosis (PMF)
  - Most severe form of chronic silicosis
  - Confluent fibrotic opacities >1 cm
  - Upper lobe predominance
  - Bilateral involvement
  - Paracicatricial emphysema
  - Radiating fibrotic bands
  - "Angel's wings" appearance

- Complications
  - Progressive respiratory failure
  - Secondary spontaneous pneumothorax
  - Tuberculosis
  - Lung cancer
  - Pulmonary hypertension

- Initial Management
  - Immediate cessation of silica exposure
  - Smoking cessation

- Treatment
  - No disease-modifying therapy available

  - Supportive management
    - Bronchodilators if airflow obstruction
    - Pulmonary rehabilitation
    - Influenza vaccination
    - Pneumococcal vaccination
    - Oxygen therapy if indicated

- Most Appropriate Management
  - Remove further silica exposure

- Advanced Disease
  - Respiratory specialist referral
  - Lung transplantation assessment

- Monitoring
  - Pulmonary function tests
  - Serial imaging

- Prognosis
  - Often progressive
  - May worsen despite exposure cessation
  - Advanced disease may require lung transplantation
