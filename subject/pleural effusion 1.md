- Child with pneumonia or persistent fever >48 hrs despite antibiotics
	→ Suspect parapneumonic effusion

- Initial assessment
	- History
		- Persistent fever
		- Tachypnoea
		- Cough
		- Respiratory distress
		- Pleuritic chest pain
		- Abdominal pain

	- Examination
		- Lethargy/unwell appearance
		- Hypoxaemia
		- Tachypnoea
		- Increased work of breathing
			- Retractions
			- Grunting
			- Nasal flaring
			- Splinting
		- Bronchial breath sounds/crackles
		- Reduced breath sounds
		- Dull percussion

- Assess severity
	- Severe respiratory distress?
	- Severe hypoxaemia/cyanosis?
	- Marked tachycardia?
	- Altered mental state?

	→ Clinical status more important than radiological size

- Differential diagnosis
	- Bacterial pneumonia
	- Tuberculosis
	- Mycoplasma
	- Malignancy
	- Foreign body
	- Congestive cardiac failure
	- Rheumatological serositis

- Investigations
	- Bloods
		- FBE
		- UEC
			- assess for SIADH/atypical HUS
		- CRP
			- monitor response
		- Blood culture
		- LFT
			- albumin
		- Coagulation profile if severe

	- Specific tests
		- Viral swabs
			- COVID-19
			- influenza
		- Pneumococcal PCR
		- TB testing if indicated

	- Imaging
		- CXR (AP)
		- Chest ultrasound
			→ key investigation
			→ differentiates:
				- simple effusion
				- complicated effusion/empyema

		- CT chest
			- not routine
			- consider if:
				- poor response
				- alternative diagnosis suspected

	- Pleural fluid analysis (if drain inserted)
		- Gram stain
		- MC&S
		- Pneumococcal PCR

- Determine stage
	- Simple effusion
		- Non-loculated
		- Sterile fluid

	- Complicated effusion/empyema
		- Loculated
		- Infected pleural fluid

- Management

	- Supportive care
		- Oxygen if needed
		- Fluid restriction
			- 2/3 maintenance
		- Analgesia
		- Encourage mobilisation

	- Antibiotics
		- Follow local guidelines
		- Empiric therapy
			- 3rd generation cephalosporin
			- Add MSSA/MRSA cover if indicated
		- Tailor to microbiology results

- Well child with small simple effusion?
	- Yes
		- Consider outpatient management
			- Oral antibiotics
			- Follow-up arranged

	- No
		- Admit to hospital

- Admission indications
	- Hypoxia
	- Respiratory distress
	- IV antibiotics required
	- Need hydration support
	- Further investigation required

- Definitive management based on stage
	- Simple effusion
		→ antibiotics alone

	- Complicated effusion/empyema
		→ drainage ± surgery

- Persistent fever/distress >48 hrs despite IV antibiotics?
	- Yes
		- Consult respiratory/surgical team
		- Consider drainage/source control

- Escalation
	- Consult paediatrics
		- all admitted children

	- Consult respiratory/surgical team
		- complicated effusion
		- severe respiratory distress
		- septic shock
		- persistent fever despite IV antibiotics

- Transfer indications
	- Surgical intervention likely
	- Significant comorbidities
	- High respiratory support requirement
	- Care exceeds local capability

- Discharge criteria
	- Afebrile
	- Off oxygen
	- Tolerating oral antibiotics
	- Adequate oral intake
