# Urinary Retention: Urethral Catheterization vs Suprapubic Catheter (SPC)

## Initial Management
- **Acute urinary retention → Attempt urethral catheterization first** (unless contraindicated).

---

# Indications for Urethral Catheterization (First-line)

Use a **urethral Foley catheter** in:
- Acute urinary retention (e.g., BPH)
- Chronic urinary retention requiring bladder drainage
- Neurogenic bladder requiring temporary drainage
- Monitoring urine output in critically ill patients
- Perioperative bladder drainage
- Immobilized patients requiring bladder decompression

---

# Contraindications to Urethral Catheterization

## Absolute (Do NOT pass a urethral catheter)
Suspect **urethral injury**, especially after trauma:
- Blood at the urethral meatus
- High-riding or non-palpable prostate on DRE
- Pelvic fracture
- Perineal/scrotal hematoma ("butterfly" bruising)
- Failed catheterization with significant resistance after trauma

➡ **Perform Retrograde Urethrogram (RUG) first.**

---

## Relative
- Known urethral stricture
- Recent urethral reconstruction
- Severe urethral pain or false passage
- Difficult catheterization after multiple failed attempts

---

# When to Perform Suprapubic Catheterization (SPC)

## Indications
Choose SPC when **urethral catheterization is impossible, unsafe, or contraindicated.**

### 1. Suspected urethral injury (major indication)
- Blood at meatus
- Pelvic fracture
- High-riding prostate
- Perineal hematoma

### 2. Failed urethral catheterization
- Multiple gentle attempts unsuccessful
- Tight urethral stricture
- False passage created
- Complete urethral obstruction

### 3. Long-term bladder drainage when urethral catheter is unsuitable
Examples:
- Severe urethral erosion
- Chronic urethral discomfort
- Recurrent catheter-associated urethritis
- Patient preference (selected cases)

### 4. After certain urethral or genital surgeries
When avoiding a urethral catheter is desired.

---

# Contraindications to Suprapubic Catheterization

## Absolute
- Non-distended bladder (unless image-guided)
- Bladder carcinoma at insertion site
- No safe access to bladder

## Relative
- Coagulopathy/anticoagulation
- Pregnancy
- Previous lower abdominal surgery (adhesions)
- Pelvic malignancy
- Morbid obesity
- Lower abdominal infection

# High-Yield Exam Pearls

- **Acute urinary retention → Foley catheter first.**
- **Blood at urethral meatus = Do NOT blindly catheterize.**
- **Pelvic fracture + urinary retention = Suspect urethral injury → RUG before catheterization.**
- **Failed urethral catheterization or urethral injury → Suprapubic catheter.**
- **SPC requires a sufficiently distended bladder unless image-guided.**
