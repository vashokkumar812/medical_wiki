- Multiple myeloma
	- Plasma cell malignancy
	- Key pathology
		- monoclonal paraprotein production
		- malignant plasma cell marrow infiltration
		- end-organ damage

- Epidemiology
	- Median age
		- older adults
	- MGUS progression risk
		- ~1%/year
	- Smouldering myeloma progression risk
		- ~10%/year

- Classic presentation = CRAB
	- C
		- hyperCalcaemia
	- R
		- Renal impairment
	- A
		- Anaemia
	- B
		- Bone lesions/bony pain

- When to suspect myeloma
	- Anaemia
		- normocytic or macrocytic
		- normal iron/B12/folate
		- no blood loss/haemolysis
	- Hypercalcaemia
		- PTH suppressed
		- vitamin D normal
		- no other explanation
	- Renal impairment
		- unexplained AKI/CKD
	- Bone disease
		- back pain
		- lytic lesions
		- vertebral crush fractures
		- pathological fractures
			- especially in younger patient
	- Elevated total protein/globulin
	- Recurrent bacterial infections
			- >2/year
	- Hyperviscosity symptoms
			- headache
			- confusion
			- vertigo
			- visual disturbance

- Anaemia in myeloma
	- Seen in ~70%
	- Usually
		- normochromic normocytic
		- sometimes mild macrocytosis
	- Features
		- fatigue
		- DOE
		- angina
	- Blood film
		- rouleaux formation

- Hypercalcaemia
	- Seen in ~13%
	- Symptoms
		- confusion
		- weakness
		- constipation
		- anorexia
		- polyuria/polydipsia
		- arrhythmias/coma
	- Labs
		- corrected calcium elevated
		- PTH suppressed

- Renal impairment
	- Seen in 20–40%
	- Causes
		- light chain deposition
		- hypercalcaemia
	- Usually asymptomatic creatinine rise
	- Severe
		- oliguria
		- uraemia

- Bone disease
	- Back pain common
			- up to 58%
	- Lytic lesions
			- ~80% at diagnosis
	- Typical lesions
			- vertebral wedge/compression fractures
			- pathological fractures
	- Imaging
		- skeletal survey
		- MRI STIR
			- more sensitive
		- CT if MRI unavailable

- MGUS
	- Asymptomatic
	- Criteria
		- paraprotein <30 g/L
		- marrow plasma cells <10%
		- no end-organ damage

- Smouldering myeloma
	- Asymptomatic
	- Criteria
		- paraprotein ≥30 g/L OR
		- marrow plasma cells ≥10%
		- no end-organ damage

- Multiple myeloma diagnostic criteria
	- Monoclonal protein
			- serum and/or urine
	- Bone marrow clonal plasma cells
			- or biopsy-proven plasmacytoma
	- Myeloma-related organ/tissue impairment
			- CRAB
			- recurrent infections
			- hyperviscosity
			- amyloidosis

- Key investigations
	- Serum electrophoresis (EPG)
			- monoclonal band
	- Immunofixation
			- identifies Ig class/light chain
			- sensitivity ~93%
	- Serum free light chains (FLC)
			- essential
			- improves sensitivity to 97–98%
	- Urine protein electrophoresis
	- FBC + blood film
	- U&E/creatinine
	- Calcium/phosphate/magnesium
	- LFTs
	- LDH
	- β2 microglobulin
	- Skeletal survey
	- Bone marrow aspirate + trephine
			- definitive diagnosis
	- Cytogenetics/FISH
	- MRI STIR
			- if skeletal survey negative but suspicion high

- Important paraprotein facts
	- IgG/IgA most common
	- IgM paraprotein
			- think Waldenström macroglobulinaemia
	- Non-secretory myeloma
			- ~2%
			- no detectable paraprotein

- Prognosis
	- International Staging System
	- Stage I
			- β2 microglobulin <3.5
			- albumin >35
			- median survival ~62 months
	- Stage III
			- β2 microglobulin >5.5
			- median survival ~29 months
	- Other prognostic markers
			- cytogenetics/FISH
			- plasma cell labelling index

- Treatment principles
	- Symptomatic myeloma
			- treat immediately
	- Younger/fit patients
			- autologous stem cell transplant
	- Initial induction therapy
			- steroids
			- cytotoxic chemotherapy
				- cyclophosphamide
				- doxorubicin
			- immunomodulator
				- thalidomide
				- lenalidomide
			- proteasome inhibitor
				- bortezomib

- Drug selection pearls
	- Bortezomib
			- preferred in renal impairment/advanced disease
	- Lenalidomide
			- preferred if peripheral neuropathy

- Maintenance therapy
	- Thalidomide ± prednisone
	- Lenalidomide
	- Benefits
			- improved progression-free survival
	- Risks
			- toxicity
			- second malignancies

- Non-transplant candidates
	- Steroids + cytotoxic + thalidomide/bortezomib

- Bisphosphonates in myeloma
	- Important adjunctive therapy
	- Benefits
			- reduce fractures
			- reduce skeletal events
			- reduce pain
	- Zoledronic acid
			- monthly IV
			- may improve survival
	- Adverse effects
			- hypocalcaemia
			- renal dysfunction
			- osteonecrosis of jaw (ONJ)
	- Prevent ONJ
			- dental work before treatment

- Monitoring MGUS/smouldering myeloma
	- Watchful waiting
	- Serial paraprotein monitoring
	- Assess for end-organ damage
	- Follow-up
			- every 3–12 months depending on risk

- AMC pearls
	- Unexplained CRAB symptoms → think myeloma
	- Young patient with “osteoporotic” crush fracture → exclude myeloma
	- Hypercalcaemia + suppressed PTH → consider myeloma
	- Normocytic anaemia + raised globulins → myeloma until proven otherwise
	- Back pain + renal failure + anaemia = classic exam stem
	- SPEP + immunofixation + FLC = core diagnostic triad
