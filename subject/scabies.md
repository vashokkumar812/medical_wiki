- Scabies

	- General measures for scabies

		- Use general measures in addition to medical treatment

		- Prevent transmission

			- Consider examining
				- Family members
				- Close contacts

			- Treat empirically or as necessary
				- Family members
				- Close contacts

		- Environmental cleaning

			- Wash
				- Clothes
				- Towels
				- Bedding

			- Include items from
				- Patient
				- Family members
				- Close contacts

			- Preferably use hot cycle
				- 60°C

			- Alternatively
				- Store in sealed plastic bag
				- For 8 days

			- Reason
				- Mites unlikely to survive >7 days away from host
				- Includes mites hatching from eggs

		- Heat treatment

			- Use
				- Iron
				- Hot clothes dryer

		- Sun exposure

			- Place in sun if possible
				- Mattresses
				- Pillows
				- Blankets

		- Vacuum thoroughly

			- House

			- Soft furnishings
				- Lounges

		- Items unable to be washed/sunned

			- No body contact for 3 days

		- Outbreaks in closed communities

			- E.g. residential aged care facilities

			- Treat all
				- Residents
				- Staff with contact exposure

			- Quarantine affected area if possible

			- If staff worked elsewhere
				- Assess those areas also

			- Identify outbreak source
				- Consider crusted scabies
					- Highly contagious

		- School-aged child with scabies

			- Notify school

			- Treatment of unaffected children
				- Not required

			- Return to school
				- After two treatments completed
				- 1 week apart

		- High prevalence regions

			- Scabies prevalence ≥10%

			- Consider ivermectin-based mass drug administration
				- MDA

			- References
				- Engelman et al 2021
				- Kearns et al 2015
				- Wong et al 2002

- Treatment of crusted scabies
	- Norwegian scabies

	- Characteristics

		- Very high mite burden

		- More likely in
			- Immunocompromised patients
			- Physically incapacitated patients
			- Remote Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities

		- Highly transmissible

		- Notifiable disease
			- Some Australian states/territories

	- Prevent transmission

		- Empirically treat
			- Family members
			- Close contacts

		- Refer
			- General measures for scabies

	- Specialist involvement

		- Infectious diseases physician

		- Dermatologist

	- Combination treatment required

		- Oral ivermectin

		- Frequent topical scabicide

			- Permethrin

			- Benzyl benzoate
				- Sometimes combined with tea tree oil

			- Sulfur

			- Crotamiton

		- Frequent topical keratolytic

			- Salicylic acid 5–10% in sorbolene cream

			- Lactic acid 5% + urea 10% in sorbolene cream

	- Topical scabicide schedule

		- Every second day
			- First week

		- Then twice weekly
			- Until clinically cleared

	- Topical keratolytics

		- Reduce scaling

		- Apply daily after washing

		- Apply on days topical scabicide not used
