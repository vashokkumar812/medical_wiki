# acne inversa / hiradenitis suppurativa
![[Pasted image 20260531200435.png]]

![[Pasted image 20260531200446.png]]

- Definition

	- Chronic inflammatory follicular occlusive disease

	- Affects intertriginous areas

	- Characterised by recurrent
		- Painful inflammatory nodules
		- Abscesses
		- Sinus tracts
		- Fistulae
		- Scarring

- Epidemiology
	- female
	- family history

- pathogenesis

	- primary event ==> Follicular occlusion is primary event
	- then, systemic inflammation

- Risk Factors

	- Smoking

	- Obesity

	- Family history

	- Androgen dysfunction

	- Insulin resistance

	- Metabolic syndrome

	- Mechanical stress
		- Tight clothing
		- Friction

- Classification

	- Hurley Stage I
		- Single or multiple abscesses
		- No sinus tracts
		- No scarring

	- Hurley Stage II
		- Recurrent abscesses
		- Sinus tracts present
		- Scarring present
		- Single or multiple widely separated lesions

	- Hurley Stage III
		- Diffuse or near diffuse involvement
		- Multiple interconnected sinus tracts

- Clinical Features

	- Typical Sites
		- Axillae
		- Inframammary folds
		- Intermammary folds
		- Groin
		- Perineum
		- Perianal region
		- Buttocks


	- Acute Lesions
		- multiple painful deep nodules + abscess

	- Chronic Lesions
		- Comedones
		- Sinus tracts
		- Fistulae
		- Scarring

	- symptoms
		- Chronic discharge
		- Malodour
		- Lymphoedema
		- Pain
			- Prominent symptom


- Diagnosis

	- Requires all 3 criteria

		- Typical lesions
			- Deep-seated painful nodules

		- Typical anatomical distribution
			- Intertriginous areas

		- Chronicity and recurrence

	- Full skin examination

	- Determine Hurley stage

	- Assess comorbidities

- Investigations

	- Blood tests
		- Inflammatory markers

	- Metabolic assessment
		- Glucose status
		- Insulin resistance
		- Lipid profile

	- Female patients
		- Androgen profile

- Comorbidities

	- Obesity

	- Acne

	- Hyperlipidaemia

	- Depression

	- Insulin resistance

	- Pilonidal sinus

	- Polycystic ovary syndrome

	- Diabetes mellitus

	- Hypertension

	- Keratosis pilaris

- Associated Conditions

	- Pyoderma gangrenosum

	- Spondyloarthropathies

	- Crohn disease

	- Follicular occlusion tetrad
		- Hidradenitis suppurativa
		- Dissecting cellulitis
		- Acne conglobata
		- Pilonidal sinus

	- Down syndrome
		- Earlier onset
		- Often prepubertal
		- More treatment resistant

- Psychological Impact

	- Social isolation

	- Anxiety

	- Depression

	- Suicide risk

	- Sexual dysfunction

	- Stigma

	- Impaired quality of life

- Differential Diagnosis

	- Recurrent boils

	- Furunculosis

	- Pilonidal disease

	- Crohn disease

	- Infected cysts

- Management

	- General



		- Smoking cessation

		- Weight loss



		- Monitor inflammatory markers

	- Topical Therapy

		- Antiseptic wash

		- Mild disease
			- Topical clindamycin

	- Systemic Therapy

		- Indications
			- Moderate disease
			- Severe disease
			- Failure of topical treatment

		- First-line
			- Doxycycline
			- Minocycline

		- Combination antibiotics
			- Clindamycin
			- Rifampicin

		- Corticosteroids
			- Short-term oral steroids for acute flares
			- Intralesional corticosteroids for localised flares

	- Biologic Therapy

		- Anti-TNF agents

			- Adalimumab
				- Approved for moderate-to-severe HS

			- Infliximab

		- Precautions
			- Infection risk
			- Tuberculosis screening required
			- Avoid live vaccines

	- Surgical Management

		- Local excision

		- Incision and drainage

		- Deroofing

		- Wide excision with grafting

		- Best for medically non-responsive lesions

		- Radical excision reserved for severe disease

	- Laser Therapy

		- Nd:YAG laser

		- Intense pulsed light (IPL)

	- Pain Management

		- Multidisciplinary approach

		- Pain assessment and scoring

		- NSAIDs

		- Paracetamol

		- Opioids

		- Gabapentin

		- Pregabalin

		- Duloxetine

		- Venlafaxine

		- Corticosteroids for acute painful flares

	- Wound Care

		- Absorbent dressings

		- Breathable dressings

		- Non-adhesive dressings

		- Reduce friction and trauma

		- Control discharge and odour

- Complications

	- Chronic pain

	- Scarring

	- Fistula formation

	- Sinus tracts

	- Lymphoedema

	- Sexual dysfunction

	- Depression

	- Social isolation

	- Squamous cell carcinoma
		- Rare
		- Associated with chronic scarring

- Prognosis

	- Progressive if untreated

	- Smoking cessation may reduce severity

	- Weight loss may improve disease activity

	- Variable clinical course

- Referral

	- Moderate disease

	- Severe disease

	- Failure of first-line therapy

	- Biologic therapy consideration

	- Significant psychological morbidity
