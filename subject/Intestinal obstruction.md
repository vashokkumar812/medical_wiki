# intussception
- Definition
	- Telescoping (invagination) of proximal bowel into distal bowel
- Most common type
	- Ileocolic intussusception 

- Epidemiology
	- Most common
		- 3 months–2 years
	- Male
- Etiology
	- Idiopathic (most common)
		- ~1/3 have recent viral illness
	- Pathological lead point (older children)
		- Meckel diverticulum
		- Lymphoma
		- Polyps (Peutz-Jeghers syndrome)
		- Recent bowel surgery
	- Associated conditions
		- Henoch-Schönlein purpura (IgA vasculitis)

- Clinical features
	- Most common
		- Intermittent (colicky) abdominal pain
		- Vomiting
	- Pain
		- Severe
		- Cyclical
		- Lasts 4–5 minutes
		- Child well between episodes
	- Vomiting
		- Early non-bilious
		- Bilious = late sign (bowel obstruction)
	- Pallor (during painful episodes)
	- Poor feeding
	- Lethargy
		- May be the only presenting feature
	- Diarrhoea (early)
		- May mimic gastroenteritis
	- Rectal bleeding / "red currant jelly" stool
		- Late sign
		- Suggests bowel ischaemia
	- Classic triad (<15%)
		- Intermittent abdominal pain
		- Palpable abdominal mass
		- Red currant jelly stool

- Examination
	- Sausage-shaped abdominal mass
		- Usually right abdomen
		- Crosses midline
	- Abdominal distension
		- Suggests bowel obstruction
	- Tenderness/guarding
		- Suggests perforation/peritonitis
	- Rectal examination
		- Rarely indicated
	- Hypovolaemic shock (infants)

- Diagnosis
	- High index of suspicion
	- Consider in infants with:
		- Intermittent distress
		- Pallor
		- Vomiting
		- Unexplained lethargy


- Investigations
	- Initial
		- Ultrasound (investigation of choice)
	- Most appropriate
		- Ultrasound
			- Target sign / doughnut sign
	- POCUS
		- Useful to confirm diagnosis
		- Not reliable to exclude
	- AXR
		- Only if obstruction/perforation suspected
		- Normal AXR does NOT exclude diagnosis
		- Findings
			- Empty right lower quadrant
			- Crescent sign
			- Soft tissue mass
			- Small bowel obstruction
			- Pneumoperitoneum
- Treatment
	- Initial
		- ABC approach
		- Secure IV access
		- IV 0.9% saline bolus if shocked
		- Maintenance IV fluids
		- Keep NBM
		- Adequate analgesia
			- Intranasal fentanyl
			- IV morphine
		- NG tube if:
			- Obstruction
			- Perforation
			- Air transfer
		- Early surgical consultation
	- Most appropriate
		- Air/contrast (pneumatic or hydrostatic) enema
			- Diagnostic and therapeutic
			- Success >80%
			- Repeat enema may increase success by ~10%
			- Consider procedural sedation
		- Surgery if:
			- Failed enema
			- Perforation
			- Peritonitis
			- Shock
			- Unstable patient

- Special type
	- Ileo-ileal intussusception
		- Usually viral
		- Mild symptoms
		- Often spontaneous resolution
		- Rarely requires enema

- Complications
	- Bowel obstruction
	- Bowel ischaemia
	- Necrosis
	- Perforation
	- Peritonitis
	- Septic/hypovolaemic shock
	- Recurrence
			- Usually within 24–48 hours

- Prognosis
	- Excellent with early reduction
	- Delayed diagnosis increases risk of bowel necrosis and surgery

- Follow-up
	- Usually admit under surgical team after reduction
	- Trial oral feeds after ~2 hours
	- Consider discharge after successful feed and observation![[Pasted image 20260610071841.png]]
