- blepharitis
	- Definition
		- Inflammation of the eyelid
	- Pathogenesis
		- Staphylococcal enzymes and toxins
		- Immune-mediated damage
	- Associated conditions
		- Seborrhoeic dermatitis
		- Acne
		- Rosacea
	- Types
		- Anterior blepharitis
			- Involves
				- Eyelid skin
				- Eyelashes
			- Symptoms
				- Burning
				- Grittiness
		- Posterior blepharitis
			- Often synonymous with MGD
			- Commonly associated with rosacea
			- Signs
				- Red eyelid margins
				- Blocked meibomian glands
				- Frothy discharge along eyelid margins
	- treatment
		- anterior blepharitis
			- If hygiene inadequate
				- Chloramphenicol 1% ointment
		- posterior blepharitis
			- If hygiene inadequate
				- Doxycycline

- definition
	- loss of homeostasis of the tear film

- classification
	- Evaporative dry eye (EDE)
		- Most common cause
			- Meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD)
			- MGD may involve
				- Inflammation
				- Hypersecretion
				- Abnormal meibomian gland excreta
			- treatment
				- lid massage
					- Several times daily
					- Towards gland openings
				- warm compresses
					- Eyes closed
					- 2–5 minutes
				- eyelid hygiene
					- Eyelid wipes
					- Eyelid cleansing solutions
	- Aqueous deficient dry eye (ADDE)
	- Both EDE and ADDE result in
		- Sjögren syndrome
			- Classically causes ADDE
			- Meibomian gland involvement may also cause EDE
	- ![[Pasted image 20260521175537.png]]


- symptoms
	- Dryness
	- Grittiness
	- Burning
	- Stinging
	- Photophobia
	- Intermittent blurry vision
	- Eye fatigue
	- Fluctuating vision
		- Often diurnal variation
	- Itching
		- Consider allergic conjunctivitis
	- worsened by
		- Environmental irritants
		- Prolonged screen use
		- Wind
		- Air conditioning
		- Low humidity
	- symptom assessment tools
		- DEQ-5 (5-item Dry Eye Questionnaire)
			- Assesses
				- Presence of symptoms
				- Frequency
				- Severity
				- Time of day symptoms are worst
			- Positive
				- Score ≥6
		- OSDI (Ocular Surface Disease Index)
			- Assesses
				- Ocular symptoms
				- Vision-related function
				- Environmental triggers
			- Severity
				- 13–22
					- Mild DED
				- 23–32
					- Moderate DED
				- ≥33
					- Severe DED

- potential causes
	- Previous transplantation
		- Graft-versus-host disease
	- facial surgery complications
		- Trigeminal nerve injury
			- Neurotrophic keratitis
		- Facial nerve injury
			- Exposure keratopathy
		- Both increase DED severity
	- Dandruff-like scalesa
		- Suggest blepharitis

- slit lamp examination
	- if slit lamp unavailable
		- Ophthalmoscope with high plus lens

- tear film height
	- Normal
		- 0.3 mm
	- <0.2 mm
		- Suggests aqueous deficient dry eye (ADDE)

- anterior chamber examination
	- Cells and flare
		- Suggest uveitis
		- Suggest intraocular inflammation
		- Associated with systemic autoimmune disease
		- Same-day ophthalmology discussion required

- investigations
	- office tests
		- Tear break-up time (TBUT)
			- Measures tear film stability
			- Procedure
				- Fluorescein strip + preservative-free saline
				- Patient blinks
				- Viewed under cobalt blue light
				- Time measured from last blink to first dry spot
			- Interpretation
				- <10 seconds
					- Abnormal
					- Suggestive of dry eye
		- Ocular surface staining
			- Uses fluorescein
			- Abnormal
				- >5 corneal spots
				- >9 conjunctival spots
				- Lid margin epitheliopathy
					- ≥2 mm length
					- ≥25% width
		- Schirmer test
			- Measures aqueous tear production
			- Procedure
				- Filter paper placed in lower lid
				- Eyes closed
				- Wetting measured after 5 minutes
			- Interpretation
				- <10 mm wetting in 5 minutes
					- Abnormal
			- Notes
				- No universal cut-off
				- Estimates reflex tear production


	- if unavailable
		- Refer to optometrist


- blood tests
	- Not routinely required
	- Perform if systemic disease suspected

- management
	- non-pharmacological management
		- Reduce screen time
		- Lower screen height
		- Increase eye-rest frequency
		- Avoid allergens and irritants
		- Avoid preserved eye drops
		- Humidify home and workplace
		- Reduce air conditioner and heater exposure
		- Avoid eye rubbing
		- Smoking cessation
		- Avoid second-hand smoke
		- Reduce alcohol intake
		- Correct contact lens use
		- Wear sunglasses
		- Use wrap-around glasses in windy environments
		- Increase dietary omega-3 intake

	- omega-3 fatty acids 
		- anti inflammatory effect

- pharmacological management
	- first-line
		- Ocular lubricants
	- gels
		- Longer relief
		- May blur vision
	- ointments
		- Useful overnight
		- Helpful for morning symptoms
	- preservative-free lubricants
		- Preferred in severe disease
		- Less epithelial toxicity
	- lipid-stabilising agents
		- Mimic natural tear film
		- Improve tear film stability
- ocular lubricants
	- aqueous tear lubricants

		- Carmellose sodium
		- Hypromellose
		- Sodium hyaluronate
		- Carbomer 980

	- lipid tear supplements

		- Phospholipid liposome spray
	- ointments
		- Liquid paraffin
		- White soft paraffin



- adjunctive therapy
	- Manuka honey drops
		- Useful in blepharitis-associated DED
		- May cause ocular irritation

	- Consider rheumatology referral

		- Close monitoring
		- Compounded therapies

		- Long-term risks

			- Cataract

	- Testosterone eye drops
