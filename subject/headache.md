- Blue flag headaches
	- Occipital headache radiating to temple
		- Worse with neck movement
		- Cervicogenic headache/cervical spondylosis
	- Headache after whiplash injury
	- Reading-related headache
		- Eye strain
	- Medication-related headache
		- Vasodilators
	- Headache with systemic viral illness
		- Influenza

- Red flag headaches
	- New headache in high-risk settings
		- Cancer
		- HIV
		- Neck manipulation/trauma
		- Mild head trauma in elderly
			- Subdural haematoma
	- Persistent new headache
	- Focal neurological signs/symptoms
	- Neurological deficits preceding/outlasting headache
	- Progressive headache
		- Suggests mass lesion
	- Sudden-onset headache
		- Suggests SAH/cerebral bleed
	- Headache with rash
		- Meningococcal meningitis
		- Lyme disease
	- Persistent unilateral temporal headache
		- Suggests cranial arteritis
	- Raised ESR with headache
		- Cranial arteritis
		- Collagen disease
		- Systemic infection
	- Headache with papilloedema
		- Raised intracranial pressure
	- Non-migraine headache in pregnancy/postpartum
		- Cerebral venous thrombosis
	- Headache triggered by cough/straining
		- Mass lesion
		- Subarachnoid bleed
	- Postural headache
		- Low CSF pressure
		- CSF leak
	- Headache with visual disturbance
		- Glaucoma
		- Optic neuritis




- Frequency-based classification
	- Episodic headache
		- <15 headache days/month
	- Chronic daily/frequent headache
		- >15 headache days/month


# Primary Headaches – High-Yield

- **Patient behaviour during attacks**
	- **Migraine**
		- Lies quietly
		- Seeks a dark, quiet room
		- Attempts to sleep
		- Avoids movement/disturbance
	- **Cluster headache**
		- Agitated/restless
		- Cannot lie still
		- Paces or walks
		- May rock, hit head, or run
	- **Photophobia with lying still may also occur in**
		- Meningitis
		- Encephalitis
		- Subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH)

---

- **Onset & time course**
	- **Migraine**
		- Gradual onset
		- Prodrome 24–48 hours before attack
		- Evolves over hours
	- **Cluster headache**
		- Abrupt onset
		- Peaks within ~5 minutes
		- Lasts 15–180 minutes
		- Gradual resolution
	- **Tension-type headache**
		- Gradual onset
		- Slow progression and resolution

---

- **Chronic daily headache (>15 days/month)**
	- **Major types**
		- Chronic migraine
		- Chronic tension-type headache
		- New daily persistent headache (NDPH)
		- Hemicrania continua

---

- **Chronic migraine**
	- **Diagnostic features**
		- ≥15 headache days/month
		- ≥4 hours/day
		- Duration ≥3 months
	- **Clinical features**
		- Previous history of migraine
		- Gradual increase in headache frequency
		- Chronic background headache
		- Episodic migraine exacerbations
			- Throbbing unilateral pain
			- Nausea/vomiting
			- Photophobia
			- Phonophobia
	- **Historical term**
		- Previously called **transformed migraine**

---

- **Chronic tension-type headache**
	- **Clinical features**
		- Gradual increase in headache frequency
		- Daily bilateral headache
		- Pressing/tight ("band-like")
		- Mild–moderate intensity
		- No migrainous features
		- No previous migraine history

---

- **New daily persistent headache (NDPH)**
	- **Hallmark**
		- Abrupt onset of continuous daily headache
	- **Clinical features**
		- Does not evolve from episodic headache
		- Usually resembles chronic tension headache
		- May have mild migrainous features
		- Moderate severity
		- Fluctuating intensity
		- Variable location
		- May have sharp jabbing pains
		- Frequently associated with lethargy

---

- **Hemicrania continua**
	- **Clinical features**
		- Continuous strictly unilateral headache
		- Moderate baseline pain
		- Severe exacerbations
		- Mild ipsilateral autonomic symptoms
	- **Key diagnostic feature**
		- Complete response to indomethacin
	- **Rare features**
		- Bilateral pain
		- Side shift

---

- **Trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias (TACs)**
	- **Shared features**
		- Severe unilateral orbitotemporal pain
		- Ipsilateral autonomic symptoms
			- Lacrimation
			- Conjunctival injection
			- Rhinorrhoea
			- Nasal congestion
			- Ptosis
			- Miosis
	- **Includes**
		- Cluster headache
		- Paroxysmal hemicrania
		- SUNCT syndrome

---

- **Cluster headache**
	- **Clinical features**
		- Severe unilateral orbital/temporal pain
		- Duration 15–180 minutes
		- Frequency
			- 1 every other day to 8/day
		- Ipsilateral autonomic symptoms
		- Restlessness during attacks
	- **Treatment**
		- Acute
			- 100% oxygen
			- Subcutaneous or intranasal sumatriptan
		- Prevention
			- Verapamil

---

- **Paroxysmal hemicrania**
	- **Clinical features**
		- Similar to cluster headache
		- >5 attacks/day
		- Duration 2–30 minutes
	- **Key feature**
		- Complete response to indomethacin

---

- **SUNCT syndrome**
	- **Meaning**
		- Short-lasting Unilateral Neuralgiform headache attacks with Conjunctival injection and Tearing
	- **Clinical features**
		- 3–200 attacks/day
		- Seconds to minutes in duration
		- Profuse tearing
		- Marked conjunctival injection

---

- **Primary stabbing headache**
	- Recurrent "ice-pick" pains
	- Seconds in duration
	- First (V1) trigeminal distribution
	- No autonomic symptoms

---

- **Hypnic headache**
	- Usually affects older adults
	- Wakes patient from sleep ("alarm clock headache")
	- Usually bilateral
	- Duration ~30 minutes
	- Less severe than cluster headache
	- No autonomic symptoms

---

- **Headaches triggered by activity**
	- **Triggers**
		- Cough
		- Exertion
		- Sexual activity
	- **Always exclude secondary causes**
		- Arnold–Chiari malformation
		- Cerebral aneurysm
		- Cervical artery dissection
		- Subarachnoid haemorrhage

---

- **AMC Pearls**
	- **Migraine**
		- Quiet patient
		- Dark room
		- Worse with activity
	- **Cluster headache**
		- Restless patient
		- Autonomic symptoms
		- Oxygen + SC sumatriptan
	- **Hemicrania continua**
		- Continuous unilateral headache
		- Responds completely to indomethacin
	- **Paroxysmal hemicrania**
		- Short frequent attacks
		- Indomethacin-responsive
	- **NDPH**
		- Abrupt onset of persistent daily headache
	- **Headache triggered by cough, exertion, or sex**
		- Exclude structural or vascular pathology first
