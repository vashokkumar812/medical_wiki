## Treatment
### General Principles
- **Underlying cause** should be corrected or withdrawn:
  - Drugs
  - Hyperglycemia
  - [[Hypercalcemia]]
  - Hypokalemia
  - [[diarrhea]]
- **Correction must be slow** to avoid cerebral edema:
  - Replace **calculated free water deficit over ~48 h**.
  - **Limit plasma Na+ correction to ≤10 mM/day**.
  - Severe hypernatremia (>160 mM) may require longer than 48 h.
- **Acute hypernatremia (<48 h) due to sodium loading**:
  - Can be corrected safely at **1 mM/h**.
### Administration of Free Water
- **Preferred route**: oral or nasogastric tube (direct free water).
- **IV option**: Dextrose-containing solutions (e.g., D5W).
  - Monitor blood glucose to prevent hyperglycemia.
- **Hypotonic saline (1/4 or 1/2 normal saline)** may be used based on:
  - Clinical volume status
  - Blood pressure
  - History
- **Normal saline** generally inappropriate unless:
  - Very severe hypernatremia (proportionally more hypotonic)
  - Frank hypotension
### Ongoing Free Water Loss
- **Urinary electrolyte-free water clearance** should be calculated (Table 53-3).
- Necessary for patients with **NDI (Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus)** or **central DI** to estimate and replace daily ongoing losses.

---

### Specific Therapy
- **Central DI** → responds to **DDAVP** (IV, intranasal, or oral).  
- **NDI due to lithium**:
  - **Amiloride (2.5–10 mg/d)** → inhibits lithium entry via ENaC, reducing polyuria.
  - Most patients compensate by **increasing daily water intake**.
- **Thiazides**:
  - Reduce polyuria by inducing mild hypovolemia → ↑ proximal tubular water reabsorption.
- **NSAIDs**:
  - Can reduce polyuria in NDI by decreasing intrarenal prostaglandins.
  - Risks: gastric/renal toxicity.
- **Note**:
  - Thiazides, amiloride, NSAIDs are **only for chronic management of NDI polyuria**.
  - **Acute hypernatremia** focus remains on **replacing free water deficits and ongoing losses**.
