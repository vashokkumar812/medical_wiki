- **Delirium Tremens (DTs)**
  - Severe, life-threatening alcohol withdrawal syndrome
  - Usually occurs after abrupt cessation/reduction of heavy alcohol intake

- **Risk Factors**
  - Previous DTs
  - Previous alcohol withdrawal seizures
  - Heavy prolonged alcohol use
  - Multiple withdrawal episodes
  - Concurrent medical illness
  - Older age

- **Time Course**
  - 6–24 hours
    - Tremor
    - Anxiety
    - Nausea
    - Insomnia
  - 12–48 hours
    - Withdrawal seizures
  - 48–72 hours (classically)
    - Delirium tremens

- **Clinical Features**
  - Delirium
    - Confusion
    - Disorientation
    - Fluctuating consciousness
    - Inattention
  - Autonomic hyperactivity
    - Tachycardia
    - Hypertension
    - Fever
    - Diaphoresis
  - Neuropsychiatric features
    - Agitation
    - Severe anxiety
    - Restlessness
  - Hallucinations
    - Visual (most common)
    - Auditory
    - Tactile
  - Coarse tremor

- **Initial Investigation
  - **Bedside blood glucose level (BGL)**

- **Most Appropriate Investigations**
  - Blood glucose
  - FBC
  - UEC
  - LFTs
  - Magnesium
  - Phosphate
  - ECG
  - Blood alcohol level (if required)
  - Investigate precipitating illness
    - Infection
    - Trauma
    - Pancreatitis

- **Initial Management**
  - **ABC approach**
    - Airway
    - Breathing
    - Circulation
	  - Continuous monitoring
	  - IV access
	  - Check BGL

- **Most Appropriate Treatment**
  - **Benzodiazepines (first-line)**
    - Diazepam
      - Preferred in most patients
    - Lorazepam
      - Consider if severe liver disease
  - Thiamine
    - Give before glucose
  - IV fluids
  - Correct electrolyte abnormalities
    - Magnesium
    - Potassium
    - Phosphate

- **Complications**
  - Arrhythmias
  - Aspiration pneumonia
  - Severe dehydration
  - Rhabdomyolysis
  - Cardiovascular collapse
  - Death

- **AMC Exam Triggers**
  - Alcohol cessation + confusion + agitation + visual hallucinations + autonomic hyperactivity = **Delirium Tremens**
  - Seizures occur before DTs
  - **Initial Ix → BGL**
  - **Initial Rx → ABC + benzodiazepine**
  - **Most appropriate definitive Rx → Diazepam**
  - **Thiamine before glucose**
