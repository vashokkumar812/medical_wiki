- Carrier Screening and Genetic Risk Assessment
	- Family History Assessment
		- Recommended for:
			- All couples planning pregnancy
			- All pregnant couples
		- If positive family history
			- Refer for genetic counselling
			- Discuss:
				- Carrier testing
				- Prenatal diagnosis
				- Preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD)

	- Timing of Screening
		- Preferred
			- Preconception
		- Advantages
			- Allows:
				- IVF
				- PGD
				- Donor gametes
				- Adoption planning
		- If first presentation in pregnancy
			- Still offer screening

	- Universal Screening Recommendations
		- Thalassaemia
			- Offer to all pregnant women
			- Initial test:
				- Full blood examination (FBE)
			- High-risk ethnic groups
				- Consider:
					- HPLC
					- Haemoglobin electrophoresis (EPG)
					- Haemoglobinopathy DNA testing

		- Panethnic Carrier Screening
			- Offer to:
				- Women planning pregnancy
				- Women in first trimester
			- Common conditions:
				- Cystic fibrosis (CF)
				- Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)
				- Fragile X syndrome

		- Expanded Carrier Screening
			- Enabled by:
				- Next-generation sequencing (NGS)
			- Screens:
				- Up to hundreds or thousands of disorders
			- Includes:
				- Autosomal recessive conditions
				- X-linked conditions

	- Ashkenazi Jewish Screening
		- Tay-Sachs disease
		- Niemann-Pick disease type A
		- Fanconi anaemia group C
		- Familial dysautonomia
		- Bloom syndrome
		- Canavan disease
		- Mucolipidosis type IV

- Screening Process
	- Screening Methods
		- Sequential Screening (Two-Step)
			- Usually:
				- Woman screened first
			- If carrier detected
				- Partner tested

		- Couple Screening (One-Step)
			- Both partners tested simultaneously
			- High probability if:
				- Both carry same autosomal recessive condition
				- Woman carries relevant X-linked condition

	- Informed Consent
		- Required before testing
		- Discuss:
			- Residual risk despite negative result
			- Not all mutations detected
			- Small possibility of identifying affected individual
		- Couple screening
			- Explain:
				- Future pregnancies with different partner require reassessment

	- Laboratory Reporting
		- Report only:
			- Class 4 variants
				- Likely pathogenic
			- Class 5 variants
				- Pathogenic
		- Do not report:
			- Variants of uncertain significance (VUS)

- Important Genetic Conditions
	- Cystic Fibrosis (CF)
		- Recurrent lung infections
		- Malabsorption
		- Reduced lifespan

	- Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)
		- Severe muscle weakness
		- Childhood death

	- Fragile X Syndrome
		- Intellectual disability
		- Autism spectrum disorder

- High-Risk Couples
	- Definition
		- Both partners carry same autosomal recessive condition
		- Woman carries clinically significant X-linked condition

	- Management
		- if already pregnant
			- Offer prenatal diagnosis 
				- Offered if high-risk couple identified during pregnancy
				- Methods:
					- Chorionic villus sampling (CVS)
					- Amniocentesis
				- Benefits:
					- Preparation for affected child
					- Consideration of pregnancy termination
					- Rarely:
						- In-utero treatment
		- else
			- Refer for genetic counselling
			- Discuss reproductive options
- Additional Support
	- Refer to disease-specific specialist where appropriate
		- Example:
			- Paediatric respiratory physician for cystic fibrosis risk
