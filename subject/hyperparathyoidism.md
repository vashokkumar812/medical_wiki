- Hypercalcaemia
	- Common causes
		- Primary hyperparathyroidism
		- Malignancy
		- Drugs
			- Lithium
			- Thiazides

	- Hypercalcaemia of malignancy
		- Commonest inpatient cause
		- Suppressed PTH

- Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT)
	- Features
		- ↑ Calcium
		- ↑ or inappropriately normal PTH
		- Often ↓ phosphate

	- Epidemiology
		- Common in postmenopausal women
		- 85%
			- Single adenoma
		- 15%
			- Multigland hyperplasia
		- Parathyroid carcinoma
			- <0.5%

	- Risk factors
		- Neck irradiation
		- Lithium

	- Classic features
		- “Bones, stones, abdominal moans, psychic groans”
			- Fractures
			- Renal calculi
			- Pancreatitis
			- Neuropsychiatric symptoms

	- Modern presentation
		- ~80% asymptomatic
		- Incidental hypercalcaemia

- Diagnosis
	- Criteria
		- Hypercalcaemia
		- ↑/inappropriately normal PTH
		- Fractional urinary calcium excretion >0.02

	- Fractional excretion formula
		- (Urine Ca × Serum Cr) ÷ (Serum Ca × Urine Cr)

	- Investigations
		- Ionised calcium
			- More accurate than corrected calcium
		- 24-hour urine calcium
			- Assess hypercalciuria/stone risk
		- Sestamibi scan
			- Localises adenoma pre-op

- Hyperparathyroidism types

| Type | PTH | Calcium | Phosphate | Causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ | Adenoma/hyperplasia |
| Secondary | ↑ | ↓/N | ↑/N | CKD, vitamin D deficiency |
| Tertiary | ↑↑ | ↑ | ↑ | End-stage renal failure |

- Indications for parathyroidectomy
	- Symptomatic disease
		- Stones
		- Fractures
		- Pancreatitis

	- Asymptomatic with risk factors
		- Calcium >2.85 mmol/L
		- Hypercalciuria
		- Reduced creatinine clearance
		- Osteoporosis (T score <–2.5)
		- Age <50 years
		- Difficult follow-up

- Surgery
	- Cure rate
			- ~95%
	- Complications uncommon
			- RLN palsy
			- Permanent hypoparathyroidism

	- Minimally invasive surgery
		- If single adenoma localised
		- Smaller incision
		- Shorter stay

	- Intraoperative success
		- >50% fall in PTH

- Medical management
	- Hydration
	- Mobilisation
	- Normal calcium + vitamin D intake
	- Avoid
		- Thiazides
		- Lithium
		- Excess calcium
		- Aluminium hydroxide

	- Drugs
		- Alendronate
			- ↑ BMD
			- Does not lower calcium
		- Cinacalcet
			- Lowers calcium
			- No major BMD effect

	- Monitoring
		- 6-monthly calcium
		- Annual BMD
		- 24-hour urine calcium

- Drug-induced hypercalcaemia
	- Lithium
		- Mimics PHPT biochemically
		- ↓ calcium sensing receptor sensitivity
		- May persist after stopping drug

	- Thiazides
		- Mild hypercalcaemia
		- ↑ renal calcium reabsorption

	- Milk-alkali syndrome
		- Hypercalcaemia
		- Metabolic alkalosis
		- Renal impairment

- Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia (FHH)
	- Autosomal dominant
	- Benign
	- Usually asymptomatic lifelong mild hypercalcaemia

	- Biochemistry
		- Normal/mildly ↑ PTH
		- Fractional urinary calcium excretion <0.01

	- Due to
		- Inactivating calcium sensing receptor mutation

	- Management
		- No surgery
		- Family screening/genetic testing

- PHPT vs FHH

| Feature | PHPT | FHH |
|---|---|---|
| Family history | Usually absent | Common |
| Fractional urinary calcium excretion | >0.02 | <0.01 |
| Symptoms | May occur | Usually asymptomatic |
| Sestamibi scan | Often adenoma | Usually negative |
| Treatment | Often surgery | No treatment |

- Familial hyperparathyroid syndromes
	- Suspect if
		- Age <40 years
		- Family history
		- Multigland disease

	- Syndromes
		- FHH
		- MEN1
		- MEN2A
		- Hyperparathyroidism-jaw tumour syndrome
