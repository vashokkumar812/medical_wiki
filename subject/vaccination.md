# hepatitis A


- prophylaxis
	- Vaccine schedule
		- Standard schedule
			- 2 doses
			- Minimum interval of 6 months
		- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander (ATSI) children
			- 18 months
			- 4 years
			- Applies in:
				- Northern Territory
				- Queensland
				- South Australia
				- Western Australia

	- Indications
		- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children
			- Living in NT, QLD, SA, WA
		- Medical risk factors
			- Chronic liver disease
				- Vaccinate as early as possible
				- Poorer vaccine response in:
					- End-stage liver disease
					- Liver transplant recipients
				- Higher mortality if acute Hepatitis A develops in:
					- Chronic hepatitis B
					- Chronic hepatitis C
			- Chronic hepatitis B
			- Chronic hepatitis C
			- Liver transplant recipients
			- Developmental disabilities
		- Occupational risk
			- Rural/remote ATSI community workers
			- Carers of ATSI children
			- Early childhood educators and carers
			- Carers of people with developmental disabilities
			- Plumbers
			- Sewage workers
		- Travellers
			- ≥1 year travelling to moderate/high endemic countries
		- Lifestyle risk
			- Men who have sex with men (MSM)
			- Anal intercourse
			- Sex industry workers
			- People who inject drugs (PWID)
			- Correctional facility inmates



	- Combination vaccine
		- Twinrix® (Hepatitis A + Hepatitis B)
		- Indicated if protection against both viruses is required
		- Schedule
			- Usually 3 doses
			- Children 1–<16 years
				- Twinrix 720/20®
				- 2-dose schedule (≥6 months apart)
			- Adults ≥16 years (rapid schedule if required)
				- Day 0
				- Day 7
				- Day 21
				- Booster at 12 months

	- Travellers
		- One dose provides short-term protection
		- Second dose 6–12 months later provides long-term protection

	- Serology
		- Before vaccination
			- Not routinely recommended
			- Consider if:
				- Born before 1950
				- Childhood in endemic country
				- Previous unexplained hepatitis or jaundice
		- After vaccination
			- Not recommended


	- Co-administration
		- Can be given before, after, or with any other vaccine
		- Can be administered with travel vaccines and NIP vaccines
		- If Twinrix unavailable
			- Give monovalent Hepatitis A + Hepatitis B vaccines simultaneously
			- Use separate syringes and injection sites

	- Interchangeability
		- Monovalent Hepatitis A vaccines are interchangeable
		- Do not routinely mix Twinrix with monovalent Hepatitis A vaccines
		- Twinrix® adult is not interchangeable with Havrix® adult
			- Contains half the Hepatitis A antigen

	- Contraindications
		- Anaphylaxis after previous Hepatitis A vaccine
		- Anaphylaxis to vaccine component
		- Twinrix contraindicated in yeast allergy

	- Precautions
		- Pregnancy
			- Not routinely recommended
			- Can be given if indicated
		- Breastfeeding
			- Can be given if indicated
		- Latex allergy
			- Avoid Vaqta® if significant latex allergy

	- Adverse effects
		- Most common
			- Injection site pain
		- Less common
			- Headache
			- Fever
			- Malaise
			- Fatigue
		- Does not affect liver enzymes
		- Safe in HIV infection
			- No effect on viral load or CD4 count

	- Vaccine
		- Inactivated vaccine
		- Highly immunogenic
		- Seroconversion nearly 100% after 4 weeks
		- Protective efficacy nearly 100%
		- One dose provides protection for several years
		- Booster doses not required

	- Storage
		- Store at +2°C to +8°C
		- Do not freeze

	- Public health
		- Notifiable disease in Australia
		- Close contacts may require post-exposure prophylaxis

	- Post-exposure prophylaxis
		- Hepatitis A vaccine or NHIG within 2 weeks of exposure
		- NHIG preferred for:
			- Infants <12 months
			- Immunocompromised patients
			- Vaccine contraindication
			- Adults >40 years (selected cases)

			- NHIG administration
				- Deep intramuscular injection
				- Never administer intravenously

# HPV vaccine
![[Pasted image 20260607234630.png]]

- Vaccine
	- Vaccine available
		- Gardasil 9
	- Dose
		- 0.5 mL IM


- Catch-up vaccination
	- Not vaccinated by age 14
		- Can vaccinate until age 25
	- More effective
		- Earlier in adolescence

- Special situations
	- Age <9 years
		- Consider vaccination if
			- Sexual abuse history
			- STI diagnosed
		- Additional routine dose required at ≥9 years
			- Ideally 12-13 years

- Adults ≥26 years
	- Not routinely recommended
	- Consider if
			- Future HPV exposure risk
			- HPV-related precancer/cancer history
	- Important
		- Does NOT treat existing infection

- Immunocompromised people
	- Require 3-dose schedule regardless of age
	- Reasons
		- Reduced immune response
		- Higher risk persistent infection
		- Higher risk HPV-related disease
	- Includes
		- Primary immunodeficiency
		- HIV
		- Malignancy
		- Haematopoietic stem cell transplant
		- Solid organ transplant
		- Immunosuppressive therapy

- Asplenia/hyposplenia
	- NOT considered higher HPV risk
	- Age 9-25 years
		- Standard 1-dose schedule

- MSM
	- Recommended at any age if unvaccinated
	- Number of doses
		- Based on age and immune status

- Cervical screening
	- Still required after HPV vaccination
	- Reason
		- Non-vaccine HPV types can cause cervical cancer
	- Check catch-up vaccination status
		- At first cervical screening visit
		- Usually age 25 years
# Vaccines not recommended in pregnancy

- HPV vaccine
	- Not recommended during pregnancy
	- If pregnancy occurs during vaccination course
		- Stop remaining doses
		- Complete after delivery

- Live attenuated influenza vaccine (LAIV)
	- Not recommended during pregnancy
	- Use
		- Inactivated influenza vaccine instead

- Yellow fever vaccine
	- Not routinely recommended during pregnancy
	- Avoid travel to endemic areas if possible
	- If travel unavoidable
		- Vaccinate despite pregnancy


# vaccination before travel
- No single "correct" vaccine list for any country
- Vaccine-preventable travel diseases
	- Food/waterborne
		- Hepatitis A
		- Typhoid
		- Cholera
	- Vector-borne
		- Japanese encephalitis
		- Yellow fever
		- Tick-borne encephalitis
	- Aerosol-borne
		- COVID-19
		- Influenza
		- Measles
		- Mumps
		- Meningococcal disease
		- Pneumococcal disease
		- Varicella
		- Mpox
		- Tuberculosis
	- Bloodborne
		- Hepatitis B
		- Mpox
	- Other
		- Rabies


- Diphtheria-Tetanus-Pertussis
	- Tetanus vaccination important before travel if:
		- High risk of tetanus-prone wound
	- Give dTpa if never received previously
		- Adds pertussis protection
	- High-risk travel
		- dTpa/dT booster if >5 years since last dose

- Hepatitis B
	- Recommended for:
		- Long-term travellers
		- Frequent travellers
		- Travel to intermediate/high endemicity regions
	- High-risk regions
		- except europe
	- Exposure risks
		- Sexual exposure
		- Emergency medical procedures
		- Dental procedures
		- Blood exposure

- Influenza
	- Consider for all travellers
	- Most common vaccine-preventable travel infection

- Pneumococcal vaccine and RSV vaccine
	- Recommended for:
		- Older travellers
		- Travellers with risk factors

- Measles-Mumps-Rubella (MMR)
	- Key AMC point
		- Major source of Australian measles outbreaks = travellers infected overseas
	- Give MMR before travel if:
		- Born during/after 1966
		- Fewer than 2 documented MMR doses
	- Infants
		- 6-12 months
		- Vaccinate if travelling to:
			- Measles-endemic areas
			- Outbreak regions
	- Contraindicated
		- Pregnancy (live vaccine)
	- Born before 1966
		- Usually considered naturally immune
		- Vaccinate if immunity uncertain rather than waiting for serology

- Varicella
	- Vaccinate if:
		- No history of disease
		- Uncertain history + serology non-immune
	- Contraindicated
		- Pregnancy (live vaccine)

- Meningococcal disease
	- Travel to meningitis belt of sub-Saharan Africa
	- Travel to epidemic regions
	- Mass gatherings
		- Hajj
		- Umrah
	- Saudi Arabia requirement
		- Proof of recent MenACWY vaccination required for Hajj/Umrah
	- Consider MenB in high-risk groups

- Poliomyelitis
	- Ensure age-appropriate polio vaccination
	- Travel to wild poliovirus endemic country
		- Incomplete primary course
			- Give IPV
		- Completed primary course
			- Give 1 booster dose
	- Documentation
		- Usually not required
	- Travel requirements
		- Can change rapidly
		- Check current country-specific requirements

- COVID-19
	- Ensure age-appropriate vaccination
	- Some countries may require proof of vaccination for entry

- Risk-based travel vaccines
	- Prioritise
		- Common diseases with significant impact
			- Influenza
			- Hepatitis A
		- Rare diseases with severe outcomes
			- Japanese encephalitis
			- Rabies
	- If departure imminent
		- Consider accelerated schedules
			- Hepatitis B
			- Hepatitis A + B
		- Accelerated schedule
			- Earlier protection
			- Requires additional dose ~1 year later for long-term immunity

- Cholera
	- Most travellers do NOT need vaccine


- Hepatitis A
	- Recommended for
		- All travellers ≥1 year
		- Essentially all developing countries
	- Exception
		- Previous infection with natural immunity
	- Human immunoglobulin
		- No longer used for traveller prophylaxis

- Japanese encephalitis (JE)
	- Recommended if
		- ≥1 month in endemic area during transmission season
			- Asia
			- Papua New Guinea
			- Outer Torres Strait islands
	- Consider even for shorter travel if
		- Wet season travel
		- Repeated travel
		- Significant outdoor exposure
		- No AC/screens/bed nets
	- Risk factors
		- Season
		- Region visited
		- Duration
		- Outdoor activity
		- Mosquito avoidance measures
	- Overall traveller risk
		- Very low
			- <1 case per million travellers

- Rabies
	- based on exposure on foreign

- Mpox
	- Transmission
		- Close sustained physical contact
		- Frequently sexual contact
	- Vaccinate before travel if
		- Eligible high-risk group
		- Not previously vaccinated
	- Vaccine
		- MVA-BN (JYNNEOS)
		- 2 doses
	- Clade I region travel
		- Vaccinate any traveller planning sexual risk activities

- Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE)
	- Endemic areas
		- Central Europe
		- Northern Europe
		- Northern Asia
	- Risk factors
		- Hiking
		- Camping
		- Forest exposure
		- Summer travel
	- Vaccination
		- Only for high-risk exposure
	- Australia
		- Vaccine not routinely registered


- Tuberculosis (BCG)
	- Recommended
		- Tuberculin-negative children <5 years
		- Stay ≥3 months in high-risk country
	- Consider
		- Tuberculin-negative children 5 to <16 years
		- Long-term stay/travel in high-risk country
	- High-risk country
		- TB incidence >40/100,000 population
	- Scheduling
		- Ideally ≥3 months before arrival
		- Other live vaccines
			- Same day OR
			- ≥4 weeks after BCG
	- Before BCG
		- TST (Mantoux) required
		- Except infants <6 months
	- Important
		- Viral infections/live viral vaccines can suppress TST response for 4-6 weeks

- Typhoid
	- Consider for travellers ≥2 years
	- Endemic regions
		- Indian subcontinent
		- Most Southeast Asia
		- Papua New Guinea
		- Several South Pacific nations
	- Especially important
		- Visiting friends and relatives

- Yellow fever
	- Recommended
		- All travellers ≥9 months
		- Travelling to/living in transmission-risk areas
	- Age ≥60 years
		- Higher risk of severe vaccine adverse events

- Yellow fever booster
	- Usually NOT required
	- Single dose
		- Long-lasting protection
	- Booster indicated if
		- Ongoing risk
		- Last dose >10 years ago
		- Specific high-risk situations

- Vaccines required for international travel
	- Yellow fever
	- Hajj/Umrah
		- Mandatory
			- MenACWY vaccination proof
