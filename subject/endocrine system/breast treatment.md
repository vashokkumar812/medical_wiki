early breast cancer management
	- Breast conservation surgery/lumpectomy/wide local excision + radiotherapy
		- Preferred treatment for
			- stage 1
			- stage 2
		- Outcomes
			- local control and survival equivalent to mastectomy
		- if local recurrence
			- salvage mastectomy
		- If surgery done WITHOUT radiotherapy
			- increased local recurrence
			- lower recurrence in:
				- smaller tumours
				- good clear margins
			- decreased survival
		- Radiotherapy details
			- daily for 7 weeks
		- Contraindications to breast conservation
			- large tumour relative to breast size
			- multicentric disease
			- recurrent disease after previous radiotherapy
			- severe collagen vascular disorders
				- poorer cosmetic outcome after radiotherapy
	
	- Mastectomy (+/- radiotherapy/breast reconstruction)
		- indications for mastectomy in early breast cancer
			- patient preference
			- contraindications of wide local excision
			- pregnancy
				- first or second trimester
				- radiotherapy contraindicated
			- inaccessible radiotherapy
		- Breast reconstruction
			- time:
				- immediate
				- delayed
		- Postmastectomy radiotherapy
			- usually NOT required
			- indications
				- tumour >5 cm (T3)
				- ≥4 positive axillary lymph nodes
	
	- axilla
		- axillary nodal status use
			- major prognostic factor and to decide chemotherapy
			- predicts survival independent of:
				- tumour size
				- tumour grade
	
		- Options for axillary management
			- axillary lymph node dissection (ALNDx)
				- Traditional operation for invasive breast cancer
				- Goal
					- remove all or nearly all accessible axillary lymph nodes
				- Benefits
					- staging the axilla
						- NHMRC recommendation
							- level I + II clearance
						- level III clearance
							- retrieves ~25 lymph nodes
						- large node sample improves staging accuracy
					- treating the axilla
						- often adequate treatment even if nodes positive
						- complete level III clearance often avoids further axillary treatment
				- Some node-positive patients still require axillary radiotherapy
				- Complications
					- lymphoedema
					- numbness
						- intercostobrachial nerve distribution
						- upper arm affected
					- shoulder stiffness
			- sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNBx)
				- indications
					- small early invasive breast cancer
				- Principle
					- identifies and removes first draining lymph node(s)
					- predicts involvement of remaining axillary nodes
				- Assumption
					- negative sentinel node
						- remaining axillary nodes also negative
				- Advantage
					- avoids full axillary clearance in node-negative women
					- reduces morbidity of complete dissection
				- Role
					- test to determine need for ALNDx
			- ALNDx + SLNBx combination
			- axillary radiotherapy
				- Uses
					- alone
						- poor surgical candidates
						- patients refusing surgery
					- after positive SLNBx
						- when ALNDx undesirable
					- after ALNDx
						- extensive extranodal disease not fully removed surgically
				- Complications
					- lymphoedema
						- ALNDx + radiotherapy combined >>  radiotherapy alone
					- radiation pneumonitis
						- occurs 6 weeks to 6 months after treatment
						- symptoms
							- shortness of breath
							- cough
							- lethargy
						- treatment
							- antibiotics
							- short-term prednisone
			- Observation
				- Rare approach
				- Reserved for:
					- extremely small early cancers
					- elderly patients
					- major comorbidities
					- surgery/radiotherapy difficult
				- DCIS
					- no lymph gland treatment required
	
	- Systemic treatment
		- uses
			- reduce risk of distant metastatic relapse
		- mechanism
			- systemic therapy destroys residual malignant cells after surgery
		- treatments used
			- chemotherapy
				- uses
					- works in hormone receptor positive and negative disease
					- High-risk cancers
						- chemotherapy significantly improves survival
						- especially women <50 years
					- Low-risk cancers
						- small
						- low grade
						- node negative
						- chemotherapy benefit minimal
				- Types
					- adjuvant
						- after surgery
					- neoadjuvant
						- before surgery
						- used in:
							- locally advanced cancer
							- inflammatory breast cancer
		
				- Effective regimens
					- CMF
						- cyclophosphamide
						- methotrexate
						- fluorouracil
					- anthracycline regimens
						- adriamycin
						- epirubicin
						- slightly better than CMF
						- more alopecia
					- taxanes
						- paclitaxel
						- docetaxel
				- Toxicities
					- reversible myelosuppression
					- alopecia
					- infertility
					- early menopause
						- higher risk near age 40
						- higher with multiple drugs
					- nausea/vomiting
						- reduced with modern antiemetics
			- endocrine therapy
				- uses
					- reduces recurrence and death in hormone receptor positive disease
				- Indication
					- ER positive or PR positive tumours
					- unless tumour very low risk
						- eg. <10 mm and low grade
				- May be used:
					- alone in moderate risk
					- after chemotherapy in high risk
				- Oestrogen sources
					- premenopausal
						- ovaries
					- postmenopausal
						- adrenal glands
						- fat
						- muscle
				- Mechanisms
					- block oestrogen receptor
					- reduce oestrogen production
				- drugs used
					- Tamoxifen
						- Oestrogen receptor blocker
						- Used in:
							- premenopausal women
							- postmenopausal women
						- Risks
							- endometrial cancer
								- ~1 extra case/1000 women/year
							- venous thrombosis
								- ~1 extra case/1000 women/year
						- Reduce thrombosis risk
							- stop around surgery or trauma
		
					- Aromatase inhibitors
						- Mechanism
							- block aromatase enzyme
							- reduce peripheral conversion of testosterone to oestrogen
						- Effective
							- only in postmenopausal women
						- Drugs
							- anastrozole (Arimidex®)
							- letrozole (Femara®)
							- exemestane (Aromasin®)
						- Advantages over tamoxifen
							- lower relapse rates
							- no increased endometrial cancer risk
							- no increased venous thrombosis risk
						- Disadvantage
							- reduced bone mineral density
							- increased fracture risk
						- Well tolerated
						- Likely future standard therapy
		
					- Ovarian suppression
						- indications
							- Used in hormone receptor positive disease
						- Methods
							- temporary suppression
								- goserelin (Zoladex)
							- laparoscopic oophorectomy
						- Not standard therapy
							- not PBS funded for early breast cancer
						- Useful especially near menopause		
						- combined chemotherapy + endocrine therapy
							- better than either alone
						- benefits persist beyond 20 years
			- Biological therapies
				- uses
					- Target receptors other than hormone receptors
				- drugs
					- Trastuzumab (Herceptin)
						- Monoclonal antibody
						- Targets HER-2 receptor
						- Active in breast cancer
						- Available in Australia for metastatic breast cancer
						- HER-2 overexpression
							- present in ~20% of breast cancers
						- In early breast cancer
							- reduces relapse risk
							- benefit additional to:
								- chemotherapy
								- endocrine therapy
						- Further research and funding decisions required
		
	
	- Comparison: Tamoxifen vs Aromatase inhibitors
	
		- Tamoxifen
			- works only in ER/PR positive tumours
			- effective in:
				- premenopausal women
				- postmenopausal women
			- increased risk:
				- endometrial cancer
				- DVT
			- hot flushes common
			- increased vaginal discharge
			- improves bone mineral density
	
		- Aromatase inhibitors
			- work only in ER/PR positive tumours
			- effective only in postmenopausal women
			- reduced risk:
				- endometrial cancer
				- DVT
			- hot flushes less common
			- increased vaginal dryness
			- reduce bone mineral density
				- increased fracture risk
	
	- Features associated with higher risk of relapse
		- axillary lymph node metastases
		- large tumours
			- >2 cm
		- high grade tumours
		- young women
			- <35 years
		- hormone receptor negative tumours
			- ER negative
			- PR negative
		- HER-2 positive tumours

## advanced breast cancer management

- Molecular subtypes of breast cancer
	- Hormone receptor positive (HR+)
		- ER and/or PR positive
		- HER2 negative
	- HER2 positive
		- HER2 receptor positive
		- may be:
			- HR positive
			- HR negative
	- Triple negative breast cancer
		- ER negative
		- PR negative
		- HER2 negative

- Systemic therapy according to subtype
	- HR+ disease
		- endocrine therapy
	- HER2+ disease
		- HER2-directed therapy
	- Hormone receptor negative disease
		- chemotherapy
	- HR+ disease resistant to endocrine therapy
		- chemotherapy

- Endocrine therapy
	- Postmenopausal women
		- aromatase inhibitors
		- tamoxifen
		- fulvestrant
	- Premenopausal women
		- tamoxifen
		- ovarian suppression + tamoxifen
		- ovarian suppression + aromatase inhibitor

- Aromatase inhibitors
	- Mechanism
		- inhibit aromatase enzyme
		- prevent oestrogen synthesis
	- Drugs
		- letrozole
		- anastrozole
		- exemestane
	- Administration
		- daily oral tablet
	- Side effects
		- fatigue
		- arthralgia
		- hot flushes
		- fluid retention
		- vaginal dryness
		- bone density loss

- Tamoxifen
	- Selective oestrogen receptor modulator (SERM)
	- Actions
		- antagonist in breast tissue
		- agonist in:
			- bone
			- uterus
	- Effects
		- improves bone density
		- increased endometrial cancer risk
	- Common side effects
		- fatigue
		- hot flushes
		- mood changes
		- fluid retention
	- Rare side effects
		- venous thromboembolism
		- endometrial carcinoma

- Fulvestrant
	- Selective oestrogen receptor degrader (SERD)
	- Monthly intramuscular injection
	- Not PBS listed
		- accessed through cost-share program
	- Side effects
		- injection site reactions
		- hot flushes
		- arthralgia

- Ovarian suppression
	- Used in premenopausal women
	- Methods
		- monthly GnRH agonist injections
			- goserelin
		- surgical bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy

- Targeted therapy
	- Everolimus
		- mTOR inhibitor
		- oral daily therapy
		- combined with exemestane
		- used in HR+ disease
		- side effects
			- fatigue
			- mucositis
			- diarrhoea

- HER2-directed therapy
	- Trastuzumab
		- monoclonal antibody against HER2 receptor
		- IV every 3 weeks
		- can also be given subcutaneously
	- Pertuzumab
		- monoclonal antibody against HER2 receptor
		- IV every 3 weeks
	- Common issue
		- cardiotoxicity
			- rare but serious
			- requires:
				- echocardiography
				- gated heart pool scans

- Trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1)
	- Antibody-drug conjugate
	- IV every 3 weeks
	- Side effects
		- cytopenias
		- cardiotoxicity

- Lapatinib
	- Tyrosine kinase inhibitor
	- Oral daily drug
	- Combined with capecitabine
	- Side effects
		- diarrhoea
		- hand-foot syndrome
		- fatigue
		- nausea
		- vomiting
		- rash

- CDK4/6 inhibitors
	- New targeted therapy class
	- Used in advanced HR+ breast cancer
	- Combined with endocrine therapy
	- Benefit
		- improved progression-free survival
	- Overall survival benefit
		- not yet confirmed
	- Common side effects
		- fatigue
		- anaemia
		- neutropenia
		- thrombocytopenia
		- diarrhoea
	- Neutropenia
		- usually manageable
		- febrile neutropenia rare

- Ribociclib
	- CDK4/6 inhibitor
	- PBS listed July 2018
	- First-line advanced HR+ breast cancer
	- Combined with aromatase inhibitor
	- Oral therapy
		 - 3 weeks on
		 - 1 week break
	- Monitoring
		- ECG required
	- Important adverse effect
		- prolonged QT syndrome

- Palbociclib
	- CDK4/6 inhibitor
	- Approved in Australia May 2017
	- Uses
		- first-line + aromatase inhibitor
		- second-line + fulvestrant
	- Oral therapy
		 - 3 weeks on
		 - 1 week break
	- Access
		- cost-share program

- Abemaciclib
	- CDK4/6 inhibitor
	- Combined with:
		- letrozole
		- fulvestrant
	- Continuous oral dosing
	- Causes more diarrhoea than other CDK4/6 inhibitors
	- Unique feature
		- single-agent activity in heavily pre-treated patients
	- Access
		- clinical trials
		- compassionate access programs

- Olaparib
	- PARP inhibitor
	- Used in germline BRCA mutant breast cancer
	- Benefit
		- delays disease progression
		- delays need for chemotherapy
	- Access
		- not PBS listed
		- compassionate access
	- Administration
		- oral daily therapy
	- Side effects
		- fatigue
		- anaemia
		- gastrointestinal upset
		- increased risk of myelodysplastic syndrome

- Signs, symptoms and investigations in advanced breast cancer

	- General metastasis
		- symptoms
			- fatigue
			- depression
			- anxiety
		- investigations
			- FBC

	- Bone metastasis
		- symptoms
			- pain
			- hypercalcaemia
			- pathological fracture
		- investigations
			- serum calcium
			- ALP
			- X-ray
			- whole-body bone scan

	- Liver metastasis
		- symptoms
			- jaundice
			- ascites
			- coagulopathy
		- investigations
			- LFT
			- ultrasound abdomen
			- CT abdomen

	- Lung metastasis
		- symptoms
			- dyspnoea
			- cough
			- haemoptysis
			- pleuritic chest pain
		- investigations
			- chest X-ray
			- CT chest

	- CNS metastasis
		- sites
			- brain
			- spinal cord
			- leptomeninges
		- symptoms
			- headache
			- cognitive impairment
			- motor deficits
			- sensory deficits
			- seizures
		- investigations
			- CT brain
			- MRI brain
			- MRI spinal cord

	- Lymph node metastasis
		- symptoms
			- brachial plexopathy
			- pain
		- investigations
			- clinical examination
			- cervical/supraclavicular node assessment
			- ultrasound
- Chemotherapy in advanced breast cancer
	- Single-agent chemotherapy preferred in palliative setting
		- fewer side effects
		- better quality of life
	- Combination chemotherapy
		- no proven overall survival benefit
		- considered when:
			- rapidly progressive disease
			- large tumour burden
			- organ dysfunction from tumour

- Single-agent chemotherapy options
	- Intravenous agents
		- taxanes
			- paclitaxel
			- nab-paclitaxel
			- docetaxel
		- doxorubicin
		- eribulin
		- vinorelbine
	- Oral agents
		- capecitabine
		- vinorelbine

- Triple negative breast cancer (TNBC)
	- Definition
		- ER negative
		- PR negative
		- HER2 negative
	- Characteristics
		- high locoregional recurrence
		- increased lung metastases
		- increased brain metastases
		- poorer prognosis
	- Treatment
		- chemotherapy is mainstay
		- endocrine therapy ineffective
		- HER2-targeted therapy ineffective
	- Immunotherapy
		- TNBC more immunogenic
		- clinical trials ongoing
		- used:
			- alone
			- with chemotherapy
		- more effective when used earlier
		- best access through clinical trials

- Atezolizumab
	- PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitor
	- Combined with nab-paclitaxel
	- First-line treatment in advanced TNBC
	- Benefits
		- improved progression-free survival
		- encouraging overall survival results

- Sacituzumab
	- Antibody-drug conjugate
	- Under investigation for TNBC

- Local management in advanced breast cancer
	- Systemic therapy remains main treatment
	- Local treatment used for:
		- symptom relief
		- local control
		- quality of life improvement

- Examples of local management
	- Fungating/ulcerated breast tumour
		- radiotherapy
		- surgery
	- Spinal cord compression from vertebral metastasis
		- radiotherapy
		- surgical decompression
	- Malignant pleural effusion
		- pleural drain
		- talc pleurodesis to prevent recurrence
	- Brain metastases
		- corticosteroids
			- reduce oedema
		- whole brain radiotherapy
		- selected patients
			- stereotactic body radiotherapy
			- surgical resection

- Management of bone metastases
	- Osteoclast inhibitors indicated
	- Options
		- zoledronic acid
			- IV monthly
		- denosumab
			- monthly injection
	- Benefits
		- reduce skeletal-related events
			- fractures
			- need for surgery
			- need for radiotherapy
			- spinal cord compression
			- malignancy-related hypercalcaemia
	- Painful bone metastases
		- radiotherapy effective

- Prognosis in advanced breast cancer
	- Median survival
		- approximately 2 years
		- wide range:
			- months to many years
	- Survival influenced by
		- tumour subtype
		- metastatic burden
		- metastatic sites
		- performance status

- Median survival by subtype
	- HR+ disease
		- 79 months
	- HER2+ disease
		- 56 months
	- Triple negative disease
		- 12 months

- Survival trends
	- Five-year survival improved
		- 10% in 1985
		- 27% in 2016
	- Major advance
		- trastuzumab introduction in 2000

- Favourable prognostic factors
	- HR+ disease
	- relapse-free interval ≥2 years
	- metastases limited to:
		- bone
		- chest wall
		- lymph nodes

- Poor prognostic factors
	- age <35 years at diagnosis
	- triple negative disease
	- brain metastases
	- liver metastases
	- lymphangitis carcinomatosis of lung

- Monitoring in advanced breast cancer
	- Clinical review
		- every 3–6 months
	- Blood tests
		- full blood count
		- urea/electrolytes/creatinine
		- liver function tests
		- comprehensive metabolic panel
		- CA 15.3 tumour marker
			- may correlate with progression and response
	- Imaging
		- CT chest
		- CT abdomen
		- CT pelvis
		- bone scan
		- PET scan
			- not previously funded
			- MBS funding planned from Nov 2019
	- Follow-up
		- continues until end of life
		- frequency adjusted according to:
			- symptoms
			- disease progression
