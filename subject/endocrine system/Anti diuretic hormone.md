# nocturia
![[Pasted image 20260603175445.png]]

- Definition
	- Waking from sleep one or more times to void
	- Each void:
		- Preceded by sleep
		- Followed by sleep

- Clinical Significance
	- Usually bothersome when:
		- >2 voids/night
	- Causes:
		- Sleep disturbance
		- Daytime fatigue
		- Poor concentration
		- Memory impairment
		- Mood disturbance
		- Falls
		- Hip fractures
	- Associated with:
		- Increased mortality

- Epidemiology
	- Increases with age
	- >70% prevalence in age ≥70 years
	- Up to 30% of adults aged 20–40 years void at least once nightly
	- Younger patients:
		- More common in females
	- Older patients:
		- Similar prevalence in both sexes

- Circadian Physiology

	- Normal
		- ADH secretion increases during sleep
		- Reduced nocturnal urine production
		- More concentrated urine

	- Elderly
		- Reduced nocturnal ADH surge
		- Increased nocturnal urine production

- Classification

	- Nocturnal Polyuria
	- Global Polyuria
	- Bladder Storage Disorders

- Frequency-Volume Chart (Voiding Diary)

	- Most important diagnostic tool

	- Record for:
		- 24–72 hours

	- Include:
		- Time of voids
		- Volume of voids
		- Sleep periods
		- Fluid intake
			- Type
			- Volume
			- Time

- Frequency-Volume Chart Definitions

	- 24-Hour Voided Volume
		- Total urine volume in 24 hours

	- Nocturnal Urine Volume
		- Total urine passed overnight
		- Includes:
			- First morning void
		- Excludes:
			- Last void before bed

	- Maximum Voided Volume
		- Largest single voided volume
		- Usually:
			- 300–600 mL

- Global Polyuria

	- Definition
		- Urine output >40 mL/kg/day

	- 24-hour urine volume
		- Increased

	- Causes
		- Primary polydipsia
		- Diabetes mellitus
		- Diabetes insipidus

- Nocturnal Polyuria

	- Definition
		- Excess urine production during sleep
		- Normal 24-hour urine volume

	- Diagnostic Criteria

		- Age <65 years
			- Nocturnal urine volume >20% of total daily urine volume

		- Age ≥65 years
			- Nocturnal urine volume >33% of total daily urine volume

	- Most common cause of nocturia

	- Causes

		- Reduced nocturnal ADH secretion
			- Ageing
			- Hypothalamic-pituitary disorders

		- Peripheral oedema
			- Recumbency causes fluid mobilisation
			- Increased nocturnal diuresis

		- Obstructive sleep apnoea
			- Increased atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP)
			- Natriuresis

		- Congestive heart failure
		- Chronic kidney disease

- Bladder Storage Disorders

	- Pathophysiology
		- Reduced bladder capacity
		- Increased urinary frequency
		- Small-volume voids

	- Features
		- Frequency
		- Urgency
		- Incontinence
		- Other LUTS

	- Causes
		- Overactive bladder
		- Bladder outlet obstruction
		- BPH
		- Bladder pathology

	- Important
		- Nocturia alone does not indicate BPH
		- BPH more likely if:
			- Hesitancy
			- Weak stream
			- Incomplete emptying

- Sleep Disorders

	- Patients wake for non-urological reasons
	- Void after awakening

	- Causes
		- Obstructive sleep apnoea
		- Insomnia

	- Clue
		- Awakens first
		- Voids second

- Clinical Assessment

	- History

		- Fluid intake
		- Evening fluid intake
		- Alcohol intake
		- Caffeine intake

		- LUTS

			- Voiding symptoms
				- Hesitancy
				- Weak stream
				- Incomplete emptying

			- Storage symptoms
				- Frequency
				- Urgency

		- Incontinence

		- Sleep history

		- Medical history
			- Heart failure
			- Renal failure
			- Diabetes mellitus
			- OSA
			- Thyroid disease
			- Lung disease
			- GORD

		- Medication review

		- OSA symptoms
			- Snoring
			- Witnessed apnoeas

	- Examination

		- Blood pressure
		- Weight
		- Waist circumference

		- Cardiovascular examination
			- Heart failure signs

		- Neurological examination
			- Sacral integrity
			- Perineal sensation
			- Anal wink

		- Digital rectal examination

- Investigations

	- Frequency-volume chart
	- Urinalysis
	- MSU if indicated
	- Urea, electrolytes, creatinine
	- Serum glucose
	- Lipid profile
	- PSA if indicated
	- Renal tract ultrasound if indicated

- Medications Associated with Nocturia

	- Increased urine production
		- Diuretics
		- SSRIs
		- Calcium channel blockers
		- Lithium
		- Tetracyclines

	- Cause insomnia/CNS stimulation
		- Stimulants
		- α-blockers
		- β-blockers
		- Methyldopa
		- Decongestants
		- Psychotropics
		- Parkinson medications
		- Phenytoin

	- Cause LUTS
		- Ketamine
		- Cyclophosphamide

- Management

	- General Measures

		- Reduce evening caffeine
		- Reduce evening alcohol
		- Limit fluids 3 hours before bed
		- Limit late evening meals
		- Pre-emptive voiding before sleep
		- Optimise sleep environment
		- Regular exercise

		- Peripheral oedema
			- Evening leg elevation

		- Medication timing
			- Diuretics:
				- Mid-afternoon
			- Calcium channel blockers:
				- Morning dosing

- Treatment by Cause

	- Global Polyuria

		- Primary polydipsia
			- Fluid restriction

		- Diabetes mellitus
			- Improve glycaemic control

		- Diabetes insipidus
			- Endocrinology referral

	- Nocturnal Polyuria

		- Treat underlying cause
			- Heart failure
			- CKD
			- OSA

		- OSA
			- Sleep study
			- CPAP if indicated

		- Desmopressin
			- ADH analogue
			- Reduces nocturnal urine production

			- Contraindications
				- Heart failure
				- Renal failure

			- Risk
				- Hyponatraemia

	- Bladder Storage Disorders

		- Urology referral

		- Further tests
			- Uroflowmetry
			- Post-void residual
			- Urodynamics
			- Cystoscopy

		- Bladder outlet obstruction/BPH

			- α-blockers
				- Tamsulosin
				- Alfuzosin
				- Prazosin

			- Adverse effect
				- Postural hypotension

			- Surgery
				- TURP
				- Laser prostatectomy

		- Overactive bladder

			- Antimuscarinics
				- Oxybutynin
				- Tolterodine
				- Solifenacin
				- Darifenacin

			- Most useful if urgency present

# siadh
![[Pasted image 20260603175035.png|severity and management of SIADH]]



- Definition
	- Euvolaemic hypotonic hyponatraemia caused by inappropriate ADH secretion
	- Most common cause of euvolaemic hyponatraemia
	- Accounts for approximately one-third of hyponatraemia cases

- Pathophysiology

	- ADH secretion occurs despite:
		- Normal plasma osmolality
		- Low plasma osmolality

	- Excess ADH causes:
		- Increased water reabsorption in collecting ducts
		- Dilutional hyponatraemia

	- Result
		- Water retention
		- Mild volume expansion
		- Secondary natriuresis
		- Clinical euvolaemia

- Causes

	- CNS Disorders
		- Subarachnoid haemorrhage
		- Subdural haemorrhage
		- Meningitis
		- Encephalitis
		- Brain tumour
		- Head injury
		- Guillain-Barré syndrome

	- Pulmonary Disorders
		- Pneumonia
		- Tuberculosis
		- Bronchiolitis
		- Asthma
		- Pneumothorax
		- Atelectasis
		- Mechanical ventilation

	- Malignancy
		- Small cell lung cancer (classic)
		- Nasopharyngeal carcinoma
		- Pancreatic cancer
		- GI malignancy
		- Lymphoma
		- Mesothelioma
		- Sarcoma

	- Medications
		- SSRIs
			- Sertraline
			- Escitalopram
		- Carbamazepine
		- Oxcarbazepine
		- Valproate
		- Antipsychotics
		- Cyclophosphamide
		- NSAIDs
		- ACE inhibitors
		- Desmopressin
		- Oxytocin
		- Ecstasy (MDMA)

	- Endocrine Disorders
		- Hypothyroidism
		- Adrenal insufficiency

- Diagnostic Criteria (Bartter & Schwartz)

	- Essential Findings
		- Hyponatraemia
		- Clinical euvolaemia
		- Low serum osmolality
			- <275 mOsm/kg
		- Inappropriately concentrated urine
			- Urine osmolality >100 mOsm/kg
		- Elevated urine sodium
			- >20 mmol/L

	- Exclusion Criteria
		- Diuretic use
		- Hypothyroidism
		- Adrenal insufficiency
		- Hyperglycaemia
		- Hyperlipidaemia
		- Hyperproteinaemia

- Classic Laboratory Pattern

	- Serum sodium
		- ↓

	- Serum osmolality
		- ↓
		- <275 mOsm/kg

	- Urine osmolality
		- ↑
		- >100 mOsm/kg

	- Urine sodium
		- ↑
		- >20 mmol/L

	- Fluid status
		- Euvolaemic

- AMC Diagnostic Triad

	- Euvolaemia
	- Low serum osmolality
	- Inappropriately concentrated urine
		- High urine sodium
		- High urine osmolality

- Differential Diagnosis of Euvolaemic Hyponatraemia

	- SIADH
	- Hypothyroidism
	- Adrenal insufficiency
	- Primary polydipsia
	- Drug-induced hyponatraemia

- Investigations

	- Serum sodium
	- Serum osmolality
	- Urine osmolality
		- Same time as serum sample

	- Urine sodium
	- Thyroid function tests
	- Morning cortisol

	- Search for underlying cause

		- Chest X-ray
			- Pulmonary disease
			- Malignancy

		- CT brain
			- CNS pathology

- Management

	- Treat underlying cause
		- Stop offending medications
		- Treat infection
		- Treat malignancy

	- First-Line Treatment
		- Fluid restriction

			- Typical restriction:
				- 800–1200 mL/day

			- Chronic SIADH:
				- Restrict to approximately 500 mL below daily urine output

- Severe Symptomatic SIADH

	- Features
		- Altered mental state
		- Seizures
		- Coma
		- Na <120 mmol/L

	- Management
		- Hospital admission
		- Close monitoring
		- Fluid restriction
		- Consider hypertonic saline (3%)

- Sodium Correction Limits

	- Maximum increase
		- ≤10 mmol/L in first 24 hours
		- ≤18 mmol/L in first 48 hours

	- Aim
		- Symptom improvement
		- Safe sodium:
			- >120–125 mmol/L

- Complication of Overcorrection

	- Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS)
		- Central pontine myelinolysis

	- Features
		- Dysarthria
		- Dysphagia
		- Quadriparesis
		- Locked-in syndrome

- AMC One-Liner
	- Euvolaemic patient with hyponatraemia, serum osmolality <275 mOsm/kg, urine osmolality >100 mOsm/kg, urine sodium >20 mmol/L, and normal thyroid/adrenal function = SIADH.

# diabetes insipidus
- Key Points (VERY HIGH-YIELD)

	- Always allow free access to water
		- NEVER restrict fluids

	- Urine output
		- NOT a reliable hydration marker

	- Close monitoring required
		- Electrolytes
		- Fluid balance

	- Nephrogenic DI
		- Avoid sodium chloride-containing fluids
			- Except emergency situations
		- Use only after specialist advice

- Definition
	- Diabetes insipidus
		- Disorder of impaired urine concentration
	- Polyuria
			- >4 mL/kg/hr over 6 hours
			- Higher normal values in neonates

- pathogenesis
	- Central DI
		- ↓ or absent ADH secretion
	-  Nephrogenic DI
		- Renal resistance to ADH

- Etiology
	- Central DI Causes
		- Head trauma
		- Brain tumour
			- Especially pituitary region
		- Post-neurosurgery
		- Genetic causes
		- Meningitis
		- Idiopathic

	- Nephrogenic DI Causes
		- Genetic
		- Acquired renal defects

	- Rare Causes
		- Systemic causes
		- Drug-induced causes


- clinical features

	- Polyuria



	- Nocturia
	- Enuresis
	- Polydipsia
		- Overnight drinking
		- Persistent drinking behaviour
		- Drinking from unusual sources
			- e.g. animal bowls

	- Infant Features
		- Irritability
		- Poor feeding
		- Slow growth
		- Excess weight
	
	- Neurological Symptoms
		- Early morning headache
		- Vomiting
		- Altered sensation
		- Weakness


	- High-Risk Situations
		- Limited Access to Water
			- Infants
			- Altered conscious state
			- NBM status
			- Severe illness
	
		- Impaired Thirst Mechanism
			- CNS midline defects
			- Hypothalamic injury


- Investigations
	- Urgent Investigation
		- BGL
			- Exclude diabetes mellitus
		- DI Pattern
			- High serum osmolality
			- Dilute urine
		- Urine osmolality <700
			- Consider partial DI
	- Imaging
		- Renal ultrasound
			- Especially in nephrogenic DI
	- Water Deprivation Test
		- Differentiates:
			- Central DI
			- Nephrogenic DI
			- Primary polydipsia
		- Specialist supervision only
		- Avoid in children <12 months	
	- If Central DI Suspected
		- MRI brain/pituitary
		- Anterior pituitary hormone assessment
- Management
	- General Principles
		- NEVER restrict fluids
		- Strict fluid balance
			- Frequent review
		- Daily weight
			- Or more frequent if required
	- Desmopressin (DDAVP)
		- ADH analogue
		- Ineffective in:
			- Complete nephrogenic DI

	- Central DI
		- Hydration
			- Assess hydration status
			- IV Fluids if required
				- 0.9% sodium chloride + 5% glucose
			- Avoid hypotonic fluids
			- Correct abnormalities slowly
		- Sodium Thresholds
			- Sodium ≥170 mmol/L
				- ICU
				- Urgent specialist involvement
			- Sodium 150–169 mmol/L
				- Slow correction over 48 hours
		- Post-neurosurgery
			- Triphasic response possible
				- DI
				- SIADH
				- Permanent DI
			- Requires close monitoring
			- DDAVP
				- PRN initially
		- Desmopressin (DDAVP)
			- Initiate with endocrinology input
			- Pharmacology
				- “All or nothing” effect
				- Dose affects duration only
					- Duration ~8–24 hours
			- Routes are NOT equivalent
		- Ongoing DDAVP Use
			- Do NOT stop or modify without specialist advice
			- Consider adrenal insufficiency
				- Give stress-dose steroids if unwell
			- Impaired thirst
				- Requires specialist-guided fluid prescription
			- Monitoring
				- Daily:
					- UEC
					- Serum osmolality
				- Paired urine osmolality
				- More frequent monitoring if unstable
		- DDAVP Administration Practice Points
			- Check sodium before dose
				- Na ≥135 mmol/L
			- Check urine specific gravity
				- <1.005 = dilute urine
			- Ensure 1–2 hours diuresis before next dose
			- If urine output >4 mL/kg/hr for 2 hours
				- Recheck sodium
				- Adjust dose
			- If no urine output for 12 hours
				- Reduce or withhold dose
			- Seek specialist advice if uncertain
	- Nephrogenic DI
		- Older children
			- Usually self-regulate intake
		- Infants
			- May require NG or PEG fluids
		- Diet
			- Low sodium
			- Low protein
		- Medications
			- Thiazide diuretics
			- Desmopressin
				- Rarely useful
				- Partial response only
		- Correct electrolytes
			- Calcium
			- Potassium
			- Magnesium
	- Hydration
		- Emergency
			- 10 mL/kg 0.9% sodium chloride bolus
		- Ongoing fluids
			- Specialist-guided
		- Preferred fluid
			- Enteral free water
		- If enteral route not possible
			- 5% glucose
		- Avoid
			- Sodium-containing fluids
				- Unless specialist advised
- Paediatrics Consultation indications
	- Any polyuria or polydipsia
- Endocrinology / Nephrology consultations indications
	- All suspected DI
	- DDAVP initiation/modification
	- Impaired thirst
	- Reduced access to water
	- Nephrogenic DI with hypernatraemia
- Transfer indications
	- Severe hypernatraemia
		- Sodium >170 mmol/L
	- Complex fluid/electrolyte management
	- Care beyond local capabiity
