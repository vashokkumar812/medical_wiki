# Adrenal incidentaloma
- Definition
	- Adrenal mass >1 cm
	- Incidentally discovered on imaging
	- No symptoms/signs suggesting adrenal disease
- Epidemiology
	- age
- Initial assessment
	- Structural assessment
		- Dedicated adrenal CT scan
			- Main question
				- Benign vs malignant
			- Benign adenoma CT features
				- Lipid-rich adenoma
				- <10 Hounsfield units (HU)
				- Smooth
				- Rounded
				- Homogeneous
				- <4 cm diameter
				- Contrast washout
					- Absolute washout >60%
					- Relative washout >40%
			- MRI
				- shows lipid content
			- Follow-up imaging
				- No further imaging if:
					- ≤10 HU
					- Homogeneous
					- <4 cm
				- Repeat CT in 6–12 months if uncertain
			- treatment
				- Lesion size
					- \>6 cm
						- resect
					- 4–6 cm
						- Grey area
	- Functional assessment
		- Exclude:
			- Cushing syndrome
			- Primary aldosteronism
			- Phaeochromocytoma
		- Initial investigations

| Disorder              | Initial investigation                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adrenal incidentaloma | Dedicated adrenal CT                                                                              |
| Cushing syndrome      | Overnight dexamethasone suppression test / midnight salivary cortisol / 24h urinary free cortisol |
| Primary aldosteronism | Aldosterone-renin ratio                                                                           |
| Phaeochromocytoma     | Plasma metanephrines or 24h urinary metanephrines                                                 |
| Adrenal insufficiency | Early morning cortisol + ACTH                                                                     |

# Cushing syndrome
- Definition
	- excess glucocorticoids ( especially steroids )
- Etiology
	- Iatrogenic
		- Chronic corticosteroid administration
		- ==Most common cause overall==
	- ACTH-Independent Causes
		- Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia
		- Adrenal adenoma
		- Adrenal adenocarcinoma	
	- ACTH-Dependent Causes
		- Pituitary ACTH excess
			- Cushing disease
		- Ectopic ACTH secretion
			- non-endocrine tumours - Oat cell carcinoma of lung
		- Rare ectopic CRH secretion
- Clinical Features
	- Body Habitus
		- Central obesity
		- Thin extremities
		- Buffalo hump
	- Facial Features
		- male pattern alopecia
		- Plethoric moon face
			- due to polycythemia
		- Acne
	- Muscle Features
		- Proximal muscle wasting
		- Proximal muscle weakness
	- Skin Features
		- Thin skin
		- Easy bruising
		- purple Abdominal striae
			- not white or pink
	- Endocrine / Metabolic Features
		- Hyperglycaemia
		- Hypertension
		- Osteoporosis
	- Reproductive Features
		- Amenorrhoea
		- Hirsutism
	- Neuropsychiatric Features
		- Depression
	- Acneiform Eruption
		- Uniform pustules
		- Comedones absent
		- Cysts absent
	- Oedema
		- Due to mineralocorticoid excess
	- Other Symptoms
		- Weakness
		- Backache
- Diagnostic Clue
	- Plethoric moon face
	- Thin extremities
	- Muscle weakness
		- Suggestive of Cushing syndrome
- differential diagnosis
	- Pseudo-Cushing syndrome
		- Excess alcohol intake
- investigations
	- Screening tests
		- 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test
			- failure to supress at 8am cortisol test
			- Normal response
				- Cortisol <50 nmol/L
			- Failure to suppress
				- autonomous cortisol production
			- Drugs Causing False-Positive Overnight Dexamethasone Suppression Test
				- Estrogen-containing preparations
					- Oral contraceptive pill
					- Menopausal hormone therapy
		- Midnight salivary cortisol
		- 24h urinary free cortisol
	- if high cortisol ==> measure Serum ACTH
		- Low ACTH
			- ACTH-independent disease
			- Suggests adrenal source; do CT/MRI adrenal glands
				- CT Adrenal
					- Detect adrenal tumour
		- High ACTH
			- ACTH-dependent disease
			- Perform
				- High-dose dexamethasone suppression test
				- CRH stimulation test
					- ACTH suppressible / CRH responsive
						- Pituitary source likely
						- MRI pituitary
					- ACTH not suppressible
						- Ectopic ACTH source likely
						- CT chest
							- Look for small cell lung carcinoma
	- Localisation Studies
		- MRI Pituitary
			- Detect ACTH-producing pituitary tumour
		- Inferior Petrosal Sinus Sampling
			- Helps confirm pituitary ACTH source

- Management
	- Pituitary tumour
		- Transsphenoidal surgery
	- Adrenal adenoma/carcinoma
		- Surgical removal
	- Pharmacological blockade of cortisol synthesis
		- Indications
			- Unclear ACTH source
			- Preoperative control
			- Failed surgery
		- Managed in specialist centres
		- Glucocorticoid replacement required;  Mineralocorticoid replacement not required
			- Until HPA axis recovery
			- Usually several months
			- Up to 1 year
	- First-Line Drug
		- Ketoconazole


# primary aldosteronism
- Definition
	- Excess aldosterone production due to Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia, Adrenal adenoma
- Causes
	- Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia (BAH)
	- Aldosterone-producing adenoma (APA, Conn syndrome)
	- Rare
		- Bilateral nodular adrenal hyperplasia
- Pathophysiology
	- Excess aldosterone
		- Sodium retention
		- Potassium loss
		- Hydrogen ion loss
	- Results in:
		- Hypertension
		- Hypokalaemia
		- Metabolic alkalosis
- Clinical Features
	- Hypertension
		- Usually asymptomatic initially
		- Often refractory or resistant hypertension
	- Symptoms Mainly Due to Hypokalaemia
		- Weakness
		- Headaches
		- Palpitations
		- Muscle cramps
		- Paraesthesia
		- Polyuria
		- Polydipsia
- Complications
	- Left ventricular hypertrophy
	- Stroke
	- Atrial fibrillation
	- Myocardial infarction
	- Heart failure
	- Proteinuria

- screening
	- Who Should Be Screened?
		- BP >150/100 mmHg on 3 separate occasions
		- Resistant hypertension
			- BP >140/90 despite 3 antihypertensives including diuretic
		- Controlled BP on ≥4 antihypertensive drugs
		- Hypertension + spontaneous hypokalaemia
		- Hypertension + diuretic-induced hypokalaemia
		- Hypertension + adrenal incidentaloma
		- Hypertension + obstructive sleep apnoea
		- Hypertension + family history of early-onset hypertension
		- Hypertension + stroke before age 40 years in family
		- First-degree relatives of patients with primary aldosteronism
	
	- Antihypertensives That Can Be Used During Workup
		- Sustained-release verapamil
		- Prazosin
		- Moxonidine
		- Hydralazine
	- Medications to Stop Before Screening
		- Group 1 (Must replace)
			- Loop diuretics
				- Frusemide
				- Bumetanide
			- Thiazide diuretics
				- Hydrochlorothiazide
				- Indapamide
				- Chlortalidone
			- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists
				- Spironolactone
				- Eplerenone
		- Group 2 (Replace wherever possible)
			- ACE inhibitors
				- Perindopril
			- ARBs
				- Olmesartan
			- Dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers
				- Amlodipine
		- Group 3 (Replace after Groups 1 & 2)
			- Beta blockers
				- Atenolol
	
	- Drug Switching Principles
		- Stop interfering drugs
		- Start replacement drugs on same day
		- Continue replacement regimen for 6 weeks
		- Wean beta blockers before switching to verapamil
	- Replacement Drugs
		- Sustained-release Verapamil
			- Common adverse effects
				- Constipation
				- Bradycardia
			- Rare
				- AV block
				- Heart failure
		- Moxonidine
			- Contraindications
				- Heart failure
				- Bradycardia
				- AV block
				- CrCl <30 mL/min
			- Adverse effects
				- Dry mouth
				- Somnolence
				- Dizziness
				- Weakness
		- Prazosin
			- Adverse effects
				- Postural hypotension
				- Dizziness
				- Palpitations
				- Dry mouth
				- Blurred vision
		- Hydralazine
			- Adverse effects
				- Flushing
				- Headache
				- Palpitations
				- Oedema
				- Dizziness
			- Rare
				- Angina
				- Drug-induced lupus
	- Potassium Management
		- Hypokalaemia reduces screening accuracy
		- Correct before ARR
		- Slow-release potassium chloride commonly used
		- Recheck potassium after 1 week
	- Special Situations
		- Heart failure with LVEF <50%
			- Do not stop ACE inhibitor or ARB
		- Frail or elderly patients
			- ARR can be measured without changing medications
		- Clue to primary aldosteronism
			- Low renin despite ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy
	- Other Drugs Affecting ARR
		- Antidepressants
		- Combined oral contraceptive pill
		- Hormone replacement therapy
	- screening investigation
		- aldosterone renin ratio
		- timing
			- morning
			- after 2 hours of rising
			- fasting not required
			- before starting anti hypertensive
		- ARR >70 pmol/L:mU/L
			- Suggests PA
	
		- Optimisation Before ARR
			- Correct hypokalaemia
			- Ensure normokalaemia
			- Replace interfering medications
			- Wait 6 weeks before ARR testing
			- Causes of false-positive ARR
				- Beta blockers
				- Clonidine
				- α-methyldopa
				- NSAIDs
				- Licorice
				- Renal impairment
				- Oral oestrogens
			- Causes of false-negative ARR
				- Diuretics
				- Dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers
				- ACE inhibitors
				- ARBs
				- Hypokalaemia
				- Salt restriction
				- Renovascular disease
				- Pregnancy
- Investigations
	- Confirmatory test
		- Saline infusion test
	- Aldosterone
		- Serum aldosterone increased
		- Urinary aldosterone increased
	- Plasma Renin
		- Low plasma renin
	- Aldosterone-to-Renin Ratio
		- Increased
	- Electrolytes
		- hypernatemia + hypokalemia
		- Metabolic alkalosis present
	- Imaging
		- CT adrenal scan
		- MRI adrenal scan
	- Subtyping
		- Adrenal venous sampling
			- Differentiate:
				- Unilateral
				- Bilateral
			- avoid if
			-  Age <40 years
			- Spontaneous hypokalaemia
			- Marked aldosterone excess
			- Unilateral adrenal adenoma on CT
- Management
	- if builateral adrenal hyperplasia
		- Medical Therapy
			- Spironolactone, eplerenone
				- Used preoperatively
				- Aldosterone antagonist
	- if aldosterone producing adenoma ==> Surgical Referral
		- Adrenal adenoma
			- Surgical excision - adrenalectomy
	- Familial Dexamethasone-Suppressible Primary Aldosteronism  
		- Rare inherited form  
		- Treatment  
			- Low-dose dexamethasone  
			- Spironolactone  
			- Amiloride
	- Differential Diagnosis of Hypertension + Hypokalaemia
		- Primary aldosteronism
			- Low renin
			- High aldosterone
		- Secondary hyperaldosteronism
			- Diuretic use
			- Renovascular hypertension
			- Renin-secreting tumours
			- High renin
			- High aldosterone
		- Cushing syndrome
			- Excess cortisol, corticosterone, deoxycorticosterone
			- Low renin
			- Low aldosterone
			- Cushingoid appearance
		- Liddle syndrome
			- ENaC gain-of-function mutation
			- Low renin
			- Low aldosterone
		- Chronic liquorice ingestion
			- Inhibits conversion of cortisol to cortisone
			- Low renin
			- Low aldosterone
		- Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
			- 11β-hydroxylase deficiency
			- Excess deoxycorticosterone
			- Low renin
			- Low aldosterone

# Phaeochromocytoma
- Definition
	- Catecholamine-secreting tumour from adrenal medulla
- etiology
	- SDH mutations
	- MEN2
	- VHL
	- NF1
- Clinical features
	- Hypertension
		- labile
	- Classic triad:
		- Episodic headache
		- Sweating
		- Tachycardia
	- spells of  
		* anxiety  
		* hypertension  
		* headache (throbbing)  
		* tremor  
		* sweating  
		* palpitations  
		* pallor/skin blanching  
		* rising sensation of tightness in upper chest and throat (angina can occur)
- Screening
	- series of 3
		- Plasma free metanephrines and Normetanephrines ( preferred)
		- 24h urinary metanephrines
	- False positives
		- Acute illness
		- Antidepressants
- differential diagnosis
	- Paraganglioma
		- Similar tumour from sympathetic ganglia
- investigations
	- Imaging
		- CT abdomen/pelvis
		- MRI abdomen/pelvis
- Treatment
	- Alpha blocker first
		- Phenoxybenzamine
		- Prazosin
	- Then beta blocker
	- Important point
		- Never start beta blocker before alpha blockade
		- Risk hypertensive crisis
	- definite treatment
		- adrenalectomy

| Drug class | Mechanism causing false-positive metanephrine test |
|------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) | Inhibition of noradrenaline reuptake → ↑ normetanephrine concentration |
| Noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors | Inhibition of noradrenaline reuptake → ↑ normetanephrine concentration |
| Serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) | Inhibition of noradrenaline reuptake → ↑ normetanephrine concentration |
| Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) | ↑ Metabolism of catecholamines to metanephrines → ↑ normetanephrine and metanephrine concentrations |
| Indirect sympathomimetics | ↑ Catecholamine release → ↑ normetanephrine and metanephrine concentrations |
| Alpha blockers | Adrenoceptor blockade with compensatory ↑ noradrenaline production → ↑ normetanephrine concentration |
| Beta blockers | Adrenoceptor blockade with compensatory ↑ noradrenaline production → ↑ normetanephrine concentration |
| Tetracyclic antidepressants | Adrenoceptor blockade with compensatory ↑ noradrenaline production → ↑ normetanephrine concentration |



# addison disease (Primary adrenal insufficiency)
- Definition
	- Destruction/dysfunction of adrenal cortex
	- ↓ Cortisol ± ↓ aldosterone
	- ↑ ACTH
- Epidemiology
	- Rare
	- Adults
		- More common in women
	- Children
		- More common in boys
- Causes
	- Autoimmune adrenalitis
		- 70–80% in Western countries
		- Most common cause
		- Autoantibody
			- CYP21A2 (21-hydroxylase)

	- Infectious
		- Tuberculosis
		- Histoplasmosis
		- Cryptococcus
		- CMV

	- Acute adrenal haemorrhage
		- Meningococcal septicaemia

	- Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (children)
		- 21-hydroxylase deficiency
		- Adrenal hyperplasia due to ↑ ACTH

- Associated autoimmune diseases
	- Autoimmune thyroid disease
	- vitiligo
	- primary gonal failure
	- celiac disease
	- pernicious anemia
	- Type 1 diabetes
	- Hypoparathyroidism
	- Polyglandular autoimmune syndromes



- Adrenal crisis
	- Medical emergency

	- Triggers
		- Sepsis
		- Stress
		- Trauma
		- Dehydration
		- Steroid withdrawal
		- Missed medications

	- Suspect if
		- Shock/collapse
		- Altered consciousness
		- Seizures
		- Hypoglycaemia
		- Hyponatraemia
		- Hyperkalaemia
		- History of steroid use
		- Features of Addison disease

- Clinical features
	- Cortisol deficiency
		- Weakness, fatigue, weight loss, anorexia, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea/constipation, hypoglycaemia
	- Aldosterone deficiency
		- Hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, dehydration, hypotension, salt craving, tachycardia
	- Excess ACTH
		- Hyperpigmentation, buccal pigmentation, palmar crease pigmentation, scars, nipples, axillae
	- Neuropsychiatric symptoms
		- Depression
		- Apathy
		- Confusion
		- Memory impairment
		- Psychosis
	
	- Key examination findings
		- Diffuse “suntan”
		- Buccal mucosal pigmentation
		- Postural hypotension
		- Dehydration
		- Shock

- Investigations
	- Initial tests
		- Hyponatraemia
		- Hyperkalaemia
		- Hypoglycaemia
		- Metabolic acidosis
	- confirmatory
		- Serum morning cortisol
			- < 140 nmol/L
			- if > 365 ==> unlikely
		- ACTH
			- /2x upper limit normal
		- 24-hour urinary cortisol
		- Synacthen test
			- Do not delay treatment
			- Standard confirmatory test
			- 250 μg Synacthen
			- Abnormal Synacthen test
				- Peak cortisol <500 nmol/L at 30 or 60 min
		- Not useful for adrenal insufficiency diagnosis
				- Midnight salivary cortisol
				- 24h urinary free cortisol
		- if antibody negative
			- adrenal CT
- treatment
	- Glucocorticoid replacement
		- Hydrocortisone
		- Cortisone acetate
	- Mineralocorticoid replacement
		- Fludrocortisone
	- Prednisolone
		- Used if poor compliance
	- Stress dosing
		- Triple oral steroid dose during illness/stress
			- Fever
			- Fracture
			- Infection
	
		- IM hydrocortisone if
			- Vomiting
			- Unable to tolerate oral meds
	
	- Osteoporosis prevention
		- Calcium
		- Vitamin D
		- Exercise
		- DEXA scan if indicated

- Monitoring
	- Postural BP
	- Weight
	- Energy levels
	- Symptoms/signs of steroid excess/deficiency
	- Renin level for fludrocortisone adequacy
- Patient education
	- Emergency card/bracelet
	- Increase steroid dose during stress
	- Seek urgent care during illness
- Acute adrenal crisis management
	- Do not delay steroids
	- Fluids
		- Normal saline boluses
		- Dextrose-containing maintenance fluids
	- Hydrocortisone
		- IV/IM immediately
	- Correct
		- Hypoglycaemia
		- Hyperkalaemia
	- Treat precipitating cause
		- Sepsis etc.
	- ICU may be required
- Hydrocortisone dosing in adrenal crisis
- Why Addison disease is missed
	- Rare disease
	- Non-specific symptoms
	- Low clinical suspicion

	- Low cortisol
	- Low/suppressed ACTH
- Causes
	- Pituitary disease
	- Hypothalamic disease

- Features
	- Mineralocorticoid preserved
	- No hyperpigmentation
	- Electrolytes often normal
	- Associated findings
		- Visual field defects
		- Other pituitary hormone deficiencies
- Investigations
		- Pituitary hormones
		- Pituitary MRI
- Treatment
		- Glucocorticoid replacement only

# CAH
- Definition
	- decrease cortisol and aldosterone
- pathogenesis
	- Autosomal recessive disorder
	- 21-hydroxylase deficiency → ↓ cortisol and aldosterone with ↑ androgen production ==> adrenal failure ± salt-losing state (SLS)
- Clinical features
	- Major problem: adrenal failure ± salt-losing state
	- Females:
		- Ambiguous genitalia (masculinisation)
		- Hirsutism before puberty
	- Males:
		- Normal urogenital development
		- Salt-losing state may be present
	- Infants (both sexes):
		- Failure to thrive
		- Vomiting
		- Dehydration (SLS)
- Complication
	- Salt-losing crisis (life-threatening)
- Management
	- Lifelong glucocorticoid therapy (e.g. prednisolone)
- Advice
	- Wear medical alert bracelet/necklace
	- Important for emergency identification
