# breast cancer screening
- Risk categories
	- Potentially high risk / mutation carrier
		- Known BRCA1/BRCA2 mutation in family
		- One first-/second-degree relative with breast cancer <45 years PLUS another relative with sarcoma <45 years
			- Suggests Li-Fraumeni syndrome
		- Family history patterns
			- Two first-/second-degree relatives on one side with breast or ovarian cancer PLUS:
				- Additional relatives with breast/ovarian cancer
				- Breast cancer before age 40
				- Bilateral breast cancer
				- Breast and ovarian cancer in same woman
				- Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry
				- Male breast cancer
	- Moderately increased risk
		- Family history patterns
			- One first-degree relative diagnosed <50 years
				- Without high-risk features
			- Two first-degree relatives on same side with breast cancer
				- Without high-risk features
			- Two second-degree relatives on same side
				- At least one diagnosed <50 years
				- Without high-risk features
	- Average/slightly higher risk
		- Family history patterns
			- No confirmed family history
			- One first-degree relative diagnosed ≥50 years
			- One second-degree relative diagnosed at any age
			- Two second-degree relatives on same side diagnosed ≥50 years
			- Two first-/second-degree relatives diagnosed ≥50 years on different sides of family


- BRCA mutations
	- Associated with
		- Breast cancer
		- Ovarian cancer
		- Male breast cancer

- SNP/polygenic risk testing
	- Not routinely recommended in general practice

	- National breast cancer screening program
	- Focuses mainly on age
	- Target population
		- Women 50–74 years
	- Screening interval
		- Every 2 years
	- Women may self-refer from

	- Average-risk women

			- Recommended age
				- 50–74 years
			- Frequency
				- Every 2 years
		- Women aged 40–49 years
			- May choose screening
	- Moderately increased risk women
		- Mammographic screening
			- Age 40–74 years
			- At least every 2 years
		- Annual mammograms
			- May be recommended
	- High-risk women
		- Ongoing surveillance may include
			- MRI
			- Mammography

- MRI in screening
	- Average-risk women
		- MRI as stand-alone screening
			- Not recommended
	- High-risk women
		- MRI may be part of surveillance


- Dense breasts
	- Current evidence insufficient for routine supplemental screening using
		- Ultrasound
		- MRI
	- Shared decision-making recommended

- Thermography
	- Not recommended
	- Not recommended as
		- Primary screening tool
		- Adjunct to mammography

# inflammatory breast disorders
- Inflammatory breast conditions

    - Breast infection/inflammation may result from
        - Lactational mastitis
        - Nonlactational mastitis
        - Periductal mastitis
        - Inflammatory breast cancer

- Mastitis

    - Lactational mastitis
        - Most common inflammatory breast condition
        - Usually occurs in first few weeks of breastfeeding

    - Clinical features
        - Breast pain
        - Swelling
        - Breast lump/lumps
        - Redness of overlying skin

    - Cause
        - Usually Staphylococcus aureus

    - Predisposing factors
        - Poor infant positioning
        - Poor attachment during feeding
        - Milk stasis

    - Treatment
        - Prompt antibiotics required

    - First-line oral antibiotics
        - Flucloxacillin
            - 500 mg QID for 10 days
        - Dicloxacillin
            - 500 mg QID for 10 days
        - Cephalexin
            - 500 mg QID for 10 days

    - Severe disease
        - Systemic symptoms
        - Significant cellulitis
            - May require IV antibiotics

    - Additional management
        - Continue frequent feeding from affected breast
        - Correct infant positioning and attachment

- Breast abscess

    - Suspect if
        - No rapid improvement with antibiotics
        - Firm discrete breast lump present

    - Investigation
        - Breast ultrasound

    - Management
        - Ultrasound-guided aspiration to dryness
        - May require repeated aspiration every few days
        - Send aspirate for microbiology

    - Severe/non-resolving cases
        - Hospital admission
        - IV antibiotics
        - Surgical drainage

- Nonlactational mastitis

    - May be associated with underlying cysts
    - Clinical presentation similar to lactational mastitis
    - Treatment
        - Antibiotics

- Periductal mastitis

    - Inflammatory condition involving nipple ducts

    - Presentation
        - Nipple redness
        - Nipple discharge

    - Epidemiology
        - Occurs in young women
        - Associated with smoking in 90%

    - Course
        - Recurrent episodes common

    - Management
        - May require surgical excision of nipple ducts

    - Surgical complication
        - Poor wound healing common

- Investigation of inflammatory breast conditions

    - Lactational mastitis
        - Can initially treat without investigation
        - Requires close clinical follow-up
        - If symptoms persist after 24–48 hours
            - Breast ultrasound

    - Nonlactational inflammatory symptoms
        - Investigate like any breast symptom

    - Benign lesions
        - Follow until complete clinical and imaging resolution

- Inflammatory breast cancer

    - Important differential diagnosis in all inflammatory breast conditions
    - Represents 1–4% of breast cancers
    - Poor prognosis

    - Clinical presentation
        - Rapid onset
        - Breast mass
        - Breast pain
        - Breast enlargement
        - Skin changes
            - Red
            - Purple
            - Peau d’orange ("orange peel")

    - Axillary lymph node involvement
        - Almost universal

    - Imaging findings
        - Increased skin thickness
        - Increased tissue density
        - May lack classic spiculated lesion/microcalcifications

    - Important principle
        - Infective lesions should completely resolve clinically and radiologically
        - If lesion does not resolve or behaves atypically
            - Consider inflammatory breast cancer

    - Investigation
        - Breast imaging
        - Fine needle biopsy
        - Core biopsy

    - Significant skin changes
        - Erythema
        - Peau d’orange
            - Skin biopsy may confirm diagnosis

- Causes of abnormal nipple discharge

    - Duct ectasia
        - Benign duct dilatation and inflammation
        - Usually bilateral
        - Yellow/green/brown discharge
        - Multiple ducts

    - Duct papilloma
        - Clear or bloodstained discharge
        - Usually benign
        - Rarely associated with breast cancer
        - Always surgically removed

    - Nipple eczema
        - Dermatitis/eczema of nipple skin
        - Can cause weeping crusty discharge
        - Especially if infected

    - Breast cancer
        - Uncommon cause of nipple discharge
        - About 3% of breast cancers present with discharge
        - Usually associated with other symptoms
            - Breast lump
            - Newly inverted nipple
        - More commonly in situ than invasive cancer

    - Paget disease
        - Breast cancer involving nipple
        - Bloodstained nipple discharge
        - Nipple ulceration/erosion

    - Hyperprolactinaemia
        - Causes galactorrhoea
        - Endocrine causes
            - Pituitary disease
            - Thyroid disease
        - Drug causes
            - Oral contraceptives
            - Hormone therapy
            - Antiemetics
            - Antipsychotics
            - Cocaine
            - Stimulants

# breast cyst

- Definition
	- Localised fluid collection
- Epidemiology
	- Peak incidence
		- Age 30–50 years
- Pathophysiology
	- normal
		- Fluid normally produced =>  reabsorbed in Milk duct , Lobular acini
	- in cyst
		- Duct blocked OR Fluid production exceeds absorption
- General Features
	- Single or multiple
	- Often impalpable
	- Often asymptomatic
	- Frequently incidental imaging finding
- Clinical Features
	- lump
		- Smooth
		- Soft to firm
		- Mobile
		- Sometimes tender
	- Tense cysts
		- May feel firm to hard
		- May be significantly tender
	- Cluster of cysts
		- May present as tender nodularity
	-  Hormonal Relationship
		- Hormonally responsive
		- Fluctuate during menstrual cycle
		-  disappear after menopause
		- May persist or newly occur in postmenopausal women on hormone therapy
- investigations
	- Mammography
		- Cysts may not be visible
		- If visible
			- Well defined round densities
			- May be multiple
	- Ultrasound
		- Better than mammography for cyst diagnosis
		- Better for distinguishing cystic vs solid lesions

- Management
	- Asymptomatic simple cysts
		- Reassure patient
		- Does not significantly increase breast cancer risk
		- No follow up

	- Rarely indicated

		- Recurrent palpable cyst despite repeated aspiration
		- Intracystic solid lesion on imaging
		- Lesion not resolving completely after aspiration
		- Atypical or suspicious cytology




# fibroadenoma
- Definition
	- Focal overgrowth of benign breast tissue
- made up of
	- Epithelial elements
	- Stromal elements
- Epidemiology
	- 21–25
- Etiology
	- Cause unknown
	- Hormonal factors important
		- Fluctuate during menstrual cycle
		- Fluctuate during pregnancy
- General Features
	

- Clinical Features
	- Solitary or multiple
	- Palpable or impalpable
	- Round or oval
	- Firm
	- Rubbery
	- Smooth
	- Very mobile
	- Usually painless
	- May be tender
		- Especially premenstrually
- Imaging Features
	- Often increasingly detected incidentally on imaging
	- Mammography
		- May not be visible
			- Same density as surrounding tissue
		- If visible
			- Well defined density
			- May be lobulated
			- May contain coarse “popcorn-like” calcification
			- ![[Pasted image 20260515083505.png]]
	- Ultrasound
		- Well circumscribed lesion
		- Homogeneous
		- Hypoechoic
		- Edge shadowing present
		- Gentle lobulations may occur
		- Coarse calcification may be visible
		- ![[Pasted image 20260515081417.png]]
- Differential Diagnosis
	- Phyllodes tumour
		- Similar clinical/imaging appearance
		- May be benign or malignant
		- Always surgically excised
	- [[breast carcinoma]]
		- High grade cellular cancers, Medullary carcinoma ==> mimics fibroadenoma
	- Other benign lesions
		- Cysts
		- Papillomas
		- Entrapped fat
- Diagnosis
	- Any suspicious fibroadenoma requires pathological diagnosis
		- Fine needle aspiration biopsy
		- Core biopsy
		- Surgical excision
	- Fine needle aspiration limitations
		- High fibrous tissue content may reduce diagnostic yield
	- Core biopsy increasingly preferred
- Management
	- Palpable Lesions

		- Women >40 years
			- New palpable fibroadenoma
				- Consider excision biopsy
					- Even if benign on triple testing
			- Reason
				- Probability of cancer increases with age

		- Women <40 years
			- Benign on triple testing
				- Options
					- Surgical excision
					- Regular clinical review
					- Regular ultrasound surveillance
				- Continue follow up until lesion stable over time

			- Atypical on any component of triple testing
				- Consider excision biopsy

	- Impalpable Lesions

		- Benign imaging features ± benign biopsy
			- Can undergo imaging surveillance

		- Surveillance interval
			- No universal consensus
			- Ultrasound every 6–12 months reasonable

	- General Considerations

		- Fibroadenoma >3–4 cm
			- Consider excision biopsy
				- Regardless of
					- Age
					- Triple testing results

		- Lesions under surveillance
			- Significant increase in size
			- Development of atypical imaging features
				- Requires
					- Repeat biopsy OR
					- Excision biopsy

# Gynaecomastia

- Definition
	- Benign proliferation of male breast glandular tissue
- Physiological causes of gynaecomastia

    - Infancy
        - Due to maternal/placental oestrogen stimulation
        - Resolves neonatally

    - Puberty
        - Usually after age 10
        - Resolves by age 17

    - Ageing
        - Increases with age

- Pathological causes of gynaecomastia

    - Drug-induced
        - Therapeutic drugs
            - Androgens
            - Anabolic steroids
            - Oestrogens
            - Cyproterone
            - Cimetidine
            - Digoxin
            - Spironolactone

        - Drugs of abuse
            - Alcohol
            - Amphetamines
            - Heroin
            - Marijuana

    - Liver cirrhosis
    - Malnutrition
    - Primary hypogonadism
    - Secondary hypogonadism
    - Testicular tumours
    - Hyperthyroidism
    - Renal disease
    - Idiopathic
        - No cause identified in 25%

- Presentation
	- Often asymptomatic
	- Possible symptoms
		- Breast pain
		- Nipple pain/tenderness
		- Breast enlargement
		- Breast lump

- Investigation

    - Main aim
        - Differentiate gynaecomastia from male breast cancer

    - Clinical features of gynaecomastia
        - Subareolar
        - Often bilateral
        - Rubbery texture

    - Features suggesting male breast cancer
        - Eccentric location
        - Unilateral
        - Firm/hard mass
        - Nipple retraction
        - Nipple discharge
        - Skin dimpling

    - if suspicious
	    - do biopsy

- Management
    - Asymptomatic patients
        - No treatment required
    - Symptomatic patients
        - Significant pain
        - Tenderness
        - Embarrassment
            - Treatment options
                - Stop causative drugs
                - Subcutaneous mastectomy
                - Medical therapy
                    - Tamoxifen
                    - Clomiphene citrate
