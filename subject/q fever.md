* Definition  
  * zoonotic infection  
* Etiology  
  * coxiella burnetii  
    * gram-negative bacterium  
* Type  
  * zoonotic infection  
* Transmission  
  * inhalation of aerosolised particles from  
    * animal birth products  
    * urine  
    * faeces  
  * possible  
    * ingestion of raw milk  
* Reservoirs  
  * cattle  
  * sheep  
  * goats  
  * dogs  
  * cats  
  * wildlife  
  * birds  
  * ticks  
* Epidemiology  
  * australia  
    * most common zoonotic disease  
    * highest rates  
      * queensland  
      * northern NSW  
  * demographics  
    * men  
    * age 40–59  
* High-risk occupations  
  * abattoir workers  
  * farmers  
  * veterinarians  
  * shearers  
  * livestock transporters  
  * wool classers  
* Pathophysiology  
  * coxiella burnetii forms  
    * large-cell variant  
      * metabolically active  
      * replicating form  
    * small-cell variant  
      * environmentally resistant  
      * survives desiccation  
  * case fatality rate  
    * \~2%  
  * untreated disease duration  
    * 2–6 weeks  
* Clinical features  
  * infection characteristics  
    * incubation period  
      * 4 days – 6 weeks  
    * 60% asymptomatic  
    * symptomatic illness  
      * often influenza-like  
  * common clinical syndromes  
    * febrile illness  
    * atypical pneumonia  
    * hepatitis  
  * typical presentation  
    * undifferentiated febrile illness  
  * common symptoms  
    * fever (may be absent)  
    * headache  
    * myalgia  
    * arthralgia  
    * fatigue  
    * rigors  
    * chills  
    * sweats  
    * weight loss  
    * diarrhoea  
  * other manifestations  
    * pneumonia  
    * hepatitis  
    * nausea  
  * rare  
    * rash  
    * jaundice  
    * meningism  
* Complications  
  * serious  
    * endocarditis  
    * myocarditis  
      * rare but fatal  
    * granulomatous hepatitis  
    * osteomyelitis (children)  
  * pregnancy complications  
    * miscarriage  
    * preterm birth  
    * neonatal death  
* Investigations  
  * typical results  
    * ↑ CRP  
    * lymphopenia  
    * thrombocytopenia  
    * ↑ AST / ALT  
  * usually normal  
    * bilirubin  
    * ALP  
    * GGT  
  * PCR  
    * best test in first week  
    * sample  
      * 5 mL EDTA blood  
    * key points  
      * positive → confirms Q fever  
      * negative → does not exclude  
  * Serology  
    * preferred method  
      * indirect immunofluorescence assay  
    * diagnostic pattern  
      * four-fold rise in titres  
    * protocol  
      * acute sample (\<7 days)  
      * convalescent sample (\>7 days later)  
      * tested in parallel  
  * antigen phases (AMC high-yield)  
    * phase 2 IgM / IgG ↑  
      * acute infection  
    * phase 1 IgG ↑  
      * chronic infection  
* Treatment  
  * acute Q fever (adults)  
    * if suspected and not pregnant  
      * doxycycline 100 mg BD for 14 days  
  * chronic Q fever  
    * long-term antibiotics  
      * up to 4 years  
    * infectious disease specialist involvement  
* Vaccination  
  * vaccine  
    * Q-Vax  
  * type  
    * whole-cell formalin-inactivated vaccine  
  * indication  
    * adults \>15 years with occupational risk  
  * mandatory pre-vaccination screening  
    * three steps  
      * clinical history  
      * skin test  
      * serology  
    * only vaccinate if all negative  
    * reason  
      * prior exposure → severe hypersensitivity reaction  
  * efficacy  
    * fully effective  
* Prevention  
  * preventive measures  
    * vaccination  
    * PPE  
    * N95 mask when handling animals  
    * hand hygiene  
    * avoid aerosolised dust  
    * wash contaminated work clothing separately

## Differential Diagnosis (AMC)

Important mimics:

| Disease | Key Differentiating Features (Exam Clues) |
| ----- | ----- |
| Leptospirosis | Animal urine exposure (flood water), conjunctival suffusion, jaundice \+ renal failure (Weil disease), severe myalgia (calf) |
| Brucellosis | Unpasteurized dairy, livestock exposure, undulating fever, hepatosplenomegaly, osteoarticular pain |
| Psittacosis | Bird exposure (parrots), atypical pneumonia \+ severe headache, relative bradycardia, dry cough |
| Legionella | Contaminated water (AC, hotels), pneumonia \+ diarrhea \+ confusion, hyponatremia, high fever |
| Dengue | Mosquito, severe myalgia (“breakbone”), retro-orbital pain, rash, thrombocytopenia, bleeding tendency |
| EBV | Infectious mononucleosis triad: fever \+ sore throat \+ lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, heterophile (+), rash with amoxicillin |
| CMV | Mono-like illness but heterophile negative, milder pharyngitis, important in immunocompromised, congenital infection |
| Influenza | Abrupt onset, high fever, myalgia, headache, dry cough, seasonal outbreaks |
| Mycoplasma pneumonia | Young adults, walking pneumonia, dry cough, bullous myringitis, cold agglutinins |
