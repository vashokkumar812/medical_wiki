- Definition
	- Underdeveloped femoral head dislocates:
		- Posteriorly
		- Superiorly
- Types / Spectrum
	- Transient Instability / Mild Subluxation
		- Hip unstable at birth
		- Stabilises spontaneously within few days
	- Frank Dislocation
		- Fully dislocated hip
- Risk Factors
	- Female sex
	- First-born child
	- Family history
	- Foot abnormalities
	- Breech presentation
	- Transverse lie
	- Oligohydramnios
	- Caesarean section

- Clinical Features
	- 40% asymmetrical; 30% bilateral
	- Tight adductors
	- Short leg
	- Limb length asymmetry
	- Skin crease asymmetry
	- Limited hip abduction
	- Limp or delayed walking in late cases

- Screening and Diagnosis
	- All Babies Should Be Examined
		- Day 1 of life
		- Before hospital discharge
		- At 6-week check
	- High-Risk Babies
		- Ultrasound screening recommended

- Examination Principles
	- Baby should be:
		- Relaxed
		- On firm surface
	- Look For
		- Limb shortening
		- Asymmetry
		- Skin crease asymmetry
- Ortolani Test
	- “IN” test
	- Detects reducible dislocated hip
	- Technique
		- Hip flexed to 90°
		- Abduct hip to 45°
		- Thumb placed on groin
		- Middle finger on greater trochanter
	- Positive Test
		- Palpable clunk
			- Femoral head reduces into acetabulum
- Barlow Test
	- “OUT” test
	- Detects unstable hip
	- Technique
		- Hip flexed to 90°
		- Hip abducted 10–20°
		- Apply backward pressure
			- Attempt dislocation
		- Apply forward pressure
			- Relocate hip
	- Positive Test
		- Clunk or jerk
			- Hip dislocates out of acetabulum
- Important Notes
	- Ortolani and Barlow tests usually become negative after 2 months
	- Ultrasound more sensitive than clinical examination
	- Ultrasound best investigation up to 6 months

- Investigations
	- Neonatal Period
		- Ultrasound
			- Best investigation
	- Less Than 3 Months
		- X-ray difficult to interpret
	- Around 3 Months
		- X-ray becomes more useful
	- After Walking Age
		- X-ray diagnostic

- Late Presentation
	- Femoral head remains dislocated
	- After 1 Year
		- Delayed walking
		- Limp
		- Limited abduction
	- Diagnosis
		- X-ray

- Treatment Guidelines
	- 0–6 Months
		- Pavlik harness
		- Abduction splint
	- 3–18 Months
		- Closed reduction or open reduction
		- Pelvic spica cast
	- More Than 18 Months
		- Open reduction
		- ± osteotomy
	- Slings not recommended
	- Early specialist referral essential

- Prognosis and Follow-Up
	- Early treatment may result in normal hip joint development

- Complications
	- Acetabular dysplasia
	- Premature osteoarthritis
	- Persistent limp
	- Avascular necrosis of femoral head

- Follow-Up
		- Pelvic X-ray during teenage years
