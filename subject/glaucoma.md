# Cataract (AMC MCQ Rapid Revision)

- **Definition**
  - Opacification (clouding) of the crystalline lens
  - Causes progressive, painless reduction in vision
  - Most common cause of reversible blindness worldwide
  - Usually age-related
  - Usually bilateral but often asymmetric

- **Risk Factors**
  - Age (most common)
  - Diabetes mellitus
  - Long-term corticosteroid use
    - Oral
    - Inhaled
    - Topical
  - Smoking
  - UV exposure
  - Trauma
  - Previous eye surgery
  - Radiation exposure
  - Congenital infections
    - Rubella
    - CMV
    - Toxoplasmosis
  - Family history

- **Pathophysiology**
  - Lens protein degeneration
    - Protein denaturation
    - Protein aggregation
  - Loss of lens transparency
  - Light scattering
  - Progressive visual impairment

- **Types**
  - Nuclear cataract
    - Most common age-related type
    - Yellow/brown lens
    - Progressive myopia
    - "Second sight" (temporary improvement in near vision)
  - Cortical cataract
    - Peripheral spoke-like opacities
    - Glare
    - Halos
    - Night driving difficulty
  - Posterior subcapsular cataract
    - Steroid use
    - Diabetes
    - Younger patients
    - Glare worse in bright light
    - Reading difficulty
  - Congenital cataract
    - Rubella
    - CMV
    - Toxoplasmosis
    - Metabolic disorders
    - Requires urgent treatment to prevent amblyopia

- **Symptoms**
  - Gradual onset
  - Progressive course
  - Painless
  - Blurred vision
  - Difficulty reading
  - Difficulty recognising faces
  - Difficulty driving
  - Poor night vision
  - Glare from headlights
  - Halos around lights
  - Colours appear faded
  - Frequent prescription changes
  - Monocular diplopia (occasionally)

- **Symptoms NOT Present**
  - Eye pain
  - Red eye
  - Sudden vision loss
  - Headache
  - Nausea
  - Vomiting

- **Examination**
  - Visual acuity
    - Decreased
  - Eye appearance
    - White eye
    - No conjunctival injection
  - Pupil
    - Normal
    - Reactive
  - Red reflex
    - Reduced
    - Absent in advanced disease
  - Slit lamp
    - Lens opacity
    - Nuclear sclerosis
    - Cortical spokes
    - Posterior subcapsular opacity
  - Intraocular pressure
    - Usually normal
  - Fundoscopy
    - Retina difficult to visualize
    - Lens opacity obstructs view

- **Diagnosis**
  - Clinical diagnosis
  - Visual acuity testing
  - Slit lamp examination (gold standard)

- **Investigations**
  - Visual acuity
  - Slit lamp examination
  - Fundoscopy
  - Ocular biometry before surgery
    - Calculates intraocular lens (IOL) power

- **Management**
  - Conservative
    - Update glasses
    - Better lighting
    - Anti-glare sunglasses
    - Regular review
  - Definitive
    - Cataract surgery
      - Phacoemulsification
      - Artificial intraocular lens implantation

- **Indications for Surgery**
  - Vision affects daily activities
  - Reading difficulty
  - Driving difficulty
  - Occupational needs
  - Reduced quality of life
  - Need retinal examination or treatment

- **Complications of Surgery**
  - Early
    - Endophthalmitis
    - Corneal oedema
    - Raised IOP
    - Wound leak
    - Inflammation
  - Late
    - Posterior capsule opacification (most common)
      - Gradual blurred vision after successful surgery
      - Treat with Nd:YAG laser posterior capsulotomy
    - Retinal detachment
    - Cystoid macular oedema
    - Lens dislocation

- **Prognosis**
  - Excellent
  - Significant improvement after surgery

# Cataract vs Open-Angle Glaucoma

- Cataract
  - Painless
  - Gradual blurred vision
  - Lens opacity
  - Reduced red reflex
  - Normal IOP
  - No optic disc cupping
  - White eye

- Open-angle glaucoma
  - Painless
  - Peripheral visual field loss
  - Optic disc cupping
  - Raised IOP (often)
  - Normal lens
  - White eye

# Cataract vs Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma

- Cataract
  - Gradual
  - Painless
  - White eye
  - Normal pupil
  - Normal IOP
  - Cloudy lens

- Acute angle-closure glaucoma
  - Sudden
  - Severe eye pain
  - Red eye
  - Mid-dilated fixed pupil
  - Cloudy cornea
  - Very high IOP
  - Headache
  - Nausea
  - Vomiting

# High-Yield AMC Clues

- Elderly patient
  - Gradual painless blurred vision
    - Cataract

- Difficulty driving at night
  - Glare from headlights
    - Cataract

- Reduced red reflex
  - Cataract

- Lens opacity on slit lamp
  - Cataract

- Colours look faded
  - Cataract

- Frequent changes in glasses prescription
  - Cataract

- Painless progressive visual loss
  - Cataract until proven otherwise

- Sudden painful red eye
  - NOT cataract
  - Think acute angle-closure glaucoma

- Peripheral vision loss
  - NOT cataract
  - Think glaucoma

# Absolute Memory Points

- Cloudy **lens** = Cataract
- Gradual + painless = Cataract
- Night glare = Cataract
- Reduced red reflex = Cataract
- Lens opacity = Cataract
- Definitive treatment = Phacoemulsification + IOL
- Most common late complication = Posterior capsule opacification
- Treatment = Nd:YAG laser capsulotomy
- Painful red eye ≠ Cataract
