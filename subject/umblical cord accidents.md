- defintiion
	- Cord presentation
		- cord below presenting part, membranes intact  
	- Cord prolapse
		- cord below presenting part, membranes ruptured  
- etiology
	- risk factors
		- Malpresentation
			- Breech
			- Transverse lie
			- Oblique lie
			- Unstable lie
		- High or mobile presenting part
		- Preterm prelabour rupture of membranes (PPROM)
		- Multiple gestation
		- Obstetric interventions causing upward displacement of presenting part
			- Vaginal examination
			- Artificial rupture of membranes (ARM)
			- Other manipulations



- pathogenesis
	- Occurs when presenting part not well applied to pelvis  
	- Cord compression → ↓ oxygenated blood flow  

- diagnosis
	- When to Suspect Cord Prolapse
		- After spontaneous rupture of membranes
		- At every vaginal examination if risk factors present
		- Abnormal fetal heart rate pattern
			- Especially soon after
				- Spontaneous ROM
				- Artificial ROM
		- Fetal bradycardia after membrane rupture
	- Cord visible at introitus  
	- Cord felt on vaginal examination  	
	- Speculum examination
	- Digital vaginal examination
	- Cord should be excluded
		- At vaginal examinations in labour
		- After ROM when risk factors present
	- Fetal heart rate
		- Auscultate after every vaginal examination
		- Auscultate after spontaneous membrane rupture
	- If normal CTG and no risk factors after ROM
		- Routine vaginal examination not indicated
- investigations
	- Antenatal Detection
	
		- Routine ultrasound
			- Not sufficiently sensitive
			- Not sufficiently specific
			- Not recommended for routine prediction
	
		- Selective ultrasound
			- Can be considered in term breech presentation
			- Especially if vaginal birth is planned
	- CTG:
		  - Deep variable decelerations  
		  - Bradycardia (if ongoing compression)  

## Management

- ![[Pasted image 20260531143357.png]]

	- Call for help immediately
	- Prepare for immediate birth
	- If not fully dilated
		- Proceed toward emergency delivery
	- Avoid manual cord replacement
		- Not recommended
	- Reduce pressure on cord:
		- All fours, head down  
		- Manual elevation of presenting part  
		- Fill bladder with 500 mL saline  
		- Minimal cord handling  
		- If outside introitus → replace into vagina (keep warm)  
### Delivery
- Emergency Caesarean section  
- Assisted vaginal delivery if fully dilated  

## complications
- if 10 min total compression
	- → cerebral damage  
- if 20 min
	- → death  

- Prognosis
	- poor if
		- Prematurity
		- Congenital malformations
		- Birth asphyxia



- Prevention
	- Transverse, oblique or unstable lie
		- Discuss elective admission after 37+0 weeks
		- Advise urgent presentation if
			- Labour starts
			- Membranes rupture
	- Non-cephalic presentation with PPROM
		- Recommend inpatient care
	- Artificial rupture of membranes
		- Avoid if presenting part high/mobile
		- If unavoidable
			- Perform where immediate caesarean section is available
	- Vaginal examinations/interventions
		- Minimise upward pressure on presenting part
	- If cord felt below presenting part
		- Do not rupture membranes
	- Cord presentation in established labour
		- Usually requires caesarean section
