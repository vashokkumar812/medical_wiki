- Urticaria (Paediatric)

	- Key Points

		- Most urticaria in children
			- Idiopathic
			- Viral

		- System involvement
			- Suspect anaphylaxis

				- Airway involvement

				- Respiratory involvement

				- Cardiovascular involvement

				- Gastrointestinal involvement

			- Give IM adrenaline urgently

		- Acute urticaria responds well to non-sedating antihistamines
- Urticaria (Hives)
	- definition
		- Recurrent transient pruritic wheals
	- etiology
		- Mast cell degranulation
		- Histamine release
		- Plasma leakage into superficial dermis

- Angioedema
	- definition
		- Deeper subcutaneous swelling
		- May occur with or without urticaria
	- Classification
		- Acute urticaria
			- <6 weeks
		- Chronic urticaria
			- >6 weeks
			- Occurs on most days
	- Causes
		- Common Causes
			- Idiopathic
				- Most common
			- Viral infection
				- Common in children
			- May occur
				- During illness
				- After illness
		- Allergy
			- Consider if
				- Rare episodic attacks
				- Trigger-specific
				- Occurs within 1–2 hours of exposure
			- Associated symptoms
				- Vomiting
				- Abdominal pain
				- Breathlessness
				- Dizziness
			- Urticaria lasting days
				- Almost never allergy
			- Antibiotic-associated urticaria
				- Often due to infection
				- Not drug allergy
		- Other Causes
			- Contact / Irritant
				- Plants
				- Animals
				- Latex
			- Physical causes
				- Pressure
				- Heat
				- Cold
				- Exercise
			- Hormonal
				- Autoimmune progesterone dermatitis
			- Systemic disease
				- Autoimmune disease
				- Connective tissue disease
				- Lymphoproliferative disease
			- Stress
				- Rare cause
				- May worsen symptoms
	- Chronic Urticaria
		- Usually
			- Idiopathic
			- Inducible
				- Physical trigger
			- Autoimmune
	- Angioedema Causes
		- Idiopathic
		- Viral infection
		- Medications
			- NSAIDs
			- ACE inhibitors
			- Antibiotics
		- Foods
		- Insect stings
		- Recurrent angioedema without wheals
			- Consider C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency
	- Assessment
		- History
			- Preceding events
				- Hours to days before onset
			- Lesion distribution

			- Lesion duration

			- Recurrence pattern

			- Pruritus

			- Triggers

			- Systemic symptoms

		- Examination

			- Urticaria Features

				- Raised wheals with central pallor

				- Erythematous border

				- Highly pruritic

				- Migratory

				- Resolve within <24 hours

				- Polymorphic

				- Transient

				- May leave temporary purple discoloration

			- Angioedema Features

				- Face and lips common

				- May involve
					- Hands
					- Feet
					- Trunk
					- Genitalia

				- Larger lesions

				- Painful more than itchy

				- May take up to 72 hours to resolve

	- Always Exclude Anaphylaxis

		- Rapid ABC assessment required

	- Differential Diagnosis

		- Urticaria

			- Itch
				- Present

			- Migration
				- Migratory

			- Lesion
				- Wheal
				- Central pallor

			- Central change
				- None

			- Duration
				- <24 hours

			- Distribution
				- Anywhere

		- Erythema Multiforme

			- Itch
				- Usually absent

			- Migration
				- Fixed lesions

			- Lesion
				- Target lesion

			- Central change
				- Purpura
				- Blister

			- Duration
				- Days

			- Distribution
				- Palms
				- Soles
				- Mucosa

	- Important Differentials

		- Urticarial vasculitis
			- >48 hours
			- Painful

		- HSP

		- Serum sickness

		- Mastocytosis

		- Juvenile rheumatoid arthritis

		- Pityriasis rosea

	- Investigations

		- Acute urticaria

			- Almost never required

			- Routine allergy testing
				- Not needed

		- Chronic urticaria

			- Consider investigations only if features suggest underlying disease

	- Management

		- Acute Urticaria

			- Most resolve spontaneously

			- General Measures

				- Remove trigger

				- Avoid heat

				- Avoid spicy food

				- Avoid NSAIDs

				- Cool compress

			- Antihistamines
				- First line treatment

				- Non-sedating preferred

			- Cetirizine dosing

			- Severe Acute Urticaria

				- If poor response
					- Prednisolone single dose

				- Steroid creams
					- Ineffective

		- Chronic Urticaria Management

			- Same initial treatment

			- Increase cetirizine up to 4× standard dose

				- ≥6 years
					- Maximum 40 mg/day

			- Add sedating antihistamine if needed

			- Specialist referral
				- Immunomodulator therapy

	- When to Consult Paediatrics

		- Fever

		- Bruising

		- Joint pain

		- Suspected vasculitis

		- Suspected systemic disease

		- Anaphylaxis

		- Angioedema

		- Age <6 months

		- Chronic urticaria
			- >6 weeks
