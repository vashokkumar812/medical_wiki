- **Ankle Injuries**
	- **Severe Ligament Sprain/Rupture**
		- Usually high-velocity injury
			- Landing on another player's foot
			- Running injuries
		- Simple rolling ankle while walking
		- less likely to cause rupture or fracture
	- **Syndesmosis Injury (High Ankle Sprain)**
		- Mechanism
			- Dorsiflexion + eversion
		- Recovery longer than lateral ligament sprain
		- Unstable injury requires urgent orthopaedic referral

- **Post-Injury Features**
	- **Ligament Rupture or Fracture**
		- Often unable to continue sport
		- May be unable to weight bear
	- **Rapid Swelling (Minutes to Hours)**
		- Suggests bleeding
		- Ligament rupture
		- Fracture/dislocation
	- **Delayed Swelling**
		- Suggests synovitis
	- **Crack/Pop Sensation**
		- Cannot differentiate fracture from ligament injury

- **Important History**
	- Previous ankle injuries
	- Previous investigations or treatment
	- Previous rehabilitation
		- Range of motion exercises
		- Proprioception training
		- Strength training
		- Sport-specific exercises
		- Usually ≥6 weeks
	- Previous brace or taping use
	- Chronic ankle instability may prolong recovery

- **Examination**
	- **Weight Bearing**
		- Inability to take 4 steps unaided → ankle X-ray required (Ottawa rules)
	- **Inspection**
		- Haematoma
		- Significant swelling
		- Suggests rupture or fracture
	- **Palpation**
		- Tender posterior edge of medial malleolus → fracture possible
		- Tender posterior edge of lateral malleolus → fracture possible
		- Tender base of 5th metatarsal → fracture possible
		- Tender proximal fibula → fracture possible
		- No ATFL tenderness effectively excludes ATFL rupture
	- **Range of Motion**
		- Limited value acutely due to pain and swelling

- **Lateral Ankle Ligaments**
	- Anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL)
		- Most commonly injured
	- Calcaneofibular ligament (CFL)
	- Posterior talofibular ligament (PTFL)

- **Special Tests**
	- Repeat after 3–5 days if swelling and pain limit examination

- **Anterior Drawer Test**
	- Knee flexed 90°
	- Increased laxity compared with opposite ankle
		- Suggests ATFL injury
		- May indicate CFL injury
	- Combination of:
		- Haematoma
		- ATFL tenderness
		- Positive anterior drawer
		- Sensitivity 98%
		- Specificity 84%
		- Indicates acute lateral ligament rupture

- **Lateral Talar Tilt Test**
	- Inversion stress applied
	- Talar tilt >15°
		- Suggests rupture of ATFL and CFL

- **Syndesmosis Injury Tests**
	- External rotation stress test
		- Dorsiflex ankle and externally rotate
		- Pain at syndesmosis = positive
	- Squeeze test
		- Compress tibia and fibula at mid-calf
		- Distal syndesmosis pain = positive
	- Interosseous membrane tenderness
		- Palpate between tibia and fibula proximally
		- Measure tenderness length

- **Ottawa Ankle Rules**
	- X-ray required if malleolar pain plus:
		- Posterior edge/tip tenderness of medial malleolus
		- Posterior edge/tip tenderness of lateral malleolus
		- Unable to bear weight immediately and in clinic
	- Foot X-ray required if midfoot pain plus:
		- Base of 5th metatarsal tenderness
		- Navicular tenderness
		- Unable to bear weight

- **Investigations**
	- **Plain X-ray**
		- Excludes most fractures
		- Guided by Ottawa ankle rules
	- **Ultrasound**
		- No evidence-based role acutely
	- **Stress Radiographs**
		- Not recommended acutely
	- **Bone Scan**
		- Sensitive for occult fractures
	- **CT**
		- Defines bony injury
		- Assists surgical planning
	- **MRI**
		- Second-line investigation
		- Ligament rupture assessment
		- Synovitis
		- Osteochondral lesions

- **Differential Diagnoses When Recovery Delayed**
	- Occult fracture
	- Osteochondral lesion
	- Chronic instability
	- Syndesmosis injury

- **Classification and Management**
	- **Mild-Moderate Lateral Ligament Sprain**
		- Early mobilisation
		- Rehabilitation program
	- **Severe Lateral Ligament Sprain/Rupture**
		- Immobilisation initially
			- Backslab
			- CAM walker boot
		- Then rehabilitation
	- **Fracture/Dislocation**
		- Orthopaedic referral
	- **Unstable Syndesmosis Injury**
		- Orthopaedic referral

- **Acute Management (First 1–5 Days)**
	- Exclude fracture/dislocation first
	- Ice
	- Compression
	- Elevation
	- Paracetamol
	- Avoid early NSAIDs
		- Increased bleeding risk
		- May impair tissue healing

- **Severe Ankle Sprain**
	- Defined as inability to weight bear for ≥3 days without fracture
	- Evidence supports:
		- Short period (~10 days) immobilisation
		- Better outcomes than immediate mobilisation alone

- **Rehabilitation**
	- Goal
		- Restore function
		- Improve proprioception
		- Prevent recurrent injury
	- Components
		- Range of motion
		- Strengthening
		- Balance training
		- Proprioception exercises
		- Sport-specific exercises
	- Often supervised by physiotherapist
	- Proprioception training
		- Can reduce recurrence risk by up to 80%
