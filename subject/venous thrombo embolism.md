# Pulmonary embolism

- Types
	- Massive PE
		- Obstructive shock
		- BP <90 mmHg

	- Submassive PE
		- Acute PE without hypotension
		- Right ventricular dysfunction present

	- Non-massive PE
		- Low-risk PE



- Clinical features
	- Chest pain
		- Retrosternal
		- Pleuritic
		- Acute onset
		- May or may not be present

	- dyspnea

	- syncope

	- Less common symptoms
		- Cough
		- Haemoptysis
		- Symptoms of concurrent deep vein thrombosis

	- Signs
		- Tachypnoea
		- tachycardia
		- Hypoxia

- Investigations
	- Definitive imaging
		- CT pulmonary angiogram (CTPA)
		- Ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) scan
			- Pregnancy
				- Use catheter to remove isotope quickly

	- Nonpregnant patients
		- Prefer CTPA

	- ECG
		- Look for T-wave inversion in V1–V4
		- ![[Pasted image 20260609232312.png]]
		- 

	- [[d-dimer]]
		- Used to exclude PE when clinical probability low/unlikely

	- Imaging urgency
		- Immediate imaging if
			- Cardiac signs present
			- Respiratory signs present

		- If imaging unavailable
			- Example
				- Remote setting
				- Weekend access issue
			- Give LMWH
			- Arrange imaging next day

- Severe/massive pulmonary embolism
	- Bedside echocardiography findings
		- Right ventricular dilatation
		- Right ventricular hypokinesis
		- Elevated right atrial pressures

## differential diagnosis
- pleuritic infarction
	- less dramatic
	- pleuritic [[subject/chest pain]] and haemoptysis

## treatment
- if massive 
	- thrombolysis
		- indications
			- severely compromised + SBP is <90 mmHg
			- HR < 40
			- hemodynamic instability, worsening respiratory insufficiency, severe right ventricular dysfunction, or major myocardial necrosis and low risk of bleeding complications’
		- drug to use
			-  heparin infusion and alteplase
- else
	- anticoagulant
		- dalteparin  **or** enoxaparin 1.5 mg/kg daily or 1 mg/kg twice daily
			- if the risk of bleeding or thrombus extension is high (eg older age, obesity, malignancy)
			- two dose
	- warfarin
		- target PTINR
			- 2 - 3
	- rivaroxaban
		- alternative to warfarin, LMWH
		- 5 mg twice daily for three weeks, then 20 mg daily
			- if cancer, recurrent unprovoked pulmonary embolism
				- 3 months
			- else
				- 6 months

# deep vein thrombosis
- DVT
	- Thrombus within deep venous system
- drugs causing/increasing risk of DVT  
	- oral contraceptive pill  
	- hormone replacement therapy  
	- tamoxifen  
	- raloxifene  
	- thalidomide  
	- lenalidomide  
	- erythropoietin  
	- corticosteroids  
	- antipsychotics  
	- chemotherapy agents
- course
	- Usually begins as distal calf DVT
- clinical features
	- Proximal DVT
		- More symptomatic
		- if untreated proximal DVT for 3 months
			- develop PE
		- more risk of PE
	- Post-thrombotic syndrome
		-  Chronic leg pain
		- Painful swelling
		- Fibrosis
		- Severe cases
			- Leg ulceration
	- Clinical diagnosis unreliable
		- Requires objective testing
- types of DVT
	- Distal DVT
		- Below popliteal trifurcation
		- Often asymptomatic
		- May spontaneously resolve

	- Proximal DVT
		- Popliteal vein
		- Femoral vein
		- Iliac vein
		- Higher PE risk



- score
	- ![[Pasted image 20260525093511.png]]
- differential diagnosis
	- Baker cyst rupture
		- bruising/ecchymosis around ankle or calf ("crescent sign")
	- Haematoma
		- history of trauma or anticoagulant use
	- Venous insufficiency
		- chronic varicose veins with skin hyperpigmentation

- investigations
	- D-dimer
		- Interpretation
			- Negative D-dimer
				- Rules out DVT
			- Positive D-dimer
				- Nonspecific
		- Less useful in
			- Inpatients
			- Pregnancy
		- False-positive causes
			- Surgery
			- Trauma
			- Haemorrhage/bruising
			- Ischaemic heart disease
			- Stroke
			- Infection
			- Malignancy
			- PAD/aneurysm
			- Pregnancy
			- Advanced age
			- Extensive burns
		- False-negative causes
			- Small thrombus
				- Isolated distal DVT
			- Delayed presentation
			- Concurrent anticoagulation
				- Heparin
				- Warfarin
	- Imaging
		- First-line
			- Compression ultrasonography (CUS)
				- Test of choice	
		- Entire leg swelling + negative CUS
			- Consider pelvic vein thrombosis
	- Additional investigations
		- Routine thrombophilia testing
			- not indicated
			- Usually does not change management
		- Consider APS testing if
			- Apparently idiopathic VTE
			- Connective tissue disease
			- Previous arterial thrombosis
				- Stroke
				- MI
			- Premature arterial thrombosis
			- Recurrent pregnancy loss
			- Fetal death
			- APS tests
				- Lupus anticoagulant
				- Anticardiolipin antibody
				- Anti-β2 glycoprotein-I antibody
- Management
	- ![[Pasted image 20260519144803.png]]
	- Delayed confirmatory testing + high suspicion
		- Start empirical anticoagulation if no contraindication
			- Example
				- LMWH
		- If no contraindication

	- Isolated distal DVT
		- Anticoagulation
			- 6 weeks–3 months usually adequate

| Indication                            | Medication                | Duration                                |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| First VTE (reversible risk)           | LMWH / Fondaparinux → OAC | 3–6 months                              |
| First idiopathic VTE                  | LMWH / Fondaparinux → OAC | ≥6 months                               |
| Cancer-associated VTE                 | LMWH (→ OAC later)        | Until cancer resolves                   |
| Antiphospholipid syndrome             | LMWH / Fondaparinux → OAC | ≥12 months                              |
| Recurrent VTE (reversible cause)      | LMWH / Fondaparinux → OAC | 6 months                                |
| Recurrent idiopathic VTE              | LMWH / Fondaparinux → OAC | ≥12 months                              |
| Pregnancy-associated VTE              | LMWH                      | ≥6 weeks postpartum AND ≥6 months total |
| Provoked distal DVT (first episode)   | LMWH → OAC                | 6 weeks                                 |
| Unprovoked distal DVT (first episode) | LMWH → OAC                | 3 months                                |
- anticoagulants and prophylaxis
	- renal impairment
		- especially elderly
		- adjust LMWH and renally cleared antithrombotics

	- prophylaxis doses
		- UFH
			- 5000 IU BD/TDS
		- LMWH
			- enoxaparin 40 mg daily
			- dalteparin 5000 IU daily
		- fondaparinux
			- 2.5 mg daily
	
	- treatment doses
		- LMWH
			- dalteparin 100 IU/kg BD
			- enoxaparin 1 mg/kg BD
		- fondaparinux
			- 7.5 mg daily
		- warfarin
			- target INR 2.5 ± 0.5
	
	- if anticoagulation contraindicated
		- mechanical prophylaxis
			- GCS
			- IPC
	
	- GCS
		- recommended in all cases
			- up to 2 years
	
	- VTE risk factors
		- ischaemic stroke
		- previous VTE
		- active cancer
		- decompensated heart failure
		- acute/chronic lung disease
		- inflammatory disease
		- age >60 years
	
	- travel thromboprophylaxis
		- very high-risk patients
			- cancer
			- previous VTE
		- consider
			- GCS during flight
			- single LMWH dose before departure
	
	- abbreviations
		- UFH = unfractionated heparin
		- LMWH = low molecular weight heparin
		- GCS = graduated compression stockings
		- IPC = intermittent pneumatic compression
		- OAC = oral anticoagulant

- Prophylaxis

	- At-risk medical patients
		- Chronic lung disease
		- Cardiac failure
		- HIH/early discharge patients

	- Drugs of choice
		- LMWH
		- Fondaparinux

	- High bleeding risk
		- Mechanical prophylaxis
			- IPC stockings
			- Graduated compression stockings (GCS)
- prevention

| Indication                                     | Medication                                         | Duration                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Orthopaedic surgery (THR / TKR / hip fracture) | LMWH / Fondaparinux \+ GCS / IPC                   | 35 days (≥10 days)                           |
| Major surgery (\>45 min, age \>40)             | LMWH / UFH / Fondaparinux \+ GCS / IPC             | Until discharge (up to 28 days if high risk) |
| Medical illness \+ risk factors                | LMWH / UFH / Fondaparinux                          | Until resolution                             |
| Pregnancy \+ postpartum \+ risk factors        | LMWH                                               | 4–6 weeks postpartum                         |
| Long travel (\>5000 km)                        | Hydration, exercise, avoid alcohol, loose clothing | Duration of flight                           |

- Prognosis
	- Worse prognosis with
		- Large DVT
		- Malignancy


## **warfarin dose**

## **💊 Warfarin – Dosing & Management (Table)**

| Clinical Situation                             | Recommendation                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Initiation dose – majority                     | 5–10 mg for 1–2 days → adjust based on INR                    |
| Initiation dose – elderly / high bleeding risk | ≤5 mg for 1–2 days → adjust based on INR                      |
| INR monitoring – initial                       | Check at 1–2 days after starting → continue until stable      |
| INR monitoring – stable                        | Weekly → fortnightly → then ≥ every 4 weeks                   |
| INR \>3 but \<5 (no bleeding)                  | Lower/omit dose → frequent monitoring → restart at lower dose |
| INR ≥5 (± bleeding)                            | Stop warfarin → refer → consider vitamin K                    |

---

### **🔧 Perioperative Management (Bridging)**

| Situation                   | Recommendation                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low VTE risk                | Stop warfarin 4–7 days pre-op                                                                                                                            |
| Intermediate VTE risk       | Stop warfarin 4–7 days pre-op → low-dose UFH/LMWH 2 days pre-op → restart LMWH \+ warfarin post-op                                                       |
| High VTE risk               | Stop warfarin 4–7 days pre-op → full-dose UFH/LMWH 2 days pre-op → stop UFH (5 hrs) / LMWH (12–24 hrs) before surgery → restart LMWH \+ warfarin post-op |
| Low bleeding risk procedure | Continue warfarin at lower dose (INR 1.3–1.5) → restart LMWH \+ warfarin post-op                                                                         |

---

## **🧠 Note**

* Low VTE risk \= no recent (\>3 months) VTE


not valid if
- symptoms have been present for >30 days
- upper limb [[deep vein thrombosis]] is suspected as a source of [[venous thrombo embolism]]
- the patient has been on anticoagulants for >72 hours
- the patient has been asymptomatic for 72 hours prior to presentation
- the patient is pregnant

## Wells Score for [[deep vein thrombosis]]

### Criteria

| Score | Clinical Feature                                                                               |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| +1    | Active cancer (treatment ongoing, within 6 months, or palliative)                              |
| +1    | Paralysis, paresis, or recent plaster immobilisation of lower extremity                        |
| +1    | Recently bedridden >3 days OR major surgery within 12 weeks under general/regional anaesthesia |
| +1    | Localised tenderness along distribution of deep venous system                                  |
| +1    | Entire leg swollen                                                                             |
| +1    | Calf swelling >3 cm compared with asymptomatic leg (10 cm below tibial tuberosity)             |
| +1    | Pitting oedema confined to symptomatic leg                                                     |
| +1    | Collateral superficial (non-varicose) veins                                                    |
| +1    | Previous documented [[deep vein thrombosis]]                                                   |
| −2    | Alternative diagnosis at least as likely as [[deep vein thrombosis]]                           |
# Scoring

| Total Score | Interpretation                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- |
| ≥ 2         | [[deep vein thrombosis]] likely   |
| < 2         | [[deep vein thrombosis]] unlikely |


## Wells Score for PE
### Criteria

| Score | Clinical Feature                                          |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| +3    | Clinical signs/symptoms of [[deep vein thrombosis]]       |
| +3    | Alternative diagnosis less likely than PE                 |
| +1.5  | Heart rate >100 bpm                                       |
| +1.5  | Immobilisation ≥3 days or surgery in last 4 weeks         |
| +1.5  | Previous [[deep vein thrombosis]]/PE                      |
| +1    | Haemoptysis                                               |
| +1    | Malignancy (on treatment, within 6 months, or palliative) |

### Scoring

| Total Score | Probability          |
| ----------- | -------------------- |
| > 6         | High probability     |
| 2 – 6       | Moderate probability |
| < 2         | Low probability      |
