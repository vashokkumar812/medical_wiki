# trichomoniasis

- Cause
	- Protozoan: Trichomonas vaginalis

- Epidemiology
	- Older people
	- Regional/remote populations
	- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people
	- Street-based sex workers

- prognosis
	- Long natural history (may persist for years)
	- Clears faster in penis than vagina if untreated

- Clinical features
	- Often asymptomatic
		- Up to 50% of vaginal infections
		- Most urethral infections

	- Vaginal infection
		- Malodorous vaginal discharge
			- Profuse
			- Frothy
		- Vulval itch
		- Vulval soreness
		- Cervicitis

	- Urethral infection
		- Urethritis
		- Urethral discharge (uncommon)
		- Dysuria (uncommon)

- Complications
	- Prostatitis
	- Premature rupture of membranes (PROM)
	- Preterm delivery
	- Low birth weight
	- Postpartum sepsis
	- Increased HIV transmission risk

- Diagnosis
	- Initial investigation
		- NAAT

	- Most appropriate investigation
		- High vaginal swab NAAT
			- Preferred in symptomatic patients with vagina
			- Ideally clinician-collected
			- Self-collected acceptable if examination declined

	- Alternative investigation
		- First-pass urine (FPU) NAAT

	- Screening
		- Not routinely recommended
		- Consider only in high-prevalence populations

	- Urethritis
		- Routine testing not recommended

- Management
	- Most appropriate treatment
		- Metronidazole 400 mg PO BD with food for 7 days

	- Alternative treatment
		- Metronidazole 2 g PO stat with food

	- Persistent/recurrent infection
		- Exclude reinfection
		- Exclude poor adherence
		- Seek specialist advice if resistance suspected

- Immediate advice
	- Avoid alcohol during metronidazole treatment
	- Avoid alcohol for 24 hours after completion
	- No sexual contact
		- For 7 days after starting treatment
		- Or until treatment completed and symptoms resolved

- Partner management
	- Treat current sexual partner(s)
	- Presumptive treatment of contacts recommended
	- Contact tracing
		- Focus on current and recent partners
		- Consider presumptive treatment if sexual contact within previous 2 weeks
	- Male partners may test negative due to spontaneous resolution

- Special situations
	- Pregnancy
		- Metronidazole: Category B2

	- Breastfeeding
		- May alter taste of breast milk
		- Avoid high-dose regimens

	- Metronidazole allergy
		- No effective alternative to 5-nitroimidazoles
		- Consider metronidazole desensitisation

	- HIV
		- 7-day metronidazole regimen preferred
		- Single-dose therapy less effective

- Follow-up
	- Review at 1 week
		- Assess symptom resolution
		- Confirm adherence
		- Confirm partner treatment/contact tracing
		- STI prevention counselling

	- Test of cure
		- Not routinely recommended
		- Only if symptoms persist

	- Retesting
		- After 4 weeks if
			- Persistent symptoms
			- Partner treatment uncertain

# Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (VVC) / Genital Candidiasis




- Cause  

	- Most common organism  
		- Candida albicans  

	- Less common species  
		- Candida glabrata  

- Clinical presentation  

	- Penile candidiasis / balanitis  

		- Erythematous genital rash  
			- Especially beneath foreskin  

		- -/+ Pruritus  

		- Foreskin swelling  
			- Severe disease  

		- Fissures  

		- Superficial erosions  

	- Vulvovaginal candidiasis  

		- Thick white clumpy vaginal discharge  
			- “Cottage cheese” appearance  

		- Discharge may be minimal or absent  

		- Vulvar/genital itch  

		- Burning sensation  

		- Soreness  

		- Superficial dyspareunia  

		- External dysuria  

		- Vulval findings  
			- Excoriation  
			- Erythema  
			- Fissures  
			- Swelling  

- Recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis (RVVC)  

	- Definition  
		- ≥4 episodes within 12 months  

	- Occurrence  
		- Seen in ~10% of women  

	- Diagnostic requirement  
		- Confirm by culture at least once  

- Differential diagnoses to exclude  

	- Dermatitis
		- Intense pruritus
	
	- Lichen sclerosus
		- White parchment-like plaques
	
	- Herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection
		- Painful vesicles/ulcers
	
	- Bacterial vaginosis
		- Fishy odour
	
	- Trichomoniasis
		- Strawberry cervix

- Diagnostic approach  

	- Usually clinical diagnosis  
		- Based on symptoms and examination findings  

	- Microscopy and culture (MCS)  

		- Vaginal swab  
			- Clinician-collected or self-collected  

		- Penile swab  

	- Benefits of culture  
		- Species identification  
		- Antifungal sensitivities  
		- Distinguishes from BV/trichomoniasis  

- Investigations  

	- Recurrent candidiasis  
		- Vaginal swab for microscopy + culture + speciation  

	- Suspect non-albicans species  
		- In recurrent or treatment-resistant disease  

	- HSV PCR  
		- Only if suggestive lesions/symptoms present  

	- Consider screening for underlying conditions  
		- Diabetes mellitus  
		- HIV infection  
		- Immunosuppression  

- Management principles  

	- Oral and intravaginal azoles have similar efficacy  

	- Topical therapy  
		- Faster symptom relief  

	- Important principle  
		- Vulvar treatment alone inadequate  
		- Vaginal reservoir also requires treatment  

	- Treatment — uncomplicated vulvovaginal candidiasis  
	
		- Vaginal azole therapy  
	
			- Clotrimazole 10% vaginal cream  
	
			- Clotrimazole vaginal pessary  
	
	
		- Oral alternative  
			- Fluconazole 150 mg PO stat  
	
	- Treatment — candidal balanitis  
	
		- Clotrimazole 1% cream  
			- Continue additional 1 week after symptoms settle  
	
	- Treatment — recurrent Candida albicans infection  
	
		- Acute episode treatment  
			- Longer azole course preferred rather than stat therapy  
	
		- Fluconazole induction regimen  
			- 150 mg PO  
			- 3 doses  
			- Each dose 3 days apart  
	
		- Maintenance regimen  
			- Fluconazole 150 mg PO weekly for 6 months  
	
		- Alternative suppressive regimen  
			- Clotrimazole 10% vaginal cream nightly for 10–14 days  
			- Then 500 mg vaginal pessary weekly  
	
	- Treatment — Candida glabrata  
	
		- Boric acid vaginal pessaries  
			- Usually compounded preparation  
	
		- Important warning  
			- Boric acid potentially fatal if ingested  
			- Contraindicated in pregnancy  
	
		- Alternative therapy  
			- Nystatin 100,000 IU vaginally for 14 days  

- Adjunctive treatment  

	- Hydrocortisone 1% cream  
		- May reduce vulvar irritation and itch  

- Treatment advice / counselling  

	- Reconsider diagnosis if no response to therapy  

	- No evidence supporting  
		- Specific diets  
		- Probiotics for recurrence prevention  

	- Fluconazole monitoring  
		- No hepatic monitoring required at standard doses  

	- Oral azoles contraindicated in pregnancy  

- Other management advice  

	- Avoid local irritants  
		- Soap  
		- Bubble bath  
		- Body wash  
		- Bath oils  
		- Spermicides  
		- Vaginal lubricants  
		- Vaginal hygiene products  

	- Vaginal antifungal creams  
		- May damage latex condoms/barrier contraception  

	- Post-coital penile hypersensitivity  
		- Can occur due to vaginal Candida colonisation  
		- May improve with partner treatment  

	- Partners  
		- Usually do not require treatment  

- Special treatment situations  

	- Severe disease  
		- May require prolonged oral or topical therapy  

	- Pregnancy  

		- Minimum 7-day topical azole regimen preferred  

		- Contraindicated  
			- Fluconazole  
			- Boric acid  

	- Allergy/intolerance to first-line treatment  
		- Use alternative antifungal regimen  

	- Refractory/recurrent disease  
		- Consider specialist referral  

- Contact tracing  

	- Not required  

- Follow-up  

	- Uncomplicated infection  
		- Routine follow-up not indicated

		- Neonatal withdrawal bleed
			- No treatment required

	- Abnormal Causes

		- Vaginal foreign body
			- Toilet paper
			- Toy

		- Severe vulvovaginitis

		- Lichen sclerosus

		- Pinworms

		- Trauma

		- Sexual abuse

		- Precocious puberty
			- <8 years

		- Urethral prolapse
			- “Doughnut” lesion

		- Tumour
			- Rare

		- Non-vaginal sources
			- Haematuria

- Examination

	- Examine non-genital areas first
		- Look for systemic signs

	- Perform perineal examination only if necessary

	- Requirements during examination
		- Senior supervision
		- Chaperone
		- Consent
		- Privacy
		- Explain process to child and parent

	- Examination positions
		- Frog-leg position
			- Supine
			- Knees flexed
			- Heels together

		- Side-lying position

	- Child may separate labia themselves if preferred

	- Do not perform
		- Internal examinations
		- Internal swabs
			- In prepubertal children

	- Describe findings
		- Use clock-face diagram

- Investigations

	- Directed towards underlying cause



	- Introital swab
		- Not internal

		- Only if
			- Profuse discharge
			- Offensive discharge

	- X-ray
		- Detect radiopaque foreign body
			- Glass
			- Metal

	- Ultrasound
		- Detect radiolucent foreign body
			- Thorn
			- Wood

	- Discuss unclear cases with gynaecology

Look for:
- Anatomy: labial adhesion, urethral prolapse, hymenal integrity  
- Colour: erythema, pale patches, bruising  
- Swelling, dryness, excoriation, discharge, bleeding  
- Cotton bud test: lateral → peri-hymenal tenderness = possible vulvodynia  

Offensive discharge/bleeding in premenstrual child → consider vaginal foreign body
