- Illicit drug overdose
	- Epidemiology
		- ~40% Australians tried illicit drugs lifetime
		- ~15% used within previous 12 months
		- Most common
			- cannabis
		- Highest mortality
			- heroin/opiates
				- >90% illicit drug deaths
		- Common age group
			- 20–29 years
		- Males
			- higher use
			- higher hospitalisation
		- Cannabis
			- highest prevalence
			- no direct overdose deaths reported
		- Amphetamines
			- second highest mortality
		- MDMA
			- associated with hyperthermia + hyponatraemia

- Opioid overdose
	- Common drugs
		- heroin
		- oxycodone
		- codeine
		- morphine
		- tramadol
		- methadone
	- Classic triad
		- CNS depression
		- respiratory depression
		- miosis
	- Key sign
		- RR <10/min strongly suggests opioid toxicity
	- Complications
		- aspiration pneumonitis
		- non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema
	- Investigations
		- end-tidal CO2
		- blood gas
			- pCO2 best measure of hypoventilation
		- chest X-ray
	- Management
		- airway + ventilation priority
		- naloxone
			- titrate to respiratory effort
			- not consciousness
		- Naloxone doses
			- 100–400 mcg IV
			- 2 mg IM/IN
		- Intranasal naloxone
			- reduced needlestick risk
		- Risks of naloxone
			- acute withdrawal
				- agitation
				- diaphoresis
				- tachycardia
			- absconding/recurrent sedation
		- All suspected heroin overdose
			- ED referral/admission

- Amphetamine/cocaine overdose
	- Toxidrome
		- sympathomimetic excess
	- Features
		- tachycardia
		- hypertension
		- agitation
		- delirium
		- hallucinations
		- psychosis
	- Major complications
		- myocardial infarction
		- coronary vasospasm
		- arrhythmias
		- seizures
	- Important investigation
		- ECG
	- Acute coronary syndrome management
		- opiates
		- benzodiazepines
		- nitrates
		- aspirin
	- Benzodiazepines useful for
		- agitation
		- hypertension
		- seizures
		- delirium
	- Cocaine-induced VT
		- due to sodium channel blockade
		- treat with sodium bicarbonate
			- 1–2 mmol/kg IV
	- Symptomatic patients
		- hospital admission
		- delirium may persist 48 hours

- MDMA/ecstasy overdose
	- Amphetamine derivative with serotonergic activity
	- Features
		- empathy/euphoria
		- bruxism
		- palpitations
		- hypertension
		- diaphoresis
		- sympathomimetic syndrome
	- Serious complications
		- serotonin syndrome
		- hyperthermia
		- dehydration
		- hyponatraemia
		- seizures
	- Management
		- similar to amphetamine toxicity
		- aggressive cooling
		- hypertonic saline if severe hyponatraemia

- Cannabis intoxication
	- Usually mild/self-limiting
	- Features
		- anxiety
		- panic
		- depersonalisation
		- disorientation
		- memory impairment
		- poor attention
		- tachycardia
		- palpitations
		- AF
		- orthostatic hypotension
	- Duration
		- inhaled
			- 2–3 hours
		- oral
			- 4–12 hours
	- Management
		- reassurance
		- benzodiazepines if agitation

- GHB overdose
	- Clear tasteless liquid
	- Rapid CNS depression
	- Short duration + rapid offset
	- Similar to benzodiazepine toxicity
	- Management
		- supportive care

- Designer drugs

- Opioid prescribing principles
	- Use biopsychosocial assessment
	- Shared decision-making essential
	- Lowest effective dose
	- Shortest duration possible
	- Discuss risks
			- dependence
			- withdrawal
			- misuse/diversion
			- falls
			- cognitive impairment
			- MVAs
	- Avoid opioids in
		- alcohol/substance use disorder
		- polydrug use
	- Long-term opioids
		- uncommon
		- use cautiously
		- regular dose reduction attempts
		- continued monitoring required
	- Prescribe from
		- one GP/practice
		- one pharmacy
	- Combination psychoactive drugs
		- especially benzodiazepines + opioids
		- high overdose risk
	- Use term
		- substance use disorder (SUD)
			- preferred over addiction/abuse/dependence
	- All patients deserve
		- respectful
		- dignified
		- safe care
