- Overview
	- Human herpesvirus
	- Causes latent lifelong infection
	- Significant disease in
		- Immunocompromised patients
		- Congenital infection
	- Associated syndromes
		- Fever
		- Hepatitis
		- Pneumonitis
		- Encephalitis
		- Retinitis

- Epidemiology
	- Adult seropositivity
		- 40–60%
	- Reactivation occurs during immunosuppression
	- Virus shed in
		- Saliva
		- Urine
		- Breast milk
	- Common transmission routes
		- Breastfeeding
		- Fomite spread
		- Contact with children
		- Sexual contact
		- Blood transfusion
		- Organ transplantation
		- Vertical transmission
		- Exposure to infected urine or saliva

- Risk groups
	- Pregnant women
	- Child care workers
	- HIV patients
	- Organ transplant recipients
	- Bone marrow transplant recipients
	- Patients on immunosuppressive therapy
	- Neonates

- Prevention
	- Hand hygiene
	- Gloves during nappy changes
	- Condom use
	- Leucocyte-depleted blood products in immunocompromised patients

# Congenital CMV

- Epidemiology
	- Most common infectious cause of congenital abnormalities in Australia
	- Congenital infection rate
		- 0.15–2%

- Clinical manifestations
	- Deafness
	- Intellectual disability
	- Hepatitis
	- Pneumonitis
	- Blindness
	- Cytomegalic inclusion disease

- Risk factors
	- Primary maternal infection
	- First trimester infection
			- Higher complication risk

- Maternal infection
	- Often asymptomatic
	- Fatigue may occur

- Long-term sequelae
	- Sensorineural hearing loss
	- Neurodevelopmental delay

# CMV infection in children

- Perinatal infection
	- Usually asymptomatic
	- Severe disease possible
		- Encephalitis
		- Hepatitis
		- Pneumonitis

- Childhood infection
	- Usually asymptomatic
	- Spread commonly through contact with other children

# CMV infection in adults

- Immunocompetent adults
	- Usually asymptomatic
	- May cause
		- Mononucleosis-like illness
		- Hepatitis
		- Encephalitis

- HIV patients
	- Retinitis
	- Gastrointestinal disease
	- High coinfection rates

- Bone marrow or stem cell transplant recipients
	- CMV pneumonitis

- Solid organ transplant recipients
	- CMV hepatitis
	- Chronic allograft rejection

- High-risk transplant group
	- CMV seropositive donor to seronegative recipient
			- D+/R−

# Clinical syndromes

- Systemic
	- Fever
	- Fatigue
	- Malaise

- Organ-specific disease
	- Hepatitis
	- Pneumonitis
	- Retinitis
	- Encephalitis
	- Gastrointestinal disease

- Long-term associations
	- Chronic allograft rejection
	- Accelerated atherosclerosis
	- Secondary bacterial infection
	- Secondary fungal infection

# Diagnosis

- Serology
	- CMV IgM
	- CMV IgG

- PCR
	- CMV viral load
	- Useful in immunocompromised patients

- Histology
	- Cytomegalic inclusion bodies

- Antigen detection
	- pp65 antigenemia assay

# Treatment

- Antivirals
	- Ganciclovir
	- Valganciclovir
	- Foscarnet
	- Cidofovir

- Supportive care

- In transplant patients
	- Antiviral prophylaxis
	- Pre-emptive therapy

# Complications

- Retinitis
- Blindness
- Hearing loss
- Hepatic failure
- Pneumonitis
- Encephalitis
- Chronic graft rejection
- Secondary infections


# Cytomegalovirus (CMV) Diagnosis and Treatment

# Diagnosis

- Serology
	- CMV IgG
		- Usually lifelong positivity
	- CMV IgM
		- Suggests recent infection
	- Highly sensitive and specific
	- Antibody levels may decline in
		- Elderly
		- Severe immunosuppression

- Viral detection techniques
	- PCR
	- Branch DNA (bDNA)
	- NASBA
	- Antigen detection assays

- Quantitative viral testing
	- Quantitative PCR
			- Plasma
			- EDTA whole blood
	- Uses
		- Guide therapy
		- Monitor treatment response
		- Assess antiviral resistance
		- Prognosis in congenital CMV

- Important limitation
	- Detection of CMV does not always indicate invasive disease
	- Clinical correlation essential

- Challenging diagnostic scenarios
	- Pregnant woman with possible primary CMV
	- Child with hearing impairment
	- Immunocompromised patient with positive PCR but no symptoms

- Diagnosis of congenital CMV
	- Maternal serology
	- Amniotic fluid PCR
	- Neonatal PCR testing
	- Hearing assessment

# Therapeutics

- Antivirals
	- Ganciclovir
		- Intravenous
	- Valganciclovir
		- Oral prodrug of ganciclovir
	- Foscarnet
		- Intravenous
	- Cidofovir
		- Intravenous
	- Fomivirsen
		- Intraocular
	- Valaciclovir
		- Oral
		- Used for prophylaxis
	- Maribavir
		- Emerging antiviral agent

# Treatment strategies

- Symptomatic CMV disease
	- Antiviral therapy indicated

- Immunocompromised patients
	- Two approaches
		- Prophylactic therapy
		- Pre-emptive therapy

- Prophylactic therapy
	- Antivirals during high-risk periods
	- Common in transplant recipients
	- Reduces CMV disease incidence

- Pre-emptive therapy
	- Treatment guided by viral load monitoring
	- Reduces unnecessary long-term antiviral exposure
	- Reduces toxic effects
	- Reduces resistance risk

- Oral antiviral therapy
	- Improved tolerability
	- Expanded outpatient management options

# Monitoring

- Clinical monitoring
	- Fever
	- Organ dysfunction
	- Retinitis
	- Pneumonitis
	- Hepatitis

- Laboratory monitoring
	- Quantitative CMV PCR
	- Full blood count
	- Renal function
	- Liver function

# Antiviral adverse effects

- Ganciclovir / valganciclovir
	- Bone marrow suppression
	- Neutropenia

- Foscarnet
	- Nephrotoxicity
	- Electrolyte abnormalities

- Cidofovir
	- Nephrotoxicity

# Prevention

- Hand hygiene
- Avoid contact with infected secretions
- Leucocyte-depleted blood products
- Antiviral prophylaxis in transplant recipients
- Monitoring of viral load in high-risk patients
