- Definition
	- Bleeding into space between epicranial aponeurosis and periosteum

- Cause
	- Rupture of emissary veins

- Prognosis
	- Potentially lethal due to large potential space
	- Massive blood loss possible
	- Mortality
		- 12–25%

- Anatomy and consequences
	- Epicranial aponeurosis extends
		- Orbital ridges → nape of neck
		- Laterally → ears

	- Subaponeurotic space
		- May hold up to 250 mL blood with only 1 cm scalp increase

	- Newborn blood volume
		- ~90 mL/kg

	- 3 kg infant
		- 54 mL blood loss ≈20% blood volume

- Complications
	- Hypovolaemic shock
	- Anaemia
	- Coagulopathy
	- Death

- Differentiation from other scalp swellings

	- Caput succedaneum
		- Due to pressure during labour
		- Serosanguinous extra-aponeurotic collection
		- Crosses sutures + midline
		- Vacuum may cause artificial caput
			- Chignon
		- Decreases within 1 hour
		- Not associated with haemorrhage

	- Cephalohaematoma
		- Bleeding between periosteum and skull
		- Confined by periosteum
		- Does not cross sutures
		- Localised soft swelling
		- May enlarge for 12–24 hours
		- Resolves over weeks
		- Usually no treatment required

- Diagnosis

	- Onset
		- Often insidious
		- Mean diagnosis time
			- 1–6 hours after birth

	- General signs
		- Tachycardia
		- Tachypnoea
		- Pallor
		- Poor activity
		- Hypotension
		- Anaemia
		- Coagulopathy
		- Acidosis

	- Suspect if
		- 5-minute Apgar <7
		- No evidence of asphyxia
		- Difficult vacuum delivery

	- Local scalp signs
		- Diffuse scalp swelling
		- Scalp laxity
		- Fluctuant swelling
		- Crosses suture lines
		- Pitting oedema
		- Swelling anterior to ears

	- Palpation
		- Fluid-filled “leather pouch” feel
		- Ballotable lesion
		- Crepitus
		- Fluid thrill

	- Progression
		- Ear lobe elevation
		- Periorbital oedema
		- Peri-auricular oedema
		- Irritable cry
		- Pain on handling

	- Head circumference
		- May increase
		- ~38 mL blood loss per 1 cm increase

- Incidence

	- Overall
		- ~0.6/1000 deliveries

	- Spontaneous vaginal delivery
		- ~0.4/1000

	- Vacuum delivery
		- ~4.6–5.9/1000

	- 60–89% associated with vacuum delivery

	- May be underdiagnosed

- Risk factors

	- Delivery-related
		- Vacuum extraction
		- Forceps delivery
		- Difficult instrumental delivery
		- Failed vacuum extraction
		- Prolonged extraction
		- Multiple pulls
		- Multiple cup detachments

	- Maternal/neonatal
		- Nulliparity
		- 5-minute Apgar <7–8
		- Paramedian cup placement
		- Cup edge <3 cm from anterior fontanelle

- Prevention

	- Patient selection

		- Vacuum contraindications
			- <34 weeks gestation
			- Relative contraindication <36 weeks
			- Suspected bleeding disorders
				- Haemophilia
				- Thrombocytopenia

	- Technical aspects

		- Cup placement
			- Centre over flexion point
			- Along sagittal suture
			- 3 cm anterior to posterior fontanelle
			- 6 cm from anterior fontanelle

		- Cup positioning
			- Even across sagittal suture
			- Avoid parietal placement
			- Edge ≥3 cm from anterior fontanelle

		- Traction technique
			- Steady traction
			- Only during contractions
			- With maternal effort
			- Confirm descent with each pull

		- Limits

			- Time
				- Maximum ~20 minutes
				- Reassess after 15 minutes

			- Pulls
				- Usually maximum 3 contractions

			- Cup detachments
				- Usually ≤2
				- Maximum 3
				- Avoid repeated reapplication

- Neonatal surveillance

	- Intensity based on
		- Clinical circumstances
		- Neonatal condition
		- Risk of SGH

	- Mean diagnosis
		- 1–6 hours after birth

	- Level 1 surveillance

		- Indications
			- Instrumental deliveries
			- Second-stage caesarean section

		- Regimen
			- Baseline observations at 1 hour
				- Activity
				- Colour
				- Heart rate
				- Respiratory rate

			- Avoid hats/bonnets

			- Monitor
				- Head shape
				- Head circumference

			- Formal assessments
				- Between 1–6 hours
				- At 24 hours

		- Escalate if
			- Scalp swelling
			- Increasing head circumference
			- Poor feeding
			- Poor activity
			- Pallor

	- Level 2 surveillance

		- Indications
			- Vacuum extraction >20 minutes
			- >3 pulls
			- >2 cup detachments
			- 5-minute Apgar <7
			- Difficult delivery
			- Abnormal level 1 findings

		- Laboratory tests
			- Cord pH/lactate
			- Haematocrit
			- Platelet count

		- Observations
			- Hourly for first 2 hours
			- Then every 2 hours for next 6 hours

		- Assess
			- Activity
			- Colour
			- Heart rate
			- Respiratory rate
			- Head size
			- Scalp swelling

	- Level 3 surveillance

		- Indications
			- Suspected SGH
			- Abnormal level 2 findings

		- Management
			- Paediatric review
			- Admit to NICU/special care
			- Laboratory tests
				- Haematocrit
				- Platelets
				- Coagulation profile

- Management of SGH

	- Medical emergency
	- High morbidity + mortality

	- Principles
		- Immediate neonatology consultation
		- Prompt evaluation
		- Rapid resuscitation
		- Supportive care

	- Management steps
		- Clinical diagnosis
		- Do not delay stabilisation for imaging

	- Treatment
		- Aggressive fluid resuscitation
		- Restore circulating volume
		- Blood products
		- Correct acidosis
		- Correct coagulopathy

	- Frequent haemodynamic reassessment

	- Head wrapping
		- Difficult
		- Not beneficial

	- Infants requiring resuscitation
		- Transfer to NICU/special care

- Vitamin K prophylaxis
	- Instrumentally delivered neonates
		- Intramuscular vitamin K
		- Give ASAP after birth

- Recommendations

	- Recommendation 1
		- Surveillance intensity based on SGH risk

	- Recommendation 2
		- Level 1 surveillance
			- Instrumental delivery
			- Second-stage caesarean section

	- Recommendation 3
		- Symptomatic SGH
			- Medical emergency
			- Immediate neonatology consultation
			- Prompt resuscitation

	- Recommendation 4
		- Instrumentally delivered neonates
			- IM vitamin K

- Key approach to reduce morbidity + mortality

	- Prevention
		- Appropriate patient selection
		- Correct vacuum technique

	- Early diagnosis
		- Risk assessment
		- Structured surveillance

	- Treatment
		- Rapid recognition
		- Prompt resuscitation
		- Supportive care

	- Multidisciplinary care
		- Obstetricians
		- Midwives
		- Neonatal/paediatric team
		- Special care nursery
