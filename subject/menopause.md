# Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM)

- Definition
	- Chronic progressive genitourinary symptoms and signs due to oestrogen deficiency
	- Formerly called vulvovaginal atrophy (VVA)
	- Affects:
		- Vulva
		- Vagina
		- Urethra
		- Bladder trigone
		- Pelvic floor
		- Sexual function
	- Does not improve spontaneously

- Etiology
	- Menopause (most common)
	- Bilateral oophorectomy
	- Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)
	- Lactation/postpartum
	- Hypothalamic amenorrhoea
	- Chemotherapy
	- Pelvic radiotherapy
	- Endocrine therapy (e.g. aromatase inhibitors)

- Risk factors
	- Smoking
	- alcohol
	- Low sexual activity
	- Nulliparity

- Clinical features
	- Vaginal
		- Dryness
		- Dyspareunia
		- Postcoital bleeding
		- Spotting
		- Discharge
	- Vulval
		- Dryness
		- Burning
		- Irritation
		- Itching
		- Fissuring
		- Entry dyspareunia
	- Urinary
		- Recurrent UTI
		- Dysuria
		- incresed urgency and frequency
		-  incontinence

	- Sexual
		- Loss of libido
		- Entry and deep dyspareunia


- Examination
	- Speculum
		- Small/paediatric speculum may be required
	- Pelvic floor
		- Weakness
		- Hypertonicity
		- Pain
		- Vaginismus
	- Bimanual examination
		- Exclude pelvic pathology

- Diagnosis
	- Clinical diagnosis based on symptoms and examination

- Investigations
	- Usually not required
	- Urinalysis ± urine culture
		- Urinary symptoms
	- Vaginal/vulval swab
		- Discharge
		- Vulvitis
	- Vulval biopsy
		- Suspicious lesion
		- Treatment failure
	- Postmenopausal bleeding
		- Always investigate for endometrial malignancy

- Treatment
	- Initial
		- Lifestyle
			- Regular sexual activity if possible
			- Smoking cessation
		- Water-based lubricants
			- During intercourse
		- Vaginal moisturisers
			- Longer symptom relief (≈3 days)
		- Vaginal laser
			- Not recommended
	- Most appropriate
		- Mild symptoms
			- Lubricants + moisturisers
		- Moderate to severe symptoms
			- Low-dose vaginal oestrogen (first-line)
				- Oestriol cream/pessary
				- Oestradiol vaginal tablet
				- Vaginal oestrogen regimen
					- Daily at night for 2 weeks
					- Then maintenance dose
					- Lowest effective dose
	- Important points
		- Progestogen not required with low-dose vaginal oestrogen
		- Breast cancer
			- Discuss with oncologist/specialist before use
	- Persistent sexual dysfunction
		- Pelvic floor physiotherapy
		- Vaginal dilators
		- Sexual therapy
		- Couples counselling
		- Review medications (especially SSRIs)
	- Second-line options
		- Ospemifene (oral SERM)
		- Vaginal DHEA (prasterone)

---

- Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT/HRT)

	- Main indication
		- Moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms
			- Hot flushes
			- Night sweats

	- Lifestyle
		- Regular exercise
		- Healthy diet
		- Weight optimisation
		- Smoking cessation
		- Stress reduction
		- Reduce alcohol
		- Reduce caffeine

	- Premature menopause
		- Premature menopause
			- <40 years
		- Early menopause
			- <45 years
		- Offer MHT
			- Until approximately age 50 years
			- Unless contraindicated

	- Choosing MHT
		- Previous hysterectomy
			- Oestrogen-only therapy
		- Intact uterus
			- Oestrogen + progestogen
				- Prevents endometrial hyperplasia/cancer

	- Vaginal symptoms only
		- First-line
			- Local vaginal oestrogen
		- No progestogen required
		- Systemic MHT usually unnecessary


	- Contraindications
		- Personal history of breast cancer
		- Active or previous VTE (avoid oral MHT)
		- Active liver disease
		- Unexplained vaginal bleeding
		- Oestrogen-dependent malignancy

	- Cardiovascular
		- Not indicated for primary prevention of CVD
		- Started within 10 years of menopause
			- Does not increase CHD risk in healthy women

	- Venous thromboembolism
		- Oral MHT increases VTE risk
		- Transdermal oestrogen preferred in women with VTE risk factors

	- Breast cancer
		- Risk increases with duration
		- Annual review required
		- Continuing >5–7 years requires individual risk-benefit assessment

	- Prescribing principles
		- Lowest effective dose
		- Shortest duration consistent with treatment goals
		- Regular review

	- Follow-up
		- First review
			- 6 months
		- Then
			- Annual review
			- Reassess benefits and risks

	- Tibolone
		- Only if >12 months postmenopause
		- Avoid earlier
			- Irregular bleeding

	- Avoid
		- Compounded ("bioidentical") hormone therapy

	- Mental health
		- Increased risk of anxiety/depression:
		- Refer if significant mood symptoms

	- Drug interaction
		- Tamoxifen
			- Avoid:
				- Paroxetine
				- Fluoxetine
