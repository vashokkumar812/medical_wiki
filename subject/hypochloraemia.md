## Overview
- Usually associated with **sodium losses**  
- Often occurs with **metabolic alkalosis** (due to sodium retention at expense of hydrogen ions)  

---

## Causes

### 1. Drugs
- Bicarbonate (Cl⁻ inversely proportional to HCO₃⁻)  
- Steroids  
- Diuretics  
- Laxatives (↑ Mg²⁺, ↑ PO₄³⁻ losses)  
- Theophylline  

---

### 2. Increased Losses  
*(Often with volume contraction and metabolic alkalosis)*  
- Excessive sweating  
- Excessive diuresis  
- Prolonged vomiting (e.g., pyloric stenosis)  
- Nasogastric (NGT) aspiration  
- Renal salt-losing nephropathy  
- Adrenocortical deficiency  
- Acute intermittent porphyria  
- **SIADH** → usually with concurrent hyponatraemia  

---

## Key Association
- **Most cases linked to metabolic alkalosis**  
  - Sodium reabsorption occurs in exchange for hydrogen ion secretion → ↓ H⁺ → ↑ HCO₃⁻ and ↓ Cl⁻
