- Hypoglycaemia (Paediatric)

	- Definition

		- Non-diabetic child
			- Symptomatic + BGL <3.0 mmol/L
			- OR BGL <2.6 mmol/L regardless of symptoms

		- Neonate <48 hours
			- BGL <2.6 mmol/L
			- May be asymptomatic

		- Severe hypoglycaemia
			- Seizure
			- Coma
			- Serious cognitive dysfunction

	- High-Risk Features Suggesting Underlying Disease

		- Age <1 year
		- Severe hypoglycaemia
		- Recurrent episodes
		- Unexplained episode
		- No prolonged fasting
		- No intercurrent illness

	- Management

		- Conscious Child
			- Enteral glucose preferred
			- Apple juice
			- Honey (>12 months)
			- Polyjoule®

		- Avoid
			- Oral rehydration solution
			- Hydralyte®
			- Icy poles
			- Low glucose content

		- Vomiting
			- Antiemetic
			- Consider NGT

		- Severe Hypoglycaemia
			- Glucose 10%
			- 2 mL/kg IV bolus

		- No IV Access
			- IO glucose
			- IM glucagon

		- Glucagon Less Effective
			- Prolonged fasting
			- Glycogen depletion

		- After Correction
			- Slow-release carbohydrate
				- Sandwich
			- OR IV fluids containing
				- 5% glucose + normal saline
				- 10% glucose + normal saline

	- Discharge Criteria

		- Cause identified or accelerated starvation likely
		- BGL >3.0 mmol/L
			- On 2 occasions
			- 3 hours apart
		- Asymptomatic
		- Adequate oral intake
		- Parent education completed
		- Follow-up arranged if screen taken

	- AMC Pearls

		- Any detectable insulin with BGL <2.6 mmol/L = inappropriate
		- Hypoglycaemia + absent ketones = think hyperinsulinism or FAO defect
		- Hypoglycaemia + ketones after fasting = accelerated starvation
		- Neonates <48 h: threshold = <2.6 mmol/L even if asymptomatic
		- Enteral glucose preferred whenever conscious and safe
		- Never delay treatment while obtaining investigations

#in adults
- recommendations
	- Individuals treated with insulin or sulfonylurea combinations should be asked about symptomatic and asymptomatic hypoglycaemia at each encounter
	- Glycaemic goals in some older adults may be relaxed as part of individualised care, but symptomatic hyperglycaemia and acute hyperglycaemic complications should always be avoided 

- hypoglycaemia

	- definition
		- BGL ≤3.9 mmol/L
		- and/or symptoms/signs of neurogenic + neuroglycopenia

	- pseudo-hypoglycaemia
		- symptoms despite normal BGL
		- occurs after prolonged hyperglycaemia becomes normalised

	- common in T2DM
		- especially problematic when symptoms unrecognised

	- high-risk groups
		- older adults
		- renal impairment
		- poor cognition
		- low health literacy

	- symptoms

		- adrenergic symptoms
			- pale skin
			- sweating
			- shaking
			- palpitations
			- anxiety
			- dizziness

		- neuroglycopenic symptoms
			- hunger
			- impaired intellectual processing
			- confusion
			- behavioural changes
				- irritability
			- paraesthesia
			- coma
			- seizures

	- causes/risk factors
		- insulin
		- sulfonylureas
		- reduced carbohydrate intake
		- renal impairment
		- excessive alcohol
		- altered physical activity

	- asymptomatic hypoglycaemia
		- biochemical hypoglycaemia
		- BGL ≤3.9 mmol/L without symptoms

	- Clark hypoglycaemia awareness survey
		- >4 “R” responses
			- indicates unawareness

	- severe hypoglycaemia
		- functional impairment
		- requires assistance from another person
		- may require
			- carbohydrate
			- glucagon
			- glucose infusion
		- BGL <3.0 mmol/L
			- higher severe hypoglycaemia risk
		- associated implications
			- driving restrictions
			- recurrence prevention planning

	- impaired hypoglycaemia awareness
		- reduced/absent warning symptoms
		- inability to recognise early hypoglycaemia
		- family/carers may identify symptoms first
		- increased severe hypoglycaemia episodes

	- associated with
		- recurrent hypoglycaemia
		- longer duration T2DM

	- management options
		- medication review
		- hypoglycaemia management review
		- continuous glucose monitoring
		- ambulatory glucose monitoring
		- potentially reversible

- hyperglycaemia

	- hyperglycaemic emergencies
		- undiagnosed T1DM
		- HHS
			- formerly HONC
		- DKA
		- euglycaemic ketoacidosis
			- associated with SGLT2i
			- ketosis despite less marked glucose elevation

	- signs
		- severe dehydration
			- polyuria
			- polydipsia
		- abdominal pain
		- nausea
		- vomiting
		- altered consciousness
		- shock
		- ketotic breath
			- DKA

	- underlying causes
		- infection
		- myocardial infarction
		- diabetes management issues

	- DKA in T2DM
		- less common than T1DM
		- increasing due to SGLT2i use
		- glucose elevation may not be severe

	- hyperglycaemic emergency criteria
		- glucose >15 mmol/L
		- 2 readings
			- 2 hours apart
		- with metabolic symptoms

	- if ketosis unavailable
		- urgent ED referral recommended

- hypoglycaemia: practice points

	- hypoglycaemia can occur
		- at any HbA1c
			- even at target

	- regular BGL monitoring
		- monitor for hypoglycaemia

	- real-time CGM
		- may reduce hypoglycaemia risk
		- cost/access limitations
		- further evaluation needed in
			- older adults
			- at-risk populations

	- deprescribing
		- may reduce hypoglycaemia risk

	- patients may underreport symptoms
		- ask specifically regarding
			- adrenergic symptoms
			- neuroglycopenic symptoms
		- especially important in
			- older adults
			- renal dysfunction

	- severe hypoglycaemia
		- implications for
			- driving
			- operating machinery/equipment

	- impaired hypoglycaemia awareness
		- refer to endocrinologist/diabetes specialist
