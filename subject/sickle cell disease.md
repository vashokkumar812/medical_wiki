- All children with sickle cell disease are managed with the assistance of the Royal Children's Hospital Victoria

- Pathophysiology

	- Caused by structurally abnormal haemoglobin
		- Hb S

	- Hb S polymerises when deoxygenated

	- Leads to
		- RBC shape change
			- Sickling

		- Obstruction of blood flow

- Types of Sickle Cell Disease

	- All treated similarly

	- Sickle cell anaemia
		- SS disease
		- Most common

	- Sickle ß Thalassemia

	- Sickle haemoglobin C disease

- Precipitating Factors for Crisis

	- Infection

	- Dehydration

	- Hypoxia

	- Sedatives

	- Local anaesthetics

	- Surgery

- Infection Risk

	- All patients become functionally asplenic

	- High risk of encapsulated organisms

		- Pneumococcus

		- Meningococcus

		- Haemophilus

- Common Presentations

	- Vaso-occlusive crisis

	- Fever

	- Acute chest syndrome

	- Acute splenic sequestration

	- Aplastic crisis

	- Stroke

	- Priapism

	- Many require
		- Urgent treatment
		- Haematology consult

	- Management

		- Consult

			- Contact RCH on-call Haematologist

		- Analgesia

			- Start immediately
				- Do NOT delay

			- Mild Pain

				- Paracetamol

				- Ibuprofen

			- Moderate–Severe Pain

				- Morphine
					- 0.05 mg/kg IV
					- Repeat as needed

				- Consider PCA
					- Patient-controlled analgesia

		- Fluids

			- Encourage oral fluids

			- IV fluids if needed

				- Bolus
					- 10–20 mL/kg normal saline

				- Maintenance
					- Plasma-Lyte + 5% glucose
					- OR Normal saline + 5% glucose

			- Avoid fluid overload
				- Risk of acute chest syndrome

		- Transfusion

			- May be required
				- Discuss with Haematologist

- Specific Conditions

	- Vaso-Occlusive Crisis

		- Treat all pain episodes initially as VOC

		- Chest pain with respiratory symptoms
			- Consider Acute Chest Syndrome

	- Acute Chest Syndrome

		- Background

			- Lung infarction
				- Life-threatening

			- Features
				- Respiratory symptoms
				- Hypoxaemia
				- New infiltrate on CXR

			- May deteriorate rapidly

		- Management

			- Follow general management

			- Do NOT delay treatment for CXR

			- Treatment

				- IV antibiotics

					- 3rd generation cephalosporin

					- Atypical coverage
						- Roxithromycin
						- Azithromycin

				- Oxygen
					- Target SpO₂ >96%

				- Early PICU referral
					- If severe

	- Fever in Sickle Cell Disease

		- Background

			- Functional asplenia
				- High infection risk

		- Management

			- Treat as VOC if pain present

			- Evaluate for acute chest syndrome
				- If respiratory symptoms

			- Treatment

				- IV antibiotics

					- 3rd generation cephalosporin

					- Atypical coverage

				- Blood cultures

				- Urine cultures

	- Acute Splenic Sequestration

		- Background

			- Hb drop
				- ≥20 g/L below baseline

			- Enlarged spleen

			- Reticulocytes increased
				- Unless aplastic crisis

		- Management

			- General management

			- Immediate

				- IV fluids
					- 10–20 mL/kg normal saline

			- Transfusion

				- Target Hb
					- 5–6 g/dL initially

				- Do NOT increase Hb >3 g/dL

			- Rapid correction
				- Hyperviscosity
				- Stroke risk

	- Aplastic Crisis

		- Background

			- Hb decreased

			- Reticulocytes <1%

			- Often due to
				- Parvovirus

		- Management

			- General management

			- Treatment

				- Maintenance fluids

				- Transfuse if
					- Symptomatic
					- Hb <5 g/dL

				- Avoid Hb rise >3 g/dL

				- IV antibiotics
					- If febrile

	- Stroke

		- Background

			- Occurs in ~10%
				- Hb SS

			- May complicate
				- Acute chest syndrome
				- Aplastic crisis

		- Management

			- Neuroimaging

				- MRI preferred

				- CT brain
					- No contrast
					- If MRI unavailable

			- Treatment

				- Transfusion

					- Target Hb ~10 g/dL

					- Then exchange transfusion
						- ICU

	- Priapism

		- Background

			- Painful erection
				- Often early morning

			- Common in adolescents

		- Types

			- Stuttering
				- 2–4 hours
				- Recurrent

			- Severe
				- >4 hours
				- Emergency

		- Assessment

			- Duration

			- Associated symptoms
				- Fever
				- Dysuria
				- Dehydration

			- Previous episodes

		- Management

			- General management

			- Initial Measures

				- Empty bladder
					- Catheter if needed

				- Exercise

				- Warm bath

				- Do NOT use ice

			- Treatment

				- Hydration

				- Oxygen

				- Analgesia

				- Urine alkalinisation

			- >4 hours
				- Urological emergency

			- Consult

				- Urology

				- Haematology

			- May require transfusion

- Key Takeaways

	- Treat pain early and aggressively

	- Avoid overhydration

	- Watch for acute chest syndrome

	- Do not overcorrect Hb

	- Always involve Haematology early
