- Acute paronychia

	- Definition
		- infection of nail fold after minor trauma

	- Cause
		- most common organism
			- Staphylococcus aureus

	- Clinical features
		- pain
		- redness
		- swelling
		- localised pus/abscess

	- Management

		- uncomplicated/localised pus
			- drainage
				- elevate nail fold
				- puncture nail fold
			- hygiene advice
			- antibiotics usually unnecessary

		- antibiotics
			- indications
				- failure to improve after drainage
				- extensive infection
			- options
				- flucloxacillin
				- dicloxacillin

		- recurrent cases
			- exclude diabetes
			- consider [[Herpetic Whitlow]]

		- complicated/subungual extension
			- small vertical incision alongside nail
			- partial nail removal
			- total nail removal
			- exclude diabetes

- [[Herpetic Whitlow]]

	- Cause
		- herpes simplex virus

	- Clinical features
		- severe pain
		- vesicles/blistering
			- key feature
		- possible history of cold sore

	- Management
		- do not incise/drain
		- oral antivirals
			- acyclovir

- Chronic paronychia

	- Definition
		- painless inflammation of nail fold
		- traumatic nail dystrophy

	- Key diagnostic feature
		- loss of cuticle

	- Causes
		- repeated cuticle manipulation
		- wet work occupations
			- chefs
			- nurses
			- housewives
			- fishmongers
		- hand dermatitis
		- habitual nail picking
		- Candida
			- secondary rather than primary cause

	- Pathophysiology
		- cuticle damage
		- water + irritants enter nail fold
		- chronic proximal nail fold inflammation

	- Investigations
		- culture organisms
		- exclude diabetes

	- Management

		- General measures
			- most important treatment
			- keep hands dry
			- avoid wet work
			- cotton-lined gloves
				- maximum 15 min use
			- avoid
				- soaps
				- detergents
				- chemicals
			- avoid cuticle trauma
				- no picking
				- no pushing cuticle back
				- no inserting objects beneath cuticle
			- use mild soap/shampoo
			- gloves for gardening

		- Medical treatment

			- Candida infection
				- miconazole 2%
					- BD for 7 days
				- clotrimazole alternative

			- Staphylococcal infection
				- mupirocin ointment

			- persistent exudate
				- 4% thymol in alcohol
					- QID
				- 10% sulfacetamide alternative

			- barrier protection
				- Vaseline
					- frequent application
						- 5–10×/day when dry

			- anti-inflammatory therapy
				- very potent topical corticosteroids

		- Referral
			- if not responding to treatment
