- **Gestational Trophoblastic Disease (GTD)**
  - Spectrum of trophoblastic disorders
  - Includes
    - Hydatidiform mole
      - Complete mole
      - Partial mole
    - Invasive mole
    - Choriocarcinoma
    - Placental Site Trophoblastic Tumour (PSTT)
    - Epithelioid Trophoblast Tumour (ETT)

- **Gestational Trophoblastic Neoplasia (GTN)**
  - GTD with
    - Persistent β-hCG elevation
    - Metastatic disease
    - Requires chemotherapy and/or surgery

- **Epidemiology**
  - Increased risk
    - Asian women
    - Age <15 years
    - Age >45 years
    - Previous molar pregnancy
  - Recurrence risk increased

- **Hydatidiform Mole**
  - Most common GTD

- **Complete Mole**
  - Genetics
    - Empty ovum (no maternal DNA)
    - Fertilized by
      - One sperm with duplication (46XX most common)
      - Two sperm
  - No fetal tissue
  - No fetal RBCs
  - Diffuse trophoblastic proliferation
  - Markedly elevated β-hCG
  - Higher risk of malignant transformation
  - Risk of persistent GTD
    - 15–25%

- **Partial Mole**
  - Genetics
    - Usually triploid
      - 69XXX
      - 69XXY
      - 69XYY
    - Usually due to dispermy
  - Fetal tissue present
  - Fetal RBCs present
  - Less trophoblastic proliferation
  - Lower β-hCG levels
  - Risk of persistent GTD
    - 0.5–4%

- **Clinical Features of Molar Pregnancy**
  - PV bleeding
    - Most common presentation
  - Excessive nausea and vomiting
  - Hyperemesis gravidarum
  - Pelvic pain
  - Enlarged uterus for dates
  - Passage of grape-like vesicles
  - Early-onset pre-eclampsia (<20 weeks)
  - Hyperthyroidism
  - Bilateral theca lutein ovarian cysts

- **Examination Findings**
  - Uterus larger than gestational age
  - Tachycardia
  - Hypertension
  - Signs of hyperthyroidism

- **Initial Investigation**
  - Urine pregnancy test

- **Most Appropriate Investigation**
  - Quantitative serum β-hCG

- **Diagnostic Investigations**
  - Pelvic ultrasound
    - Complete mole
      - "Snowstorm" appearance
      - No fetus
    - Partial mole
      - Fetal tissue may be present
  - FBC
  - Group and hold
  - UEC
  - LFTs
  - TFTs
  - Chest X-ray if GTN suspected

- **Diagnostic Pathology**
  - Histology of products of conception
  - If uncertain
    - Ploidy analysis
    - p57 immunostaining

- **Ultrasound Findings**
  - Complete mole
    - Diffuse echogenic material
    - Multiple cystic spaces
    - No fetus
  - Partial mole
    - Abnormal placenta
    - Fetal tissue may be present

- **Most Appropriate Initial Management**
  - Suction curettage (suction evacuation)

- **Management**
  - Suction evacuation by experienced gynaecologist
  - Histological examination of all products
  - Anti-D for Rh-negative women
  - Avoid oxytocics before evacuation
  - Prostaglandins may be used for cervical preparation

- **β-hCG Follow-up**
  - Weekly β-hCG
    - Until 3 consecutive normal results
  - Complete mole
    - Monthly β-hCG for 6 months after normalization
  - Partial mole
    - Stop monitoring after normalization

- **Persistent GTD**
  - Suspect if β-hCG fails to normalize

- **Diagnostic Criteria for Persistent GTD**
  - β-hCG plateau
    - <10% decline over 3 weeks (4 values)
  - β-hCG rise
    - >10% increase over 2 weeks (3 values)
  - Histological diagnosis of choriocarcinoma
  - Metastatic disease

- **Clinical Features of Persistent GTD**
  - Persistent PV bleeding
  - Pelvic pain
  - Enlarging uterus
  - Pelvic mass
  - Persistently elevated β-hCG

- **Next Step if Persistent GTD Diagnosed**
  - Metastatic work-up
  - WHO risk scoring
  - Refer to trophoblastic disease centre

- **Common Metastatic Sites**
  - Lungs (most common)
  - Vagina
  - Liver
  - Brain

- **Vaginal Metastases**
  - Usually in vaginal fornices
  - Highly vascular
  - Do NOT biopsy
    - Risk of catastrophic haemorrhage

- **Choriocarcinoma**
  - Highly malignant GTN
  - β-hCG markedly elevated
  - No chorionic villi
  - Extensive haemorrhage and necrosis
  - Can follow
    - Complete mole (most common)
    - Miscarriage
    - Ectopic pregnancy
    - Normal pregnancy

- **Clinical Features of Choriocarcinoma**
  - PV bleeding
  - Pelvic mass
  - Haemoptysis
  - Neurological symptoms
  - Symptoms from metastases

- **Key Choriocarcinoma Facts**
  - Lung metastasis most common
  - Can cross placenta
  - Neonate requires urinary β-hCG screening

- **Placental Site Trophoblastic Tumour (PSTT)**
  - Rare
  - Slow growing
  - Occurs years after pregnancy
  - β-hCG relatively low for tumour burden
  - Less chemosensitive
  - Primary treatment
    - Hysterectomy

- **Epithelioid Trophoblast Tumour (ETT)**
  - Rare
  - Derived from intermediate trophoblast
  - Low β-hCG
  - Often follows term pregnancy
  - Poor response to chemotherapy
  - Primary treatment
    - Hysterectomy

- **Second Evacuation**
  - Consider if
    - Retained tissue
    - Persistent bleeding
  - Avoid if
    - β-hCG >5000 IU/L
    - Metastatic disease present

- **Contraception During Follow-up**
  - Pregnancy must be avoided
  - Combined OCP acceptable
  - Avoid IUD until
    - β-hCG normalized
    - ≥6 weeks after evacuation

- **Future Pregnancy**
  - Recurrence risk
    - Approximately 1 in 70
  - Measure β-hCG
    - 6 weeks after completion of every future pregnancy

- **Hysterectomy**
  - Consider if
    - Family complete
    - Disease confined to uterus
  - Reduces chemotherapy requirement
  - Does not eliminate need for β-hCG surveillance

- **Chemotherapy**
  - Indicated for GTN
  - Multi-agent chemotherapy
    - High-risk disease
  - Future fertility generally preserved

- **Important AMC Associations**
  - PV bleeding + uterus larger than dates + very high β-hCG + snowstorm US = Complete mole
  - Early pre-eclampsia (<20 weeks) = Molar pregnancy until proven otherwise
  - Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy + very high β-hCG = Molar pregnancy
  - Persistent β-hCG after evacuation = Persistent GTD until proven otherwise
  - Vaginal lesion in GTN = Do NOT biopsy
  - Most common metastatic site = Lung
  - Most appropriate initial treatment = Suction evacuation
  - Rh-negative woman = Anti-D required
  - Persistent irregular bleeding after pregnancy = Check β-hCG
