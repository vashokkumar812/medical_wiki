- Hereditary Haemochromatosis (HH) — AMC High-Yield
  :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

	- Definition
		- Autosomal recessive disorder
		- Excess intestinal iron absorption
		- Progressive iron overload
		- Iron deposition → organ damage

	- Genetics
		- HFE gene mutation
		- Most common mutation
			- C282Y homozygous ⭐ (>90%)
		- Less common
			- C282Y/H63D compound heterozygote
			- H63D homozygote (rarely causes disease)

	- Risk factors
		- Northern European/Celtic ancestry
		- Family history
		- Male sex
		- Women protected until menopause (menstruation/pregnancy)

	- Pathophysiology
		- ↓ Hepcidin
		- ↑ Intestinal iron absorption
		- No physiological iron excretion
		- Progressive iron deposition
			- Liver
			- Pancreas
			- Heart
			- Joints
			- Pituitary
			- Skin

---

- Clinical Features

	- Early
		- Fatigue ⭐
		- Arthralgia
		- Loss of libido
		- small testies
		- Upper abdominal discomfort

	- Late
		- Cirrhosis
		- Hepatomegaly
		- Diabetes mellitus ("bronze diabetes")
		- Cardiomyopathy
		- Arrhythmias
		- Hypogonadism/impotence
		- MCP joint arthritis (2nd & 3rd MCP) ⭐
		- Skin pigmentation

---

- Who to Test

	- First-degree relatives
	- Family history of HH
	- Unexplained liver disease
	- Raised LFTs
	- Early fatigue or arthralgia
	- Diabetes + liver disease
	- Cardiomyopathy
	- Hypogonadism
	- MCP arthritis

---

- Initial Investigations ⭐

	- Fasting transferrin saturation (TS)
		- Earliest abnormality
		- Abnormal
			- >45% (men)
			- >55% (women)

	- Serum ferritin (SF)
		- Reflects iron stores
		- Abnormal
			- >300 μg/L (men/postmenopausal women)
			- >200 μg/L (premenopausal women)
		- Acute-phase reactant

---

- Diagnosis

	- ↑ TS + ↑ Ferritin
		- Suspect HH
		- Perform HFE genetic testing

	- Positive HFE
		- Confirm diagnosis
		- Assess iron overload

	- Ferritin >1000 μg/L ⭐
		- High risk of cirrhosis
		- Refer to hepatology
		- Consider liver biopsy/MRI

---

- Genetic Results

	- C282Y homozygote
		- Highest risk
		- Lifelong monitoring
		- Venesection if iron overload

	- C282Y/H63D
		- Low risk (~1%)
		- Periodic iron studies

	- H63D homozygote/heterozygote
		- Usually carrier only
		- Rarely clinically significant

---

- Treatment ⭐

	- Venesection (first-line)
		- Initial
			- 1–2/week
		- Maintenance
			- Every 3–4 months
		- Lifelong

	- Target ferritin
		- <50 μg/L (traditional target)
		- Some guidelines
			- <300 μg/L (men)
			- <200 μg/L (women)

	- If venesection contraindicated
		- Iron chelation (desferrioxamine)

---

- Lifestyle

	- Avoid iron supplements
	- Avoid vitamin C supplements ⭐
	- Limit alcohol (especially if ferritin elevated)
	- Low-iron diet not routinely recommended

---

- Monitoring

	- Ferritin
	- Transferrin saturation
	- Liver function tests
	- Screen for cirrhosis if present
		- Ultrasound every 6 months
		- AFP every 6 months

---

- Prognosis

	- Early diagnosis + venesection
		- Normal life expectancy ⭐
	- Cirrhosis
		- Irreversible
		- Increased HCC risk
	- Liver fibrosis
		- May improve
	- Arthritis
		- Often persists despite treatment

---

- AMC Pearls ⭐

	- Most common mutation = **C282Y homozygous**
	- Earliest abnormality = **↑ Transferrin saturation**
	- Best screening tests = **Fasting TS + Serum ferritin**
	- Ferritin >1000 μg/L = **Think cirrhosis → hepatology referral**
	- Classic joint = **2nd & 3rd MCP**
	- Treatment = **Lifelong venesection**
	- Avoid **vitamin C** and **iron supplements**
	- Early treatment = **Normal life expectancy**
