# acute pancreatitis
- Chronic Pancreatitis

	- Core pathology
		- Progressive irreversible pancreatic inflammation
		- Leads to
			- Fibrosis
			- Parenchymal loss
			- Calcification (may occur)

	- Consequences
		- Chronic pain
		- Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)
		- Endocrine failure (diabetes)
		- Structural complications

- When to suspect chronic pancreatitis

	- Chronic epigastric abdominal pain
	- Malabsorption
	- Steatorrhoea
	- Unexplained diabetes
	- Recurrent acute pancreatitis

- Diagnostic approach

	- Step 1 – Clinical evaluation

		- Assess
			- Pain pattern
			- Weight loss
			- Steatorrhoea
			- Diabetes
			- Alcohol intake
			- Smoking history
			- Previous pancreatitis episodes

	- Step 2 – Imaging (morphological tests)

		- CT scan
			- First-line structural imaging

		- MRCP
			- Non-invasive ductal imaging

		- Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)
			- Detects early disease

		- ERCP
			- Previously gold standard
			- Not recommended for diagnosis due to complications

		- Imaging aims to detect
			- Calcifications
			- Duct obstruction
			- Strictures
			- Pseudocysts

	- Step 3 – Functional testing

		- Used when
			- Imaging is inconclusive
			- Malabsorption suspected

		- Faecal elastase-1
			- <200 μg/g
				- Indicates pancreatic exocrine insufficiency

	- Practical diagnosis

		- Usually requires a combination of
			- Clinical symptoms
			- Imaging findings
			- Functional testing

		- No single definitive diagnostic test

- Clinical presentation-based management

	- Chronic abdominal pain

		- Typical pain pattern
			- Epigastric
			- Radiates to back

		- Pain patterns
			- Intermittent attacks
			- Persistent chronic pain

		- Step 1 – Imaging

			- CT
			- MRCP
			- EUS

			- Assess for
				- Pancreatic duct obstruction
				- Calculi
				- Strictures
				- Pseudocysts

		- Step 2 – Structural cause present?

			- Duct obstruction
				- Refer for intervention
					- Surgical drainage
					- Resection procedures
					- Endoscopic stenting (distal duct strictures)

			- Large pseudocyst
				- Endoscopic drainage

		- Step 3 – No structural cause identified

			- Pain likely due to
				- Nerve irritation from chronic inflammation

			- Analgesic ladder

				- Non-opioids
					- Paracetamol
					- NSAIDs

				- Weak opioids
					- Codeine
					- Tramadol

				- Strong opioids
					- Morphine
					- Oxycodone

			- Adjunct
				- Celiac plexus block

		- Possible adjunct therapy

			- Antioxidants
				- Vitamin C
				- Vitamin E
				- Selenium
				- Methionine

			- Evidence limited

	- Malabsorption

		- Clinical clues
			- Weight loss
			- Steatorrhoea
			- Bulky stools
			- Offensive stools

		- Cause
			- Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency

		- Diagnosis
			- Faecal elastase-1 <200 μg/g

		- Treatment

			- Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT)

				- Enteric-coated enzyme preparations

				- Dose
					- Titrate according to symptom response

			- If inadequate response
				- Add acid suppression
					- Proton pump inhibitor (PPI)
					- H₂ receptor blocker

				- Reason
					- Gastric acid degrades pancreatic enzymes

		- Diet
			- No major dietary restrictions once enzyme replacement is adequate

	- Diabetes in chronic pancreatitis

		- Also called
			- Pancreatogenic diabetes

		- Characteristics
			- Occurs late in disease
			- Islet cells affected late

		- Management
			- Similar principles to other forms of diabetes
			- Insulin often required

		- Recommendation
			- Initial referral to diabetes specialist clinic

	- Acute pancreatitis as presentation

		- Acute pancreatitis may indicate underlying chronic pancreatitis

		- Follow-up assessment
			- Exocrine insufficiency
			- Diabetes
			- Structural complications

		- Gallstone pancreatitis

			- Management
				- Cholecystectomy during same admission
				- Or early after admission

			- Reason
				- Prevent recurrence

- Lifestyle and risk-factor management

	- Alcohol

		- No safe threshold
		- Complete abstinence recommended

	- Nutrition

		- Encourage
			- Regular balanced meals

		- Avoid replacing meals with
			- Tobacco
			- Coffee
			- Alcohol

		- These behaviours may trigger attacks

- Chronic disease model

	- Management components
		- Pain control
		- Pancreatic enzyme replacement
		- Diabetes monitoring
		- Lifestyle modification
		- Psychosocial support

	- Common associated issues
		- Alcohol dependence
		- Opioid dependence

- Follow-up monitoring

	- Monitor for
			- Weight loss
			- Malabsorption
			- Diabetes
			- Recurrent pancreatitis

	- Investigations
			- Blood glucose
			- Faecal elastase
			- Repeat imaging if symptoms worsen

- Pancreatic cancer risk

	- Chronic pancreatitis increases pancreatic cancer risk

	- Routine screening
		- Not recommended

	- Red flags requiring repeat imaging
		- New-onset diabetes
		- Change in pain pattern
		- Worsening pain

- AMC MCQ Pearls

	- Chronic pancreatitis = irreversible pancreatic fibrosis and destruction
	- Most common symptoms
		- Chronic epigastric pain radiating to back
		- Steatorrhoea
		- Diabetes
	- CT scan = first-line imaging
	- MRCP and EUS are useful adjuncts
	- ERCP should not be used routinely for diagnosis
	- Faecal elastase <200 μg/g = exocrine pancreatic insufficiency
	- PERT is first-line treatment for steatorrhoea and malabsorption
	- Add PPI if inadequate response to enzyme therapy
	- Pancreatogenic diabetes often requires insulin
	- Alcohol cessation is mandatory
	- Chronic pancreatitis increases pancreatic cancer risk
	- New diabetes or worsening pain warrants repeat pancreatic imaging
	- Gallstone pancreatitis → cholecystectomy during same admission (or early after)

				- Late finding
	
		- Causes
			- Alcohol most common
	
		- Diagnostic clues
			- Pancreatic calcification
			- Previous pancreatitis
			- Heavy alcohol use
			- Duct obstruction
	
		- Lipase/imaging may be normal

Features
			- Insidious pain
			- Weight loss
			- Anorexia
			- Jaundice
	
		- Investigations
			- Ultrasound
				- Detects biliary dilatation
				- Poor tumour sensitivity
	
			- CT abdomen
			- ERCP
			- MRI
