## Overview
- **Definition**: Serum K⁺ > 5.5 mmol/L  
- **Emergency**: Risk of life-threatening arrhythmias  

---

## Resuscitation
- **A, B, C**
- Large bore IV access → fluid resuscitation (enhance renal perfusion & K⁺ elimination)  
- Bloods: FBC, U+E, CK, [[Arterial blood gas]]/VBG  
- Monitoring: ECG, NIBP  

---

## Stepwise Management

### 1. Membrane Stabilisation (Immediate)
**Calcium salts**
- **10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate** (2.2 mmol Ca²⁺)  
- **10 mL of 10% calcium chloride** (6.8 mmol Ca²⁺)  
- Onset: minutes, duration: 30–60 min  
- *Protects heart, does not lower K⁺*  
- Risks: [[bradycardia]], arrhythmias, severe tissue necrosis if extravasated  

---

### 2. Shift K⁺ Into Cells (Temporary)

**Sodium Bicarbonate**
- **1 mmol/kg IV** (100 mL of 8.4%)  
- Works if **metabolic acidosis present**  
- Additive with insulin/dextrose & salbutamol  
- ⚠️ Don’t co-administer with Ca²⁺ (precipitation)  
- Risks: hypernatraemia, pulmonary oedema, tetany (if [[Hypocalcaemia]])  

**Insulin + Dextrose**
- **10U Actrapid + 50 mL of 50% glucose IV**  
- ↓K⁺ by **0.65–1.0 mmol/L/hr**  
- Risks: hypoglycaemia → monitor glucose  

**Salbutamol (β₂-agonist)**
- **20 mg neb** OR **0.5 mg IV**  
- Stimulates Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase → K⁺ shift intracellular  
- IV > neb in potency  
- Risks: tachyarrhythmia, tremor, [[anxiety disorders]], flushing  

---

### 3. Increase K⁺ Elimination (Definitive)

**Diuretics**  
- Mannitol, frusemide (limited evidence, not first-line)  

**Dialysis**
- **Best method** in severe/refractory cases  
- IHD removes **25–40 mmol/hr (~1 mmol/L/hr)**  
- More effective if ↑ blood flow, ↑ dialysate flow, ↓ dialysate K⁺, ↑ bicarbonate conc.  

**Cation Exchange Resins**  
- **Calcium resonium 15–30 g PO/PR**  
- Sodium polystyrene sulphonate  
- Slow, unsuitable for emergencies  
- Risks: [[constipation]], intestinal necrosis → always give with laxative  

---

### 4. Treat Underlying Cause
- Stop K⁺-sparing drugs, ACEi/ARB, NSAIDs, heparin  
- Treat sepsis, acidosis, dehydration etc.  

---

## Hyperkalaemic Cardiac Arrest
- Continue resuscitation until K⁺ normalised  
- **[[adrenaline]]** → drives K⁺ into cells  
- **Calcium chloride**  
- **NaHCO₃** (if acidosis)  
- At ROSC → insulin/dextrose infusion  
- **Dialysis during CPR** → IHD, CVVH, CVVHDF, even peritoneal dialysis reported with good recovery  
- Consider **ECMO** if refractory  

---

## ECG Changes in Hyperkalaemia
1. Tall peaked T waves  
2. Shortened QT  
3. PR prolongation  
4. Widened QRS  
5. Sine-wave pattern → asystole
