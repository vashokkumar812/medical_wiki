- Hand, foot and mouth disease (HFMD)

- Epidemiology
    - Common in
        - Summer
        - Early autumn
    - Outbreaks common
        - Childcare centres
        - Schools
    - Most affected
        - Children <10 years
    - Not related to animal foot-and-mouth disease

- Aetiology
    - Most common
        - Coxsackievirus A16

    - Less common
        - Coxsackie A viruses
        - Coxsackie B viruses
        - Enterovirus 71 (EV71)

- Important complications
    - Meningoencephalitis
    - Myocarditis
    - Neurological complications
        - Especially EV71 outbreaks

- Transmission
    - Direct contact
    - Droplet spread
    - Virus shed in
        - Saliva
        - Faeces
            - May continue for weeks

    - Incubation period
        - 3–6 days

    - Most infectious
        - Until blisters resolve

- Clinical presentation

    - Prodrome
        - Fever
        - Sore throat
        - Anorexia

    - Oral lesions
        - Vesicles
            - Cheeks
            - Gums
            - Tongue
        - Progress to painful ulcers

    - Skin lesions
        - Papulovesicular rash
            - ~75%

        - Common sites
            - Palms
            - Fingers
            - Soles
            - Toes
            - Buttocks
            - Genitals
            - Limbs

        - Features
            - Non-pruritic
            - May appear haemorrhagic

    - Duration
        - 7–10 days

- Diagnosis
    - Usually clinical

    - Laboratory testing
        - Rarely required
        - Viral isolation
        - PCR
            - Blood
            - CSF
            - Stool

    - Immunity
        - Develops to specific viral strain
        - Reinfection possible with different enteroviruses

- Management
    - Symptomatic treatment
    - Analgesia
    - Hydration
    - Antivirals generally not required

- Pregnancy considerations
    - Late pregnancy infection
        - May transmit to fetus

    - Neonatal complications
        - Meningoencephalitis
        - Thrombocytopenia
        - DIC
        - Cardiomyopathy
        - Hepatitis

- School/childcare exclusion
    - Notification not required
    - Exclusion not practical
    - Faecal shedding may continue for weeks

- Prevention
    - Hand hygiene after contact with
        - Respiratory secretions
        - Faeces
        - Skin lesions

- Key points
    - Common childhood viral illness
    - Usually mild/self-limiting
    - Typical triad
        - Fever
        - Oral vesicles/ulcers
        - Distal extremity rash
    - Management supportive
