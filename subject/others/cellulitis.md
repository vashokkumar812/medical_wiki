# Cellulitis (AMC MCQ Notes)

- Definition
	- Acute bacterial infection of:
		- Dermis
		- Subcutaneous tissue
	- Usually follows skin barrier disruption

- Common Organisms
	- Non-purulent cellulitis
		- **Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A Streptococcus)** ⭐
		- Staphylococcus aureus
	- Purulent cellulitis / abscess
		- **Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA)** ⭐
	- Animal bites
		- Pasteurella multocida
	- Water exposure
		- Freshwater → Aeromonas
		- Seawater → Vibrio vulnificus

---

# Risk Factors

- Skin trauma
- Tinea pedis ⭐
- Chronic oedema / lymphoedema
- Venous insufficiency
- Diabetes
- Obesity
- Immunosuppression
- IV drug use
- Previous cellulitis

---

# Clinical Features

- Unilateral erythema ⭐
- Warmth
- Swelling
- Tenderness
- Ill-defined margins
- Fever
- Malaise
- Regional lymphadenopathy
- Lymphangitis

---

# Red Flags

- Rapid progression
- Severe pain out of proportion ⭐
- Bullae
- Skin necrosis
- Crepitus
- Hypotension
- Altered mental state
- Immunocompromised patient

→ Suspect **necrotising fasciitis**

---

# Diagnosis

- Primarily clinical ⭐

## Investigations

- Mild uncomplicated cellulitis
	- No investigations usually required

- Moderate–severe infection
	- FBC
	- CRP
	- U&E
	- Blood cultures (if septic/systemically unwell)

- If purulent
	- Swab/pus culture

- Ultrasound
	- If abscess suspected

---

# Differential Diagnosis

- DVT
- Venous eczema
- Contact dermatitis
- Gout
- Septic arthritis
- Necrotising fasciitis
- Erysipelas
- Insect bite reaction

---

# Severity

## Mild
- Local infection
- No systemic features

## Moderate
- Systemic symptoms
- Stable

## Severe
- Sepsis
- Rapid progression
- Failed oral antibiotics
- Significant comorbidity

---

# Management

## General

- Elevate affected limb ⭐
- Analgesia
- Treat portal of entry
	- e.g. tinea pedis
- Mark erythema margins

---

## Antibiotics

### Mild cellulitis (oral)

- Flucloxacillin ⭐
- Cephalexin

If penicillin allergy
- Clindamycin

---

### Moderate–Severe cellulitis (IV)

- Flucloxacillin IV ⭐
- Cefazolin IV

If severe penicillin allergy
- Clindamycin IV

---

### Suspected MRSA

- Vancomycin IV ⭐

Alternatives
- Linezolid
- Daptomycin (selected cases)

---

### Animal bite cellulitis

- Amoxicillin–clavulanate ⭐

---

### Diabetic foot infection

- Broad-spectrum antibiotics
- Surgical assessment

---

# Abscess

- Incision and drainage ⭐
- Antibiotics if:
	- Cellulitis
	- Systemic illness
	- Immunocompromised
	- Large abscess

---

# Admission Indications

- Sepsis
- Necrotising infection suspected
- Rapid progression
- Immunocompromised
- Failure of oral antibiotics
- Inability to tolerate oral therapy
- Significant facial/orbital cellulitis

---

# Complications

- Abscess
- Bacteraemia
- Sepsis
- Osteomyelitis
- Septic arthritis
- Necrotising fasciitis
- Recurrent cellulitis
- Chronic lymphoedema

---

# Prevention of Recurrent Cellulitis

- Treat tinea pedis ⭐
- Weight reduction
- Control oedema
- Compression therapy (if appropriate)
- Good skin care
- Manage diabetes
- Prophylactic penicillin for frequent recurrences (selected patients)

---

# AMC Pearls ⭐

- Cellulitis is usually **unilateral**
- **Group A Streptococcus** is the commonest cause of non-purulent cellulitis
- Purulent cellulitis → think **Staphylococcus aureus**
- Cellulitis + abscess → **Incision & drainage**
- Severe pain out of proportion → **Necrotising fasciitis until proven otherwise**
- Blood cultures are **not routinely required** in uncomplicated cellulitis
- Always look for and treat the **portal of entry** (especially **tinea pedis**)
- Elevation of the affected limb is an important adjunct to treatment
