- ABG Interpretation Approach
	- Step 1: Assess pH
		- pH < 7.35
			- Acidaemia
		- pH > 7.45
			- Alkalaemia
		- pH 7.35–7.45
			- Normal or compensated disorder
	- Step 2: Identify primary disorder
		- PaCO₂ abnormal
			- Respiratory disorder
		- HCO₃⁻ abnormal
			- Metabolic disorder
	- Step 3: Assess compensation
	- Step 4: Assess oxygenation
	- Step 5: Calculate anion gap if metabolic acidosis
	- Step 6: Look for mixed acid-base disorders

- Normal ABG Values
	- pH
		- 7.35–7.45
	- PaCO₂
		- 35–45 mmHg
		- 4.7–6.0 kPa
	- HCO₃⁻
		- 22–26 mmol/L
	- PaO₂
		- 80–100 mmHg
		- 10.7–13.3 kPa
	- SaO₂
		- 95–100%
	- Base Excess
		- −2 to +2

- Respiratory Acidosis
	- ABG Pattern
		- pH ↓
		- PaCO₂ ↑
	- Causes
		- COPD exacerbation
		- Severe asthma
		- Opioid overdose
		- Benzodiazepine overdose
		- CNS depression
		- Neuromuscular disease
		- Obesity hypoventilation syndrome
		- Airway obstruction
	- Acute Compensation
		- For every 10 mmHg rise in PaCO₂
			- HCO₃⁻ rises by 1 mmol/L
	- Chronic Compensation
		- For every 10 mmHg rise in PaCO₂
			- HCO₃⁻ rises by 4 mmol/L

- Respiratory Alkalosis
	- ABG Pattern
		- pH ↑
		- PaCO₂ ↓
	- Causes
		- Pulmonary embolism
		- Anxiety
		- Panic attack
		- Pregnancy
		- Sepsis
		- High altitude
		- Early salicylate poisoning
		- Mechanical overventilation
	- Acute Compensation
		- For every 10 mmHg fall in PaCO₂
			- HCO₃⁻ falls by ~2 mmol/L
	- Chronic Compensation
		- For every 10 mmHg fall in PaCO₂
			- HCO₃⁻ falls by ~5 mmol/L

- Metabolic Acidosis
	- ABG Pattern
		- pH ↓
		- HCO₃⁻ ↓
	- Step
		- Calculate anion gap
	- High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA)
		- GOLD MARK
			- G
				- Glycols
					- Ethylene glycol
					- Propylene glycol
			- O
				- Oxoproline
					- Chronic paracetamol use
			- L
				- Lactic acidosis
			- D
				- D-lactic acidosis
			- M
				- Methanol
			- A
				- Aspirin
			- R
				- Renal failure
			- K
				- Ketoacidosis
					- DKA
					- Alcoholic ketoacidosis
					- Starvation ketoacidosis
	- Normal Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (NAGMA)
		- Diarrhoea
		- Renal tubular acidosis
		- Acetazolamide
		- Ureteric diversion
		- Pancreatic fistula
		- Excess normal saline infusion
	- Compensation
		- Winter's Formula
			- Expected PaCO₂
				- (1.5 × HCO₃⁻) + 8 ± 2
			- Actual PaCO₂ > Expected
				- Concurrent respiratory acidosis
			- Actual PaCO₂ < Expected
				- Concurrent respiratory alkalosis

- Metabolic Alkalosis
	- ABG Pattern
		- pH ↑
		- HCO₃⁻ ↑
	- Causes
		- Vomiting
		- Nasogastric suction
		- Loop diuretics
		- Thiazide diuretics
		- Hyperaldosteronism
		- Cushing syndrome
		- Excess bicarbonate administration
		- Severe hypokalaemia
	- Compensation
		- Expected PaCO₂
			- (0.7 × HCO₃⁻) + 20 ± 5

- Anion Gap
	- Formula
		- AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)
	- Normal
		- 8–12 mmol/L
	- Increased AG
		- Unmeasured anions present
	- Common Causes
		- DKA
		- Lactic acidosis
		- Renal failure
		- Methanol poisoning
		- Ethylene glycol poisoning
		- Salicylate poisoning
	- Albumin Correction
		- Low albumin lowers AG
		- Corrected AG
			- AG + [2.5 × (4 − albumin g/dL)]

- Mixed Acid-Base Disorders
	- Suspect When
		- pH near normal despite major abnormalities
		- Compensation exceeds expected values
		- Compensation less than expected values
		- Both PaCO₂ and HCO₃⁻ markedly abnormal
	- Examples
		- COPD + vomiting
			- Respiratory acidosis + metabolic alkalosis
		- Sepsis + renal failure
			- Respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis
		- Salicylate poisoning
			- Respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis
		- Cardiac arrest
			- Respiratory acidosis + metabolic acidosis

- Oxygenation Assessment
	- PaO₂
		- Normal
			- 80–100 mmHg
		- Mild hypoxaemia
			- 60–79 mmHg
		- Moderate hypoxaemia
			- 40–59 mmHg
		- Severe hypoxaemia
			- <40 mmHg
	- Causes of Hypoxaemia
		- V/Q mismatch
		- Shunt
		- Diffusion defect
		- Hypoventilation
		- Low inspired oxygen

- A–a Gradient
	- Formula
		- A–a Gradient = PAO₂ − PaO₂
	- Approximate Normal
		- (Age ÷ 4) + 4
	- Increased A–a Gradient
		- Pulmonary embolism
		- Pneumonia
		- Pulmonary oedema
		- ARDS
		- Interstitial lung disease
	- Normal A–a Gradient
		- Hypoventilation
		- High altitude

- Classic AMC ABG Patterns

	- COPD Exacerbation
		- pH ↓
		- PaCO₂ ↑
		- HCO₃⁻ mildly ↑
		- Respiratory acidosis

	- Stable COPD
		- Near-normal pH
		- PaCO₂ ↑
		- HCO₃⁻ ↑
		- Chronic compensated respiratory acidosis

	- Pulmonary Embolism
		- pH ↑
		- PaCO₂ ↓
		- PaO₂ ↓
		- Respiratory alkalosis

	- Severe Asthma
		- Early
			- Respiratory alkalosis
		- Late/fatigue
			- Respiratory acidosis
			- Life-threatening sign

	- Diabetic Ketoacidosis
		- pH ↓
		- HCO₃⁻ ↓
		- PaCO₂ ↓
		- High AG metabolic acidosis

	- Vomiting
		- pH ↑
		- HCO₃⁻ ↑
		- Metabolic alkalosis

	- Opioid Overdose
		- pH ↓
		- PaCO₂ ↑
		- Respiratory acidosis

	- Salicylate Poisoning
		- Early
			- Respiratory alkalosis
		- Late
			- Mixed respiratory alkalosis
			- High AG metabolic acidosis

	- Septic Shock
		- Early
			- Respiratory alkalosis
		- Late
			- Lactic acidosis
			- High AG metabolic acidosis

- AMC Exam High-Yield Pearls
	- Pulmonary embolism
		- Most common ABG
			- Respiratory alkalosis + hypoxaemia
	- Severe asthma
		- Normal PaCO₂ is dangerous
		- Rising PaCO₂ suggests impending respiratory failure
	- DKA
		- High AG metabolic acidosis
		- Low PaCO₂ due to compensation
	- Diarrhoea
		- Normal AG metabolic acidosis
	- Vomiting
		- Metabolic alkalosis
	- COPD
		- Chronic respiratory acidosis with raised HCO₃⁻
	- Salicylate toxicity
		- Mixed disorder is classic exam finding
	- Always assess expected compensation
		- Inappropriate compensation = mixed disorder

- 30-Second ABG Exam Method
	- Check pH
		- Acidosis or alkalosis
	- Check PaCO₂
		- Respiratory cause?
	- Check HCO₃⁻
		- Metabolic cause?
	- Assess compensation
	- Calculate anion gap if acidosis present
	- Check PaO₂
	- Consider mixed disorders
	- Match to clinical scenario
