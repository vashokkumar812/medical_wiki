- minor burn
	- superficial burn
		- <10% TBSA in adults
		- <5% TBSA in children
	- definition not absolute
		- depends on
			- age
			- comorbidities
			- social factors
			- burn location
	- suitable for GP management
		- small superficial burns expected to heal spontaneously
	- many burns <10% TBSA may still need hospital/outpatient care
		- dressing requirements
		- pain management

- always consider
	- non-accidental injury
		- especially children and elderly

- acute management principles
	- first aid
	- analgesia
	- assess burn size + depth
	- manage factors affecting healing
		- rest
		- elevation
	- dressings
	- blister management/debridement
	- review

- first aid
	- gold standard
		- cool running water (~15°C)
		- for at least 20 min
	- apply ASAP
	- beneficial
		- at least within 1 hour
		- possibly longer
	- benefits
		- ↓ pain
		- ↓ inflammation
		- earlier healing
		- ↓ scarring
	- avoid
		- ice
	- avoid hypothermia
		- cool burn, warm patient
	- hydrogel dressings
		- Burnshield/Burnaid
		- temporary dressing after first aid
		- useful if running water unavailable

- analgesia
	- partial thickness burns are painful
	- pain relief
		- cooling
		- occlusive dressing
		- oral analgesia
	- may require admission if pain severe
	- elevation reduces
		- oedema
		- pain
		- stiffness

- burn size assessment
	- palm incl fingers
		- ~1% TBSA
	- larger burns
		- rule of nines (adults)
		- Lund and Browder chart (children)

- burn depth assessment
	- superficial partial thickness
		- moist
		- pink
		- brisk capillary refill
		- very painful/tender
	- deep/full thickness
		- dry
		- insensate
		- poorly perfused
	- mid-dermal burns
		- sluggish capillary refill
		- some sensation preserved
		- healing uncertain initially
		- reassess over days

- factors delaying healing
	- elderly
	- comorbid illness
	- oedema
	- infection
	- lower limb/foot burns
		- may worsen if not elevated

- oedema management
	- elevation
	- elastic tubular bandage over dressing if needed
	- benefits
		- ↓ pain
		- ↓ stiffness
		- improves healing

- infection
	- uncommon in small superficial burns
	- suspect if
		- wound deepens
		- delayed healing
	- prolonged healing
		- ↑ poor scarring
	- refer if unlikely to heal by 14 days

- dressings
	- moist wound healing preferred
		- faster healing
		- less pain
	- initial phase
		- absorptive dressing
			- paraffin gauze
			- Melolin
	- later phase
		- non-adherent occlusive dressing
			- Tegaderm
			- Opsite
			- Duoderm
			- Comfeel
	- review within 48 hours
		- reassess burn depth
		- change soaked dressing
	- healing phase
		- less frequent dressing changes

- silver dressings
	- antimicrobial
	- benefit in small burns not established
	- silver sulfadiazine
		- may delay healing in partial thickness burns

- contaminated wounds
	- eg river/dam water
	- management
		- wound cleaning
		- topical antimicrobial
		- tetanus prophylaxis
		- oral antibiotics if indicated

- blister management
	- hallmark of superficial partial thickness burns
	- small blisters <6 mm
		- usually leave intact + dress
	- large blisters
		- debride initially or at first review
		- allows wound assessment
	- aspiration
		- may reduce discomfort/pressure
	- ruptured blisters
		- debride loose skin + necrotic tissue
	- blister roof
		- may act as biological dressing
		- do not leave if contaminated
	- accurate depth assessment
		- usually requires debridement

- post-acute care
	- burns healing within 2 weeks
		- low hypertrophic scar risk
	- may still develop hyperpigmentation
		- especially dark-skinned patients

- post-healing care
	- avoid sun exposure during inflammatory phase
	- apply low-irritant sunscreen liberally
	- newly healed skin
		- fragile
		- blisters easily with minor trauma
	- small cystic/pimple-like lesions
		- common
		- usually resolve spontaneously
	- moisturiser
		- helps dryness after healing
	- if healed well and promptly
		- usually no ongoing scar management needed
	- persistent hand stiffness
		- often due to poorly controlled oedema
		- refer to hand therapist

- refer to burns unit if
	- severe burns
	- extensive burns
	- complex burns
	- deep partial thickness burns
	- full thickness burns
	- small deep burns
		- usually require excision + grafting

- key points
	- cool burn, warm patient
	- burn assessment predicts healing time
	- burns evolve over time
	- reassess at 24–48 hours
	- good acute care includes
		- analgesia
		- dressings
		- oedema control
	- burns not healed by 14–21 days
		- ↑ risk of poor scarring

# Burns (AMC MCQ)

- **Burn depth**
	- Superficial
		- Red, painful
		- No blisters
	- Superficial partial thickness
		- Blisters
		- Wet, pink, painful
	- Deep partial/full thickness
		- Pale/white
		- Less painful
		- Reduced sensation

- **First aid**
	- Cool running water
		- 20 min within 3 h
	- Remove clothing/jewellery
	- Avoid ice

- **Burn zones (Jackson)**
	- Coagulation
		- Irreversible damage
	- Stasis
		- Salvageable
	- Hyperaemia
		- Recovers spontaneously

- **Blister management**
	- Debridement preferred
		- Reduces infection
		- Improves dressing contact
		- Reduces pressure/pain

- **Initial dressing**
	- Nanocrystalline silver dressing
		- First 48 h
		- Antimicrobial

- **Dressings after 48 h**
	- Hydrocolloid
		- Low exudate
	- Foam
		- High exudate
	- Hydrogel
		- Dry/sloughy burns
	- Alginate
		- Highly exudative wounds

- **Aims of dressings**
	- Prevent infection
	- Moist wound healing
	- Reduce pain
	- Prevent burn progression

- **Burns requiring referral**
	- Full-thickness burns
	- Face/hands/genitals
	- Circumferential burns
	- Electrical/chemical burns
	- Large TBSA burns
	- Inhalation injury

- **Inhalation injury clues**
	- Facial burns
	- Soot in mouth/nose
	- Hoarseness
	- Stridor

- **Complications**
	- Infection
	- Hypertrophic scarring
	- Contractures

- **Scar risk**
	- Healing >21 days
		- ↑ hypertrophic scar risk

- **Post-healing care**
	- Moisturiser
	- SPF50 sun protection
	- Avoid prolonged sun for 2 years

- **AMC pearls**
	- Cool water first aid improves outcome
	- Ice worsens tissue injury
	- Deep burns may be painless
	- Circumferential burns can compromise circulation

# Chemical Burns (AMC MCQ)

- **Important principle**
	- Chemical burns continue causing damage until removed

- **Initial management**
	- PPE for clinician
	- Remove contaminated clothing
	- Brush off dry chemicals first
	- Copious water irrigation
		- Mainstay of treatment
		- Use warm water
		- Prolonged irrigation may be needed

- **Avoid**
	- Routine chemical neutralisation
		- May worsen burn via heat production

- **Assessment**
	- ABCDE approach
	- Assess burn depth
	- Reassess frequently
		- Burns may deepen over 48–72 h

- **Alkali burns**
	- More severe than acids
	- Cause liquefactive necrosis
	- Deep progressive injury

- **Acid burns**
	- Cause coagulative necrosis
	- Usually more superficial

- **Hydrofluoric acid burns**
	- Can cause severe systemic toxicity
	- Hypocalcaemia
	- Arrhythmias
	- Treatment
		- Calcium gluconate

- **Tar burns**
	- Deep thermal + chemical injury
	- Do not forcibly remove tar initially
	- Use oils/mineral oil for removal

- **White phosphorus**
	- Continues burning in air
	- Keep covered with wet dressing
	- Remove particles

- **Complications**
	- Infection
	- Hypothermia from prolonged lavage
	- Electrolyte abnormalities

- **Referral indications**
	- Deep/full-thickness burns
	- Large burns
	- Face/hands/genitals
	- Ocular burns
	- Inhalation injury
	- Hydrofluoric acid exposure

- **AMC pearls**
	- Water irrigation = first-line treatment
	- Alkali burns penetrate deeper than acids
	- Chemical burns may appear superficial initially
	- HF burns → hypocalcaemia + arrhythmias
