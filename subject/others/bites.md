# bee sting
- Insect/tick stings
	- Common insects
		- Bees
		- Wasps
		- Ants
		- Ticks

	- Single stings
		- Usually local pain only

	- Multiple stings
		- Severe pain
		- Widespread skin reaction
		- Facial/oral stings
			- Severe swelling
			- Airway obstruction
			- Respiratory distress
			- Urgent medical care required

	- Clinical features
		- Minor
			- Pain
			- Redness
			- Swelling

		- Severe
			- Anaphylaxis
				- Facial/tongue swelling
				- Dyspnoea
				- Dysphonia
				- Collapse
				- Generalised rash
				- Abdominal pain
				- Vomiting
			- Airway obstruction
				- Due to:
					- Anaphylaxis
					- Mouth/tongue stings

	- Bee stings
		- Leave venom sac behind
		- Remove immediately
			- Scrape sideways
				- Fingernail
				- Knife blade
		- Avoid squeezing venom sac

	- Tick bites
		- Usually asymptomatic/mild irritation

		- Important complications
			- Anaphylaxis
				- May occur without prior exposure
			- Neurotoxic paralysis
				- Rare
				- [[Ixodes]] paralysis tick
			- Mammalian red meat allergy

		- Management
			- Small ticks
				- Permethrin cream
			- Adult ticks
				- Freeze in place
					- Ether-containing spray
			- Avoid treatment near
				- Eyes
				- Genitals
				- Ear canal

			- Remote settings
				- History of tick anaphylaxis
					- Use [[adrenaline]] auto-injector
				- No anaphylaxis history
					- Remove with fine forceps
					- Grasp close to skin
					- Do not squeeze tick

			- Avoid cold packs
				- May trigger allergic reaction
- management
	- First aid
		- If anaphylaxis
			- Follow anaphylaxis protocol
		- Local treatment
			- Ice/cold compress
				- Except tick bites
			- 20% aluminium sulphate
				- Stingose
			- OR methylated spirits
			- Rest/elevate limb

	- Venom immunotherapy
		- Types
			- Honey bee
			- Wasp
		- Notes
			- Honey bee uses pure venom antigen
			- [[Vespula]] requires specific hyposensitisation
			- No cross-allergy between
				- Honey bee
				- Yellow jacket wasp
				- Paper wasp
		- Indications
			- Severe sting reaction with asthma
			- ≥3 progressively severe reactions
			- Occupational exposure with severe reactions
			- Positive allergy testing
				- Venom-specific IgE
				- Positive skin prick test

# centipede and scorpion bites
- Clinical features
	- Main symptom
		- Pain
			- Severe
			- Prolonged

- Treatment
	- First aid
		- Apply local heat
			- Example
				- Hot water with ammonia
					- Household bleach
		- Clean site
		- Local anaesthetic
			- Example
				- 1–2 mL of 1% lignocaine infiltrated around site
		- Consider opioids for pain
			- Morphine
			- Fentanyl
		- Check tetanus immunisation status

- **Organisms**
	- Polymicrobial
	- *Eikenella corrodens*
	- Streptococci
	- Anaerobes

- **Complications**
	- Cellulitis
	- Abscess
	- Septic arthritis
	- Osteomyelitis

- **Assessment**
	- Neurovascular exam
	- Tendon/joint involvement
	- X-ray if fight bite/foreign body

- **Management**
	- Irrigation + debridement
	- Leave wound open
	- Elevation

- **Antibiotics**
	- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate**
	- IV antibiotics if severe

- **Tetanus**
	- Update prophylaxis

- **Indications for surgery**
	- Joint involvement
	- Tendon injury
	- Abscess
	- Deep hand infection

- **AMC pearls**
	- Fight bites need aggressive management
	- Do not primarily close infected hand bites

- Snake bite
	- Bite site
		- Usually leg
		- Often painless

	- Bite marks
		- May be absent
		- Paired fang marks
		- Single puncture mark
		- Scratch mark
		- Local redness/bruising uncommon in Australian snake bite

	- Clinical features
		- Early symptoms
			- Nausea/vomiting
				- Reliable early sign
			- Abdominal pain
			- Sweating
			- Severe headache
			- Dizziness

		- Neurotoxic features
			- Blurred/double vision
			- Ptosis
			- Dysarthria
			- Dysphagia
			- Dyspnoea
			- Limb weakness
			- Paralysis
			- Respiratory weakness/arrest
			- Ophthalmoplegia

		- Lymphatic features
			- Tender lymphadenopathy
				- Groin
				- Axilla of bitten limb

		- Haematological features
			- Coagulopathy
			- Haematuria
			- Bleeding
			- Muscle damage

		- Greatest dangers
			- Respiratory failure
			- Catastrophic bleeding
			- Collapse with cardiac arrest
				- Most common cause of death
				- Usually within 10–60 min
				- Often prehospital
				- Requires immediate CPR

		- Brown snake bite
			- Initial collapse/confusion
			- Followed by apparent recovery
			- May be only sign of envenomation

	- Investigations
		- Monitor for coagulopathy
			- Haemoptysis
			- Bleeding wounds
			- Bleeding IV sites

		- Whole blood clotting time
			- Plain glass tube
			- Normal
				- <5–8 min
			- Abnormal
				- >15 min

		- Venom detection kit
			- Best sample
				- Wound swab
			- Urine
				- Less reliable
				- Delayed positivity
			- Swab before washing wound

	- First aid
		- Keep patient still/reassured
		- Pressure immobilisation bandage
		- Splint limb

		- Do not
			- Wash wound
			- Cut/suck wound
			- Apply ice
			- Use arterial tourniquet
			- Manipulate wound
			- Give alcohol/stimulants

		- Bandaging
			- Apply immediately
			- Firm like sprained ankle bandage
			- Use
				- 15 cm elastic/crepe bandage
			- Extend above bite
				- Eg ankle → knee
			- Purpose
				- Reduce lymphatic venom spread

		- If unresponsive + apnoeic
			- CPR immediately
		- If unconscious but breathing
			- Recovery position/supportive care
		- No venom transmission risk during CPR

		- Snake identification
			- Do not kill/catch snake
			- If safe
				- Photograph snake

	- Hospital management
		- IV normal saline
		- Monitor
			- Vitals
			- Neurology
			- Respiration
			- Coagulation
		- Basic life support if needed

	- Antivenom
		- Indications
			- Systemic symptoms
			- Sudden collapse
			- Seizures
			- Cardiac arrest
			- Ptosis/ophthalmoplegia
			- Bleeding/coagulopathy
			- Elevated INR/APTT
			- CK >100
			- Leukocytosis
			- Positive venom test
			- Muscle damage

		- Administration
			- Usually 1 vial
			- Dilute
				- 1:10 normal saline
				- 1:5 in children/fluid restriction
			- Infuse over 30 min

		- During infusion
			- Keep ready
				- [[adrenaline]]
				- Antihistamines
				- Oxygen
				- Steroids

			- Avoid [[adrenaline]]
				- Brown snake bite with coagulopathy

		- Bandage removal
			- Only under medical supervision
