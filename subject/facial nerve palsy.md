# bell's palsy

- Definition
	- Acute unilateral lower motor neuron (LMN) facial nerve palsy
- Epidemiology
	- Most common cause of acute unilateral LMN facial paralysis
- Prognosis
	- Complete recovery within 3 months in most patients
	- Some develop permanent weakness, synkinesis and contractures
- Aetiology
	- Exact cause unknown
	- Suspected viral reactivation
		- Herpes simplex virus (HSV)
		- Varicella zoster virus (VZV)
	- Facial nerve inflammation and oedema
	- Compression within narrow facial canal
- Risk Factors
	- Pregnancy
	- Severe pre-eclampsia
	- Obesity
	- Hypertension
	- Diabetes mellitus
	- Recent upper respiratory tract infection
		- Influenza
- pathophysiology
	- Facial Nerve Function
		- Muscles of facial expression
		- Lacrimal glands
		- Salivary glands
		- Stapedius muscle
	- Taste
		- Anterior two-thirds of tongue
	- Sensation
		- Posterior ear canal
		- Tympanic membrane
- Red Flag Symptoms
	- Diplopia
	- Dysphagia
	- True facial numbness
	- Dizziness
	- Progressive symptoms over weeks
	- Recurrent palsy
	- New neurological deficits
	- Facial diplegia
- diagnosis
	- Diagnosis of exclusion
	- Acute unilateral facial weakness
		- Rapid progression over minutes to hours
		- sudden onset
	- Forehead involvement
	- Inability to close eye
	- Drooping mouth
	- Dry eye
	- Dry mouth
	- Hyperacusis
	- Taste disturbance
	- Aural fullness
	- UMN vs LMN Facial Palsy
		- LMN (Bell's palsy)
			- Entire half of face affected
			- Forehead involved
			- Inability to wrinkle forehead
		- UMN (Stroke)
			- Forehead spared
			- Lower face predominantly affected
- staging
	- House–Brackmann Grading
		- Grade I
			- Normal
		- Grade II
			- Mild dysfunction
		- Grade III
			- Moderate dysfunction
		- Grade IV
			- Moderately severe dysfunction
		- Grade V
			- Severe dysfunction
		- Grade VI
			- Complete paralysis
- Investigations
	- Typical Bell's palsy
		- Usually clinical diagnosis
		- No routine imaging required
	- Indications for Imaging
		- Progressive symptoms
		- Atypical features
		- Failure to improve
		- Recurrent palsy
		- Suspicion of malignancy
	- MRI
		- Brainstem
		- Cerebellopontine angle
		- Parotid gland
		- Facial nerve course
	- CT Temporal Bone
		- Intratemporal facial nerve
		- Temporal bone pathology
- Management
	- Corticosteroids
		- Mainstay of treatment
		- Start within 72 hours
		- Prednisolone 50 mg daily for 10 days
		- OR
		- Prednisone 60 mg daily for 5 days
			- Then taper over 5 days
		- Benefits
			- Improved recovery
			- Reduced residual dysfunction
	- Antivirals
		- Not effective alone
		- May provide small additional benefit when combined with steroids
		- Shared decision-making recommended
		- Valacyclovir
			- 1000 mg TDS
		- Acyclovir
			- 400 mg five times daily
		- Adverse effects
			- Nausea
			- Vomiting
			- Diarrhoea
	- Eye Protection
		- Essential if incomplete eye closure
		- Lubricating eye drops
		- Lubricating ointment
		- Tape eyelid closed at night
		- Sunglasses outdoors
		- Urgent review if
			- Eye pain
			- Redness
			- Visual change

- Complications

	- Persistent facial weakness

	- Synkinesis

	- Facial contractures

	- Exposure keratitis

	- Corneal ulceration

	- Vision loss

	- Depression

	- Reduced quality of life

- Prognostic Factors

	- Better prognosis

		- Early improvement

		- Early steroid therapy

		- Partial paralysis

	- Worse prognosis

		- Complete paralysis

		- Delayed recovery

		- Progressive symptoms

		- Underlying malignancy
