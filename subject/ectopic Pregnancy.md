- ectopic pregnancy
	- diagnosis
		- clinical features
			- may or may not be present
			- amenorrhoea
			- irregular vaginal bleeding / spotting
			- abdominal pain
			- shoulder tip pain
			- cervical motion tenderness
			- tachycardia
			- hypotension
			- palpable adnexal mass (~50%)

		- investigations
			- positive β-hCG
			- no intrauterine pregnancy on ultrasound

- management overview
	- expectant
		- stable + low/falling β-hCG

	- medical
		- stable + suitable for methotrexate

	- surgical
		- unstable
		- rupture
		- contraindications

- expectant management
	- indications
		- haemodynamically stable
		- no rupture
		- β-hCG <1500 IU/L and falling
		- minimal/no pelvic fluid
		- tubal mass <3 cm
		- pain-free
		- reliable follow-up

	- caution
		- avoid if follow-up uncertain

	- follow-up
		- β-hCG every 48 h × 8 days
		- then weekly until negative
		- ultrasound if indicated

	- advice
		- avoid conception until resolution

- medical management ([[Methotrexate]])
	- indications
		- haemodynamically stable
		- no rupture
		- no active bleeding
		- normal FBE and LFTs

	- contraindications
		- methotrexate allergy
		- unreliable follow-up / remote location
		- significant comorbidity
		- breastfeeding
		- β-hCG >5000 IU/L
		- mass >3 cm
		- fetal cardiac activity
		- unable to transfuse

	- regimen
		- β-hCG ≤3000 IU/L
			- IM methotrexate

		- β-hCG >3000 IU/L
			- IV regimen

	- follow-up
		- serial β-hCG (protocol-based)
		- ultrasound at 1 week
			- then as needed

		- fetal cardiac activity
			- refer MFM

	- counselling
		- avoid conception for 4 months

- surgical management
	- indications
		- haemodynamic instability
		- suspected/confirmed rupture
		- persistent heavy bleeding
		- heterotopic pregnancy
		- failure/contraindication to other methods

	- procedure
		- laparoscopy preferred

	- laparotomy if
		- unstable
		- technically difficult

- post-treatment follow-up
	- after salpingostomy
		- weekly β-hCG until negative

	- after salpingectomy
		- urine β-hCG at 3 weeks

	- general
		- GP review at 14 days
		- ultrasound if indicated

	- conception interval
		- usually 0–3 months
		- uncertain evidence

- additional care considerations
	- during expectant/medical management
		- risk of rupture with
			- intercourse
			- pelvic exam

	- change management if
		- β-hCG not falling
		- persistent pain/bleeding
		- rupture
		- patient preference

- general care
	- review histopathology
	- give anti-D if indicated
	- provide analgesia
	- communicate with GP/other providers

- next pregnancy
	- early ultrasound at 5–6 weeks
