- First antenatal visit
  - Should occur by 10 weeks gestation
- Low-risk pregnancy
	- No routine ultrasound required before 12 weeks
	- Dating ultrasound if gestational age uncertain
- Nulliparous woman without complications
	- Approximately 10 antenatal visits

- Clinical assessment
  - Detailed medical history
  - Physical examination
  - Measure height
  - Measure weight
  - Calculate BMI

- Routine investigations at booking visit

  - Full blood examination (FBE)
    - Assess haemoglobin
      - Anaemia
    - Assess MCV
      - Iron deficiency
      - Thalassaemia
    - Assess platelet count
      - Thrombocytopenia

  - Blood group and antibody screen
    - Blood group only needs documenting once
    - Antibody screen required every pregnancy

  - Rubella immunity
    - Check rubella antibody titre in every pregnancy
    - Immunity may decline over time

  - Syphilis screening
    - Recommended for all pregnant women
    - Preferred tests
      - TPHA
      - TPPA
    - Less sensitive tests
      - RPR
      - VDRL

  - Midstream urine (MSU)
    - Urinalysis
    - Urine culture
    - Detect asymptomatic bacteriuria

  - HIV screening
    - Offer to all pregnant women
    - Pre-test counselling required

  - Hepatitis B screening
    - Offer to all pregnant women
    - If positive
      - HBV DNA
      - HBeAg status
      - Liver function tests
      - Specialist referral

  - Hepatitis C screening
    - Risk-based or universal depending on jurisdiction
    - If antibody positive
      - HCV RNA PCR
      - Liver function tests
      - Specialist referral

- STI screening

  - Chlamydia
  - Gonorrhoea
  - Consider if
    - Age <30 years
    - Known risk factors
    - High prevalence setting
  - Repeat testing may be required in high-risk areas
  - Untreated infection associated with
    - Preterm birth
    - Maternal complications
    - Neonatal complications

- Varicella
  - Ask about previous infection
    - Chickenpox
    - Shingles
  - If non-immune
    - Avoid exposure
    - Report exposure urgently

- Cervical screening
  - Perform if due
  - Safe during pregnancy
  - Follow National Cervical Screening Program guidelines

- Screening for fetal chromosomal conditions
  - Discuss available screening options
  - Offer according to guidelines

- Carrier screening
  - Discuss genetic carrier screening options

- Additional tests that may be considered

  - Haemoglobinopathy screening
    - Screen all women with MCV
    - Low MCV requires
      - Ferritin
      - Haemoglobin electrophoresis (HbEPG)
      - HPLC
    - Consider alpha-thalassaemia DNA testing if indicated
    - Partner testing required for fetal risk assessment

- Tests NOT recommended routinely

  - Vitamin D
    - Do not routinely screen

  - Cytomegalovirus (CMV)
    - Do not routinely screen
    - Consider CMV IgG in women with frequent contact with young children

  - Toxoplasmosis
    - Do not routinely screen

  - Thyroid function (TSH)
    - Do not routinely screen
    - Consider only in high-risk women

- Mental health assessment
  - Routine part of antenatal care
  - EPDS commonly used in Australia
  - Consider psychosocial assessment
    - ANRQ

- Family violence screening
  - Routine component of antenatal care
  - Must be undertaken when woman is alone
  - Use validated local screening tool
  - Refer women identified at risk


- General advice in early pregnancy
  - Avoid teratogens
    - Certain medications
    - Alcohol
    - High-dose ionising radiation (X-rays)
  - Lifestyle advice
    - Smoking cessation
    - Avoid alcohol
    - Avoid recreational drugs
    - Healthy diet
    - Food safety precautions
    - Appropriate gestational weight gain according to BMI
    - Exercise during pregnancy
    - Work precautions
    - Travel precautions
  - Vaccinations
    - Influenza vaccine
    - Pertussis (dTpa) vaccine
  - Vitamin and mineral supplementation
  - Discuss model of care
    - Antenatal visit schedule
    - Birth location
    - Pregnancy and delivery costs
  - Prevention of infections
    - CMV
    - Other teratogenic infections
  - Antenatal education options

- Subsequent antenatal visits
  - Objectives
    - Preventive care
    - Monitor maternal wellbeing
    - Monitor fetal wellbeing
    - Identify pregnancy complications early
    - Prepare for labour and postpartum period
    - Provide ongoing pregnancy advice
  - Sleep advice
    - From 28 weeks gestation
    - Advise sleeping on side
  - By 36 weeks
    - Discuss labour and birth plan
      - Mode of delivery
      - Analgesia options

- Clinical assessment at every visit
  - Maternal wellbeing
  - Blood pressure
  - Symptoms of pregnancy complications
  - Fetal wellbeing assessment

- Fetal growth assessment
  - Symphysis-fundal height (SFH)
    - Measure routinely
    - Record serially
    - Plot on customised growth chart
  - Abnormal growth concerns
    - Investigate with ultrasound
  - Women at risk of fetal growth restriction
    - Serial growth ultrasounds

- Routine obstetric ultrasound

  - First-trimester ultrasound
    - Ideally 12–13+6 weeks
    - Confirm viability
    - Accurate dating
    - Determine number of fetuses
    - Assess gross fetal anatomy
    - Nuchal translucency (NT) if requested

  - Morphology scan
    - Around 20 weeks
    - Assess fetal anatomy
    - Placental localisation

  - Additional scans
    - As clinically indicated

- Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)
  - Universal screening recommended
  - Usually screened at 24–28 weeks

- Group B Streptococcus (GBS)
  - Follow local screening and management guidelines

- Rh antibody screening
  - Rh-negative women
    - Repeat antibody screen at approximately 28 weeks
  - Rh-positive women
    - Repeat testing based on clinical discretion

- Full blood examination at 28 weeks
  - Repeat haemoglobin
  - Repeat platelet count
  - Investigate if
    - Anaemia detected
    - Thrombocytopenia detected

- Repeat infectious disease screening

  - Consider at 28–32 weeks in high-risk women
    - Syphilis
    - HIV
    - Hepatitis B
    - Hepatitis C

  - Syphilis in high-risk or high-prevalence settings
    - Booking visit
    - 28 weeks
    - 36 weeks
    - At birth
    - 6 weeks postpartum

  - Reassess infection risk at each visit

- Vaccination during pregnancy

  - Influenza vaccine
    - Recommended during pregnancy

  - COVID-19 vaccination
    - Recommended during pregnancy

  - Pertussis vaccine (dTpa)
    - Recommended every pregnancy
    - Single dose
    - Best given between 20–32 weeks
    - Third trimester traditionally recommended

- Post-dates pregnancy

  - After 41 weeks
    - Consider fetal surveillance
    - Advise monitoring fetal movements
    - Reduced fetal movements require assessment

  - Surveillance options
    - Twice-weekly CTG
    - Ultrasound
      - Amniotic fluid assessment

  - ≥42 weeks
    - Frequent fetal wellbeing assessment strongly recommended
    - Assess liquor volume
    - Re-evaluate risks and benefits of continuing pregnancy
