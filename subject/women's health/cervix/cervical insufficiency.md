## definition
- Cervical length (CL)
	- Best predictor
		- Short cervix → spontaneous Preterm birth


## Screening
- Gold standard
	- Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS)
- Timing
	- Mid-trimester morphology scan (18–22 weeks)
- Low-risk singleton pregnancy
	- transabdominal ultrasound
		- CL ≥ 35 mm
			- No TVUS required
		- CL <35 mm
			- do TVUS
		- Cervix poorly visualised
			- Perform TVUS
- High-risk pregnancy
	- Direct TVUS cervical length surveillance

## management
- Cervical length management
	- CL >25 mm
		- Routine antenatal care
	- CL 20–25 mm
		- Repeat TVUS in 1–2 weeks
		- Consider vaginal progesterone
	- CL <20 mm
		- Vaginal progesterone
			- 200 mg nocte
			- Continue until 37 weeks
	- CL <10 mm
		- Consider individualised cerclage

- Cervical surveillance
	- Continue until viability
	- No benefit after 26 weeks

- Vaginal progesterone
	- Indication
		- Short cervix (<20 mm)
	- Benefits
		- Reduces spontaneous PTB
		- Improves neonatal outcomes
	- Safe in pregnancy
	- Minor adverse effects
		- Vaginal irritation
		- Headache
		- Dizziness

- Cervical cerclage
	- Elective
		- High-risk women
			- Previous PTB
			- Cervical insufficiency
			- Previous cervical surgery
		- Best before 23 weeks
	- Rescue
		- Pre-viable cervical dilatation
		- Progressive shortening despite progesterone
	- Not routinely recommended
		- Incidental short cervix in otherwise low-risk pregnancy
