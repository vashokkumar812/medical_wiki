- Definition
	- Absence of menstruation
- Classification
	- Primary amenorrhoea
		- No menarche by age 13 years with absent breast development
		- No menarche by age 15 years despite normal growth and breast development
		- No menarche within 5 years of breast development if breast development occurred before age 10 years
	- Secondary amenorrhoea
		- Absence of menstruation >3 months in previously regular cycles
		- Absence of menstruation >6 months in previously irregular cycles
- variants
	- Oligomenorrhoea
		- Fewer than 9 menstrual cycles per year
		- Requires assessment if >2 years after menarche

- pathophysiology

| Feature                 | Hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism                           | Hypergonadotrophic hypogonadism                            |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary defect          | Hypothalamic or pituitary disorder                       | Primary ovarian disorder                                   |
| LH                      | ↓                                                        | ↑                                                          |
| FSH                     | ↓                                                        | ↑                                                          |
| Ovarian stimulation     | ↓                                                        | Impaired despite ↑ gonadotrophins                          |

- Common Causes of Primary Amenorrhoea
	- Constitutional delay of puberty
		- Most common cause
		- family history present
	- Hypothalamic amenorrhoea
		- Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea
			- Stress
			- Excessive exercise
			- Systemic illness
			- Eating disorders
			- Very low body weight
		- Isolated GnRH deficiency
			- Kallmann syndrome
				- Delayed puberty + anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism
	- Pituitary disorders
		- Hypopituitarism
		- Prolactinoma
	- Ovarian causes
		- Gonadal dysgenesis
			- Streak gonads
		- Premature ovarian insufficiency
		- turner syndrome
			- Primary amenorrhoea + short stature + streak ovaries + webbed neck + 45,XO
	- Outflow tract abnormalities
		- Müllerian agenesis
			- Absent uterus and/or vagina
		- Imperforate hymen
			- Primary amenorrhoea + cyclical pelvic pain + bulging bluish hymenal membrane (haematocolpos)
	- Disorders of androgen action
		- Androgen insensitivity syndrome
			- Primary amenorrhoea + normal breast development + sparse/absent pubic hair + absent uterus + 46,XY
		- 5-alpha reductase deficiency
			- 46,XY + undervirilised external genitalia at birth + virilisation at puberty + normal male internal genitalia
	- Less common causes
		- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
		- Nonclassical congenital adrenal hyperplasia

- Secondary amenorrhoea
	- Definition
		- Absence of menstruation >3 months in females with previously regular cycles
		- Absence of menstruation >6 months in females with previously irregular cycles
	- Common causes
		- Pregnancy
		- Perimenopause (age ≥45 years)
		- Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)
		- Hypothalamic amenorrhoea
			- Stress
			- Excessive exercise
			- Systemic illness
			- Eating disorders
			- Very low body weight
		- Pituitary tumours causing hyperprolactinaemia
		- Hypopituitarism
			- Pituitary tumours
			- Iron storage disorders
		- Premature ovarian insufficiency
		- Early menopause
		- Intrauterine adhesions
			- Asherman syndrome
		- Medication-induced
			- Hypothalamic/pituitary suppression
				- Opioids
				- Corticosteroids
				- Combined oral contraceptive pill
				- Depot medroxyprogesterone
				- Goserelin
			- Hyperprolactinaemia
				- Antipsychotics
				- Metoclopramide
				- Domperidone
				- Verapamil
				- Ranitidine
				- SSRIs
				- Tricyclic antidepressants
	- Other causes
		- Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
		- Hyperthyroidism
		- Hypothyroidism
	- Hormonal pattern
		- Elevated FSH + Elevated LH + Low estradiol
			- Premature ovarian insufficiency
			- Hypergonadotrophic hypogonadism
- Transvaginal ultrasound assess
	- Follicle count assessment
	- Premature ovarian insufficiency
	- Suspected PCOS
	- Transabdominal ultrasound is an alternative if TVS is unavailable or declined
- NB3: Hormonal investigations may need repeating after 4–6 weeks to clarify or confirm diagnosis
- NB4: Investigate androgen excess if hyperandrogenism suspected
	- PCOS
	- Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
- NB5: Ultrasound may show
	- Polycystic ovaries
	- Ovarian tumours
	- Structural abnormalities associated with some causes of premature ovarian insufficiency
- NB6: Premature ovarian insufficiency
	- Has multiple causes
	- Includes Turner syndrome



- Treatment of amenorrhoea
	- Primary amenorrhoea
		- specialist referral
- Estrogen therapy
	- indications
		- Hypothalamic disorders
		- Pituitary disorders
		- Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea
		- premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)
- Bone health
	- in prolonged secondary amenorrhoea
		- Perform bone mineral density (BMD) assessment 
	- Long-term estrogen deficiency causes bone loss
	- Estrogen therapy partially protects bone density
- Functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea
	- Weight gain required
	- Improved nutrition required
	- Correction of energy imbalance required
- Cardiovascular health
	- Cardiovascular risk increased in
		- Premature ovarian insufficiency
		- Other estrogen-deficient states

# Turner syndrome
- clinical features
	- Primary amenorrhoea ( sometimes econdary )
	- Short stature
	- Absent breast development
- complications
	- Aortic dilatation
	- Aortic dissection
	- Hypertension
- management
	- during childhood ==> Growth hormone therapy
	- Estrogen replacement	under specialist supervision required
	- Pubertal induction in Turner syndrome
		- increase estrogen dose x 3 years
			- Give MHT
		- cyclical progestogen indications
			- When breakthrough bleeding occurs
			- Or after 2 years of estrogen therapy
		- if withdrawal bleeding
			- continuous progestogen 
		- Continue hormone therapy until at least normal menopausal age (~51 years) unless contraindicated
	- if hypertension
		- avoid
		- Ethinylestradiol-containing COC
		- vaginal ring

# Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI)
- **Definition**
	- Decreased ovarian function before the age of 40 years
	- Irrespective of the cause
- **Epidemiology**
	- Affects approximately:
		- 1% of women before 40 years
- **Aetiology**
	- **Idiopathic**
		- Most common cause
	- **Genetic** (~10%)
		- Monosomy X
			- Turner syndrome
		- Fragile X premutation
	- **Autoimmune** (~20%)
		- Coeliac disease
		- Type 1 diabetes mellitus
		- Addison disease
	- **Iatrogenic**
		- Ovarian surgery
		- Chemotherapy
		- Radiotherapy
	- **Infectious**
		- Mumps
		- Tuberculosis
		- Malaria
		- Shigella
	- **Metabolic**
		- Galactosaemia
- **Prognosis**
	- If untreated:
		- Osteoporosis
		- Cardiovascular disease
		- Cognitive impairment
		- Premature mortality
- **Diagnosis**
	- **Investigations**
		- FSH
			- Elevated
		- Estradiol
			- Low
- **Management**
	- **Menopausal Symptoms (Moderate–Severe)**
		- Assess uterine status
			- **Uterus absent**
				- Low-dose oestrogen-only therapy
				- Review after 6 months
					- Symptoms controlled
						- Continue therapy
					- Symptoms not controlled
						- Refer to specialist
			- **Uterus present**
				- Low-dose oestrogen + progestogen
					- Or SERM
				- Continue MHT until median age of natural menopause
		- Options if contraception required
			- Combined oral contraceptive (COC)
			- Vaginal contraceptive ring
		- Options if contraception not required
			- Combined estrogen-progestogen menopausal hormone therapy (MHT)
			- Medium- to high-dose estrogen preparations
		- Important counselling
			- COC, vaginal ring and MHT may mask return of spontaneous ovulation
			- MHT does not provide contraception
	- **Vaginal Dryness (Oestrogen Deficiency)**
		- Topical vaginal oestrogen
	- **Specialist Referral**
		- Symptoms not controlled with MHT
		- Severe symptoms with contraindications to MHT
		- MHT adverse effects not corrected by routine measures
