- Pruritus and rash in pregnancy
  - Consider
    - Pregnancy-specific dermatoses
    - Non-pregnancy-specific causes

- Pregnancy-specific dermatoses
  - Polymorphic eruption of pregnancy (PEP/PUPPP)
  - Pemphigoid gestationis
  - Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP)
  - Atopic eruption of pregnancy (AEP)

- High-yield AMC concept
  - Conditions associated with fetal risk
    - Pemphigoid gestationis
    - Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP)
    - Pustular psoriasis of pregnancy (PPP)
  - Conditions without fetal risk
    - PEP/PUPPP
    - AEP

- Polymorphic eruption of pregnancy (PEP/PUPPP)
  - Most common in
    - Primigravida
    - Third trimester
  - Associated with
    - Multiple pregnancy
    - Excessive maternal weight gain
    - Rapid abdominal distension
  - Clinical features
    - Intensely pruritic urticarial papules
    - Coalesce into plaques
    - Begin in abdominal striae
    - Spread to limbs
    - Face usually spared
    - Palms and soles usually spared
    - Periumbilical region spared
  - Diagnosis
    - Clinical
    - Biopsy rarely required
    - Direct immunofluorescence negative
  - Treatment
    - Topical corticosteroids
    - Antihistamines
    - Severe disease
      - Prednisolone 40–60 mg/day
  - Prognosis
    - Resolves after delivery
    - No fetal risk
    - Usually does not recur

- Pemphigoid gestationis
  - Rare autoimmune blistering disorder
  - Associated autoimmune diseases
    - Graves disease
    - Vitiligo
    - Pernicious anaemia
  - Usually presents
    - Late pregnancy
    - May occur earlier
  - Clinical features
    - Intense pruritus
    - Papules
    - Plaques
    - Vesicles
    - Bullae
    - Starts on abdomen
    - Umbilical involvement present
    - Face and mucosa usually spared
  - Key distinguishing feature from PEP
    - Periumbilical involvement
  - Diagnosis
    - Skin biopsy
    - Direct immunofluorescence
      - Linear C3 ± IgG along basement membrane
  - Treatment
    - Topical corticosteroids (mild)
    - Prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day (moderate/severe)
    - Dermatology and obstetric involvement
  - Fetal risks
    - Prematurity
    - Small-for-gestational-age infant
    - Stillbirth
  - Recurs in future pregnancies

- Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP)
  - Most important feature
    - Pruritus occurs before rash
  - No primary skin lesions
  - Skin findings due to scratching
    - Excoriations
    - Erosions
    - Prurigo nodules
  - Usually occurs
    - 2nd trimester
    - 3rd trimester
  - Clinical features
    - Sudden onset severe generalised itch
    - Palms and soles commonly affected
  - Diagnosis
    - Elevated serum bile acids
    - Mildly elevated ALT/AST
  - Treatment
    - Ursodeoxycholic acid
      - 15 mg/kg/day
      - Or 1 g/day
  - Fetal risks
    - Prematurity
    - Fetal distress
    - Stillbirth
  - Management
    - Maternal-fetal medicine referral
    - Delivery often by 38 weeks
  - Recurs in future pregnancies

- Atopic eruption of pregnancy (AEP)
  - Most common dermatosis of pregnancy
  - Includes
    - Eczema in pregnancy
    - Prurigo of pregnancy
    - Pruritic folliculitis of pregnancy
  - Usually presents
    - 1st trimester
    - 2nd trimester
  - No maternal or fetal risks

- Eczema in pregnancy
  - Most common subtype of AEP
  - May be
    - New-onset eczema
    - Exacerbation of pre-existing eczema
  - Distribution
    - Flexures
    - Trunk
    - Limbs

- Prurigo of pregnancy
  - Incidence
    - ~1 in 300 pregnancies
  - Clinical features
    - Excoriated papules
    - Nodules
    - Trunk
    - Extensor surfaces
  - Differential
    - ICP
  - Distinguishing point
    - Rash occurs first
    - In ICP, itch occurs first

- Pruritic folliculitis of pregnancy
  - Rare
  - Incidence
    - ~1 in 3000 pregnancies
  - Clinical features
    - Follicular papules
    - Pustules
  - Exclude
    - Bacterial folliculitis
    - Fungal folliculitis

- AEP diagnosis
  - Clinical diagnosis
  - Histology non-specific
  - Direct immunofluorescence negative
  - IgE may be elevated

- AEP treatment
  - Emollients
  - Topical corticosteroids
  - Antihistamines
  - Severe disease
    - Short-course oral corticosteroids
    - Narrow-band UVB

- Pustular psoriasis of pregnancy (PPP)
  - Also called
    - Impetigo herpetiformis
  - Variant of generalized pustular psoriasis
  - Usually presents in
    - 3rd trimester
  - Recurs in future pregnancies
  - Clinical features
    - Sterile pustules
    - Intertriginous onset
    - Centrifugal spread
    - Fever
    - Chills
    - Vomiting
    - Diarrhoea
  - Laboratory findings
    - Neutrophilia
    - Elevated inflammatory markers
    - Hypocalcaemia
    - Hypophosphataemia
    - Low vitamin D
  - Cultures
    - Negative
  - Histology
    - Spongiform pustules
    - Neutrophilic infiltration
  - Direct immunofluorescence
    - Negative
  - Treatment
    - Systemic corticosteroids
    - Cyclosporine
    - Infliximab
    - Topical corticosteroids
    - Calcipotriol
  - Maternal risks
    - Delirium
    - Tetany from hypocalcaemia
  - Fetal risks
    - IUGR
    - Stillbirth

  - PEP/PUPPP
