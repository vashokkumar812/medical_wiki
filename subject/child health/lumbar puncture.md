- Lumbar puncture (LP) – paediatric

- Key points
    - Discuss decision with senior clinician
    - Seriously unwell child
        - Give antibiotics first
        - Do not delay >30 minutes
    - Perform after
        - Full neurological examination
        - Contraindication review
    - Careful preparation essential
        - Analgesia
        - Anaesthesia
        - Sedation
        - Experienced assistant

- Background
    - LP performed at or below L4
        - Conus medullaris
            - ~L3 at birth
            - L1–2 in adulthood

    - Prefer CSF collection before antibiotics

    - Fever + purpura
        - LP may not alter management
            - Suspected meningococcal disease

    - Term infants
        - Sitting position often best tolerated

    - Failed LP ×2
        - Senior review
        - Consider image guidance

    - CT brain
        - Not useful for most meningitis cases
        - Normal CT does not exclude raised ICP

- Assessment

- Consent
    - Discuss
        - Benefits
        - Risks
    - Use parent information sheet

- Indications
    - Suspected meningitis
    - Suspected encephalitis
    - Suspected SAH with normal CT

    - Diagnosis/monitoring/treatment of
        - Haematological disorders
        - Malignancy
        - Neurological disorders
            - Idiopathic intracranial hypertension

- Contraindications

- Absolute contraindications
    - GCS <8
    - Fluctuating/deteriorating LOC

    - Signs of raised ICP
        - Diplopia
        - Abnormal pupils
        - Decorticate posture
        - Decerebrate posture
        - Bradycardia + hypertension + irregular respirations
        - Papilloedema

    - Bulging fontanelle alone
        - Not a contraindication

- Relative contraindications
    - Septic shock
    - Haemodynamic compromise
    - Significant respiratory compromise
    - New focal neurology
    - Seizure within previous 30 minutes
    - Ongoing reduced consciousness
    - INR >1.5
    - Platelets <50 ×10⁹/L
    - Anticoagulant use

    - Tachycardia/tachypnoea alone
        - Not contraindications

- Complications
    - Failed/traumatic tap
        - Common
    - Pain
    - Post-dural headache
        - 5–15%
    - Paraesthesia
        - Very uncommon
    - Respiratory arrest from positioning
        - Rare
    - Infection
        - Very rare
    - Spinal haematoma
        - Very rare
    - Spinal epidermoid tumour
        - Extremely rare
        - Reduced with stylet use
    - Brain herniation
        - Extremely rare if contraindications excluded

- Procedure

- Staff
    - Proceduralist
    - ≥1 assistant

- Equipment
    - Sterile gloves
    - Sterile drapes

    - Skin preparation
        - 2% chlorhexidine + 70% alcohol
        - Povidone iodine
        - Neonates
            - 0.1% chlorhexidine

    - 1% lidocaine
    - 25–30G needle
        - Local infiltration

    - Spinal needle
        - 22–25G
        - Bevelled
        - With stylet

    - CSF tubes
    - Manometer
        - If opening pressure required

- Spinal needles
    - 22G or 25G bevelled with stylet
    - 25G pencil-point
        - Older children
    - Avoid no-stylet needles

- Median depth formula
    - Neonates/infants
        - 2 × weight (kg) + 7 mm
    - Children
        - 0.4 × weight (kg) + 20 mm

- Analgesia
    - Non-pharmacological techniques
    - Child Life involvement
    - Topical anaesthetic
        - 45–60 minutes prior
    - 1% lidocaine SC
        - >12 months
    - Oral sucrose
        - Infants
    - Nitrous oxide
        - >12 months
        - May alter opening pressure
    - Other sedation
        - Senior discussion

- Monitoring
    - Cardiorespiratory monitoring
    - Oxygen saturation monitoring

- Position
    - Sitting or lateral position
    - Back at 90° to bed
    - Hips/shoulders aligned
    - Maximal spinal flexion
    - Avoid neck overflexion in infants

    - Iliac crest line
        - Corresponds to L3–4

    - Preferred insertion level
        - L3–4
        - L4–5

- Lumbar puncture steps
    - Midline insertion

    - Bevel orientation
        - Sitting position
            - Sideways
        - Lateral position
            - Upwards

    - Aim needle toward umbilicus
    - Advance until reduced resistance felt
    - Remove stylet

- If CSF obtained
    - Collect 2–4 tubes
    - 5–10 drops per tube
        - 10 drops ≈0.5 mL

    - Slow CSF flow
        - Rotate needle 90°

- Blood-stained CSF
    - Send for culture
    - If clearing
        - Use final tube for cell count
    - Consider SAH
    - If persistent blood
        - Repeat at different level

- If no CSF
    - Replace stylet
    - Reposition
    - Reattempt carefully

- After collection
    - Replace stylet
    - Remove needle
    - Apply pressure
    - Send specimens urgently

- Post-procedure care
    - Occlusive dressing
    - Bed rest not beneficial
    - Continue observations as indicated
        - Vital signs
        - Neurological observations

    - Document
        - Analgesia/anaesthesia used
