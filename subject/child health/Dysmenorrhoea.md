- Key Points
	- Primary dysmenorrhoea ≈90%
	- Investigations not routinely required
	- First-line
		- NSAIDs ± hormonal therapy
	- Consider secondary dysmenorrhoea if:
		- Atypical features
		- Persistent pain despite 3–6 months treatment

- epidemiology
	- Most common menstrual symptom in adolescents
	- Affects 50–90% of young women
	- Leading cause of recurrent short-term school absenteeism

- Classification
	- Primary Dysmenorrhoea (≈90%)
		- Painful menstruation without pelvic pathology
		- Pathophysiology
			- ↑ prostaglandins
			- → ↑ uterine contractions
			- → uterine ischaemia/hypoxia
			- → pain
		- Onset
			- 6–12 months after menarche
		- features
			- Cramping lower abdominal pain
			- Starts just before/with menses
			- Duration ≤72 hours
		- Associated symptoms
			- Headache
			- Nausea/vomiting
			- Diarrhoea
			- Breast tenderness
			- Muscle cramps

	- Secondary Dysmenorrhoea (≈10%)
		- Due to pelvic pathology
		- Causes
			- Endometriosis (most common)
			- Obstructive anomalies
			- Adenomyosis
			- PID/infection
			- Ovarian cysts
		- features
			-  Pain from menarche
			- Pain worsens towards end of bleeding
			- Dysuria/dyschezia during menses
			- PID features/history
			- Family history of endometriosis
			- Persistent symptoms despite treatment

- Associated symptoms
	- PMS
	- Menorrhagia
	- Migraine
	- Urinary/bowel symptoms

- Functional impact
	- School absenteeism
	- Reduced sports/social activity


- Vaginal examination
	- Rarely indicated
	- Senior discussion first
	- Perform once only if needed

- Investigations
	- Not routinely required

	- Consider
		- β-hCG
			- Sexually active
			- Missed/late period
		- STI screening
			- If PID suspected
		- Pelvic ultrasound
			- Atypical features
			- Poor treatment response

- Management
	- First-line
		- NSAIDs ± hormonal therapy
	- Non-pharmacological
		- Adjunct or alternative therapy
	- NSAIDs
		- Naproxen
		- Mefenamic acid
			- with food
		- Timing
			- Start 1–2 days before menses
			- Continue first 2–3 days
		- Benefits
			- ↓ pain
			- ↓ bleeding
			- ↓ GI symptoms

	- Antifibrinolytic
		- Tranexamic acid
			- 1 g TDS during heavy bleeding
		- Benefits
			- ↓ blood loss
			- May ↓ pain

	- Hormonal Therapy
		- Combined OCP
			- Continuous regimen preferred
			- Examples
				- Levlen®
				- Norimin®
		- Oral progestogen
			- If estrogen contraindicated
			- No ↑ VTE risk
			- Norethisterone 5 mg BD continuous
		- Subdermal implant
			- Implanon®
			- ↓ pain + bleeding
			- ~20% amenorrhoea
		- LNG-IUD
			- Mirena® / Kyleena
			- ↓ pain + bleeding
			- ~20% amenorrhoea
			- Safe post-menarche

- Follow-up
	- Review at 3 months
	- Earlier review if:
		- Side effects
		- Poor tolerance

- Referral
	- Suspected secondary dysmenorrhoea
	- Atypical features
	- Poor response to treatment
