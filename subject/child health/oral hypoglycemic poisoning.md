# Sulfonylurea and Biguanide Overdose in Adolescents

## Key Points
- The two groups of oral hypoglycaemic agents used in adolescents are **Sulfonylureas** and **Biguanides**.  
- This review focuses on **sulfonylurea overdose**.  
- Sulfonylurea overdose and symptomatic hypoglycaemia should be treated with both **intravenous dextrose and octreotide**.  
- Any child with possible sulfonylurea exposure should be **observed for 24 hours**, as delayed hypoglycaemia may occur.  
- A single sulfonylurea pill (as small as 2 mg) can cause hypoglycaemia in young children.  
- For 24-hour advice, contact the **Poisons Information Centre (13 11 26)**.

---

## Background
Oral hypoglycaemic agents are used for managing **type II diabetes mellitus** (non-insulin dependent diabetes).

### Sulfonylureas
- Increase **pancreatic insulin secretion**.  
- Most important cause of **hypoglycaemic toxicity**; may cause **prolonged and life-threatening hypoglycaemia** after accidental paediatric ingestion or deliberate self-poisoning.  
- A single tablet in a toddler can cause **life-threatening hypoglycaemia**.  
- Onset of hypoglycaemia may be **delayed up to 18 hours** after ingestion.  
- Large overdoses may require **treatment for several days**.  
- Admission for a minimum of **12–24 hours** is indicated for blood glucose monitoring.  
- Discharge should only occur **during daylight hours**.

### Biguanides
- **Metformin** decreases carbohydrate absorption from the gut, increases glucose uptake in peripheral tissues, and reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis.  
- Metformin ingestion is **not associated with hypoglycaemia** in normal patients, but may cause **lactic acidosis** in large overdoses, especially with renal or cardiac failure or co-ingestants.  
- Asymptomatic patients following **accidental exposure** do not require hospital assessment, decontamination, or investigation.  
- Children who have taken **≤1700 mg** do not require hospital assessment.  
- **Nausea and vomiting** may occur in smaller overdoses.  
- **Haemodialysis** resolves acidosis and removes metformin.

---

## Pharmacokinetics (Sulfonylureas)
- Onset of action unchanged, but **duration of action increases** in overdose.  
- **Peak plasma concentrations** within 8 hours of acute ingestion (may be delayed in overdose).  
- **Metabolized by the liver**; some agents have **active metabolites** renally excreted.  
- **Extensively protein bound**.  
- Duration, hepatic metabolism, and renal excretion determine management of related hypoglycaemia.

---

## Children Requiring Assessment
- All children with **deliberate self-poisoning** or **significant accidental ingestion**.  
- Any **symptomatic** child.  
- **Acute ingestion of unknown quantity**.  
- Any child whose developmental age is inconsistent with accidental poisoning (consider **non-accidental poisoning**).

---

## Risk Assessment

### History
- Determine whether **intentional or accidental**.  
- Focus on **dose**, **timing**, and **co-ingestants**.

### Examination
**Manifestations of hypoglycaemia:**
- **Autonomic:** tachycardia, sweating, [[anxiety disorders]].  
- **CNS:** drowsiness, altered mental status, coma, seizures.  
- Check for **Medicalert bracelet**, **fingerprick marks**, or other medical indicators.

---

## Investigations
- **Serial BSLs (blood glucose levels)**.  
- **UEC (urea, electrolytes, creatinine)**.  
- **Insulin levels** (if available) may assist in sulfonylurea overdose (seek toxicology advice).  
- **VBG/ABG (including lactate)** for metformin overdose to confirm lactic acidosis.  
- For co-ingestants, consider **paracetamol level** and **ECG**.

---

## Acute Management

### 1. Resuscitation
- Standard procedures and supportive care.

**Sulfonylurea management:**
- Obtain **IV access**.  
- Administer concentrated **IV glucose** if hypoglycaemic.  
- **Paediatric doses:**
  - 5 mL/kg of 10% glucose IV bolus.  
  - Then 1–2 mL/kg/hour of 10% glucose IV infusion.  
- Check BSL **hourly**.  
- Send bloods including **serum insulin level**.  
- Commence **octreotide infusion** (see below).

### 2. Decontamination
- Consider **activated charcoal** if massive ingestion and within 1 hour, or after airway protection.  
- Discuss with a **toxicologist**.

### 3. Specific Treatment

#### Antidote – [[Octreotide]]
- Long-acting synthetic analogue of somatostatin.  
- Suppresses insulin release from pancreatic cells.

**Dose:**
- **Children:**  
  - 1 microgram/kg IV bolus (max 50 micrograms), then 1 microgram/kg/hour continuous infusion (max 25 micrograms) for ≥24 hours.  
  - Alternatively: 1–2 micrograms SC (max 50 micrograms) every 8 hours.  
- **Adolescents/Adults:**  
  - 50 micrograms IV bolus, then 25 micrograms/hour continuous infusion for ≥24 hours.  
  - Alternatively: 50 micrograms SC every 8 hours.

**Notes:**
- Once octreotide infusion is running, **normoglycaemia** is typically maintained without glucose supplementation.  
- If hypoglycaemia recurs, correct with glucose and **double the infusion rate**.  
- Octreotide infusion may be ceased when:
  - 10% glucose infusion stopped ≥4 hours prior.  
  - No symptoms of hypoglycaemia.  
  - BSLs >2.5 mmol/L for >4 hours.  
  - During **daylight hours only**.

**Post-Cessation Monitoring:**
- Check BSLs **hourly for at least 12 hours** after stopping octreotide/dextrose before discharge.

---

## Consultation Guidelines

### Consider Consultation With Local Paediatric Team When:
- Any child or young person presents with **intentional overdose**.  
- Any child with **sulfonylurea exposure** (requires 12–24 hr glucose monitoring).  
- **Contact Victorian Poisons Information Centre (13 11 26)** for advice.

### Consider Transfer When:
- Children require care beyond the comfort level of the current hospital.

**For emergency or ICU transfers:**  
Call **Paediatric Infant Perinatal Emergency Retrieval (PIPER) Service: 1300 137 650**.

---

## Discharge Criteria
- **Normal BSLs maintained** for 12 hours after stopping octreotide infusion.  
- Patient is **on a normal diet**.  
- **Plasma insulin level** (if available) normal at 6 hours post cessation.  
- Discharge only during **daylight hours** for sulfonylurea exposure.

---

## Assessing Risk and Connecting to Community Services
- Prior to discharge, adolescents with **deliberate ingestions** need a **risk assessment** for self-harm or repeated ingestion.  
- Assess for **drug and alcohol use**.  
- If discharge is safe but ongoing mental health or substance needs exist, link the adolescent to **appropriate community services** (refer to state-specific mental health and drug/alcohol services in Victoria).
