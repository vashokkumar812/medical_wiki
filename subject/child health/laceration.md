- Paediatric lacerations

- Key points
    - Thorough assessment before closure
    - Adequate analgesia/anaesthesia
        - Better cosmesis
        - Better function
        - Reduced infection
        - Improved experience
    - If sedation needed for closure
        - May also need sedation for suture removal
    - Use absorbable sutures where appropriate

- Background
    - Minor lacerations common in childhood
    - Goals
        - Best cosmetic outcome
        - Best functional outcome
        - Minimal distress
    - Distress worsens outcomes

- Assessment

    - Assess for
        - Contamination
            - Dirt
            - Foreign body

        - Associated injuries
            - Head injury
            - Cervical spine injury
            - Eye injury
            - Dental injury

        - Deep structure injury
            - Tendons
            - Joints
            - Nerves

        - Facial injuries
            - Facial nerve injury
            - Parotid duct injury
            - Lacrimal duct injury
            - Medial canthus injury

        - Inadequate wound examination
            - Refer specialty team

        - Impaired blood supply
            - Dusky tissue/flap
                - Urgent specialty review

            - End-arterial areas
                - Nose tip
                - Fingertips
                - Ear lobes

        - Cause of injury
            - Bite injuries
                - Consider tetanus
                - Consider antibiotics

- Management

- General principles
    - Fast from arrival
    - Ensure
        - Experienced staff
        - Adequate time
        - Appropriate equipment
        - Assistance
        - Sedation/analgesia availability

    - If uncertain
        - Discuss senior ED clinician
        - Consider specialty referral

    - Consider sedation
        - Nitrous oxide
        - Ketamine

- Anaesthesia

- Topical
    - ALA gel
        - Adrenaline/lignocaine/tetracaine
        - Apply 20–30 minutes before

    - EMLA/AnGel
        - Most effective on limbs

- Local
    - Lignocaine ± adrenaline
    - Avoid adrenaline in end-arterial areas

- Regional block
    - Ring block
    - Digital nerve block
    - Use lignocaine without adrenaline

- Bier block
    - Trained clinician only
    - Child must tolerate tourniquet

- General anaesthesia
    - Extensive debridement
    - Complex repair

- Cleaning

- Superficial wounds
    - Saline
    - Aqueous chlorhexidine
    - Avoid alcohol chlorhexidine

- Deep wounds
    - Anaesthetise first
    - Remove foreign bodies
    - Remove grease
        - Water + cetrimide
    - Irrigate under pressure

- Gravel rash
    - Scrub after anaesthesia
    - Larger areas may need sedation/GA

- Ragged wounds
    - Trim non-viable tissue

- Glass injuries
    - Explore deeply
    - X-ray if retained glass suspected
    - Small fragments may not be visible
    - Evacuate haematomas

- Closure

- Non-surgical closure

- Dressing only
    - Small
    - Superficial
    - Non-gaping
    - Clean wounds

    - Puncture wounds
        - Usually leave open

- Adhesive strips
    - Slight wound separation
    - Avoid if high tension/movement
    - Leave gaps for drainage
    - Keep dry 72 hours

- Tissue adhesive
    - Clean edges
    - No tension
    - No deep sutures required
    - Best for wounds <3 cm
    - Avoid mucosa
    - Protect eyes near forehead
    - Apply glue superficially only
    - Hold 30 seconds
    - Do not place glue inside wound
    - Achieve haemostasis first
    - Falls off in 1–2 weeks

- Surgical closure
    - Optimise conditions first
    - Young children often need sedation
    - Anticipate sedation for removal
    - Prefer absorbable sutures
    - Monofilament
        - Lower infection risk
    - Vicryl Rapide
        - Higher scarring risk on face

- Site-specific management

- Scalp
    - Control bleeding with pressure
    - Do not shave
    - Comb hair away
    - Petroleum jelly may help
    - Hair ties ± glue possible
    - Close in 2 layers
        - Galea
        - Skin
    - Remove sutures/staples
        - 7 days

- Forehead
    - Minimal debridement
    - Do not shave eyebrow
    - Superficial wounds
        - Cleaning ± Steri-Strips
    - Remove sutures
        - 5–7 days

- Cheek
    - Assess for fractures
        - Zygoma
        - Orbit
    - Assess facial nerve
    - Ophthalmology if
        - Hyphema
        - Swollen shut eye

- Eyelids
    - Lid margin/tarsal plate injury
        - Ophthalmology referral
    - Simple injuries
        - Glue or low-tension sutures

- Lips
    - Accurate vermilion border alignment essential
    - Experienced clinician required
    - Uncooperative child
        - GA/surgical repair

    - Inner lip
        - Rarely requires closure

    - Gum margin injury
        - Dental/maxillofacial referral

- Hand/digits

    - Subungual haematoma
        - <50%
            - Ice + analgesia
        - >50% with pain
            - Trephine

    - Fingertip skin loss
        - ≤1 cm²
            - Dressings
        - >1 cm²
            - Specialty referral

    - Partial amputation/crush
        - Assess nail bed
        - X-ray distal phalanx
        - Possible open fracture
        - Refer

    - Palm injuries
        - Careful neurovascular assessment
        - Compound injury
            - Antibiotics
            - Specialist review

- Palate
    - Consider posterior pharyngeal injury
    - Rarely requires sutures

- Tongue
    - Most do not require sutures
    - Large/full-thickness/bleeding injuries
        - Plastics referral

- Ear
    - Full-thickness/cartilage injury
        - Specialist referral

- Limbs
    - Upper limb
        - May need tourniquet

    - Lower limb
        - Debride thoroughly
        - Avoid tension
        - Pretibial wounds high risk

    - Immobilise joint above/below
    - Remove sutures
        - 7–10 days

- Trunk
    - Remove sutures
        - 10–14 days

- Tetanus
    - Follow tetanus-prone wound guidance

- Antibiotics
    - Not indicated for simple lacerations
    - Indications
        - Bite wounds
        - Extensive contamination
    - Proper decontamination most important

- Consultation
    - Hands/lips commonly need specialty input

- Transfer
    - Beyond local capability
    - Retrieval services if ICU needed

- Discharge
    - Wound cleaned and closed adequately
    - Arrange early GP wound review
