- Tube issues

	- Blocked tube

		- Causes
			- poorly crushed medications
			- failure to flush tube after feeds
			- thick feeds/lumps of powder
			- vitamised food through tube
			- formula curdling within tube

		- Management
			- flush with:
				- 10–20 mL carbonated drink
					- mineral water
					- diet cola
			- attempt aspiration with syringe

- Peri-stomal issues

	- Leakage around tube

		- Causes
			- tube too small for stomal tract
			- deflated/ruptured balloon
			- tube deterioration
			- tube migration

		- Management
			- larger replacement tube if loose fit
			- assess balloon:
				- drain balloon
				- refill with water
				- recheck volume
			- burst balloon:
				- tube replacement required
			- tube migration:
				- apply gentle traction
				- adjust retention disc snugly to abdominal wall

	- Skin excoriation

		- Cause
			- leakage of gastric secretions around gastrostomy

		- Barrier ointments
			- ointment preferred over cream
			- options:
				- Calmoseptine™
				- Ilex™
				- zinc ointment

		- If candidal/thrush infection present
			- avoid zinc-based products
			- alternatives:
				- Orabase™
				- Coloplast™ paste

		- Acid suppression
			- ranitidine
			- omeprazole
			- prescribe only by treating team

		- Treatment of inflamed/oozing skin
			- foam dressing
			- gauze
			- Sofwick
			- topical magnesium + aluminium hydroxide
				- eg Mylanta
			- hydrocolloid powder
				- reduces bleeding
				- absorbs moisture
			- thin hydrocolloid dressing
				- eg Duoderm wafer

	- Granulation tissue

		- usually develops ~6 weeks post-surgery
		- may result from excessive tube movement
		- anchor devices may help
			- eg FlexiTrak

		- Treatment options
			- foam dressing for pressure
			- silver nitrate daily
				- apply only to granulation tissue
			- topical steroid cream

	- Infections

		- Candida infection
			- commonly beneath skin flange
			- treatment:
				- topical clotrimazole
				- topical nystatin

		- Cellulitis
			- usually staphylococcal
			- may involve enteric organisms
			- features:
				- red
				- swollen
				- painful
				- oozing site

			- Management
				- oral/IV flucloxacillin
				- consider tube removal if severe swelling/pain
				- if discharge present:
					- send wound swab for microscopy + culture before antibiotics
