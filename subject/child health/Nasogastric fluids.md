- Key points
    - Prefer enteral route
        - Oral
        - NG
    - Shock
        - Requires IV resuscitation
    - Serial weights
        - Best measure of acute fluid status
    - Rehydration treats dehydration
        - Does not treat underlying cause
    - Requires regular clinical review

- Background
    - NG fluids
        - Safe and effective for hydration/rehydration
        - Well tolerated
            - Especially preschool children

    - Advantages over IV fluids
        - Reduced electrolyte derangement risk
        - Faster recovery
            - Example: gastroenteritis
        - Allows nutritional fluids
            - Expressed breast milk
            - Formula

    - Notes
        - Neonates
            - Separate guidelines
        - Applies only to short-term rehydration/maintenance
        - Long-term NG feeding
            - Requires specialist input

- Assessment

    - Indications for NG fluids

        - Rehydration
            - GI losses
            - Poor intake

        - Maintenance
            - Bronchiolitis
            - Gingivostomatitis
            - Viral illness

        - Replacement of ongoing losses

    - Contraindications

        - Nil by mouth/fasting
        - Shock
        - Suspected bowel obstruction
            - Bilious vomiting
            - Severe distension
            - Ileus
        - Altered consciousness
        - Critically unwell child
        - Facial trauma
        - Base of skull fracture

    - Red flags requiring senior review

        - Age <6 months
        - Severe dehydration
        - Electrolyte abnormality
        - Hypoglycaemia
        - Risk of increased ADH
            - CNS conditions
            - Pulmonary conditions
        - Short gut syndrome
        - Chronic GI disease
        - Complex conditions
            - Diabetes
            - Cardiac disease
            - Metabolic disease
            - Renal disease
        - Craniofacial abnormalities

- Management

    - Investigations

        - Usually not required

        - Consider
            - Glucose
            - Electrolytes

        - Consider testing in
            - Young infants
            - Ongoing losses
            - NG fluids beyond 4–6 hours

        - Important note
            - ORS is hypotonic
            - Risk of hyponatraemia

- Treatment

    - NG tube insertion

        - Confirm placement
            - Gastric aspirate pH 1–5

        - If placement uncertain
            - Do not use tube

        - Chest X-ray
            - Only if uncertain

    - Choice of fluid

        - Rehydration
            - Oral rehydration solution (ORS)
                - Gastrolyte
                - HYDRAlyte
                - Pedialyte
            - Use in all children >1 month
            - No significant difference between brands

        - Maintenance

            - Infants
                - Resume breast milk/formula
                - Stop feed fortification during GI illness

            - Older children
                - Return to normal diet ASAP
                - ORS only short term

        - Special situations
            - Long-term enteral nutrition
            - Special fluid requirements
                - Specialist/dietitian input

    - Fluid calculation and rate

        - Most unwell children
            - Use 2/3 maintenance

        - Especially important in
            - CNS infections
            - Respiratory illness

        - Full maintenance appropriate if
            - Poor intake
            - Recovery phase gastroenteritis

    - Maintenance fluid calculation (4-2-1 rule)

        - 3–10 kg
            - 100 mL/kg/day
            - 4 mL/kg/hr

        - 10–20 kg
            - 1000 + 50 × (weight − 10) mL/day
            - 40 + 2 × (weight − 10) mL/hr

        - 20–60 kg
            - 1500 + 20 × (weight − 20) mL/day
            - 60 + 1 × (weight − 20) mL/hr

        - >60 kg
            - Maximum 2400 mL/day
            - Maximum 100 mL/hr

        - Important note
            - Most unwell children need approximately 2/3 of maintenance

    - Important feeding notes

        - Healthy infants usually need
            - 150–200 mL/kg/day

        - Do not use normal infant requirements for unwell child calculations

        - Return to normal feeding during recovery

- Replacement of deficit

    - Administration methods

        - Continuous feeds
            - Better for
                - Vomiting
                - Diarrhoea
                - Respiratory distress

        - Bolus feeds
            - Better tolerated in
                - Mild/moderate illness
                - Recovery phase
            - Example
                - 3-hourly feeds

        - Breastfeeding
            - Can continue throughout treatment

- Rapid NG rehydration

    - Indication
        - Moderate dehydration
            - Example: gastroenteritis

    - Not suitable for
        - Respiratory illness
        - Meningitis
        - Electrolyte imbalance
        - Age <6 months
        - Significant comorbidities
        - Abdominal pain

    - Severe dehydration
        - Requires IV fluids

    - Regimen
        - ORS 10–25 mL/kg/hr for 4 hours
        - Use clinical judgement

- Slow NG rehydration

    - Indication
        - Moderate dehydration where rapid fluids unsafe

    - First 24 hours
        - Replace 5% deficit over first 6 hours
        - Then maintenance over next 18 hours

    - Subsequent 24 hours
        - Replace remaining deficit plus maintenance

- After rehydration

    - Return to normal diet ASAP
    - Continue breastfeeding

    - Key principles
        - No need to dilute feeds
        - No gradual reintroduction required

    - Persistent diarrhoea
        - Consider lactose-free diet for 1–2 weeks
        - Avoid juice/fructose

- Replacement of ongoing losses

    - Replace mL-for-mL with ORS

    - Example
        - 200 mL diarrhoea over 4 hours
            - Replace 50 mL/hr over next 4 hours

- Fluid balance monitoring

    - Daily hydration assessment
    - Daily weight
        - Best marker
    - Input/output chart
    - 12-hour subtotals

    - If vomiting
        - Slow feeds
        - Consider ondansetron if age ≥6 months

    - Ongoing vomiting or abdominal pain
        - Senior review required

- Escalation

    - Consult paediatrics if
        - Severe dehydration
        - Electrolyte abnormality
        - Glucose abnormality

    - Transfer
        - If care exceeds local capability
