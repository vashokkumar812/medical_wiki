- Paediatric distress minimisation

	- Key points
		- Protect children from unnecessary healthcare-induced trauma and distress
			- Especially children requiring ongoing medical care

		- Minimise distress
			- Keep child with carer
			- Allow most comfortable posture
			- Reduce external stimuli

		- Unwell children may deteriorate if
			- Upset
			- Sedated
			- Repositioned

		- Tailor assessment and management
			- Specific needs of child
			- Consider strategies for neurodiverse children

	- Background
		- Sick child may deteriorate with handling

		- Increased distress in unwell neonate, infant or child can
			- Increase heart rate
			- Increase respiratory rate
			- Increase blood pressure
			- Cause deoxygenation
				- Especially neonates
			- Contribute to deterioration
				- Moderate → severe
				- Severe → critical

	- Assessment

		- General approach
			- When clinically appropriate
				- Allow child to settle quietly on carer’s lap
				- Allow position of choice
				- Enter child’s space slowly
				- Enter quietly
				- Avoid rushing towards child

		- History

			- Speak
				- Softly
				- Calmly

			- Position yourself
				- Sit
				- Kneel
				- At or below height of child

			- Build rapport
				- Ask about likes/dislikes
					- Pets
					- Siblings
					- Favourite toys
					- Books
					- TV shows

			- Younger children
				- Ask carers what may comfort child
					- Toys
					- Books
					- Music
					- Videos

			- Ask about
				- Comfort strategies
				- Settling strategies
				- Coping strategies

			- Previous hospital experiences
				- What worked well
				- What did not work well

			- Use age-appropriate and developmentally appropriate language

			- History of neurodiversity

		- Examination

			- Much assessment gained from general observation
				- Before approaching child
				- Before physical examination

			- Prioritise critical examination components

			- Avoid prolonged examination

			- Opportunistic examination
				- Child on carer’s lap
				- If clinically appropriate

			- Demonstrate examination
				- On carer
				- On toy
				- Before using
					- Stethoscope
					- Hands
					- Otoscope

			- Use carer assistance
				- E.g. holding stethoscope in place

			- Offer choices when practical
				- Which arm for BP cuff
				- Which ear for temperature

			- Leave most distressing parts until end
				- Blood pressure
				- Oropharyngeal examination

	- Management

		- Investigations

			- Minimise unnecessary investigations
				- If not affecting acute management

			- Group interventions together

			- If IV access required
				- Use comfort positioning
				- Appropriate analgesia
				- Topical anaesthetic cream
				- Distraction techniques

			- See
				- Procedural sedation

		- Treatment

			- Parent or carer

				- Keep child with carer

				- Encourage family presence
					- Unless family member agitated
					- Or causing further distress

				- Carers can help with
					- Distraction
					- Soothing words
					- Comfort positioning

				- Ensure
					- Language needs met
					- Cultural needs met

			- Positioning

				- Allow most comfortable position
					- If clinically appropriate

				- Sitting upright
					- Reduces distress
					- Enhances sense of control
					- Increases comfort during procedures

				- Secure/comfort/hugging holds
					- Assist child
					- Not restrain child
					- For necessary examination/interventions

				- Skin-to-skin
					- Kangaroo care

				- Useful in
					- Preterm infants
					- Term infants

				- Swaddling

				- Facilitated tucking

			- Environment

				- Appropriate treatment area
					- Appropriate acuity of care
					- Skilled paediatric medical staff
					- Skilled paediatric nursing staff

				- Dim lighting

				- Reduce number of people in room

				- Speak softly and calmly

				- Reduce noise and noxious stimuli
					- Monitor alarms
					- Loud conversations

			- Minimise intervention

				- Minimise examination
					- If not impacting acute management

				- Minimise unnecessary investigations

				- Minimise/group cares and interventions
					- Observations
					- Medications

			- Provide comfort

				- Comforting toy

				- Blanket

				- Books

				- Photos

				- Videos

				- Family members

				- Comfort feeds
					- If safe

				- Breastfeeding
					- Multimodal comfort strategy
						- Skin-to-skin contact
						- Comfort of sucking
						- Rocking
						- Transfer of endogenous opiates from breastmilk

			- Distraction strategies

				- Blowing bubbles

				- Reading story

				- Looking at pictures

				- Animated video

				- Interactive game

				- Virtual reality
					- If age-appropriate

				- Utilise available services
					- Child life therapists
					- Play therapists
					- Diversional therapists
					- Occupational therapists
					- Psychologists
					- Music therapists
					- Clown doctors

			- Relaxation techniques

				- Music therapy

				- Deep breathing

				- Tummy breathing

				- Grounding exercises
					- Older children

				- Child blowing bubbles

				- Continue working self-soothing strategies

				- Screens may be helpful

	- Consider consultation with local paediatric team when
		- Clinician experienced in caring for children required
		- Advice regarding escalation of care required

	- Consider consultation with Child Life/play therapy
		- If available

		- Children anxious/distressed

		- Children requiring preparation/procedural support

	- Consider transfer when
		- Required level of care above local hospital capability
