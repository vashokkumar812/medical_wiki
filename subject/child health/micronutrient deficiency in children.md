

- Key points
	- Consider micronutrient deficiencies
		- In children with highly restricted diets
		- Restricted variety and poor nutritional balance
		- Due to neurodevelopmental conditions
			- ASD
			- Cognitive impairment

	- Normal growth parameters
		- Do not exclude micronutrient deficiency

	- Micronutrient deficiencies may cause
		- Permanent physical disability
			- E.g. blindness
		- Death

	- Clinical features may be subtle

	- Consider deficiency in children with highly restricted diets presenting with
		- Non-specific symptoms

- Background
	- Micronutrient deficiency may occur despite
		- Adequate caloric intake
		- Acceptable growth

	- Risk factor
		- Diet significantly restricted in variety

	- Can occur
		- Without underlying medical condition
		- Without eating disorder

	- Different from fussy eating
		- Common in early childhood
		- Usually resolves over time

	- Common in children with

		- Neurodevelopmental conditions
			- Autism spectrum disorder

		- Avoidant restrictive food intake disorder
			- ARFID

		- Multiple food allergies

		- Elective dietary restrictions
			- Cultural
			- Religious
			- Environmental

	- Children with underlying medical conditions affecting absorption/utilisation
		- Require specialist consultation

- Assessment

	- History

		- Highly limited diet variety
			- Missing food groups
			- <10 foods/food types regularly consumed
			- Minimal meal-to-meal variability

		- Dietary patterns predisposing to deficiency
			- Veganism

		- Consider Australian Healthy Eating Guidelines

		- Clinical features suggestive of specific deficiency

		- Previous micronutrient deficiency

		- Use of vitamin/mineral supplements

		- Neurodevelopmental disorder
			- Suspected
			- Confirmed
			- ASD
			- Cognitive impairment

	- Examination

		- General assessment for micronutrient deficiency

			- Skin changes
				- Pallor

			- Dry eyes

			- Oral mucosa changes

			- Gum changes

			- Goitre

			- Bony deformity

			- Neuropathy

			- Hair changes

			- Nail changes

		- Growth measurements
			- Routinely perform

		- Child may have
			- Normal growth
			- Overweight status

		- Consider clinical features associated with specific deficiencies

- Management

	- Children at risk based on history
		- Strongly consider investigations

	- Consider investigations even if asymptomatic
		- Some complications irreversible

	- Symptomatic children
		- Prioritise urgent assessment
		- Prioritise treatment initiation
		- Consider empirical supplementation

	- Dietician referral recommended
		- Dietary change
		- Supplementation

	- If micronutrient deficiency identified
		- Consider other diagnoses in addition to dietary causes

- Investigations

	- Blood collection challenges
		- Distress
		- Trauma

	- Consider performing all first-line investigations together
		- Single blood collection

	- Exception
		- If only specific deficiency suspected

	- Opportunistic testing
		- During sedation
		- During anaesthesia

	- Tests may need prioritisation
		- Limited sample volume

	- Estimated blood volume
		- 10–15 mL whole blood

	- Confirm collection details with local laboratory

	- First-line investigations

		- Blood

			- FBE

			- UEC

			- LFT

			- Calcium

			- Magnesium

			- Phosphate

			- Iron studies

			- Vitamin A
				- Light sensitive
				- Cover tube with foil

			- Thiamine
				- Cover tube with foil
				- Send on ice

			- Folate

			- Vitamin B12

			- Vitamin C
				- Cover tube with foil
				- Send on dry ice

			- Vitamin D
				- See flowchart for criteria

			- Zinc

	- Second-line investigations

		- Consider if
			- Specific deficiency risk
			- Clinical features identified

		- Blood

			- Coagulation profile
				- Marker of vitamin K deficiency

			- Thyroid function test
				- Marker of iodine deficiency

			- Vitamin E
				- Cover tube with foil
				- Send on ice pillow

			- Selenium

			- Copper

		- Urine

			- Vitamin B3

- Treatment

	- Specific deficiencies
		- Treat with supplementation

	- Dosing advice
		- Consult local guidelines
		- Consult pharmacist
		- Especially if multiple deficiencies

	- Consider multivitamin
		- In all at-risk children

	- Seek dietician advice
		- Choice of multivitamin
		- Dosage
		- Appropriate daily intake for age
		- Formulation tolerability

			- Liquid
			- Tablet
			- Powder

	- Occupational therapy/speech therapy
		- Administration strategies
		- Address food aversions

	- Feeding services
		- Specialised feeding services
		- Feeding therapists
		- Telehealth options available in some regions

	- After initial replacement
		- Continue maintenance dosing

	- Continue maintenance until
		- Dietary risk factors addressed
		- Dietary risk factors resolved

- Consider consultation with local paediatric team when
	- Symptomatic micronutrient deficiency
	- Cause unclear
	- Oral supplementation unsuccessful
	- Feeding support required
		- Nasogastric tube
		- Gastrostomy

- Consider transfer when
	- Required care beyond local hospital capability

- Consider discharge when
	- Micronutrient deficiency successfully treated
	- Risk factors addressed
