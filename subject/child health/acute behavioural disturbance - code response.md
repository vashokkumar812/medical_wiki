- Key points
    - Prioritise safety and non-restraint strategies first
    - Procedure should be performed by Code Response team
    - Physical restraint
        - Last resort only
        - Used only to facilitate rapid pharmacological treatment
    - Code Grey and Code Black
        - Used interchangeably across states
        - "Code Response" used here as universal term

- Background

    - Principles
        - Physical restraint only to facilitate rapid sedation
        - Respect child autonomy
        - Restraint/sedation removes autonomy
            - Use only as last resort
        - Child acting out without acute medical/psychiatric need
            - Discharge to safe environment rather than restrain
        - If restraint required
            - Coordinated team approach essential
            - Roles clearly defined
            - Swift action required
            - Never restrain without Code Response team available

- Management

    - Alternative calming strategies
        - Use de-escalation techniques
        - Crisis prevention
            - Anticipate early irritability
            - Review past behaviour history
            - Early mental health involvement
        - Provide safe containing environment
            - Calm confident staff approach
            - Minimise stimuli
        - Communicate simply and calmly
        - Offer collaborative sedation
            - Example: oral medication voluntarily

    - Emergency sedation
        - Follow acute behavioural disturbance protocols

    - Indications for emergency sedation/restraint
        - Physical restraint only to facilitate rapid pharmacological treatment

    - Contraindications/cautions
        - Child acting out without acute medical/psychiatric illness
            - Prefer discharge to safe environment
                - Home
                - Police
                - Child protection
        - Consider
            - Previous medications
            - Substance use
        - Alternative safe containment possible
            - Including voluntary oral sedation
        - Inadequate personnel
        - Unsafe environment
        - Inadequate equipment
        - Situation too dangerous
            - Example: child with weapon

    - Consent
        - Seek consent whenever possible
            - Even in unsafe situations
        - Ideally from
            - Child
            - Guardian
        - Adolescents may consent if capacity present
        - If child/adolescent dangerous to self/others
            - Consent not required
            - Follow relevant Mental Health Act

- Procedure

    - Performed by Code Response team
    - Team leader allocates roles before approach
    - Staff safety
        - Gloves
        - Goggles
    - Prepare medications
    - Secure child quickly/calmly
        - Least force possible
        - Minimum five staff required
    - Position
        - Supine
        - Avoid prone position
            - Increased asphyxiation risk
    - Medication administration
        - IM injection preferred
            - Lateral thigh
            - Ventrogluteal
            - Dorsogluteal
        - Beware needlestick injury
        - Further titrated doses may be required
        - IV route possible if safe to cannulate
    - Post-sedation monitoring required
    - Mechanical restraint
        - Extremely rare
        - Requires specialist mental health involvement/presence

- Post-sedation monitoring

    - Monitor for complications of restraint
        - Pressure injuries
        - Respiratory depression
        - Asphyxiation
        - Inability to clear vomitus

- Ongoing care

    - Detailed medical assessment required
    - Detailed mental health assessment required
    - Some children require inpatient mental health transfer
    - Continuously reassess sedation need
        - Use least restrictive option
    - Physical restraints
        - Only for medication administration
    - Monitor risk as sedation wears off

- Documentation

    - Record
        - Response to sedation
        - Complications
        - Ongoing observations
            - At least every 15 minutes
        - Time and duration of restraint
        - Medication chart details
        - Code response reporting form if applicable

- Defusing/debriefing

    - Restraining distressed child can distress staff
    - Critical incident stress debriefing may be needed
    - Ideally facilitated by uninvolved objective person
    - Consider Employee Assistance Program (EAP)

- Consult paediatric team when
    - Ongoing observation required
    - Medical stabilisation required
        - Example: drug toxicity
    - Mental health team involved in ongoing care

- Consider transfer when
    - Transfer to tertiary mental health facility required
    - Sedation complications occur
    - Required care exceeds hospital capability
