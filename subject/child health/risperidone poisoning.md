- Key points
    - Risperidone
        - Atypical antipsychotic
    - Common effects
        - Tachycardia
        - Acute dystonic reactions
    - Serious effects
        - Arrhythmias
        - CNS depression
        - Seizures
        - Extrapyramidal symptoms
    - Management
        - Mainly supportive/symptomatic
    - Poisons Information Centre
        - 13 11 26

- Background

    - Mechanism
        - Selective monoaminergic antagonist
        - Strong affinity for serotonin 5-HT2 receptors
        - Weaker affinity for dopamine D2 receptors

    - Dopamine blockade
        - Causes extrapyramidal symptoms

    - Alpha-1 adrenergic blockade
        - Causes orthostatic hypotension

    - Histamine receptor affinity
        - Contributes to anticholinergic effects

- Pharmacokinetics

    - Symptoms
        - Usually develop within 1–2 hours
        - Peak by 4–6 hours

    - Delayed onset possible
        - Slow metabolisers

    - Resolution
        - May take several days
        - Half-life variable
            - 3–30 hours

- Children requiring assessment

    - Any symptomatic child
    - Unknown quantity ingestion
    - Deliberate self-poisoning
    - Significant accidental ingestion

    - Urgent assessment required

        - Drug-naive children

            - <12 years
                - ≥1 mg potentially toxic

            - ≥12 years
                - >5 mg potentially toxic

        - Children on regular risperidone
            - >5× usual single dose potentially toxic

        - Developmental age inconsistent with accidental poisoning
            - Consider non-accidental poisoning

- Risk assessment

    - History

        - Intentional overdose
        - Accidental ingestion
        - Therapeutic error
        - Amount ingested
        - Time of ingestion
        - Co-ingestants
            - Alcohol
            - Cannabis
                - Increase CNS depression

    - Examination

        - Toxicity usually apparent within 4 hours

    - Mild-moderate toxicity

        - Cardiac
            - Tachycardia
            - Hypotension

        - CNS
            - Reduced consciousness
            - Somnolence
            - Extrapyramidal symptoms

    - Severe toxicity

        - Cardiac
            - QT prolongation
            - Ventricular arrhythmias

        - CNS
            - Severe extrapyramidal symptoms
            - Respiratory depression
            - Seizures
            - Coma
            - Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

    - Always assess for underlying medical conditions
        - Medicalert bracelet
        - Fingerprick marks

- Investigations

    - Screening
        - 12-lead ECG
        - BSL
        - Paracetamol level

    - Specific investigations
        - ECG at presentation
        - Repeat ECG at 4 hours
            - Tachycardia may be delayed up to 20 hours

    - ECG findings
        - Sinus tachycardia common
        - QT prolongation rare

- Acute management

    - Resuscitation

        - Standard supportive care
        - Treat hypotension
            - IV fluids first
            - Persistent hypotension
                - Norepinephrine preferred

        - Avoid
            - Epinephrine
            - Dopamine
                - Beta stimulation may worsen hypotension in alpha blockade

    - Decontamination

        - Activated charcoal
            - Very limited role
            - Discuss with toxicologist/Poisons Centre

    - Specific treatment

        - Seizures
            - IV benzodiazepines
            - Persistent/recurrent seizures
                - Propofol
                - Barbiturates

            - Avoid
                - Phenytoin
                - Fosphenytoin

        - Extrapyramidal symptoms
            - May occur days later
            - Treat with benzatropine
            - May require repeat dosing after 15 minutes
            - Avoid discharge if ongoing benzatropine needed

- Consult paediatric team when

    - Intentional overdose
    - Symptomatic patients
    - Cardiovascular disease present
    - Neurological disease present
    - Discuss with Poisons Information Centre

- Transfer indications

    - Significant CNS depression
    - Seizures
    - Cardiovascular instability
    - Care exceeds local capability

- Discharge criteria

    - Normal GCS
    - Normal ECG
    - Completed observation period

    - Discharge advice
        - Extrapyramidal movements may occur up to 3 days later
