- Infant crying / unsettled infant

- Key points
    - Crying
        - Normal infant communication
        - Reflects
            - Physical needs
            - Emotional needs
            - Developmental needs
    - Assessment aims
        - Exclude medical causes
        - Exclude inflicted injury
        - Assess parental mental health
    - Management
        - Education
        - Reassurance
        - Shared decision making
    - Avoid medications
        - Limited benefit
        - Potential harm

- Background
    - Crying peaks at 6–8 weeks
        - Average 2–3 hours/day
    - Families often receive conflicting advice
    - Settling approaches
        - Structured
        - Flexible
    - “Infant colic”
        - No longer preferred terminology

- Assessment

    - Goals
        - Exclude medical causes
        - Exclude traumatic causes
        - Assess parental mental health/supports
        - Determine if crying pattern is developmentally normal

- History
    - Pregnancy history
    - Birth history
    - Feeding patterns
    - Sleep-wake patterns
    - Growth/development
    - Current strategies/treatments
    - Family/social context
    - Parent/carer mental health

- Examination
    - Assess for
        - Illness
        - Injury
        - Pathology contributing to unsettled behaviour

- Red flags
    - Sudden persistent crying
    - Acutely unwell infant
    - Faltering growth
    - Feeding difficulty
    - Oral aversion
    - Rapid head circumference growth crossing percentiles
    - Unexplained injuries
    - Risk factors for abusive head trauma
    - Postnatal depression
    - Social isolation

- Postnatal depression screening
    - Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale

    - Concerning scores
        - Mothers
            - >12
        - Fathers
            - >9
        - Question 10 score >1
            - Suicidal thoughts concern

- Acute causes to consider
    - Incarcerated inguinal hernia
    - Intussusception
    - Raised intracranial pressure
    - Accidental injury
    - Inflicted injury
    - Infection/sepsis
    - Hair tourniquet
    - Corneal abrasion
    - Corneal foreign body

- Other causes to consider
    - Inadequate caloric intake
    - Non-IgE-mediated food allergy
    - Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease
        - Not common cause of unexplained crying
        - Differentiate physiological reflux from disease
        - No evidence for “silent reflux”
    - Functional lactose overload
    - Eczema/atopic dermatitis

- Typical infant crying pattern
    - Communicates need for comfort/proximity
    - Wide normal variation
    - Peaks at 6–8 weeks
    - Improves by 3–4 months
    - Often worse in afternoon/evening
    - Night waking normal <12 months
    - Cluster feeding common during growth spurts

    - Infant dyschezia
        - Straining
        - Crying
        - Facial redness before soft stool
        - Self-resolving coordination issue

- Investigations
    - Not required if history/examination consistent with normal crying

- Management approach

    - After excluding red flags
        - Reassurance
        - Education
        - Shared decision making

    - Multidisciplinary support
        - Lactation consultant
        - Child health nurse
        - GP
        - Paediatrician
        - Perinatal mental health services
        - Infant mental health services
        - Parenting support services

    - Discussion points
        - Validate caregiver concerns
        - Reassure regarding normal exam/development
        - Explain normal crying patterns
        - Address parental stress/mental health
        - Provide educational resources
            - PURPLE crying
        - Offer practical strategies
        - Arrange early follow-up

    - Significant parental mental health concerns
        - Urgent assessment/support required

- Treatment

    - Feeding-related interventions
        - Breastfeeding support
            - Improve latch/feeding effectiveness
        - Flexible on-demand feeding recommended
        - Diet/formula changes
            - Rarely useful unless food allergy suspected
        - Formula/solids
            - Do not improve sleep/crying
        - Bottle/teat changes
            - Usually ineffective

    - Probiotics
        - Some evidence in exclusively breastfed infants <3 months
        - Lactobacillus reuteri DSM 17938

- Not recommended/potential harm
    - PPIs
    - H2 blockers
    - Simethicone
    - Herbal colic mixtures
        - Gripe water
    - Anticholinergics
        - Dicyclomine
        - Cimetropium
    - Spinal manipulation

- Lingual frenotomy
    - Most tongue ties
        - No intervention needed
    - Feeding ineffective
        - Assessment by trained professional
    - Optimise first
        - Positioning
        - Latch
        - Feeding frequency

- Consult paediatrics when
    - Medical cause suspected
    - Infant clinically unwell
    - Concern for inflicted injury
    - Significant parental mental health concerns

- Transfer indications
    - Infant requires higher level care
    - Parent requires inpatient mental health support

- Discharge criteria
    - Medical causes excluded/managed
    - Injury excluded/managed
    - Parental mental health addressed
    - Family provided
        - Reassurance
        - Education
        - Follow-up support
