# Osteomyelitis and Septic Arthritis

## Key points
- Suspect in any child with:
	- fever
	- pain
	- reduced use of limb/joint

- Septic arthritis requires urgent surgical management
	- do NOT delay antibiotics

- Always consider alternative diagnoses:
	- trauma
	- non-accidental injury
	- inflammatory disease
	- malignancy

---

## Background
- Septic arthritis
	- infection within synovial joint

- Osteomyelitis
	- infection within bone

- May coexist

- Usually spread haematogenously
- May occur after penetrating injury

### Common organisms
- Staphylococcus aureus
	- most common

- Organisms by risk group
	- Neonates
		- Group B Streptococcus

	- Age 6 months–4 years
		- Kingella kingae

	- Unimmunised
		- Haemophilus influenzae

	- Sickle cell disease
		- Salmonella

### Discitis
- Infection/inflammation of intervertebral disc
- Usually lumbar spine
- Typically preschool-aged children
- Requires high index of suspicion

---

# Assessment

## History

### Site of pain
- Septic arthritis
	- hip
	- knee
	- any synovial joint

- Osteomyelitis
	- femur
	- tibia
	- pelvis
	- humerus
	- any bone

### Other history
- Acute or chronic onset
- Fever
	- may be absent in infants
- Trauma/fall
- Refusal to weight bear
- Reduced limb use
- Limited ROM
- Infant irritability
	- e.g. during nappy change

### Risk factors for severe/disseminated disease
- Immunocompromised
- Neonate
- Delayed diagnosis/presentation

---

## Examination

### General
- Fever
- Signs of sepsis

### Bone/joint examination
- Look
	- resting limb position
		- e.g. hip flexed/abducted/externally rotated
	- swelling
	- erythema
	- wounds
	- soft tissue infection

- Feel
	- tenderness
	- warmth
	- effusion

- Move
	- active movement
	- passive ROM
	- compare both sides

### Additional assessment
- Examine spine
- Assess gait/posture
	- refusal to weight bear
	- limp

---

## Differential diagnosis
- Transient synovitis
- Trauma
- Non-accidental injury
- Inflammatory arthropathy
- CRMO
- Acute rheumatic fever
- Pyogenic myositis
- Discitis
- Malignancy
	- e.g. leukaemia

# Investigations

## Investigations may NOT be required if ALL:
- Systemically well
- Afebrile
- Using limb comfortably
- Clear working diagnosis
- Early follow-up arranged

---

## Investigations REQUIRED if:
- Systemically unwell
- Significant pain
- Limited movement
- Suspected septic arthritis/osteomyelitis

	→ urgent orthopaedic discussion

---

## Pathology

### Blood tests
- FBE
- ESR
- CRP
	- may be normal early
	- useful for monitoring response

- Blood film
	- exclude malignancy

- Blood cultures
	- before antibiotics if possible

### Synovial fluid aspiration
- Suspected septic arthritis
- Discuss with orthopaedics
- Do NOT delay antibiotics

---

## Imaging

### X-ray
- Excludes fracture/tumour
- Often normal first 7–10 days in osteomyelitis

### Ultrasound
- Detects joint effusion
- Sensitive but non-specific

### MRI with contrast
- Preferred investigation for osteomyelitis
- Detects:
	- marrow involvement
	- subperiosteal abscess
	- myositis

### Technetium bone scan
- If MRI unavailable
- Helps localise multifocal disease
- Positive = non-specific
- Negative does NOT exclude infection

### CT
- Limited role
- Not substitute for MRI/bone scan

---

# Treatment

## Septic arthritis
- Urgent aspiration ± arthrotomy/washout
- Do NOT delay antibiotics

---

## Antibiotics

### Initial empiric IV therapy
- Follow local antimicrobial guidelines

### Switch to oral antibiotics when:
- Clinically improving
- Afebrile
- Improving inflammatory markers

- Usually after 2–3 days IV therapy

### Typical total duration
- Osteomyelitis
	- 3–4 weeks

- Septic arthritis
	- 2–3 weeks

### Longer duration required for
- Small bone infection
- Vertebral infection
- Flat bone infection
- Resistant/unusual organisms

	→ discuss with Infectious Diseases

---

# Consultation

## Consult paediatrics/orthopaedics when
- Suspected osteomyelitis
- Suspected septic arthritis

## Consult Infectious Diseases if
- Poor clinical/biochemical response
- Resistant/unusual organism
- Chronic infection (>2 weeks)
- Disseminated infection
- Unusual site
- Age <3 months
- Immunocompromised

---

# Transfer
- Care exceeds local capability

---

# Discharge criteria
- Tolerating oral antibiotics or outpatient IV therapy
- Afebrile
- Clinically improving
- Improved inflammatory markers
- Weight-bearing/using limb
- Family understands return precautions

## Additional Notes

### Potential Causative Organisms

| Organism | Higher Risk Groups/Settings |
|-----------|-----------------------------|
| *Staphylococcus aureus* | All ages; consider MRSA risk |
| *Kingella kingae* | Infants and preschoolers |
| Group B Streptococci | Neonates (though *S. aureus* still most common) |
| Group A Streptococci | Older infants and children |
| *Streptococcus pneumoniae* | Immunocompromised or unimmunised |
| *Escherichia coli* | Neonates |
| *Salmonella* species | Children with sickle-cell disease |
| *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* | Penetrating foot injury |
| *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* | Sexually active adolescents |

## Key Clinical Features of Common Differentials

| Feature | Osteomyelitis | Septic Arthritis | Pyogenic Myositis | Discitis | Transient Synovitis |
|----------|----------------|------------------|------------------|----------|--------------------|
| **Fever** | May be absent | Usually present | Usually present | Typically absent or low-grade | Typically absent or low-grade |
| **Onset** | Subacute limp/non-weight bearing | Acute limp/non-weight bearing | Subacute onset, may involve limp | Subacute irritability, back pain, limp or refusal to walk | Subacute or acute limp, often post-viral |
| **Localised features** | Limb pain, poorly localised | Hot, swollen, painful, immobile joint | Well localised pain; possible abdominal pain (psoas) | Refusal to bend forward, loss of lordosis, spinal tenderness, hip pain, neuro signs, ileus (higher lesions) | Weight-bearing with limp |
| **Systemic features** | ± systemic illness | Systemic symptoms more severe | Systemic symptoms more severe | Irritable ± systemic illness | Systemically well, recent viral symptoms |
