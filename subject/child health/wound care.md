### Examination
- Fully examine injured area

- Assess for
    - Nerve injury
    - Vessel injury
    - Tendon injury
        - Especially hand/palmar wounds
        - Example: fight bite

    - Tissue damage/loss

---

## 🔬 Investigations

- X-ray
    - Fracture
    - Radiopaque foreign body
        - Glass
        - Metal

- Ultrasound
    - Radiolucent foreign body
        - Thorn
        - Splinter

- Swab for MCS
    - Only if considering antibiotics

---

## 💧 Management

- Wound cleaning
    - Reduces bacterial load

    - Irrigation method
        - 0.9% normal saline
        - 30 mL syringe
        - Large-bore non-bevelled needle
        - Continuous irrigation
        - Syringe held just above wound edge
        - Volume
            - ~50–100 mL per cm wound length
        - Consider splash guard

- Contaminated/inadequately cleaned wounds
    - Chlorhexidine 0.05%
    - Povidone-iodine 1%

- Adequate analgesia/anaesthesia/sedation

- Antibiotics
    - Not routinely required if wound cleaned properly

    - Indications
        - Animal bites
        - Human bites
        - Penetrating wounds involving vital structures
            - Eye
        - Contaminated wounds
        - Deep wounds
        - Severe water-exposed wounds
        - Contaminated water-exposed wounds
        - Delayed cleaning >8 hours
        - Extensive tissue damage
        - Massive contamination

---

#### Referral indications

- Refer for plastic/general surgical review if
    - Cosmetically sensitive wounds
    - Deep wounds
    - Significant tissue loss
    - Tendon involvement
    - Nerve involvement
    - Vessel involvement

## 🩼 Dressing principles

### General principles of dressing selection

| Stage | Goal | Example dressing |
|---|---|---|
| Debridement | Remove necrotic tissue | Hydrogel |
| Granulation | Maintain moisture | Low-adherent moisture dressing |
| Epithelialisation | Protect new tissue | Low-adherent dressing |

---

## 🩹 Dressing options by wound type

| Wound Type | Role | Options | Review Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graze – clean | Keep dry | Film, tulle, fixation sheet | 2 days |
| Graze – soiled | Keep dry | Dry or tulle | 2 days |
| Laceration (sutured/glued) | Protect, minimise irritation | Open/dry, paper tape post-suture | 3–7 days |
| Puncture | Keep dry, allow visualisation | Open or dry | 1–2 days |
| Slough – dry | Hydration, promote debridement | Hydrocolloid, semi-permeable | 3–4 days |
| Slough – moist | Absorption, protect skin | Alginate, foam | — |
| Necrotic – dry | Hydration, promote debridement | Hydrocolloid, semi-permeable | 3–4 days |
| Infected | Antimicrobial, moist healing | Alginate or hydrocolloid | 1–2 days |
| Chronic (ulcers, PEG site) | Moisture retention/absorption | Hydrocolloid, alginate, foam | 5 days |

## 🧾 Dressing types overview

| Type | Examples | Advantages | Disadvantages | Indications | Contraindications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Semi-permeable film | OpSite, Tegaderm | Allows inspection, barrier, reduces pain | Pools exudate, traumatic removal | Superficial wounds | Highly exudative wounds |
| Non-adherent moist (tulle) | Jelonet, Bactigras | Moist healing, low cost | No absorption, allergy risk | Burns, secondary intention | Allergy |
| Non-adherent dry | Melolin, Melolite | Absorbs light exudate | Can stick if dry | Moderate exudate wounds | Dry wounds |
| Fixation sheet | Fixomull, Hypafix | Conforms, pain relief, 5–7 day wear | Requires oil soak before removal | Mild exudate wounds | Infected wounds, adhesive allergy |
| Calcium alginate | Kaltostat | Absorbs exudate, promotes haemostasis | Odour, needs removal | Exudative wounds | Dry wounds |
| Foam dressing | PolyMem | Absorbent, protective | Daily change needed | Mild–moderate exudate | Dry wounds |
| Hydrocolloid | Duoderm, Comfeel | Retains moisture, painless removal | Malodour, dermatitis | Burns, abrasions | Infection |
| Hydrogel | Intrasite Gel | Moisture balance, cooling | Needs secondary dressing | Dry wounds | Gram-negative infection |
| Paper adhesive | Micropore, Steristrips | Support, low allergy | Non-absorbent | Small wounds | Large/exudative wounds |
| Antimicrobial | Acticoat, Aquacel Ag | Reduces bacterial load | Expensive | Infected wounds, burns | Thyroid disease (iodine dressings) |

## 🏠 Post-dressing care & discharge

### Parent/patient education
- Monitor for infection signs
- Avoid sun exposure
- Begin scar massage
    - 2–4 weeks after closure
    - 5–10 minutes twice daily

- Moisturisers for scar softening
    - Vitamin E
    - Aloe vera
    - Sorbolene

---

## 👩‍⚕️ Consultation & transfer

### Consider consultation with
- Local paediatric team
- Surgical/plastics team if
    - Complex dressing
    - Specialised dressings unavailable

### Consider transfer when
- Beyond local capability
- Requires emergency surgical/ICU care
    - Retrieval services if required

### Discharge criteria
- Dressing applied
- Follow-up arranged
