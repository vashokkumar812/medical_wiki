- Mechanism / Properties  
	- Adsorbs toxins within gastrointestinal lumen  
	- Decreases systemic drug absorption  
	- Interrupts enterohepatic and enteroenteric circulation  
	- Multi-dose activated charcoal (MDAC)  
		- Produces “gastrointestinal dialysis” effect  

- Investigations  
	- Drug levels may be measured  
	- Results commonly return too late to guide early charcoal decision-making  

- Acute Management  

	- Resuscitation first  
		- Standard ABC approach  
		- Airway stabilisation priority  
		- Consider  
			- Intubation  
			- Mechanical ventilation before charcoal  

	- Critical principle  
		- Never delay resuscitation to administer charcoal  

- Indications for activated charcoal  
	- All criteria should be satisfied  

		- Initial resuscitation completed  

		- Early presentation  
			- Usually within 1–2 hours of ingestion  

		- Potentially severe or life-threatening ingestion  

		- Toxicologist consulted  

		- No contraindications present  

		- Airway protected if reduced consciousness/seizure risk  

		- NG tube position confirmed on chest X-ray if NG administration used  

- Common toxins/drugs where charcoal may be indicated  

	- Amisulpride  

	- Chloroquine  
		- Hydroxychloroquine  
		- Quinine  

	- Calcium channel blockers  
		- Verapamil  
		- Diltiazem  

	- Carbamazepine  

	- Colchicine  

	- Beta blockers  

	- Flecainide  

	- Methotrexate  

	- Paraquat  
		- Diquat  

	- Important note  
		- Always refer to individual poisoning guideline  

- Contraindications / poor adsorption toxins  

	- Corrosives  
		- Acids  
		- Alkalis  

	- Cyanide  

	- Alcohols  
		- Ethanol  
		- Methanol  
		- Glycols  

	- Essential oils  
		- Eucalyptus oil  

	- Fluoride  

	- Hydrocarbons  

	- Metals/electrolytes  
		- Lithium  
		- Iron  
		- Potassium  
		- Lead  

	- Mineral acids  
		- Boric acid  

- Major contraindications to administration  

	- Unprotected airway  

	- Significant aspiration risk  

	- Gastrointestinal obstruction/perforation  

	- Ileus  

	- Active vomiting with poor airway reflexes  

- Adverse effects  

	- Respiratory complications  
		- Aspiration pneumonitis  
		- Progressive respiratory failure  
		- Death  

	- High-risk situations for aspiration  
		- Drowsiness  
		- CNS depressant overdose  
		- Seizure-producing toxins  

	- Gastrointestinal complications  
		- Black stools  
		- Vomiting  
		- Constipation  
		- Charcoal bezoar  
			- Intestinal obstruction  

- Administration  

	- Preparation  
		- Highly viscous suspension  

	- Dilution  
		- Minimum dilution approximately  
			- 1:4 activated charcoal : water  

	- Enteral delivery  
		- Large-bore nasogastric tube preferred  
			- 10–14 French  

	- Safety step  
		- Confirm NG position with chest X-ray before use  

- Multi-Dose Activated Charcoal (MDAC)  

	- Principle  
		- Enhances elimination of certain toxins  
		- Pharmacokinetic benefit established  
		- Clinical outcome benefit less certain  

	- Stop MDAC immediately if  
		- Bowel sounds disappear  
		- Ileus develops  
		- Significant abdominal distension occurs  

- Monitoring during MDAC  

	- Clinical status  

	- Neurological status  

	- Respiratory status  

	- Drug concentrations if available  

	- Bowel function  

- Life-threatening poisonings where MDAC may help  

	- Carbamazepine  

	- Dapsone  

	- Phenobarbitone  

	- Phenytoin  

	- Quinine  

	- Theophylline  

- Admission / consultation criteria  

	- Intentional overdose  
			- Especially adolescents  

	- Severe poisoning  

	- Requirement for activated charcoal  

	- Airway protection required  

	- Need for ongoing monitoring  

- Early specialist involvement  

	- Toxicologist  

	- Poison information centre  

	- Local paediatric team
