- Daytime urinary incontinence in children

- Key points
    - Goal
        - Distinguish
            - Neurological causes
            - Anatomical causes
            - Functional causes
    - Use bladder diary

- Background

    - Definition

        - Urinary incontinence
            - Day wetting in child >5 years
            - >1 episode/month
            - Duration ≥3 months

        - Daytime continence
            - Usually achieved by 4 years

        - Epidemiology
            - ~10% in 5–6 year olds
            - Decreases with age

    - Normal bladder capacity
        - (Age + 2) × 30 mL

- Functional causes

    - Overactive bladder (OAB)
        - Urgency most important feature

    - Voiding postponement
        - Habitual delayed voiding
        - Overfilling
        - Leakage

    - Underactive bladder
        - Infrequent urination
        - Overflow incontinence
        - Large post-void residual common

    - Dysfunctional voiding
        - Failure to relax urethral sphincter/pelvic floor
        - Interrupted urinary stream
        - Prolonged voiding

- Assessment

- Red flags
    - Never achieved dryness
    - Continuous incontinence/dribbling
        - Not intermittent
    - Urinary retention
    - Spraying urinary stream
    - Excessive tiredness
    - Weight loss
    - Polydipsia/polyuria
    - Growth faltering
    - Hypertension
    - Distended bladder
    - Renal mass

- History

    - Previously dry during daytime?
        - Never dry/continuous dribbling
            - Consider anatomical abnormality

- Urinary symptoms
    - Voiding frequency
    - Incontinence
    - Urgency
    - Nocturia
    - Polyuria
    - Holding manoeuvres
        - Tiptoe standing
        - Leg crossing
        - Squatting with heel pressed into perineum
    - Straining
    - Weak stream
    - Intermittency
    - Dysuria

    - Completed bladder diary

- Additional history
    - Post-micturition dribble

    - Girls
        - Consider urethrovaginal reflux
            - Leakage after standing post-void

    - Boys
        - Consider incomplete emptying
        - Dysfunctional voiding

    - Urinary retention
    - Spraying stream

- Comorbidities
    - Constipation
    - UTI
    - Enuresis

    - Excessive tiredness/weight loss
        - Consider chronic illness/renal impairment

    - Polydipsia/polyuria
        - Consider
            - Diabetes mellitus
            - Diabetes insipidus
            - Renal tubular disease
            - Psychogenic causes

- Examination

    - Height/weight/BP
        - Growth faltering
        - Weight loss
        - Hypertension

    - Abdomen
        - Distended bladder
        - Renal mass
        - Faecal mass

    - Perineum/external genitalia/perianal area
        - Exclude epispadias
        - Boys
            - Dorsal penile opening
        - Girls
            - Patulous urethra

    - Lower back/spine
        - Exclude occult spinal dysraphism
        - Exclude tethered cord

    - Neurology
        - Lower limb examination
        - Gait assessment

- Investigations

    - Routine
        - Urinalysis
        - Urine culture

    - Repeat urinalysis if
        - Previous UTIs
        - Concern for renal/metabolic disease

    - Secondary investigations
        - Renal tract ultrasound
            - Pre-void residual
            - Post-void residual

- Ultrasound indications
    - Failed behavioural management
    - Red flags present
    - Recurrent UTIs
    - Established daytime incontinence
    - Previous imaging inadequate/outdated

- Initial management

    - Behavioural modification
        - Standard first-line treatment

    - Education
        - Lower urinary tract anatomy/function
        - Explain pathophysiology/treatment

    - Voiding behaviour
        - Timed voiding
        - Avoid holding manoeuvres
        - Optimal voiding posture

    - Use bladder diary
        - Monitor progress

- Fluid intake/diet
    - Drink with meals/snacks
    - Spread fluids evenly through day
    - Routine healthy diet

- Re-education
    - Address adherence issues
    - Re-educate if required

    - Continue active management for 3 months
        - ≈2 cycles of re-education

    - If unsuccessful
        - Refer paediatrician

- Treat comorbidities

    - Constipation/soiling
        - Treat simultaneously

    - UTI
        - Treat simultaneously
        - Recurrent UTIs
            - Refer paediatrician

    - Enuresis
        - Treat daytime symptoms first

- Pharmacological management (OAB)

    - Second-line treatment
    - Initiated by paediatric/continence team
    - Continue behavioural modification

- Oxybutynin

    - First-line medication
    - Oral route
    - Transdermal option available

    - Transdermal patch
        - Useful if cannot swallow tablets
        - Useful if oral intolerance
        - Do not cut patches

- Practice points

    - Monitor adverse effects
        - Constipation
        - Dry mouth
        - Flushing

    - Consider laxatives
        - Constipation worsens incontinence

    - Clinical response
        - Usually within 2 months

    - Treatment duration
        - Usually 3–6 months

    - Wean gradually
        - Regular reassessment

    - Monitor for
        - Incomplete bladder emptying
        - Urinary retention

- Referral indications

    - Consult paediatric/continence service
        - Red flags present

    - Refer urology if
        - Suspected anatomical abnormality
        - Imaging abnormality

    - Refer nephrology/paediatrics if
        - Significant haematuria
        - Proteinuria
        - Polyuria/polydipsia after excluding diabetes mellitus

    - Mental health referral
        - Significant behavioural concerns
        - Psychological comorbidities
