- Key points
	- guideline focuses on gynaecological causes of abdominal/pelvic pain
	- always consider non-gynaecological causes of acute abdominal pain
	- obstetric emergencies require urgent referral:
		- ectopic pregnancy
		- miscarriage
		- placental abruption
		- uterine rupture
	- gynaecological emergencies require urgent referral:
		- ovarian torsion
		- adnexal torsion
	- ovarian/adnexal torsion:
		- clinical diagnosis
		- referral should not be delayed for imaging

- Background
	- gynaecological causes increase after puberty
	- pain characteristics + associated symptoms help differentiate causes

- Assessment

	- History

		- Menstrual history
			- menarche
			- LMP
			- cycle frequency
			- duration
			- flow
			- dysmenorrhoea
			- flooding
			- clots >2 cm
			- pad/tampon frequency

		- Vaginal discharge

		- Pain characteristics
			- location
			- onset
			- duration
			- sudden vs gradual
			- quality
			- severity
			- triggers
			- associated bowel/bladder symptoms
			- fever
			- nausea/vomiting

		- Sexual history
			- contraception
			- STI risk
			- safe sex discussion

		- Systemic symptoms
			- fever
			- weight loss
			- vomiting
			- headache

		- HEADSS assessment

		- Consider chronic conditions in recurrent/non-specific pain
			- constipation
			- abdominal migraine
			- functional abdominal pain

	- Examination
		- pallor
		- hypotension
		- tachycardia
		- abdominal/pelvic mass
			- uterine
			- ovarian
		- vaginal examination
			- rarely indicated
			- senior discussion required
			- perform once only if needed

- Gynaecological causes of acute abdominal pain

	- Critical causes

		- Ovarian/adnexal torsion
			- sudden acute pelvic pain
			- sharp unilateral pain
				- usually RLQ
			- may become generalised
			- intermittent pain possible
			- adnexal mass
			- nausea/vomiting

		- Ectopic pregnancy
			- abdominal/pelvic pain
			- missed period
			- vaginal bleeding
			- shoulder tip pain possible

		- Acute placental abruption
			- vaginal bleeding
			- abdominal/back pain
			- uterine contractions
			- DIC possible
			- occurs any gestation
				- peak 24–26 weeks

		- Uterine rupture
			- uterine tenderness
			- peritoneal irritation
			- vaginal bleeding
			- abnormal fetal heart rate

		- Molar pregnancy
			- vaginal bleeding
			- pelvic pain
			- enlarged uterus
			- severe vomiting

	- Infectious causes

		- Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)
			- unprotected sex risk
			- post-coital bleeding
			- mucopurulent discharge
			- lower abdominal/pelvic pain
			- fever

	- Other causes

		- Pregnancy
			- breast tenderness
			- vomiting
			- bleeding
			- pelvic discomfort

		- Dysmenorrhoea
			- crampy intermittent lower abdominal pain
			- suprapubic/back pain
			- associated:
				- nausea
				- vomiting
				- headache
				- dizziness
				- fainting

		- Mittelschmerz
			- mild unilateral recurrent mid-cycle pain
			- due to follicular enlargement

		- Ruptured ovarian cyst
			- sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain
			- may follow strenuous activity

		- Spontaneous miscarriage
			- early pregnancy crampy pelvic pain
			- vaginal bleeding
			- passage of products of conception

		- Endometriosis
			- cyclical crampy pelvic pain
			- associated with menses
			- possible:
				- dyschezia
				- dysuria
				- dyspareunia

		- Imperforate hymen
			- amenorrhoea
			- cyclical pain
			- vaginal distension causing:
				- back pain
				- painful urination
				- painful defaecation

		- Retained foreign body
			- vaginal discharge
			- intermittent bleeding/spotting

		- Functional abdominal pain
			- periumbilical/diffuse pain
			- variable location
			- worsened by stress
			- chronic symptoms
			- functional impairment disproportionate to pain

- Investigations

	- many non-gynaecological causes need no investigations

	- Pregnancy testing
		- ßhCG:
			- urine
			- blood if quantitative result needed
		- obtain consent

	- If significant bleeding
		- assess/manage as heavy menstrual bleeding

	- Suspected critical cause
		- ovarian/adnexal torsion:
			- urgent gynaecology referral
			- do not delay for ultrasound

	- Pelvic ultrasound
		- transabdominal
			- full bladder
		- transvaginal
			- sexually active adolescents
			- consent required

	- Suspected infection
		- urine MCS
			- exclude UTI
		- STI testing:
			- first-pass urine NAAT
				- chlamydia
				- gonorrhoea
				- trichomonas
			- self-collected vaginal swab
			- clinician-collected endocervical swab
		- FBE
		- CRP
		- blood cultures if concern for:
			- PID
			- sepsis

- Treatment
	- depends on underlying cause

- Consult paediatric team when
	- haemodynamic instability
	- severe pain
	- unclear diagnosis

- Consult gynaecology/surgical team when
	- ovarian/adnexal torsion suspected
	- ectopic pregnancy suspected
	- miscarriage/non-viable pregnancy
	- surgical pathology
	- PID

- Consider transfer when
	- escalation beyond local capability required

- Discharge criteria
	- clinically stable
	- GP review arranged
