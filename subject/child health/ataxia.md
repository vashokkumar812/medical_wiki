# Assessment
  - Thorough evaluation for serious and treatable causes including:
    - CNS infection or inflammation
    - Trauma
    - Stroke
    - Toxin ingestion
    - Mass lesions
  - Post-infectious acute cerebellar ataxia is the most common cause
    - Self-limiting
    - Diagnosis of exclusion
  - Some conditions present with an unsteady gait due to [[weakness]] or pain
  - A frightened or very unwell child may also appear ataxic

- **Background**
  - Ataxia = unsteadiness, imbalance or clumsiness due to uncoordinated muscle movements
  - Presents as:
    - Abnormal gait
    - Speech changes
    - Motor skill problems
### Classification & Causes of Ataxia

| **Type**                                   | **Causes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Acute** (<72 hrs, previously well child) | - Post-infectious* <br> - Toxins* <br> - Tumours* <br> - Trauma* <br> - Metabolic <br> - Infections <br> - Vascular (may require urgent lysis, see Stroke) <br> - Immune (e.g. ADEM, Guillain Barre Syndrome) <br> - Functional neurological disorder |
| **Chronic**                                | - Brain tumours <br> - Hydrocephalus <br> - Metabolic <br> - Nutritional <br> - Congenital malformations <br> - Hereditary ataxia <br> - Trauma <br> - Functional neurological disorder                                                               |
| **Recurrent**                              | - Episodic ataxia <br> - Basilar artery [[migraine]] <br> - Seizure disorder <br> - Metabolic <br> - Demyelinating pathology <br> - Functional neurological disorder                                                                                      |

\*Most common causes
### Common Causes of Acute Ataxia

| **Cause** | **Timing** | **Clinical Features** |
|-----------|------------|------------------------|
| **Post-infectious acute cerebellar ataxia** <br> (Viral: varicella, EBV, HHV6, enteroviruses; Mycoplasma) | 5–10 days after prodromal illness | - Common in children 2–7 yrs <br> - Features maximal at onset, usually improve within 48–72 hrs <br> - Full recovery in 10–21 days (>90% by 2 months) |
| **Toxins** <br> (Antihistamines, antiepileptics, ethanol, ethylene glycol, isopropyl, sedative-hypnotics) | Variable, dose-related | - History of access to meds/toxins/environment <br> - Altered GCS or toxidrome |
| **Tumours** <br> (Oedema, obstructive hydrocephalus, haemorrhage) | Acute or acute on chronic | - Normal conscious level <br> - Features of raised ICP <br> - Constitutional symptoms <br> - Focal neurological deficits |
| **Trauma** <br> (Haemorrhage, contusion, diffuse axonal injury, concussion) | Both acute & chronic | - History/clinical signs of head injury <br> - Risk factors for NAI |
| **ADEM** <br> (Autoimmune demyelination of CNS) | 1–4 weeks after febrile illness | - Lethargy, irritability <br> - Multiple neuro deficits: pyramidal signs, optic neuritis, hemiplegia, ataxia |
### Assessment

#### History
- **Time course**
  - Acute (<72 hours)
- **Antecedents**
  - Recent viral illness
  - Rash
  - Head trauma
  - Earache
- **Symptoms of ataxia**
  - Clumsiness, falls, broad-based or staggering gait
  - Difficulty sitting upright
  - Slurred speech
  - Swallowing difficulties
- **Other neurological symptoms**
  - Behavioural change
  - Photophobia
  - Vertigo
- **Family history**
  - Metabolic disease
  - Hereditary ataxia
  - [[migraine]]
  - Seizure disorder
- **Exposures**
  - Drugs/toxins
  - Environmental exposures

#### Examination
- Conscious state, orientation
- Thorough neurological examination (essential)
- **Exclude signs of meningoencephalitis**
  - Fever
  - Meningism
- **Features suggestive of cerebellar ataxia**
  - Cerebellar signs (dysdiadochokinesis, dysmetria, dysarthria, tremor, nystagmus)
  - Gait abnormalities (staggering and/or wide-based)
- **Features suggestive of sensory ataxia**
  - Positive Romberg test
  - Loss of light touch, vibration, proprioception
  - Hyporeflexia
- **Features suggestive of vestibular ataxia**
  - Nystagmus without other cerebellar signs

#### Red flag features
- Signs of raised intracranial pressure
- Altered conscious state
- Focal neurology
- Meningism
- Loss of proprioception, vibration sense, tactile discrimination
- [[weakness]]
- Abnormal deep tendon reflexes

### Investigations
- If features are consistent with post-infectious acute cerebellar ataxia, then no investigations may be necessary  

- If ataxia does not resolve within 72 hours, worsens any time after onset or another cause is suspected, consider:  

**Bloods**
- Blood glucose  
- Venous Blood gas  
- Electrolytes  
- Liver function tests  
- Anticonvulsant level, ethanol  

**Imaging**
- Neuroimaging (CT or MRI depending on likely cause and availability)  

**Other**
- Toxicology  
- Metabolic screening  
- Viral studies  
- Vitamin B12 levels  
- Lumbar puncture (after neuroimaging)  
- EEG  

---

### Treatment
- Treatment will depend on cause, children with a presentation consistent with post-infectious acute cerebellar ataxia generally require no treatment  

#### Consider consultation with local paediatric team when
- A child with an acute ataxia of unclear aetiology  
- A child with chronic or recurrent ataxia  

#### Consider transfer when
- Other focal neurological signs  
- Raised ICP present  
- History not typical of post-infectious acute cerebellar ataxia or aetiology is unclear  
- Child requiring care beyond the comfort level of the hospital  
- For emergency advice and paediatric or neonatal ICU transfers, see Retrieval Services  

#### Consider discharge when
- The cause of ataxia is clear, the child is clinically well and there is a follow up arranged
