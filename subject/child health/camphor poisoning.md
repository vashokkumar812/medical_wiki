- key points  
  - camphor is highly toxic  
  - onset of symptoms  
    - within 15 minutes  
    - ingestion >30 mg/kg  
  - neurologic complications common  
    - seizures  
      - within 90 minutes  
  - 24-hour advice  
    - victorian poisons information centre  
      - 13 11 26  

- background  
  - camphor  
    - highly toxic  
    - readily absorbed from skin  
    - most toxicity → ingestion  
  - common sources  
    - vicks inhaler  
    - rubs  
      - dencorub  
      - tiger balm  
      - emu oils  
      - vicks vaporub  
    - mothballs  
      - may contain  
        - naphthalene  
        - paradichlorobenzene  
  - poisons centre advice  
    - not recommended  
    - no proven benefit  
    - unsafe in homes  
  - co-toxicity  
    - other ingredients may be toxic  
      - e.g. salicylates  

- pharmacokinetics  
  - onset  
    - 5–15 minutes post ingestion  
  - peak levels  
    - 1–3 hours  
  - resolution  
    - within 24 hours  
  - metabolism  
    - oxidised and conjugated in liver  
  - excretion  
    - renal  
  - dose toxicity  
    - 0.5–1 g  
      - lethal in children  

- children requiring assessment  
  - deliberate self-poisoning  
  - significant accidental ingestion  
  - any symptomatic patient  
  - dose thresholds  
    - >10 mg/kg  
      - GIT irritation  
      - CNS depression  
    - >30 mg/kg  
      - serious toxicity  
  - concern for non-accidental poisoning  
    - developmental age inconsistent  

- risk assessment  

  - history  
    - intentional vs accidental  
    - dose taken  
    - calculate mg/kg  
    - co-ingestants  
      - e.g. paracetamol  

  - examination  
    - characteristic odour  
    - neurologic  
      - seizures  
        - within 90 minutes  
      - preceding features  
        - muscle fasciculation  
        - confusion  
        - delirium  
        - hallucinations  
        - restlessness  
    - respiratory  
      - depression  
        - often post-seizure  
    - GIT  
      - oral burning  
      - epigastric burning  
      - nausea  
      - vomiting  
    - CVS  
      - cardiomyopathy (case reports)  
      - prolonged QRS  
      - prolonged QTc  
    - always check  
      - medicalert bracelet  
      - signs of underlying disease  
        - fingerprick marks  

- investigations  
  - paracetamol level  
    - all intentional overdoses  
  - ECG  
  - CXR  
    - if symptomatic  

- acute management  

  - resuscitation  
    - manage seizures  

  - decontamination  
    - wash skin  
    - remove clothes  
    - charcoal  
      - not recommended  

  - observation  
    - if asymptomatic  
      - observe 4 hours  
      - discharge if no symptoms  

- consultation (paediatric team)  
  - intentional overdose  
  - significant/persistent vomiting or diarrhoea  
  - seizures  
  - decreased conscious state  
  - respiratory depression  
  - contact  
    - victorian poisons information centre  
      - 13 11 26  

- transfer indications  
  - persistent seizures  
  - decreased conscious state  
  - requires respiratory support  
  - care beyond local capacity  
  - PIPER service  
    - 1300 137 650  

- discharge criteria  
  - normal conscious state  
  - stable after 4 hours symptom-free  
  - low risk of further self-harm
