# NPA: Admission of Oncology Patients with Known or Suspected Respiratory Infections

## Background
- Most viral respiratory infections are transmitted via direct contact with respiratory droplets or fomites.  
- Influenza, parainfluenza, RSV, adenovirus, human metapneumovirus, and *Bordetella pertussis* may also be transmitted by large particle droplets created by coughing and sneezing.  
- Transmission of large-particle droplets requires close contact between source and recipient because droplets do not remain suspended in the air and generally travel only short distances (<1 m).  
- Special air handling and ventilation are not required to prevent droplet transmission.  
- Some viruses (e.g., RSV) can persist on hands or environmental surfaces for up to several hours.

---

## Management of Any Patient with Known or Suspected Respiratory Infections
- Patients with respiratory infections, including suspected or laboratory-confirmed viral infections, should be managed in a **single room** with their own ensuite or **cohorted** with patients with similar infections.  
- These patients should be kept away from those at **increased risk of complications** from viral infections:
  - Oncology/haematology patients  
  - Congenital cyanotic heart disease  
  - Chronic respiratory illness  
  - Congenital or acquired immunodeficiency  
  - Neonates  
- Early post-BMT patients are particularly vulnerable.  
- Negative pressure rooms may be used when not required for other airborne infections (e.g., tuberculosis, varicella/chickenpox, measles).  
- Positive pressure rooms should **not** be used, as airborne viruses can spill into connecting corridors.

---

## Management of an Oncology Patient with a Symptomatic Respiratory Infection
- Oncology patients with respiratory infections (suspected or confirmed) should **ideally be managed on wards other than the 6th floor**.  
  - This is the *Gold Standard* approach to limit nosocomial transmission.  
- The Oncology Unit (6th floor) should only be used when:
  - Other wards are unavailable, or  
  - Specialist oncology care is required.  
- Examples where management on the 6th floor is appropriate:
  - High-risk treatment protocols (e.g., ALL in induction, AML, stage IV neuroblastoma, pre/post HSCT, relapse disease).  
- Patients must be in a **single room** (or occasionally double if both have the same confirmed virus) with their own ensuite.  
- The door should remain closed.

### To Avoid Nosocomial Transmission
- **Contact Precautions** are recommended for all respiratory illnesses:
  - Gown for close patient contact.  
  - Strict hand hygiene.  
- **Droplet Precautions** are additionally required for:
  - RSV, influenza, parainfluenza, human metapneumovirus, adenovirus, *B. pertussis*.  
  - Mask during aerosol-generating procedures (e.g., suctioning, chest physio).  
- Parents:
  - Are at risk of infection.  
  - Should limit contact with other families.  
  - Must observe hand hygiene when leaving the room.  
  - If symptomatic, should wear a mask when exiting.  
- **NPA or nasal swab** (if thrombocytopenic):
  - Perform only if clinically indicated and after medical review.  
  - Results are not required before ward transfer from ED in symptomatic patients.

---

## Management of an Asymptomatic Oncology Patient with Recently Documented Viral Respiratory Infection
- Immunosuppressed patients may shed virus for prolonged periods and act as reservoirs of transmission.  
- **Asymptomatic patients with evidence of viral shedding:**
  - Manage with **contact precautions**.  
  - Can be admitted to the 6th floor if in a **single room** (or double if both have same confirmed virus) with own ensuite.  
- **Asymptomatic and non-neutropenic** patients:
  - May be placed with non-immunocompromised patients if contact precautions are maintained.  
- **Asymptomatic patients with confirmed respiratory virus in the last 8 weeks:**
  - Manage based on the most recent NPA result if a repeat is not available.  
  - Do not delay admission from ED while awaiting repeat NPA.  
- Following admission:
  - Contact precautions can be stopped if repeat results are negative.  
  - Specimens for respiratory viral testing should not be performed more frequently than weekly.
