# Acute Pain Management (Child)

## ⭐ Key Points
- **Regular assessment + reassessment** is essential
- Use **combined non-pharmacological + pharmacological strategies**
- Follow a **graded pharmacological approach**

---

## 📌 Background
- Pain is difficult to distinguish from **anxiety/distress**, especially in **pre-/non-verbal children**
- Consider:
  - Age
  - Cognitive ability
  - Environment
  - Cause of pain
- **Undertreated pain →**
  - Poor future pain tolerance
  - Negative healthcare experiences
  - Impaired healing & development

---

## 🩺 Assessment

### General Principles
- **Proactive + repeated assessment**
- Reassess after interventions (**based on drug onset time**)

### History / Communication
- Use **age-appropriate terms**:
  - “ouchy”, “hurting”, “yucky”
- Ask:
  - Pain severity
  - Prior effective strategies

### Behavioural Assessment
- Activity level
- Quiet/withdrawn child may still be in pain

### Physiological Assessment
- Heart rate
- Blood pressure
- Movement
- Activity level

### Other Important Inputs
- **Parental report**
- **Cultural differences in pain expression**

## 💊 Management Principles
- Combine **non-drug + drug therapy**
- Use **regular dosing (not PRN)** for persistent pain
- Use **paracetamol + ibuprofen together (synergistic)**
- Consider **long-acting agents** if ongoing pain expected
- **Moderate–severe pain → early opioids**

---

## 🌿 Non-Pharmacological Management

### General Strategies
- Parental presence + comforting touch
- Distraction:
  - Video, music, toys, bubbles, storytelling
- Child life / play therapy
- Breathing techniques

### Infants
- Swaddling
- Feeding
- Skin-to-skin
- Dummy

### Physical Methods
- Tactile stimulation (rubbing, pressure, “Buzzy”)

### Injury-Specific
- Immobilisation (splint/backslab)
- Ice + elevation
- Early burn dressing
- Consider **nerve block** (limb/finger)

---

## 💉 Pharmacological Management

### 🧴 Local / Topical Analgesia

| Context | Agent |
|--------|------|
| Limb/finger injury | Lignocaine, bupivacaine, ropivacaine |
| Open wounds | ALA / Laceraine® |
| Gingivostomatitis | Lignocaine gel |
| Mouth ulcers | Triamcinolone (Kenalog®) |
| Eye pain | Topical anaesthetic (exam only) |
| Ear pain | 2% lignocaine / Auralgan® |
| Procedures (IV, LP etc.) | EMLA®, LMX4®, AnGel® |
| Nasal procedures | CoPhenylcaine Forte® |

---

### 💊 Systemic Analgesia (Stepwise)

#### 🟢 Mild Pain
- **Paracetamol**
  - PO preferred
  - 15 mg/kg 4–6 hourly
- **Ibuprofen (>3 months)**
  - 10 mg/kg 6–8 hourly

---

#### 🟡 Moderate Pain
- Paracetamol + Ibuprofen (regular)
- Add **oral opioid (e.g. oxycodone)**

---

#### 🔴 Severe Pain
- **IV opioids early**
  - Morphine
  - Fentanyl (intranasal for rapid effect)

---

### ⚡ Key Drug Notes
- **Paracetamol**
  - Onset: 30 min (PO), 5–10 min (IV)
- **Ibuprofen**
  - Avoid: renal impairment, dehydration
- **Oxycodone**
  - Short-term use only
- **Morphine**
  - Rapid onset (5–10 min)
- **Fentanyl (IN)**
  - Onset ~5 min
- **Sucrose**
  - Effective in infants (<6 months)

---

## ⚠️ Special Considerations

### Ongoing Severe Pain
- Always combine **opioids + simple analgesia**
- Consider:
  - Compartment syndrome
  - Need for regional block / PCA

---

### Renal Impairment
- Prefer **fentanyl > morphine**
- Reduce oxycodone dose
- Monitor for accumulation

---

### Opioid Safety
- Requires **close monitoring**
- Risks: respiratory depression

---

### Opioid Toxicity
- Airway + oxygen + ventilation
- **Naloxone**:
  - 10 mcg/kg initially
  - Escalate if no response

---

## 🧾 Prescribing Principles
- Keep courses **short**
- Involve GP if >1 week
- Prescribe **aperients with opioids**
- Check prescribing databases

---

## 👨‍⚕️ Escalation

### Consult Paediatrics
- Inadequate analgesia despite treatment

### Transfer
- Beyond local care capability

---

## ✅ Discharge Criteria
- Pain adequately controlled
- Anticipated pain addressed
