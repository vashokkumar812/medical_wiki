- Gastrostomy acute replacement of displaced tubes

- Key principles
	- displaced gastrostomy tubes require urgent attention
	- replacement tube/device should be inserted within 4 hours
	- if tract closes:
		- surgical replacement may be required

- Timing since initial insertion

	- <2 months since insertion
		- tract may not be mature
		- contact original inserting team:
			- gastroenterology
			- general surgery
		- seek specialist advice before replacement

	- >2 months since insertion
		- replacement generally safe

	- previous difficult replacements
		- seek gastroenterology/general surgery advice before reinsertion

- Personnel
	- balloon tubes:
		- may be replaced by experienced staff/parents
		- in ED:
			- senior medical staff only
				- fellow/consultant
	- non-balloon low-profile tubes:
		- usually replaced under anaesthesia + endoscopic confirmation
		- should NOT be acutely replaced
		- temporary tube still required within 4 hours to maintain tract

- Types of tubes

	- Non-balloon
		- Bard button
			- duration up to 5 years
				- average ~2 years
		- Malecot tube

	- Balloon
		- MIC-key
			- duration 3–6 months
		- Corpak
		- long balloon tube
		- short balloon/button tube

- Suitable replacement devices
	- original tube if intact
	- replacement gastrostomy tube
	- Foley catheter
		- use largest snug-fitting size possible
		- adult Foley may be required
		- loose tube:
			- replace with larger size
		- larger tube may avoid tract dilation surgery

- Important tube considerations
	- original tube preferred where possible
	- use alternative tube/Foley if:
		- tract damaged
		- tract narrowed
	- skin flange:
		- snug against skin
		- avoid excessive tightness
			- risk of pressure necrosis

- Checking balloon
	- balloon tubes have:
		- feeding lumen
		- balloon inflation lumen
	- before insertion:
		- inflate balloon
		- ensure no leak

- Analgesia/sedation
	- reinsertion may be painful
	- options:
		- parenteral opioids
		- rectal paracetamol
		- topical viscous lignocaine
		- nitrous oxide sedation

- Cleaning site
	- site/device should be:
		- clean
		- not necessarily sterile

- Tube insertion technique
	- deflate balloon before insertion
	- lubricate with water-based lubricant
	- insert smoothly into tract
	- snug fit expected
	- gentle twisting may help
	- avoid forceful insertion

- Warning signs during insertion
	- excessive resistance:
		- may indicate wrong angle
		- risk of:
			- false passage
			- peritoneal placement

- Balloon inflation
	- 5 mL balloon:
		- inflate with ~4.5 mL water
	- 10 mL balloon:
		- inflate with ~7 mL water

- Foley catheter insertion
	- insert ~5 cm
		- varies with patient size
	- inflate balloon
	- pull back gently until resistance felt

- Confirming tube placement
	- always:
		- aspirate gastric contents
		- test aspirate with litmus paper
	- obtain dye study if:
		- firm resistance encountered
		- multiple insertion attempts required

- Caution
	- if uncertain about placement:
		- contact on-call gastroenterologist
		- consider:
			- x-ray
			- dye study

- Restarting feeds
	- if placement confirmed:
		- feeds may restart immediately
	- if confirmation delayed:
		- assess hydration/fluid status
		- provide IV fluids if required

- Complications after replacement

	- Ileus
		- features:
			- abdominal pain
			- reduced bowel sounds
			- bloating

	- Leakage into peritoneum
		- features:
			- pain/distress
			- abdominal distension

	- Management if complications suspected
		- stop feeds immediately
		- contact on-call gastroenterologist
