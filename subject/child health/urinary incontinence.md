- **Definition**
	* urinary incontinence:  
	  * day wetting \>5 years  
	  * 1 episode/month for ≥3 months  
	* normal continence:  
	  * achieved by \~4 years  
* epidemiology:    
	* decreases with age
* **Normal Bladder Capacity**
	* Capacity (mL) = (age + 2) x 30

- causes
	-  Functional Causes
		* overactive bladder (OAB)  
		  * key feature
			  * urgency  
		* voiding postponement  
		  * habitual delay → overfilling → leakage  
		* underactive bladder  
		  * infrequent voiding  
		  * overflow incontinence  
		  * large post-void residual  
		* dysfunctional voiding (non-neurogenic)  
		  * failure to relax sphincter/pelvic floor  
		  * interrupted flow  
		  * prolonged voiding
- red flags
	* never been dry  
		* anatomical abnoramility
	* continuous incontinence/dribbling (not intermittent)  
		* * anatomical abnoramility
	* urinary retention  
	* spraying urinary stream  
	* excessive tiredness or weight loss  
	* polydipsia or polyuria  
	* growth faltering / weight loss / hypertension  
	* distended bladder or renal mass
* check for holding manoeuvres:  
  * tiptoeing  
  * leg crossing  
  * squatting with heel pressure  
  * girls  
	  * urethro-vaginal reflux  
	  * leakage after voiding on standing  
	* boys  
	  * incomplete emptying  
	  * dysfunctional voiding  

- **Comorbidities**
	* constipation  
	* UTI  
	* enuresis  
	* excessive tiredness / weight loss  
	  * consider chronic illness / renal disease  
	* polydipsia / polyuria  
	  * diabetes mellitus  
	  * diabetes insipidus  
	  * renal tubular disease  
	  * psychogenic causes
* examination
	* perineum / genitalia  
	  * inspect external genitalia  
	  * perianal exam if constipation  
	* exclude epispadias  
	  * boys: dorsal urethral opening  
	  * girls: patulous urethra  
	* spine / lower back  
	  * occult spinal dysraphism  
	  * tethered cord  
	* neurology  
	  * lower limb exam  
	  * gait observation

- Investigations
	- Routine Investigations
		- Urinalysis and culture
		- Repeat urinalysis if:
			- Previous UTIs
			- Concern for renal/metabolic disease
	- Secondary Investigations
		- Renal tract ultrasound
			- Pre-void and post-void residual
		- Indications
			- Failure of initial behavioural management
			- Red flags present
				- Continuous incontinence
				- Recurrent UTIs
		- Established daytime incontinence
			- Review previous ultrasound
			- Repeat if:
				- Inadequate
				- Not recent
- Initial (Standard) Management
	- Behavioural Modification
		- First-line management
		- Includes
			- Education of child and parents
				- LUT anatomy and function
				- Demystification of pathophysiology
				- Therapeutic approaches
			- Micturition behaviour
				- Timed voiding
				- Avoid holding manoeuvres
				- Optimal voiding posture
			- Bladder diary
				- Reference source
				- Documentation of progress
			- Fluid intake and diet
				- Drink with each meal and snack
				- Spread fluids across the day
				- Follow routine nutritional guidelines
	- Re-education
		- Address adherence issues
		- Re-educate if required
		- Active management for 3 months
			- Allows ~2 cycles of re-education
		- If unsuccessful
			- Refer to local paediatrician
- Treat Comorbidities
	- Constipation and/or soiling
		- Treat simultaneously
	- UTI
		- Treat simultaneously
		- Recurrent UTIs
			- Refer to local paediatrician

	- Enuresis
		- Treat daytime symptoms first
		- Especially in combined day-night wetting

- Pharmacological Management for Overactive Bladder (OAB)

	- Second-line treatment
	- Commenced by:
		- Local paediatric team
		- Continence service
	- Continue behavioural modification throughout treatment

	- Oxybutynin
		- First-line pharmacological option
		- Oral route preferred

		- Transdermal route
			- Consider if:
				- Unable to swallow tablets
				- Intolerant to oral oxybutynin
			- Do not cut/divide patches
				- Alters drug release characteristics

- Practice Points

	- Monitor adverse effects
		- Constipation
		- Dry mouth
		- Flushing

	- Consider laxatives
		- Constipation may worsen incontinence

	- Clinical response
		- Expected within 2 months

	- Treatment duration
		- Usually 3–6 months
		- Gradual weaning
		- Regular reassessment

	- Monitor for:
		- Incomplete bladder emptying
		- Urinary retention
		- Especially with ongoing anticholinergic therapy

- Consider Consultation with Local Paediatric Team / Continence Service When

	- Red flags for other disorders present
		- Refer appropriately

	- Refer to Urology
		- Suspected anatomical abnormality
			- Continuous wetting
		- Abnormal imaging findings

	- Refer to Nephrologist / Paediatrician
		- Significant haematuria
		- Significant proteinuria
		- Polyuria / polydipsia
			- After excluding diabetes mellitus

	- Concurrent mental health referral
		- Significant behavioural concerns
		- Psychological comorbidity
