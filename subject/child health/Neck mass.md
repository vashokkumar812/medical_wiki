- Red flags for cancer
	- Symptoms >2 weeks
	- Recent voice change
	- Dysphagia
	- Odynophagia
	- Ipsilateral otalgia
	- Nasal obstruction
	- Epistaxis
	- Loss of weight
	- Loss of appetite

- Risk factors for cancer
	- Age >40 years
	- Smoking
	- Alcohol use
	- Previous head and neck malignancy
	- Cutaneous lesions

- HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer
	- Risk factors
		- Younger age
		- Male sex
		- Higher number of
			- Oral sexual partners
			- Vaginal sexual partners
		- Little/no tobacco exposure
		- Low alcohol consumption
		- Marijuana use
		- Higher education level
		- Higher socioeconomic status

	- Presentation
		- May be asymptomatic
		- Cystic lymph node
		- Common misdiagnosis
			- Branchial cleft cyst

	- Examination
		- Otoscopy
			- Unilateral middle ear effusion may suggest nasopharyngeal carcinoma

### Ancillary investigations and rationale

| **Ancillary investigation**                                 | **Rationale**                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full blood examination                                      | infection , lymphoma                                          |
| Anti-neutrophil antibody (ANA)                              | autoimmune disease                                            |
| Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)                        | autoimmune disease , chronic inflammation                     |
| Thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH)                           | Thyroid pathology (e.g. multinodular goitre, Graves’ disease) |
| Parathyroid hormone (PTH)                                   | parathyroid adenoma                                           |
| Thyroid ultrasonography                                     | thyroid nodules or parathyroid adenomas                       |
| CT chest with contrast                                      | lung malignancy, tuberculosis, or sarcoidosis                 |
| Specific infection tests (e.g. HIV, EBV, CMV, tuberculosis) | infectious cause                                              |
