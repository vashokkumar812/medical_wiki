#### Primary vs secondary osteoporosis

| Feature             | Primary osteoporosis           | Secondary osteoporosis             |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Cause               | Age or hormone related         | Due to underlying disease or drugs |
| Common age          | Elderly                        | Any age                            |
| Sex                 | Common in postmenopausal women | Both sexes                         |
| Onset               | Gradual                        | Often earlier or sudden            |
| Estrogen role       | Estrogen deficiency common     | Not primary cause                  |
| Associated diseases | None                           | Endocrine, renal, GI, inflammatory |
| Drug induced        | No                             | Yes (steroids, anticonvulsants)    |
| Bone loss pattern   | Trabecular > cortical          | Depends on cause                   |
| Examples            | Postmenopausal, senile         | Steroid-induced, hyperthyroidism   |
| Management          | Anti-osteoporotic drugs        | Treat cause + bone therapy         |


- Osteoporosis
	- Epidemiology / risk factors
		- Female sex
		- Ageing
			- women >50 years
			- men >60 years
			- associated sarcopenia
		- Thin build
		- Low BMI <20
		- Asian/Caucasian ethnicity
		- Family history
			- maternal hip fracture <75 years
		- Early menopause <45 years
		- Oestrogen deficiency/amenorrhoea
		- Smoking
		- Alcohol >2 standard drinks/day
		- Vitamin D deficiency
		- Low calcium intake
		- Physical inactivity
		- Prolonged immobilisation

	- Secondary causes
		- Endocrine
			- Cushing syndrome
			- diabetes mellitus
			- hyperparathyroidism
			- thyrotoxicosis
			- hypogonadism
			- acromegaly
		- Malabsorption
			- coeliac disease
		- Eating disorders
			- anorexia nervosa
		- Rheumatoid arthritis
		- Chronic organ failure
			- renal
			- liver
			- cardiac
			- pulmonary

	- Drugs causing osteoporosis
		- Corticosteroids
			- >3 months
			- ≥7.5 mg/day
		- Enzyme-inducing antiepileptics
		- PPIs
		- Thiazolidinediones
		- Long-term heparin
		- Excess thyroxine replacement
		- Hormonal therapy
			- prostate cancer
			- breast cancer

	- Diagnosis
		- Gold standard
			- DEXA scan
		- Measures
			- bone mineral density (BMD)
		- Main sites
			- lumbar spine
			- proximal femur/femoral neck
				- most useful
		- Lower BMD → higher fracture risk
		- X-ray
			- poor sensitivity
			- changes only after 40–50% bone loss
			- useful for fractures
		- Consider myeloma screen
			- focal/atypical osteoporosis
		- Fracture risk assessment
			- FRAX tool

	- T score
		- Used in adults ≥50 years
		- Compared to healthy 30-year-old BMD
		- Interpretation
			- ≥ −1
				- normal
			- −1 to −2.5
				- osteopenia
			- ≤ −2.5
				- osteoporosis
			- < −2.5 + fracture
				- severe osteoporosis
		- Treat if T score < −2.5

	- Z score
		- Used in
			- age <50 years
			- premenopausal women
			- younger men
			- children
		- Z score < −2
			- suggests secondary cause

	- BMD subsidised indications
		- Age >70 years
		- Low-trauma fracture
		- High-risk drugs/conditions
		- Low BMI
		- Monitoring intervals
			- 1–5 years depending on risk

	- Treatment principles
		- Treat confirmed osteoporosis
			- reduce hip + vertebral fractures
		- First-line
			- bisphosphonates
			- denosumab
		- Men
			- bisphosphonates if clinical osteoporosis
		- Osteopenia
			- treat only if high fracture risk
		- Do not routinely use menopausal hormone therapy solely for osteoporosis
		- Routine BMD monitoring during first 5 years not needed

	- Bisphosphonates
		- inhibit bone resorption
		- adverse effects
			- oesophagitis
			- jaw osteonecrosis
		- Alendronate
			- 70 mg weekly oral
			- empty stomach
		- Risedronate
			- 35 mg weekly or 150 mg monthly
		- Zoledronic acid
			- annual IV infusion
		- Duration
			- oral 5 years
			- IV 3 years

	- Denosumab
		- monoclonal antibody
		- 60 mg SC every 6 months
		- ensure adequate calcium + vitamin D
		- adverse effects
			- hypocalcaemia
			- jaw osteonecrosis
			- may worsen heart failure

	- Other drugs
		- Raloxifene
			- SERM
			- 60 mg daily
		- Teriparatide
			- second-line
			- increases bone formation
			- 20 mcg SC daily
		- HRT
			- not long-term therapy

	- Prevention
		- Stop smoking
		- Alcohol ≤2 standard drinks/day
		- Maintain BMI 18–25
		- Weight-bearing exercise
			- brisk walking
				- 30 min
				- 4 times/week
			- jogging
			- basketball
			- tennis
			- dancing
			- gymnastics
		- Falls prevention strategies

	- Calcium
		- Daily requirement
			- women >50 years
				- 1300 mg/day
			- men >70 years
				- 1300 mg/day
			- younger adults
				- 1000 mg/day
		- Diet preferred over supplements
		- Dairy = main source
		- Calcium citrate absorbed better than carbonate
		- Carbonate taken with food

	- Calcium-rich foods
		- fortified milk
		- yoghurt
		- cheese
		- fish with bones
		- nuts/seeds

	- Vitamin D
		- target >50 nmol/L
		- deficiency
			- mild
				- 30–49
			- moderate
				- 12.5–29
			- severe
				- <12.5
		- Supplement
			- cholecalciferol 1000–2000 IU/day
		- Sun exposure
			- summer
				- 6–7 min
			- winter
				- 7–40 min

	- Monitoring
		- Repeat BMD
			- 2 years after starting treatment
			- 1–2 years after therapy change
			- earlier if high risk
				- glucocorticoids
				- transplant

	- Osteoporosis in children
		- usually secondary
		- causes
			- steroids
			- inflammatory disease
			- immobility
			- malnutrition
		- rare primary
			- osteogenesis imperfecta
		- assessment
			- DEXA + Z scores
		- specialist referral required

	- Refer if
		- children/young adults
		- complex secondary osteoporosis
		- pathological fractures
		- significant height loss
