- Cardiac Murmurs in Children — AMC High-Yield

	- Definition
		- Turbulent blood flow causing audible cardiac vibrations
		- May be innocent (physiological) or pathological

	- Causes
		- Innocent (most common) ⭐
			- Normal flow through normal heart
		- Pathological
			- Congenital heart disease (CHD)
			- Rheumatic heart disease (RHD)
			- Acute rheumatic fever (ARF)
			- Other acquired heart disease

	- Epidemiology
		- ~50% of children have a murmur
		- <1% due to CHD ⭐
		- CHD incidence
			- ~9/1000 live births
		- Most common CHDs
			- VSD ⭐ (34%)
			- ASD
			- PDA
			- Pulmonary stenosis
			- TOF
			- Coarctation of aorta
			- TGA
			- Aortic stenosis

	- Red Flags in History ⭐
		- Poor feeding
		- Failure to thrive
		- Diaphoresis during feeds
		- Tachypnoea
		- Dyspnoea
		- Cyanosis
		- Low oxygen saturation
		- Recurrent chest infections
		- Exercise intolerance
		- Fatigue
		- Palpitations
		- Chest pain
		- Syncope/presyncope
		- Previous ARF/RHD
		- Kawasaki disease
		- Prematurity (PDA)
		- Positive family history
			- CHD
			- Sudden cardiac death
			- Marfan syndrome

	- Red Flags on Examination ⭐
		- Failure to thrive
		- Respiratory distress
		- Cyanosis
		- Clubbing
		- Oedema
		- Hepatomegaly
		- Weak femoral pulses
		- Radiofemoral delay
		- Shock
		- Arrhythmia
		- Abnormal S2
		- Thrill
		- Heave
		- Harsh murmur
		- Grade ≥3/6 murmur ⭐
		- Pansystolic murmur ⭐
		- Diastolic murmur ⭐
		- Murmur louder on standing ⭐

	- Innocent Murmur ⭐
		- Soft (<Grade 3)
		- Systolic
		- Short
		- Localised
		- No radiation
		- Loudest when supine
		- Decreases/disappears on standing ⭐
		- Normal S1/S2
		- No symptoms
		- Normal pulses
		- No cyanosis

	- Pathological Murmur ⭐
		- Grade ≥3
		- Harsh
		- Long
		- Pansystolic
		- Diastolic
		- Continuous
		- Radiates
		- Same/louder on standing
		- Click/opening snap
		- Abnormal S2
		- Associated symptoms/signs present

	- Classic Innocent Murmurs
		- Still's murmur
			- Lower left sternal border/apex
		- Pulmonary flow murmur
			- Upper left sternal border
		- Venous hum
			- Continuous
			- Supraclavicular area
		- Peripheral pulmonary stenosis
			- Neonates

	- Important Murmur Associations ⭐

		- VSD
			- Harsh pansystolic murmur
			- Lower left sternal border

		- ASD
			- Wide fixed split S2 ⭐
			- Ejection systolic murmur
			- Upper left sternal border

		- PDA
			- Continuous "machinery" murmur ⭐
			- Upper left sternal border
			- Bounding pulses

		- Coarctation of aorta
			- Weak femoral pulses ⭐
			- Radiofemoral delay
			- Upper limb hypertension

		- Pulmonary stenosis
			- Ejection systolic murmur
			- Upper left sternal border
			- Ejection click

		- Aortic stenosis
			- Ejection systolic murmur
			- Upper right sternal border
			- Radiates to neck

		- Tetralogy of Fallot
			- Cyanosis
			- Single S2
			- Ejection systolic murmur
			- Murmur softer during hypercyanotic spell ⭐

		- TGA
			- Severe neonatal cyanosis
			- Loud single S2
			- Often no murmur ⭐

	- Syndromes Associated with CHD ⭐
		- Down syndrome
			- AVSD
			- VSD
		- Turner syndrome
			- Coarctation
			- Bicuspid aortic valve
		- DiGeorge syndrome
			- TOF
			- Truncus arteriosus
		- Noonan syndrome
			- Pulmonary stenosis
		- Williams syndrome
			- Supravalvular aortic stenosis
		- Marfan syndrome
			- Aortic root dilatation
			- Mitral valve prolapse

	- Investigations
		- Pulse oximetry ⭐
			- Especially neonates
		- Echocardiography ⭐ (gold standard)
		- ECG
			- Arrhythmia
			- Chamber hypertrophy
		- Chest X-ray
			- Cardiomegaly
			- Pulmonary vascularity
			- Characteristic heart shapes

	- Refer to Paediatric Cardiology ⭐
		- Any murmur <3 months old
		- Any pathological murmur
		- Cyanosis
		- Failure to thrive
		- Cardiac symptoms
		- Abnormal pulses
		- Syndromic child
		- Family history of CHD/sudden death
		- Suspected ARF/RHD

	- AMC Pearls ⭐
		- Innocent murmur = Soft, systolic, disappears on standing
		- Diastolic murmur = Always pathological
		- Grade ≥3 murmur = Pathological until proven otherwise
		- Wide fixed split S2 = ASD
		- Continuous machinery murmur + bounding pulses = PDA
		- Harsh pansystolic murmur at LLSB = VSD
		- Weak femoral pulses = Coarctation of aorta
		- Murmur + cyanosis = Consider TOF/TGA
		- Normal examination does not exclude CHD
		- Echocardiography = Investigation of choice
