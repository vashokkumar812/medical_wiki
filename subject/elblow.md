# Medial Epicondylitis (Golfer's Elbow)
## Epidemiology
- less common than tennis elbow
- Most common age
	- 30–40 years
## Pathophysiology
- tendinopathy of common flexor tendon (CFT)
- Repetitive elbow use
	- Repetitive gripping
	- Repetitive valgus loading

## Risk Factors
- Repetitive wrist flexion
- Repetitive forearm pronation
- Repetitive gripping
- Repetitive valgus stress
- Sports
- Occupations
	- Heavy lifting (>20 kg)
	- Repetitive forceful gripping
	- Exposure to vibration
	- Plumbers
	- Carpenters
	- Construction workers
##  Associated Conditions
- Ulnar neuropathy
- Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) insufficiency
- Carpal tunnel syndrome
- Lateral epicondylitis
- Rotator cuff tendinopathy

##  Clinical Features
- Medial elbow pain
	- onset
		- insidious
	- Worse
		- wrist and forearm movement
		- gripping
	- May have
		- Numbness in ulnar fingers
		- Tingling in ulnar fingers
- Examination
	- Tenderness
		- 5–10 mm distal and anterior to medial epicondyle
	- May have
		- Soft tissue swelling
		- Warmth
	- Provocative test
		- Pain with resisted wrist flexion
		- Pain with resisted forearm pronation
	- Assess for associated conditions
		- Ulnar neuropathy
			- Tinel sign over ulnar nerve
			- Reduced sensation in ulnar distribution
			- Hypothenar wasting
		- UCL injury
			- Valgus stress testing

## Investigations
- X-ray
	- Usually normal
	- May show calcification of
		- Common flexor tendon
		- UCL
- Ultrasound
	- Shows focal tendon degeneration
- MRI
	- Investigation of choice
	- Indications
		- Unclear diagnosis
		- Suspected UCL injury
		- Persistent medial elbow pain
	- Findings
		- Tendinosis of common flexor tendon
		- Tendon disruption
		- T2 hyperintensity
		- Peritendinous oedema
- Nerve conduction studies / EMG
	- If ulnar nerve compression suspected

## Management
- First-line (Conservative)
	- Rest
	- Ice
	- Activity modification
	- Avoid throwing for 6–12 weeks
	- Physiotherapy
		- Stretching exercises
	- Counterforce brace
	- Kinesiology taping
	- NSAIDs
- Corticosteroid Injection
	- May provide short-term symptom relief
	- Avoid repeated injections
	- Complications
		- Skin depigmentation
		- Subcutaneous atrophy
		- Tendon weakening/rupture
		- Ulnar nerve injury
- Surgery
	- Indications
		- Failure of ≥6 months conservative treatment
		- Severe symptoms affecting quality of life
		- Clear diagnosis
	- Procedure
		- Debridement of diseased flexor-pronator tissue
		- Reattachment of flexor-pronator origin
- Prognosis
	- Majority improve with conservative treatment


# Lateral Epicondylitis (Tennis Elbow)
## Definition
  - Enthesopathy involving the origin of the:
    - Extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB)
  - Degenerative tendinosis rather than an inflammatory condition

## Epidemiology
  - Peak incidence
    - 40–50 years
  - No sex predilection
  - Only ~10% of affected patients play tennis

## Risk Factors
  - Manual labour
  - Use of vibrating tools
  - Dominant arm involvement

## Pathophysiology

  - Overuse injury of extensor muscles

  - Degeneration at ECRB tendon insertion (enthesis)

  - Histological findings
    - Vascular proliferation
    - Hyaline degeneration
    - Fibroblastic proliferation
    - Calcific debris

## Clinical Features

  - Lateral elbow pain
    - Insidious onset
    - May radiate distally along extensor muscle mass
	  - Aggravated by
	    - Wrist extension against resistance
	    - Finger extension against resistance
	    - Lifting activities
	    - Forearm pronation

	  - Usually not associated with trauma

- Natural History

  - Self-limiting
  - Symptoms last
    - 6–24 months
  - Complete resolution
    - Within 1 year in most patients

- Examination

  - Local tenderness
    - Anterior and distal to lateral epicondyle
    - At origin of ECRB and extensor carpi radialis longus

  - Provocation Tests

    - Maudsley's Test

      - Method
        - Elbow flexed 90°
        - Forearm pronated
        - Patient extends middle finger against resistance
      - Positive test
        - Lateral elbow pain

    - Cozen's Test

      - Method
        - Elbow fully extended
        - Forearm pronated
        - Patient clenches fist and extends, pronates and radially deviates wrist against resistance
      - Positive test
        - Lateral elbow pain


## Investigations

  - Usually not required

  - Plain X-rays
    - Indications
      - Restricted range of motion
      - Crepitus
      - Loose body symptoms

    - Views
      - AP
      - Lateral
      - Radiocapitellar

  - Ultrasound
    - May show
      - Tendon thickening
      - Hypoechogenicity
      - Enthesis defects

  - MRI
    - May show
      - Tendon defects
      - Ligament tears
      - Signal changes

    - Not required for diagnosis
    - Imaging findings do not correlate with prognosis

## Management

  - General Principles
    - Most cases improve spontaneously
    - No treatment proven superior to natural history in long-term outcomes
    - Patient education is essential

  - Lifestyle Modification
    - Avoid aggravating activities

  - Analgesia
    - NSAIDs

  - Supportive Measures
    - Splinting/bracing
    - Physiotherapy
    - Occupational therapy

- Non-Operative Treatment

  - Eccentric Exercises

    - Improve pain and function
    - Significant benefit at 6 weeks

    - Eccentric contraction
      - Active muscle lengthening under load

  - Brace + Physiotherapy
    - More effective than either alone

  - Topical Glyceryl Trinitrate (GTN)
    - Reduced pain
    - Reduced local tenderness
    - Short- to medium-term benefit

  - Topical NSAIDs
    - Effective for short-term pain relief

  - Corticosteroid Injection

    - Better pain relief at 8 weeks

    - No benefit at 24 weeks

    - Worse pain outcomes at 12 months compared with placebo

  - Treatments with No Proven Long-Term Benefit
    - Physical therapy alone
    - Corticosteroid injections
    - Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)
    - Botulinum toxin injections
    - Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT)
    - Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS)
