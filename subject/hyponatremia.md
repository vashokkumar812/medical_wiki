- Hyponatraemia

	- Definition
		- Serum sodium <135 mmol/L

	- Normal Sodium
		- 135–145 mmol/L

	- Key Points
		- Assess fluid status first
			- Determines cause
			- Guides treatment
		- Hyponatraemic seizures are a medical emergency
			- May be refractory to anticonvulsants
			- Do not delay sodium correction
		- In non-seizing children:
			- Correction should not exceed:
				- 8 mmol/L in 24 hours
		- Target correction rate:
			- 6–8 mmol/L/24 hours
		- Rapid correction can cause:
			- Osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS)

	- Pathophysiology
		- Usually due to:
			- Excess water intake
			- Impaired free water excretion

		- Acute hyponatraemia
			- Water shifts into brain cells
			- Cerebral oedema

		- Chronic hyponatraemia (>48 hours)
			- Cerebral adaptation occurs
			- Symptoms may be subtle

	- Severity

		- Mild
			- Na 125–135 mmol/L
			- Usually asymptomatic

		- Moderate
			- Na 120–125 mmol/L
			- Nausea
			- Malaise

		- Severe
			- Na <120 mmol/L
			- Headache
			- Obtundation
			- Seizures

	- Classification by Volume Status

		- Hypovolaemic Hyponatraemia

			- GI losses + free water replacement
				- Gastroenteritis
				- Secretory diarrhoea
				- Osmotic diarrhoea

			- Skin losses
				- Burns
				- Cystic fibrosis

			- Third spacing
				- Intra-abdominal losses

			- Hyperglycaemia

			- Renal sodium loss
				- Thiazide diuretics
				- Cerebral salt wasting
				- Renal tubular disorders
				- Hypoaldosteronism
				- Metabolic alkalosis

		- Euvolaemic Hyponatraemia

			- SIADH

				- Pulmonary causes
					- Pneumonia
					- Bronchiolitis
					- Mechanical ventilation

				- CNS causes
					- Infection
					- Trauma
					- Tumour

				- Other triggers
					- Surgery
					- Pain
					- Trauma

				- Endocrine causes
					- Hypothyroidism
					- Adrenal insufficiency

			- Excess hypotonic fluid intake
				- Dilute formula
				- Oral rehydration solutions
				- Excess water intake

			- Psychogenic polydipsia

			- Medications
				- Cyclophosphamide
				- Vincristine
				- Platinum chemotherapy
				- Valproate
				- Carbamazepine
				- Oxcarbazepine
				- Vasopressin

		- Hypervolaemic Hyponatraemia

			- Excess IV fluids
			- Nephrotic syndrome
			- Cirrhosis
			- Heart failure
			- Acute renal failure
			- Chronic renal failure
			- Obstructive uropathy

	- Fluid Types

		- Isotonic Fluids
			- Sodium ≥125–160 mmol/L
			- Examples
				- 0.9% saline
				- Plasma-Lyte 148
				- Hartmann solution

		- Hypotonic Fluids
			- Sodium <125 mmol/L
			- Examples
				- Oral rehydration solution
				- Dilute formula
				- Free water

	- Clinical Features

		- Mild/Chronic
			- Weakness
			- Fatigue
			- Irritability
			- Restlessness

		- Progressive Symptoms
			- Nausea
			- Vomiting
			- Headache

		- Severe Neurological Features
			- Decreased consciousness
			- Obtundation
			- Seizures
			- Cerebral oedema

	- History

		- Fluid intake
		- Fluid losses
		- Recent infections
		- Trauma
		- Malignancy
		- Drugs causing SIADH
		- Neurological symptoms
		- Duration of symptoms

	- Red Flags
		- Nausea
		- Vomiting
		- Irritability
		- Headache
		- Reduced conscious state
		- Seizures

	- Examination

		- Fluid status assessment
			- Hypovolaemia
			- Euvolaemia
			- Hypervolaemia

		- Weight

		- Signs of cerebral oedema

		- Evidence of underlying disease
			- Infection
			- Malignancy

	- Investigations

		- Recommended if:
			- Na <130 mmol/L

		- Blood Tests
			- Serum osmolality
			- Blood glucose level
			- Blood gas if significantly unwell

		- Urine Tests
			- Urine osmolality
			- Urinary sodium

	- Pseudohyponatraemia

		- Exclude:
			- Hyperglycaemia
			- Mannitol therapy
			- Sorbitol administration

	- Corrected Sodium in Hyperglycaemia

		- Formula

			- Corrected Na =
				- Measured Na + (BSL − 5)/3

	- Management Principles

		- Determine:
			- Presence of seizures
			- Conscious state
			- Fluid status

		- Treat underlying cause

		- Monitor:
			- Strict fluid balance
			- Daily weight
			- Serial sodium measurements

		- Avoid overcorrection
			- Maximum:
				- 8 mmol/L in 24 hours

	- Hyponatraemic Seizures

		- Medical emergency

		- Priorities
			- Immediate sodium correction
			- Do not rely solely on anticonvulsants

		- Reason
			- Cerebral oedema is the underlying problem

	- Complications

		- Hyponatraemia
			- Cerebral oedema
			- Brain herniation
			- Seizures

		- Overcorrection
			- Osmotic demyelination syndrome

	- Consult Paediatric Team

		- Na <130 mmol/L
		- Symptomatic child
		- Correction >8 mmol/L/24 h
		- Complex fluid requirements
			- Parenteral nutrition

	- Transfer to Higher-Level Centre

		- Na <125 mmol/L
		- Seizures
		- Altered conscious state
		- Requires care beyond local capability

	- AMC Pearls

		- Most important initial step:
			- Assess volume status

		- Acute hyponatraemia
			- Causes cerebral oedema

		- Chronic hyponatraemia
			- Causes cerebral adaptation
			- Symptoms may be subtle

		- SIADH
			- Most common euvolaemic cause

		- Hyponatraemic seizures
			- Treat sodium, not just seizure

		- Maximum correction rate:
			- 8 mmol/L/day

		- Rapid correction:
			- Osmotic demyelination syndrome

		- Hyperglycaemia may cause:
			- Pseudohyponatraemia
