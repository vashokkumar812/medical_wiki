## Table 1. Sinus Rhythm ECG Markers of Arrhythmia

| Electrocardiographic Sign | Implication / Consideration |
|---|---|
| Pre-excitation / delta wave | WPW – AVRT |
| Left atrial enlargement, frequent PACs, sinus bradycardia | Atrial fibrillation |
| Left ventricular hypertrophy | Atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia |
| Frequent PVCs | Ventricular tachycardia |
| Q waves | Ischaemic heart disease – atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia |
| Widespread T wave inversion across precordial leads, LVH, Q waves and ST-segment changes | Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy – risk of atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia |
| Long or short QT interval, Brugada pattern, early repolarisation pattern | Genetic arrhythmia syndromes – risk of sudden cardiac death |
| Inverted T waves or epsilon waves across right precordial leads (V1–V3)* | ARVC – risk of sudden cardiac death |

> **Abbreviations:**  
> ARVC = arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy  
> AVRT = atrioventricular reciprocating tachycardia  
> ECG = electrocardiogram  
> LVH = left ventricular hypertrophy  
> PACs = premature atrial contractions  
> PVCs = premature ventricular contractions  
> WPW = Wolff–Parkinson–White syndrome  

> *In patients without right bundle branch block

## Table 2. Ambulatory ECG Monitoring – Choice of Investigation

| Investigation | Investigation of Choice: Symptom Frequency | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12-lead ECG | — | Readily available<br>Inexpensive | Rarely performed during arrhythmia |
| 24–48 hour Holter monitor | Daily to every second day | Usually available<br>Does not require activation<br>Can detect asymptomatic arrhythmia | Low yield except for daily arrhythmias |
| Loop/event recorder (1–4 weeks) | Weekly–monthly | Increased yield and cost-effectiveness versus Holter | Usually patient-triggered only<br>Not useful for asymptomatic arrhythmia or syncope<br>Often only one-week recorders available<br>Discomfort with prolonged monitoring |
| Implantable loop recorder | Months to years | High yield<br>Long-term monitoring (~3 years)<br>Automatic brady/tachy detection plus patient-triggered recording | Cost<br>Not available in all centres<br>Typically approved only for syncope or cryptogenic stroke |
| Handheld ECG | Months to years | High yield<br>Permanently available to patient | Patient cost<br>Delay activating device before arrhythmia terminates<br>Large data volume<br>No Medicare rebate<br>Data ownership/subscription issues |

> **Abbreviation:** ECG = electrocardiography

- Patient presents with palpitations
	- Initial principles
		- Most palpitations are benign
		- Not all palpitations are arrhythmic
		- Assessment usually occurs between episodes
		- Goal
			- Identify dangerous arrhythmia or structural heart disease

- Step 1: Clarify symptom
	- True palpitations?
		- Sensation of heartbeat/pulsation
	- Alternative symptoms?
		- Chest pain
		- Dyspnoea
		- Presyncope/syncope
		- Anxiety symptoms

- Step 2: Assess severity and red flags
	- Associated symptoms
		- Syncope/presyncope
		- Chest pain
		- Breathlessness
		- Haemodynamic compromise

	- High-risk background
		- Structural heart disease
		- Prior myocardial infarction
		- Heart failure
		- Cardiomyopathy
		- Family history sudden cardiac death
		- Known arrhythmia syndrome

	- Significant ECG abnormalities
		- Pre-excitation/WPW
		- Long QT
		- Short QT
		- Brugada pattern
		- Pathological Q waves
		- Significant conduction disease

	- Red flag present?
		- Yes
			- Specialist referral indicated
			- Consider urgent evaluation/admission
		- No
			- Continue outpatient assessment

- Step 3: Characterise palpitations

	- Subjective awareness of normal heartbeat
		- Forceful heartbeat
		- Often at night
		- Not particularly rapid
		- Often anxiety related
		→ Usually benign reassurance

	- Momentary skipped beats
		- Brief pause/thump sensation
		- Recurrent intermittent episodes
		- Worse with stress/fatigue/illness
		→ Likely ectopic beats

		- Frequent ectopics?
			- Yes
				- Further evaluation
			- No
				- Usually benign

	- Sustained gradual-onset tachycardia
		- Gradual onset/offset
		- Associated stress/anxiety
		→ Likely sinus tachycardia

		- Consider secondary causes
			- Thyrotoxicosis
			- Anaemia
			- Hypovolaemia

	- Sustained sudden-onset regular tachycardia
		- Sudden onset/termination
		- Minutes to hours
		- Regular rhythm
		→ Suggests SVT

		- Features supporting SVT
			- Triggered by bending/exertion
			- Young patient
			- Palpitations with light-headedness
			- Responds to Valsalva

		- Syncope present?
			- Yes
				- Higher risk
				- Specialist referral

	- Sustained irregular palpitations
		- “All over the place”
		- Irregularly irregular
		- Dyspnoea
		→ Suggests atrial fibrillation

		- Associated clues
			- Polyuria
			- Nocturnal onset
			- Sleep apnoea symptoms

- Step 4: Assess contributing factors
	- Medical conditions
		- Cardiovascular disease
		- Thyroid disease
		- Anaemia
		- Sleep apnoea

	- Medications/substances
		- Stimulants
		- Weight-loss agents
		- Alcohol
		- Illicit drugs

	- Exercise history
		- Endurance athletics
			- Risk cardiomyopathy/AF

	- Psychological factors
		- Anxiety/panic disorder
		- Do NOT assume anxiety alone
			- Arrhythmia commonly missed

- Step 5: Examination
	- Vitals
		- Heart rate
		- Blood pressure
		- Postural vitals

	- Cardiac exam
		- Murmurs
		- Heart failure signs

	- Systemic exam
		- Thyroid disease
		- Anaemia
		- Obesity
		- Fluid status

	- Abnormal resting HR?
		- <60 bpm
		- >100 bpm
		→ Increases likelihood arrhythmia

- Step 6: Baseline investigations
	- All patients
		- 12-lead ECG

	- ECG findings suggesting arrhythmia substrate
		- WPW
			- Short PR
			- Delta wave
		- Long QT
		- Short QT
		- Brugada pattern
		- Conduction disease
		- Prior MI

	- Additional investigations
		- Echocardiogram
			- If symptoms/risk factors/abnormal ECG

		- Exercise stress test
			- Symptoms on exertion

		- Blood tests
			- Thyroid function
			- FBE
			- Electrolytes

- Step 7: Ambulatory ECG monitoring
	- Frequent symptoms
		- Holter monitor

	- Intermittent symptoms
		- External loop recorder

	- Rare symptoms (<1/month)
		- Implantable loop recorder

	- New technologies
		- Hand-held ECG devices
			- Useful but limited

	- Key principle
		- Correlate symptoms with rhythm

- Step 8: Specialist referral indications
	- Frequent/persistent palpitations
	- Sustained tachycardia
	- Syncope/presyncope
	- Chest pain/breathlessness
	- Structural heart disease
	- Abnormal ECG/echo
	- Family history sudden death
	- High suspicion despite normal work-up
	- WPW/pre-excitation
	- Ventricular arrhythmia suspicion

- Step 9: Electrophysiology referral
	- Indications
		- Classic SVT history
		- High clinical suspicion
		- Recurrent symptomatic episodes
		- Syncope with palpitations
		- Structural heart disease

	- Options
		- Electrophysiology study
		- Ablation
		- Implantable loop recorder

- Step 10: Management
	- Benign awareness/ectopics
		- Reassurance
		- Education

	- SVT
		- Teach Valsalva manoeuvre
		- Consider electrophysiology/ablation

	- AF
		- Rate/rhythm management
		- Stroke risk assessment

	- Dangerous arrhythmia or structural disease
		- Specialist-directed therapy
