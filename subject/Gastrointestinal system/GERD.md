## definition
- Symptoms or oesophagitis due to reflux of gastric contents into distal oesophagus

	- Symptoms should occur:
		- ≥1 day/week

	- Major mechanism
		- Lower oesophageal sphincter relaxation

	- Hiatus hernia
		- Commonly associated
		- Not the direct cause


- Diagnosis
	- Clinical diagnosis
	- Based on:
		- Symptom pattern

	- Endoscopy reserved for selected patients

- Typical symptoms
	- Heartburn
		- Burning sensation
		- Epigastrium → retrosternal → neck

		- Triggered by:
			- Meals
			- Postural change
			- Medications
			- Stress
	
		- Relieved by:
			- Antacids

	- Associated symptoms
		- Regurgitation
		- Waterbrash
		- Chest pain
		- Cough
		- Hoarseness
		- Sore throat
		- Bloating
		- Belching
		- Nausea

	- Associated disease
		- Asthma

	- Significant negative effect on quality of life

- Alarm symptoms
	- Dysphagia
	- Odynophagia
	- Haematemesis
	- Weight loss

- Disease course
	- Usually chronic + relapsing
	- Relapse common after stopping acid suppression

- Endoscopy indications
	- Alarm symptoms
	- Diagnostic uncertainty
	- Failure of initial treatment
	- Longstanding troublesome symptoms
	- Older patients
		- Especially Asian background

	- Preoperative assessment
	- Barrett’s oesophagus assessment
	- Persistent patient anxiety/reassurance

- Endoscopy findings
	- Symptoms poorly correlate with oesophagitis severity
	- <50% with typical symptoms have visible oesophagitis

- Barium studies
	- Poor for oesophagitis diagnosis
	- Useful for:
		- Motility disorders
		- Strictures
		- Large hiatus hernias
		- Pharyngeal pouches

- pH monitoring
	- Highly specialised
	- Indications
		- Uncertain diagnosis
		- Failed therapeutic trial

- Helicobacter pylori
	- Relationship with GORD controversial
	- Routine testing not recommended

	- Some experts recommend eradication if:
		- Long-term daily PPI needed

- Management goals
	- Relieve symptoms
	- Improve quality of life
	- Heal oesophagitis
	- Reduce complications

- Lifestyle/nonpharmacological management
	- Avoid provoking foods
	- Avoid bending/stooping
	- Avoid reflux-triggering activities
	- Small meals
	- Avoid late meals
	- Elevate head of bed

	- Possible provoking substances/drugs
		- Alcohol
		- Anticholinergics
		- Calcium channel blockers
		- Bisphosphonates
		- Theophylline
		- NSAIDs
		- Aspirin

	- Smoking cessation
		- General health benefit
		- GORD benefit uncertain

	- Weight loss
		- General health benefit
		- GORD benefit uncertain

- Pharmacological treatment
	- Depends on:
		- Severity
		- Chronicity
	- Not dependent on presence of oesophagitis

	- PPIs
		- Most effective treatment
		- Better than H2 antagonists

	- Antacids/H2 blockers
		- Suitable for mild intermittent symptoms

- Step-down therapy
	- Current preferred strategy

	- Initial treatment
		- Standard-dose PPI for 4–8 weeks

	- Examples
		- Omeprazole 20 mg
		- Pantoprazole 40 mg
		- Lansoprazole 30 mg
		- Rabeprazole 20 mg

	- If refractory
		- Twice-daily dosing

	- After symptom control
		- Reduce to maintenance dose
		- Trial withdrawal
		- Step down to:
			- H2 antagonists
			- Antacids

	- Failure of symptom resolution
		- Requires endoscopy

- Relapse management
	- ~70% relapse within 6 months after stopping PPI

	- Severe oesophagitis
		- Usually require ongoing therapy

	- Mild symptoms
		- Intermittent/on-demand treatment possible

- Esomeprazole
	- Superior in severe oesophagitis

- Prokinetics
	- Domperidone/metoclopramide
		- Unclear benefit

	- Cisapride
		- Withdrawn
		- Cardiotoxicity

- Surgical treatment
	- Usually unnecessary
	- Consider if:
		- Medical failure
		- Medication side effects
		- Patient unwilling for long-term medication

	- Procedure
		- Laparoscopic fundoplication

	- Outcomes
		- ~90% symptom remission at 10 years

	- Complications
		- Dysphagia
		- Early satiety/fullness
		- Flatulence

- Complications of GORD
	- Stricture
	- Ulcerative oesophagitis
	- Barrett’s oesophagus
	- Oesophageal adenocarcinoma

- Barrett’s oesophagus
	- Long-term GORD complication
	- Diagnosed only on endoscopy

	- Pathology
		- Squamous epithelium replaced by columnar epithelium

	- Cancer risk
		- Increased adenocarcinoma risk

	- Types
		- Long segment
			- >3 cm

		- Short segment
			- <3 cm
			- Lower cancer risk

	- Prevalence in endoscopy patients
		- Long segment
			- 3–5%

		- Short segment
			- 10–15%

	- Management
		- Endoscopic surveillance

	- High-grade dysplasia
		- May require:
			- Ablation
			- Local resection
			- Oesophagectomy

- Key points
	- GORD is primarily a clinical diagnosis
	- Endoscopy reserved for alarm features/treatment failure
	- PPIs are first-line
	- Step-down therapy preferred
