# Hepatitis A
* Cause
  - Hepatitis A virus (HAV)
  - Small, single-stranded RNA virus
* Overview
  - Acute infection of the liver
  - Most infections occur through contaminated food or water
  - Faeco-oral transmission may also occur during sexual contact, particularly among men who have sex with men (MSM)
  - Does not cause chronic hepatitis
  - Management is usually supportive
  - Infection confers lifelong immunity
* Epidemiology
  - Usually asymptomatic in children
  - More severe illness in elderly and pregnant people
  - Usually resolves within 1 month
  - Offer HAV immunisation to people at increased risk
    - MSM
    - People who inject drugs
    - Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people
    - People in custodial settings
    - Sex workers
* Transmission
  - Faeco-oral route
    - Contaminated food
    - Contaminated water
    - Sexual contact, particularly among MSM
  - Infectivity
    - Begins 2 weeks before onset of prodromal symptoms
    - Continues until 1 week after onset of jaundice
* Clinical presentation
  - Incubation period
    - 15–50 days
    - Mean: 28 days
  - Acute hepatitis
    - Initial symptoms for a few days
      - Lethargy
      - Nausea
      - Fever
      - Anorexia
    - Followed by
      - Jaundice
      - Pale stools
      - Dark urine
  - Children
    - Usually asymptomatic
  - Elderly and pregnant people
    - More severe illness
* Complications
  - Liver failure
    - Rare
  - Severe hepatitis may require hospitalisation
* Diagnosis
  - Routine screening is not recommended
    - Not recommended during routine sexual health screening
    - Not recommended before HAV immunisation
    - Exceptions: previous infection reported or considered likely
  - Blood tests
    - AST and ALT
      - Raised in acute hepatitis
    - Bilirubin
      - Raised in acute hepatitis
    - Anti-HAV IgM
      - Indicates acute HAV infection
      - Persists for 3–6 months
    - Anti-HAV Ig-total
      - Indicates previous infection or vaccination
      - Includes IgM, IgG and IgA
  - Exclude other causes of acute hepatitis
    - Hepatitis B
    - Hepatitis C
* Management
  - Symptomatic infection
    - Supportive care
    - Hospitalisation if severe illness or clinical deterioration
  - Immediate management
    - Avoid sexual contact
      - During acute illness
      - For at least 1 week after onset of jaundice
    - Occupational exclusion
      - Food handlers
      - Healthcare workers
      - Childcare workers
      - Exclude from work for at least 1 week after onset of jaundice
    - Commence contact tracing
    - Provide patient factsheet
    - Notify state or territory health department
    - Consider comprehensive STI testing depending on patient history
* Special treatment situations
  - Complicated infection
    - Severe hepatitis may require hospital admission
  - Pregnancy
    - Seek specialist advice
    - Severe hepatitis can be more serious in pregnant people
* Contact tracing and post-exposure prophylaxis
  - Notifiable condition
  - Contact tracing
    - Important to prevent reinfection and reduce transmission
    - Trace contacts from 50 days before symptom onset
    - Diagnosing doctor is responsible for initiating and documenting contact tracing discussion
  - Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)
    - Used to prevent secondary cases among close contacts
    - Hepatitis A vaccine
    - OR normal human immunoglobulin (NHIG)
      - Passive immunisation
  - Refer to Australasian Contact Tracing Manual – Hepatitis A
* Follow-up
  - Review after 1 week
    - Confirm contact tracing has been completed
    - Offer additional contact tracing support if needed
  - High-risk patients, e.g. MSM
    - Provide further sexual health education
    - Provide prevention counselling
* Auditable outcomes
  - 100% of HIV-positive MSM are vaccinated or immune
  - 100% of susceptible close contacts receive vaccination
