- Hepatitis B serology (quick interpretation)
	- HBsAg (surface antigen)
		- Positive → Current infection
		- Negative → No current infection
	- HBeAg
		- Positive → High infectivity/high viral replication
	- Anti-HBs (surface antibody)
		- Positive → Immune
			- Vaccination or past infection
		- Negative → Not immune
	- Anti-HBc (core antibody)
		- Positive → Natural infection (past or current)
		- Negative → Never infected
	- IgM anti-HBc
		- Positive → Acute or recent infection
		- Negative → Chronic infection or past infection

	- Anti-HBe
		- Positive → Lower infectivity

- Common patterns
	- HBsAg (+), Anti-HBs (−), IgM anti-HBc (+)
		- Acute hepatitis B
	- HBsAg (+), Anti-HBs (−), IgM anti-HBc (−), Anti-HBc (+)
		- Chronic hepatitis B
	- HBsAg (−), Anti-HBs (+), Anti-HBc (+)
		- Past infection, now immune
	- HBsAg (−), Anti-HBs (+), Anti-HBc (−)
		- Vaccinated
	- HBsAg (−), Anti-HBs (−), Anti-HBc (−)
		- Susceptible (never infected, not vaccinated)
	- HBsAg (−), Anti-HBs (−), Anti-HBc (+)
		- Isolated core antibody
			- Window period
			- Remote infection
			- False positive
			- Occult HBV

- AMC memory trick
	- **Surface Ag = Sick**
	- **Surface Ab = Safe**
	- **Core Ab = Came into contact naturally**
	- **IgM Core = Immediate (acute)**
	- **e Ag = Extremely infectious**
# Liver function tests (LFTs)
- Indications for ordering LFTs
	- Suspected liver disease
	- Monitoring diseases affecting liver
		- Malignancy
		- Hypoxia
	- Monitoring hepatotoxic medications
		- Valproate
		- Methotrexate

- Patient counseling
	- Fasting not necessary
	

- Laboratory factors affecting results
	- Bilirubin samples
		- Protect from light if bilirubin elevated
		- Haemolysis may falsely lower bilirubin
	- Albumin measurement
		- Prefer same laboratory for serial testing

- Hepatocellular injury pattern
	- Raised ALT and AST suggest hepatocyte injury
	- Severe elevation
		- >10× upper reference limit
		- Suggests:
			- Acute viral hepatitis
			- Drug toxicity
			- Hypoxia
	- Mild elevation
		- Up to 5× upper reference limit
		- Suggests:
			- Infection
			- Alcohol
			- Fatty liver
			- Medications
	- Alcoholic liver disease
		- AST often > ALT
		- AST may be >2× ALT
	- Transaminase levels do not directly correlate with severity
		- Cirrhosis may have near-normal values

- Cholestatic pattern
	- Cholestasis
		- Interruption of bile flow
	- Typical findings
		- ALP markedly elevated
		- ALP >3× ALT
	- Causes
		- Biliary obstruction
		- Pregnancy
		- Drugs
			- Erythromycin
			- Oestrogen
		- Infiltrative disease
			- Malignancy

- Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)
	- Produced in:
		- Liver
		- Bone
		- Intestine
		- Placenta
	- Raised GGT supports hepatic origin
	- Isolated raised ALP usually due to bone disease
		- Paget disease
		- Vitamin D deficiency
		- Bone metastasis
	- ALP liver and bone isoforms may be measured
		- If diagnosis unclear
		- If ALP >1.5× upper reference limit

- Gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT)
	- Useful for confirming liver origin of ALP
	- Associated with alcohol use
	- Remains elevated:
		- 3 weeks after heavy drinking cessation
		- After liver injury

- Bilirubin
	- Elevated in:
		- Cholestatic disease
		- Hepatocellular injury
	- Adults usually have predominantly conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia

- Unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
	- Causes
		- Gilbert syndrome
		- Haemolysis

- Gilbert syndrome
	- Benign impairment of bilirubin conjugation
	- Affects up to 5% population
	- Features
		- Persistent isolated bilirubin elevation
			- Usually 2–3× upper reference limit
		- Worse during:
			- Illness
			- Fasting
	- No further investigation needed

- Albumin
	- Low albumin may indicate severe liver disease
	- More common causes
		- Pregnancy
		- Inflammation
		- Malnutrition
		- Protein-losing states

- Prognostic markers in cirrhosis
	- Low albumin
	- High bilirubin
	- Associated with reduced survival

- Repeating LFTs
	- Minor unexpected abnormalities
		- Repeat within ~1 week
	- Stable liver disease or medication monitoring
		- Usually no more than monthly
	- Some drugs
		- Every 3 months
			- Methotrexate
	- Acute toxic or hypoxic injury
		- Daily or twice-weekly monitoring

- Significant serial change thresholds
	- ALT/AST
		- >30% change
	- GGT
		- >20% change
	- ALP
		- >15% change
	- Bilirubin
		- >40% change
	- Albumin
		- Very low intraindividual variation

- Components of LFTs and origins
	- Bilirubin
		- Haem metabolite
		- Conjugated in liver
	- Albumin
		- Synthesized in liver
		- Half-life ~20 days
	- Total protein
		- Includes albumin, immunoglobulins, carrier proteins
	- GGT
		- Canalicular surface of hepatocyte
	- ALP
		- Canalicular surface of hepatocyte
		- Also from:
			- Bone
			- Intestine
			- Placenta
	- AST
		- Hepatocyte cytoplasm
		- Hepatocyte mitochondria
		- Skeletal muscle
		- Cardiac muscle
	- ALT
		- Hepatocyte cytoplasm

- Cholestatic laboratory pattern
	- ALP >200 IU/L
	- ALP >3× ALT
	- Causes
		- Biliary obstruction
		- Pregnancy
		- Drugs
		- Malignancy/infiltration

- Hepatocellular laboratory pattern
	- ALT >200 IU/L
	- ALT >3× ALP
	- Causes
		- Viral hepatitis
			- Hepatitis A
			- Hepatitis B
			- Hepatitis C
			- EBV
			- CMV
		- Alcohol
		- Fatty liver
		- Drugs
			- Paracetamol
		- Metal overload
			- Haemochromatosis
			- Copper overload
		- Hypoxia
			- LD usually >1.5× AST
		- Autoimmune disease

- Mildly elevated transaminases
	- Common causes
		- Hepatitis C
		- Fatty liver
		- Hereditary haemochromatosis

- Recommended evaluation
	- Ask about:
		- Blood-borne virus risk factors
		- Alcohol intake
	- Perform:
		- Hepatitis B serology
		- Hepatitis C serology

- Alcoholic hepatitis vs nonalcoholic steatohepatitis
	- Very similar clinically and biochemically
	- No biochemical test reliably confirms/excludes alcohol abuse

- Fatty liver
	- Risk factors
		- Overweight
		- Obesity
			- ~6-fold increased risk
	- Ultrasound finding
		- Bright hyperechoic liver

- Hereditary haemochromatosis
	- Frequency
		- ~1:150 in Australia
	- Initial screening
		- Fasting iron studies
			- Ferritin
			- Transferrin saturation

- Case example
	- 66-year-old man
		- Weight loss
		- Fatigue
		- Normocytic anaemia
	- LFT pattern
		- ALP markedly raised relative to ALT
		- Suggests cholestasis
	- Additional clues
		- No jaundice
		- Low albumin
		- Significant symptoms
	- Likely diagnosis
		- Intrahepatic cholestasis due to liver metastases
	- Appropriate next investigation
		- Ultrasound

# jaundice in neonatal period
- complication
	- Kernicterus
		- Rare
		- Caused by severe unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
		- May cause permanent neurological impairment
- epidemiology
	- higher in preterm
- classification
	- Physiological Jaundice
		- Most common cause
		- Appears after first 24 hours
		- Infant otherwise well
		- Normal stools and urine
		- Exaggerated normal neonatal bilirubin response
		- Usually resolves by:
			- 2–3 weeks
	
	- Breast Milk Jaundice
		- Diagnosis of exclusion
		- Infant otherwise healthy
		- Breastfeeding should continue
		- Do NOT stop breastfeeding
		- May persist up to 6 weeks
		- No repeat bilirubin levels required unless worsening
	
	- Haemolytic Jaundice
		- Causes
			- ABO incompatibility
			- Rh incompatibility
			- G6PD deficiency
			- Hereditary spherocytosis
		- Features
			- Early onset (<24 hours)
			- Rapid bilirubin rise
			- Anaemia
			- Positive Coombs test (immune causes)
	
	- Conjugated Hyperbilirubinaemia
		- Always pathological
		- Conjugated bilirubin >10% of total bilirubin
		- Causes
			- Biliary atresia
			- Neonatal hepatitis
			- Sepsis
			- Metabolic disease
		- Features
			- Dark urine
			- Pale stools
			- Hepatomegaly
	
	- Biliary Atresia
		- Extrahepatic biliary obstruction
		- Presents with
			- Persistent jaundice
			- Pale stools
			- Dark urine
			- Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
		- Early diagnosis improves outcomes
		- Not excluded by normal abdominal ultrasound
		- Requires urgent specialist referral

- diagnosis
	- Pathological Jaundice Red Flags
		- Onset <24 hours of life
		- Unwell or febrile infant
		- Prolonged jaundice (>2 weeks)
		- Elevated conjugated (direct) bilirubin
		- Dark urine
		- Pale/acholic stools
		- Hepatosplenomegaly
		- Significant weight loss (>10%)
		- Rapidly rising bilirubin

	- History
		- Age of onset
			- <24 hours = pathological
			- \>2 weeks = prolonged jaundice
		- Urine and stool history
			- Dark urine → cholestasis/biliary obstruction
			- Pale stools → biliary obstruction

- **Investigations**
	- First-line
		- Total serum bilirubin (SBR)
			- Unconjugated (indirect)
			- Conjugated (direct)
	- Additional investigations depending on cause
		- FBE
		- Blood group
		- Direct Coombs test
		- Sepsis screen
		- Thyroid function tests
		- G6PD testing

	- Transcutaneous Bilirubinometer (TCB)
		- Screening tool only
		- Suitable from:
			- 24 hours to 2 weeks of age
			- Near-term infants
		- Confirm with serum bilirubin if:
			- Within 50 micromol/L of treatment threshold
		- Less reliable:
			- After phototherapy
		- Rebound bilirubin monitoring
			- Always use serum bilirubin

	- Interpretation
		- Plot bilirubin level on gestation-specific nomogram
		- Treatment decisions based on:
			- Age in hours
			- Gestational age
			- Bilirubin level
			- Clinical risk factors


- Treatment
	- Treat bilirubin elevation
	- Treat underlying cause

	- Phototherapy
		- First-line treatment for significant unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
		- Monitor:
			- Bilirubin levels
			- Weight
			- Hydration status
		- Serial serum bilirubin measurements required
	
	- **Exchange Transfusion**
		- Indications
			- Severe hyperbilirubinaemia
			- Failure of phototherapy
			- Risk of kernicterus
		- Performed in NICU setting
	
	- **Cause-Specific Management**
		- Sepsis
			- IV antibiotics immediately
		- Haemolysis
			- Specialist paediatric input
		- Dehydration
			- Optimise hydration
			- Feeding support
			- Lactation consultant involvement
		- Hypothyroidism
			- Paediatric/endocrine review
		- Biliary obstruction
			- Urgent tertiary referral

- **Consult Paediatrics**
	- Unwell infant
	- Unclear diagnosis
	- Conjugated bilirubin >10% of total bilirubin
	- Suspected biliary atresia
	- Rising bilirubin despite treatment

- **Transfer Indications**
	- Need for exchange transfusion
	- Bilirubin rising despite adequate therapy
	- Requirement for NICU care

- **Discharge Criteria**
	- Clinically well infant
	- Feeding adequately
	- Serious causes excluded
	- Bilirubin stable or improving

- **Follow-Up**
	- Early GP or maternal-child health review
	- Especially if:
		- <7 days old
		- Exclusively breastfed
		- Borderline bilirubin levels
	- Repeat bilirubin in 24–48 hours if:
		- Borderline treatment threshold
		- Bilirubin still rising

- **Parent Advice**
	- Sunlight exposure is NOT recommended treatment
	- Seek review if:
		- Jaundice persists beyond 2–3 weeks
		- Jaundice worsens
		- Poor feeding develops
		- Infant becomes unwell
