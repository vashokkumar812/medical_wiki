- Definition
	- Inflammation of the appendiceal wall
- Epidemiology
	- Most common surgical cause of acute abdomen
	- Peak incidence
		- 10–30 years
- Classification
	- Uncomplicated appendicitis
		- Inflamed appendix alone
	- Complicated appendicitis
		- Gangrenous 
		- Perforation
			- higher risk
				- Children
				- Elderly
				- Diabetes
				- Pregnancy
				- Delayed presentation
		- Periappendiceal abscess
		- Peritonitis
		- Sepsis
		- Septic shock
- Clinical features
	- Symptoms
		- Abdominal pain
			- Initially periumbilical
			- Migrates to RIF within 6–12 hours
			- Sometimes colicky initially
			- Progressively worsens
			- Worse with
				- Walking
				- Movement
				- Coughing
			- Patient may limp
		- Nausea
		- Vomiting
			- Usually after onset of pain
		- Sudden anorexia
		- Diarrhoea
		- Constipation

	- General signs
		- Appears unwell
		- Initially flushed
		- Later pale
		- Furred tongue
		- Halitosis
		- Low-grade fever
		- Tachycardia

	- Abdominal signs
		- Superficial hyperaesthesia
		- RIF tenderness
			- Maximal at McBurney point
		- Rebound tenderness
		- Percussion tenderness
		- Cough tenderness
		- Guarding
		- Rigidity
	- Classic AMC triad
		- RIF pain
		- Nausea/vomiting
		- Guarding
	- Special signs
		- Rovsing sign
			- RIF pain on palpation of LIF
		- Retrocaecal appendix
			- Positive psoas sign
				- Pain on extension of right hip
				- Pain on resisted flexion of right hip
				- Pain on raising right leg
			- Loin tenderness
			- Less 
				- guarding
				-  rebound tenderness
				-  rigidity
	
		- Pelvic appendix
			- Positive obturator sign
				- Pain on flexion and internal rotation of right hip
			- Urinary frequency
			- Diarrhoea
			- Tenesmus
			- Tender PR examination
			- Less abdominal rigidity

- Special populations
	- Children
		- Difficult diagnosis
		- Rapid perforation

	- Elderly
		- less pain and delayed presentation
		- May mimic bowel obstruction
		- High perforation risk

	- Pregnancy
		- Commonest in second trimester
		- Pain may be higher and more lateral
		- Difficult diagnosis
		- high perforation risk

- Investigations
	- Laboratory
		- FBC
			- Leukocytosis
			- Neutrophilia
			- Left shift
		- Elevated CRP
		- UEC
			- Assess hydration
		- Urinalysis
			- Exclude UTI
		- β-hCG
			- Exclude ectopic pregnancy
	- Imaging
		- Ultrasound
			- Thickened non-compressible appendix
			- Preferred in
				- Children
				- Pregnancy
				- Young females
		- CT abdomen
			- Best investigation when diagnosis uncertain
			- Useful in
				- Elderly
				- Females
				- Atypical presentations
			- Assesses for complicated appendicitis
		- MRI
			- Highly sensitive and specific
			- Useful in pregnancy
			- Useful when US inconclusive
		- Plain X-ray
			- Limited role
		- Diagnostic laparoscopy
			- Diagnostic and therapeutic
- Scoring systems
	- Alvarado score - MANTRELS
		- Migration of pain
		- Anorexia
		- Nausea/vomiting
		- RIF tenderness ( 2 )
		- Rebound tenderness
		- Fever
		- Leukocytosis ( 2 )
		- Left shift
	- Interpretation
		- ≤4
			- Low probability
		- 5–6
			- Possible appendicitis
		- ≥7
			- Likely appendicitis
- Differential diagnosis
	- Gastroenteritis
		- Vomiting before pain
		- Diffuse cramps
		- Prominent diarrhoea
		- No focal peritonism
	- Mesenteric adenitis
		- Common in children
		- Post-viral URTI
		- Less toxic
	- Meckel diverticulitis
		- Mimics appendicitis
		- May cause painless GI bleeding
	- Crohn disease
		- Chronic diarrhoea
		- Weight loss
		- Perianal disease
- Management
	- Initial
		- ABC assessment
		- NBM
		- IV fluids
		- Analgesia
		- Antiemetics
		- Early surgical review
		- Broad-spectrum antibiotics
	- Antibiotic principles
		- Cover
			- Gram-negative enteric organisms
			- Anaerobes
		- Common Australian regimens
			- Ceftriaxone + metronidazole
			- Cefotaxime + metronidazole
			- Amoxicillin-clavulanate
		- Alternative ETG regimen
			- Gentamicin or tobramycin
			- Plus metronidazole
			- Plus amoxicillin or ampicillin
		- Severe penicillin allergy
			- Gentamicin/tobramycin plus clindamycin
			- Consider metronidazole if source control delayed
	- Uncomplicated appendicitis
		- Laparoscopic appendicectomy
			- Treatment of choice
			- Gold standard
		- Nonoperative treatment
			- Selected patients only
			- Antibiotics
			- Observation
			- Shared decision with surgeon
			- Recurrence after nonoperative treatment
				- Up to 40% require appendicectomy within 5 years
	- Complicated appendicitis
		- Surgical source control
		- Appendicectomy
		- Abscess drainage if required
	- Perforation
		- Urgent surgery
	- Appendiceal abscess
		- Radiological drainage
		- Interval appendicectomy sometimes
	- Appendiceal mass/ phlegmon
		- Conservative initially
			- Antibiotics
			- Observation
	- Antibiotic duration
		- Uncomplicated appendicitis
			- Stop after appendicectomy if
				- No perforation
				- No gangrene
				- No abscess
				- No peritonitis
				- No sepsis
		- Nonoperative management
			- Total antibiotic course 7–10 days
		- Complicated appendicitis
			- After adequate source control
			- Total antibiotic duration about 4 days

- Safety-net discharged patients
	- Return if
		- Increasing pain
		- Fever
		- Persistent vomiting
		- Unable to tolerate fluids
