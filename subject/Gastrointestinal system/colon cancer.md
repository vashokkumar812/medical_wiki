

- Screening

	- Average risk
		- Age 45–74 years
		- iFOBT every 2 years
		- No routine colonoscopy

	- Moderately increased risk
		- One FDR with CRC <55 years
		- OR two FDRs with CRC (same side)
		- Colonoscopy every 5 years
		- Start
			- Age 50
			- OR 10 years before youngest affected FDR
			- Earlier of the two
		- Stop age 74

	- Potentially high risk (non-Lynch)
		- Strong family history
		- Colonoscopy every 5 years
		- Start
			- Age 40
			- OR 10 years before youngest affected FDR
			- Earlier of the two
		- Stop age 74

- Family history categories

	- Category 1 (near-average)
		- One relative ≥55 years
		- Routine screening

	- Category 2 (moderate)
		- One FDR <55 years
		- Two FDRs any age
		- One FDR + ≥2 SDRs

	- Category 3 (high)
		- ≥3 FDR/SDR including one <55 years
		- OR ≥3 FDRs

- Refer for genetics

	- CRC <50 years
	- CRC + Lynch-associated cancer
	- CRC + family history suggestive of Lynch
	- Strong family history of CRC/endometrial cancer

- Aspirin chemoprevention

	- Higher-than-average CRC risk
	- Age 45–70 years
	- Aspirin 100 mg daily
	- Balance bleeding risk

---

- Hereditary syndromes

	- Lynch syndrome
		- Test all CRCs for MSI/MMR
		- Colonoscopy surveillance
		- Aspirin from surveillance (≈25 years)

	- MUTYH polyposis
		- Consider genetic testing
			- ≥20 adenomas
			- ≥10 adenomas + suggestive features
		- Colonoscopy every 2 years from 18–20 years
		- Annual if polyps
		- Colectomy if uncontrolled

	- Juvenile polyposis
		- Colonoscopy from 12–15 years
		- Every 1–3 years

	- Serrated polyposis
		- Colonoscopy every 1–3 years
		- Remove all polyps ≥5 mm

---

- Suspected CRC

	- Positive iFOBT
	- New symptoms
		- Colonoscopy
		- Ideally within 120 days

- definition
	- malignant tumour arising from the colon or rectum
	- most are adenocarcinomas

- risk factors
	- increasing age
	- family history of colorectal cancer
	- hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer (Lynch syndrome)
	- familial adenomatous polyposis
	- inflammatory bowel disease
		- ulcerative colitis
		- Crohn disease involving the colon
	- adenomatous polyps
	- obesity
	- smoking
	- high intake of red and processed meat
	- low fibre diet
	- type 2 diabetes mellitus
	- alcohol excess

- protective factors
	- regular physical activity
	- healthy body weight
	- diet high in fibre
	- aspirin in selected high-risk patients

- common pathology
	- adenocarcinoma
	- develops through the adenoma to carcinoma sequence

- symptoms
	- rectal bleeding
	- change in bowel habit
	- iron deficiency anaemia
	- abdominal pain
	- weight loss
	- fatigue
	- bowel obstruction
	- tenesmus in rectal cancer

- examination
	- pallor
	- abdominal mass
	- hepatomegaly
	- digital rectal examination
		- rectal mass
		- blood
	- enlarged left supraclavicular lymph node in advanced disease

- right-sided colon cancer
	- occult bleeding
	- iron deficiency anaemia
	- fatigue
	- weight loss
	- abdominal mass
	- obstruction uncommon

- left-sided colon cancer
	- altered bowel habit
	- constipation
	- visible rectal bleeding
	- abdominal pain
	- bowel obstruction
	- pencil-thin stools

- rectal cancer
	- rectal bleeding
	- tenesmus
	- urgency
	- sensation of incomplete evacuation
	- change in stool calibre

- investigations
	- full blood count
		- iron deficiency anaemia
	- liver function tests
	- carcinoembryonic antigen
		- baseline and follow-up
		- not a screening test
	- colonoscopy with biopsy
		- gold standard for diagnosis
	- computed tomography scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis
		- staging
	- magnetic resonance imaging of the pelvis
		- local staging of rectal cancer
	- endorectal ultrasound
		- selected early rectal cancers

- screening
	- faecal immunochemical test
		- population screening
	- positive faecal immunochemical test
		- colonoscopy

- staging
	- tumour, node, metastasis staging system
	- stage determines prognosis and treatment

- common sites of metastasis
	- liver
	- lungs
	- peritoneum
	- distant lymph nodes

- management
	- multidisciplinary team approach
	- surgery
		- primary treatment for localised disease
	- chemotherapy
		- stage three colon cancer
		- selected stage two colon cancer
		- metastatic disease
	- radiotherapy
		- mainly for rectal cancer
		- often before surgery
	- targeted therapy
		- selected metastatic disease
	- immunotherapy
		- selected mismatch repair deficient or microsatellite instability high tumours

- surgical options
	- right hemicolectomy
	- left hemicolectomy
	- sigmoid colectomy
	- anterior resection
	- abdominoperineal resection
	- temporary or permanent stoma if required

- complications
	- bowel obstruction
	- bowel perforation
	- gastrointestinal bleeding
	- liver metastases
	- pulmonary metastases
	- recurrence

- follow-up
	- colonoscopy
	- carcinoembryonic antigen monitoring
	- computed tomography surveillance
	- clinical review

- hereditary syndromes
	- Lynch syndrome
		- increased risk of colorectal, endometrial and ovarian cancers
	- familial adenomatous polyposis
		- hundreds to thousands of adenomatous polyps
		- almost certain progression to colorectal cancer without prophylactic colectomy

- amc pearls
	- colonoscopy with biopsy is the diagnostic test of choice
	- computed tomography scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis is used for staging colon cancer
	- magnetic resonance imaging of the pelvis is the preferred staging investigation for rectal cancer
	- carcinoembryonic antigen is useful for monitoring, not for screening
	- iron deficiency anaemia in an older adult should be considered colorectal cancer until proven otherwise
	- positive faecal immunochemical test requires colonoscopy
	- liver is the most common site of distant metastasis
	- right-sided cancers commonly present with occult bleeding and iron deficiency anaemia
	- left-sided cancers commonly present with bowel obstruction and altered bowel habit
	- neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy is commonly used for locally advanced rectal cancer before surgery
