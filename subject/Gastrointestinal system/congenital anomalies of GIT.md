# Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis (HPS)
- Definition
	- Hypertrophy and hyperplasia of pyloric muscle
	- Causes gastric outlet obstruction
- Epidemiology
	- Typical age
		- 2-12 weeks
	- Rare after 12 weeks
- Risk factors
	- Infant factors
		- Male sex
		- First-born infant
		- Prematurity
	- Family history
		- Positive parental history
			- Especially maternal history
		- young smoking mom
		- formula feeding
	- Drug exposure
		- Macrolides
			- Erythromycin
			- Azithromycin
- Clinical features
	- volume and force wise Progressive and projectile Non-bilious vomitting immediately after food
	- Hungry vomiter
	- Poor weight gain
	- Dehydration
	- Lethargy
- Examination
	- Abdominal findings
		- Visible gastric peristalsis
			- More obvious after feeding
		- Pyloric olive
			- Olive-shaped mass
			- Right upper quadrant
			- Lateral border of rectus muscle
	- Early disease
		- Pyloric olive may be absent
		- Gastric peristalsis may be absent
		- Diagnosis should still be considered
- Investigations
	- Biochemical abnormalities
		- Early disease
			- May be normal
		- Late disease
			- Hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis
			- Hypokalaemia
	- Imaging
		- IOC : Abdominal ultrasound
			- Pyloric muscle thickness >3 mm
			- Early disease
				- May be equivocal
				- Repeat if clinical suspicion remains high
		- Upper GI contrast study
			- Consider if ultrasound non-diagnostic

- Management
	- Initial stabilisation
		- IV access
		- Stop oral feeds
		- Correct dehydration
		- Correct electrolyte abnormalities
		- Correct acid-base abnormalities
	- Ongoing vomiting
		- Insert nasogastric tube
	- Fluid therapy
		- Resuscitation
			- 0.9% saline
		- Deficit replacement
			- 0.9% saline + 5% glucose
		- Maintenance
			- 0.9% saline + 5% glucose
		- Potassium replacement
			- Only after adequate urine output
				- 1-2 mL/kg/hour
- Definitive treatment
	- Pyloromyotomy
		- Curative treatment
	- Timing
		- Surgery can be delayed
			- Until dehydration corrected
			- Until electrolytes corrected
			- Until metabolic alkalosis corrected
	- Important pre-operative goal
		- Normal serum bicarbonate
			- Reduces postoperative apnoea/hypoventilation risk

- Follow-up
	- Post-pyloromyotomy
		- Excellent prognosis
		- Low complication rate
	- Routine paediatric follow-up
		- Feeding assessment
		- Growth monitoring


# duodenal atresia

- Definition
	- Congenital complete or partial obstruction of duodenum
- Embryology
	- Failure of duodenal recanalisation
- Associations

	- Down syndrome (Trisomy 21)
	- Congenital heart disease
- Prenatal diagnosis
	- Ultrasound findings
		- Polyhydramnios
			- Due to impaired fetal swallowing
		- Double-bubble sign
		- No distal bowel gas/fluid
	- Further assessment
		- Fetal echocardiography
		- Genetic testing for down syndrome evaluation
- Postnatal presentation
	- Vomiting within first 48 hours
		- classically bilious vomiting
			- if obstruction distal to ampulla
	- Upper abdominal distension
- Diagnosis
	- Initial imaging
		- Abdominal X-ray
			- Double-bubble sign
			- Minimal or absent distal bowel gas
	- Further investigations
		- Echocardiography
		- Renal ultrasound
		- Cranial ultrasound
		- Genetic testing
- Initial management
	- Stabilisation
		- Nil by mouth
		- Nasogastric/orogastric tube
			- Gastric decompression
		- IV fluids
		- Electrolyte correction
	- Nutrition
		- Total parenteral nutrition (TPN)
			- Via central access/PICC
	- Assess for associated anomalies
- Definitive treatment
	- Duodenoduodenostomy
	- Timing
		- Once neonate stabilised
		- Not usually an immediate surgical emergency if adequately decompressed and hydrated
	- after surgery
		- TPN until bowel function returns
		- Gastric decompression continued
		- Gradual introduction of feeds
			- Breast milk preferred
- Complications
	- Feeding intolerance
	- Delayed bowel function
	- Infection
	- Need for blood transfusion
	- Poor weight gain
- Prognosis
	- Generally excellent after repair
	- Depends largely on associated anomalies
			- Especially congenital heart disease
			- Down syndrome
- Follow-up
	- Routine paediatric care
# hirsprungh disease
- definiton
  - Congenital absence of ganglion cells
  - Defective enteric innervation of distal bowel
  - Causes functional bowel obstruction
- Associations
- Down syndrome
  - Male > female

- Classification
    - Short-segment (80–85%)
      - Aganglionosis of rectum + sigmoid colon
    - Long-segment
      - Aganglionosis extends proximal to sigmoid colon
    - Total colonic
      - Entire colon aganglionic
    - Small bowel aganglionosis
      - Entire colon + variable length of small bowel aganglionic

- Clinical Features

    - Neonates (first 24–48 h)
      - Failure to pass meconium
      - Abdominal distension
      - Vomiting
      - Fever

    - Older children
      - Severe constipation
      - Failure to thrive
      - Poor appetite
      - Watery stools
      - Sepsis

- Investigations

    - Abdominal X-ray
	    - initial
      - May show bowel obstruction
      - Not diagnostic

    - Contrast enema
      - Demonstrates bowel anatomy
      - Identifies abnormal segments

    - Rectal suction biopsy
      - gold standard
      - Demonstrates absence of ganglion cells
      - Performed via anus

    - Full-thickness rectal biopsy
      - If suction biopsy inconclusive
      - Older infants (>3 months)

    - Anorectal manometry
      - Assesses bowel function
      - Mainly used in older children

    - Urine sodium
      - Monitors sodium losses

- Management

    - initial
      - Rectal bowel washouts
        - Remove stool and gas
        - Performed until surgery

    - Stoma formation (if required)
      - Colostomy or ileostomy
      - Temporary diversion of stool
      - Allows bowel recovery and growth

    - Definitive treatment
      - Pull-through procedure
        - Resection of aganglionic bowel
        - Ganglionated bowel anastomosed to anus

      - Techniques
        - Swenson
        - Soave
        - Duhamel

- Postoperative Care

    - Anal dilatation
      - Prevents anastomotic narrowing

    - Stoma closure
      - Usually 3–4 months after pull-through

    - Sodium supplementation
      - May be required with prolonged ileostomy

- Complications

    - Hirschsprung-associated enterocolitis (HAEC)
      - Colonic inflammation/infection
      - Potentially severe

- Long-term Issues

    - Constipation
    - Diarrhoea
    - Faecal incontinence
    - Delayed toilet training

- Bowel Management
    - Diet modification
    - Laxatives
    - Enemas
    - Goal
      - Daily bowel motion
      - Continence between motions

# meckel diverticulum


  - Definition
    - Congenital true diverticulum
    - Persistent vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct remnant
    - Located on antimesenteric border of ileum

  - Epidemiology
    - Most common congenital GI anomaly
    - Rule of 2s
      - 2% population
      - 2 inches long
      - Within 2 feet of ileocaecal valve
      - 2 types of ectopic tissue
        - Gastric (most common)
        - Pancreatic
      - Presents before age 2 years

  - Pathophysiology
    - Ectopic gastric mucosa → acid secretion
    - Ileal ulceration → painless bleeding

  - Clinical Features

    - Most common presentation
      - Painless lower GI bleeding

    - Other presentations
      - Iron deficiency anaemia
      - Abdominal pain
      - Diverticulitis (mimics appendicitis)
      - Intestinal obstruction
      - Intussusception
      - Perforation

  - Investigations

    - Initial
      - FBC
        - Anaemia from chronic blood loss

    - Most appropriate / Investigation of choice
      - Technetium-99m pertechnetate scan (Meckel scan)
        - Detects ectopic gastric mucosa

    - If acute abdomen / obstruction
      - Abdominal X-ray ± CT abdomen

  - Management

    - Initial
      - ABCDE
      - IV access
      - IV fluids
      - Blood transfusion if significant bleeding

    - Definitive / Most appropriate
      - Surgical resection
        - Diverticulectomy
        - Segmental ileal resection if broad base, ulceration, or complications

  - Complications
    - GI bleeding
    - Iron deficiency anaemia
    - Intestinal obstruction
    - Intussusception
    - Diverticulitis
    - Perforation
