- Mirizzi syndrome
	- Stone impacted in gallbladder neck
	- Compresses common hepatic duct

	- Features
		- Dilated bile ducts
		- Inflammation
		- Swelling

	- Complications
		- Stone erosion into hepatic duct
		- Complex surgery
		- Need for biliary reconstruction

	- Temporary management
		- ERCP
		- Stenting
		- Antibiotics





# acalculous cholecystitis

* Risk factors  
  * Critically ill (mechanical ventilation, sepsis, burns, trauma)  
  * HIV  
  * TPN  
  * Pregnancy → prolonged labour  
  * Diabetes mellitus  
  * Major surgery  
  * Vasculitis  
* Clinical features  
  * charcoat triad
  * shock
  * sepsis
  * INVESTIGATIONS  
* Laboratory  
  * Increased  
    * Total count  
    * ALP  
    * Bilirubin  
  * Transaminitis  
  * Positive blood cultures  
* Imaging  
	* US:   
	  * distended acalculous gallbladder, thickened wall, pericholic fluid  
	* To confirm the diagnosis  
	  * CT: confirms above changes  
	* If still uncertain  
		* Hepatobiliary Imino-Diacetic Acid (HIDA) scan:  
		  * Nuclear imaging using Technetium-99m IV  
		  * Normal uptake in the GB should occur within 1 hour  
		  * Absence of uptake → cholecystitis / cystic duct obstruction  
		  * Used if the diagnosis is uncertain  
* MANAGEMENT  
	* NPO
	* Early broad-spectrum antibiotic coverage:   
	  * Cefuroxime \+ Metronidazole
	*  if rapidly deteriorating or perforated  
		  * Early surgical consultation  
		* Drainage (percutaneous) – guided by U/S or CT  
		* Cholecystectomy  
* prognosis  
  * High incidence of gangrene → perforation  
  * High mortality & morbidity


# acute cholangitis
- definition
	- infection and inflammation of the biliary tree
- clinical features
	- charcoat triad
		- Fever
		- Right upper quadrant pain
		- Jaundice
	- Reynolds pentad
		- charcoat triad
		- Hypotension
		- Confusion
	- Additional supportive features
		- Tachycardia
- investigations
	- cholestatic [[liver]]
		- increased
		    - ALP ↑↑
		    - GGT ↑↑
		    - conjugated bilirubin ↑
        - if significant obstruction
		    - mild AST ↑
		    - mild ALT ↑
	    - PT/INR
	        - prolonged in severe/chronic cholestasis due to vitamin K deficiency
	- Imaging
		- Ultrasound abdomen
			- First-line
			- Duct dilatation
		- MRCP
			- If diagnosis unclear
		- CT abdomen
			- If complications suspected
- treatment
- Decision Node
	- Is sepsis or septic shock present?
		- YES
			- Follow sepsis protocol
			- Urgent source control
				- Urgent ERCP
				- if not possibale percutaneous drainage
		- NO
			- empirical antibiotics
- Empirical Antibiotic Selection
	- Can aminoglycoside be used?
		- YES
			- Preferred regimen
				- Gentamicin IV or tobramycin IV
			- PLUS
				- Amoxicillin IV OR Ampicillin IV
		- NO
			- Use non-aminoglycoside regimen
				- Ceftriaxone IV
				- Cefotaxime IV
				- Piperacillin + tazobactam I
