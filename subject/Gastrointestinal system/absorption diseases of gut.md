# dermatitis herpetiformis

  - Clinical features
    - Intensely pruritic
    - Symmetrical grouped papules/vesicles
    - Extensor surfaces
    - Excoriations common (vesicles often absent)
  - Diagnosis
    - Gold standard
      - Skin biopsy + direct immunofluorescence (DIF)

    - DIF
      - Granular IgA deposits in dermal papillae


# Coeliac Disease
- when to test
	- Persistent unexplained GI symptoms
	- Chronic diarrhoea
	- Unexplained abdominal pain
	- IBS
	- Weight loss
	- Fatigue
	- Iron deficiency ± anaemia
	- Folate deficiency
	- Vitamin B12 deficiency
	- Vitamin D deficiency
	- Osteoporosis
	- Osteomalacia
	- Autoimmune thyroid disease
	- Type 1 diabetes
	- Elevated liver enzymes
	- Infertility
	- Recurrent miscarriage
	- Neuropathy
	- Ataxia
	- Dermatitis herpetiformis
		- ![[Pasted image 20260617021309.png]]
		- 
	- First-degree relative with coeliac disease
	- Down syndrome
	- Turner syndrome

- diagnosis
	- prerequisite
		- Patient must be consuming gluten
		- Do not start GFD before investigation

	- screening serology
		- Preferred
			- tTG-IgA + DGP-IgG
		- False negatives
			- Gluten-free diet
			- Immunosuppression
			- IgA deficiency

	- IgA deficiency
		- Present in ~2–3% of coeliac patients
		- Causes false-negative IgA-based tests
		- DGP-IgG useful

	- confirmatory test
		- Gastroscopy with duodenal biopsy
		- Gold standard
		- Multiple biopsies required
			- Due to patchy disease
		- Histology
			- Villous atrophy
			- Crypt hyperplasia
			- Increased intraepithelial lymphocytes

	- biopsy pitfalls
		- Gluten-free diet may normalise histology
		- Immunosuppression may mask changes
		- Villous atrophy not specific for coeliac disease


- HLA genotyping
	- Tests
		- HLA-DQ2
		- HLA-DQ8
	- Utility
		- Exclusion test
	- Absence of both
			- Coeliac disease highly unlikely (<1%)
	- Presence
			- Does not confirm diagnosis
	- Useful when
			- Already on GFD
			- Equivocal serology
			- Equivocal biopsy
			- Failure to improve on GFD
			- High-risk screening

- gluten challenge
	- Indication
		- Already commenced GFD before diagnosis
	- Regimen
			- 3–6 g gluten daily
			- Ideally ≥6 weeks
	- Equivalent
			- 2–4 slices bread/day
	- Symptoms during challenge
			- Not diagnostic
	- Requires objective testing
			- Serology
			- Biopsy

- paediatric considerations
	- Children <3 years
		- tTG less sensitive
		- Add DGP-IgG
	- Selected children may avoid biopsy if:
		- Typical symptoms
		- tTG-IgA >10× ULN
		- Positive EMA on second sample
		- Positive HLA-DQ2/DQ8
		- Specialist paediatric assessment

- management
	- Lifelong strict gluten-free diet
	- Avoid
			- Wheat
			- Rye
			- Barley
			- Oats
	- Refer to experienced dietitian
	- Coeliac society support
	- Correct deficiencies
			- Iron
			- Folate
			- Vitamin B12
			- Vitamin D
	- Long-term supplementation
			- Calcium
			- Vitamin D


- follow-up
	- Annual review
	- tTG usually normalises
		- Within ~12 months
	- Persistently positive serology
		- Ongoing gluten exposure
	- Repeat gastroscopy
		- Consider after ~2 years in adults
		- Assess mucosal healing

- family screening
	- Screen all first-degree relatives
	- Consider second-degree relatives if multiple affected family members
	- HLA-DQ2/DQ8 + serology preferred
	- If HLA negative
			- No future screening required
	- If HLA positive and serology negative
			- At risk of future disease
