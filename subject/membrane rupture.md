# Term prelabour rupture of membranes (Term PROM)

- Definition
	- Rupture of fetal membranes
		- Before onset of labour
		- At or beyond 37 weeks gestation

- Key concepts
	- Commonly called
		- "Breaking of waters"
	- Spontaneous labour occurs in
		- ~70% within 24 hours
	- Diagnosis may be uncertain
		- Additional investigations may be required

- Risks and complications

	- Immediate (uncommon but important)
		- Cord prolapse
		- Cord compression
		- Placental abruption

	- Delayed
		- Maternal infection
		- Neonatal infection

	- Neonatal complications of infection
		- Death
		- Chronic lung disease
		- Cerebral palsy

	- Chorioamnionitis
		- Associated with cerebral palsy in term infants

- Assessment

	- Initial assessment
		- Confirm diagnosis
		- Assess maternal wellbeing
		- Assess fetal wellbeing
		- Determine GBS status

	- If diagnosis is uncertain
		- Sterile speculum examination
		- If uncertainty persists
			- Amniotic fluid protein detection test
				- e.g. AmniSure®

	- Biochemical amniotic fluid tests

		- False-positive results
			- Blood
			- Semen
			- Alkaline antiseptics
			- Certain lubricants
			- Trichomonas
			- Bacterial vaginosis
			- May also occur with intact membranes during labour

		- False-negative results
			- Prolonged membrane rupture
			- Minimal residual amniotic fluid

		- Interpretation
			- Always interpret with clinical findings

		- US FDA warning
			- Do not rely on biochemical tests alone
			- Adverse outcomes reported
			- Not recommended for routine assessment

	- pH-based tests
		- Less reliable than amniotic fluid protein detection tests

	- Digital vaginal examination
		- Avoid unless immediate induction is planned
		- Increases neonatal infection risk

- Management

	- General principles
		- Discuss risks and benefits of
			- Expectant management
			- Active management
		- Discussion should include
			- Woman
			- Partner
			- Caregivers
		- Most women labour spontaneously
		- If labour does not occur
			- Recommend induction within 24 hours where practical
			- Reduces maternal infection
			- Reduces neonatal infection

	- Outcomes

		- Immediate induction
			- Defined as induction within 24 hours
			- Associated with better outcomes

		- Expectant management at home
			- Higher neonatal infection risk than hospital expectant management

	- Active management

		- Indications
			- Induction or delivery within 24 hours where practical

			- According to GBS status

| GBS status                      | Management                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Positive or treated as positive | IV prophylactic antibiotics + early planned birth + induce labour as soon as practical              |
| Negative                        | Induction/delivery within 24 hours; routine antibiotics not recommended if timely induction planned |
| Unknown                         | Induction/delivery within 24 hours; follow local GBS guidelines                                     |

	- Caesarean section
		- Timing depends on local service capability

- Expectant management

	- Suitable if all of the following are present
		- Fixed cephalic presentation
		- GBS negative
		- No previous infant with early-onset GBS disease
		- No evidence of infection
			- Fever
			- Maternal tachycardia
			- Uterine tenderness
		- Normal CTG
		- Normal fetal movements
		- Clear amniotic fluid
		- Adequate staffing and resources
		- Reliable transport available
		- Clearly documented follow-up plan
		- Willingness to monitor
			- Maternal temperature
				- Every 4 hours while awake
			- Vaginal loss
			- Fetal movements

- Antibiotics

	- GBS positive
		- IV prophylactic antibiotics
		- Early planned birth

	- GBS negative
		- Routine antibiotics not beneficial
		- Appropriate if timely induction planned

	- Unknown GBS status
		- Follow local GBS management guideline

	- Chorioamnionitis
		- Expedite delivery
		- Broad-spectrum antibiotics

- Induction methods

| Method                                | Notes                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Oxytocin                              | Standard induction method in Australia     |
| Prostaglandins                        | Useful if cervix is unfavourable           |
| Mechanical methods (balloon catheter) | Limited evidence; increased infection risk |

- High-yield recommendations

	- GBS colonisation
		- Prophylactic antibiotics
		- Early planned birth

	- If labour has not started
		- Recommend induction within 24 hours where practical



# PPROM
- Preterm prelabour rupture of membranes (PPROM)

	- Definition
		- Rupture of fetal membranes
			- Before onset of labour
			- Before 37+0 weeks gestation

	- Key concepts
		- Major causes of neonatal death
			- Prematurity
			- Sepsis
			- Pulmonary hypoplasia

	- Risks and complications

		- Maternal
			- Chorioamnionitis (intrauterine infection)
			- Amnionitis
			- Placental abruption

		- Obstetric
			- Preterm labour
			- Cord prolapse

		- Fetal / neonatal
			- Prematurity
			- Pulmonary hypoplasia
			- Limb positioning deformities
			- Perinatal mortality
			- Neonatal sepsis

	- Initial assessment

		- Maternal assessment
			- History
			- General examination

		- Abdominal examination
			- Fetal size
			- Presentation

		- Sterile speculum examination (preferred)
			- Visualise pooling of liquor
				- Note vernix
			- Exclude cord prolapse
			- Estimate cervical dilatation
			- Collect cervical and vaginal swabs
				- Including GBS

		- Confirm rupture of membranes

			| Test | Findings / Notes |
			|------|------------------|
			| Nitrazine (Amnicator®) | Blue/purple colour change if positive; false-positive rate ~17% |
			| Ferning test | Microscopic fern pattern |
			| Amniotic fluid protein tests | Actim® PROM, AmniSure®, ROM Plus® |

	- Maternal transfer

		- If neonatal facilities inadequate
			- Consult tertiary centre

		- Preferred strategy
			- Maternal transfer before delivery if birth not imminent

		- Neonatal retrieval
			- If delivery anticipated

	- Fetal surveillance

		- CTG
			- Assess fetal wellbeing

		- Ultrasound
			- Liquor volume
			- Presentation

		- Formal ultrasound
			- Number of fetuses
			- Estimated fetal weight
			- Presentation
			- Morphology
			- Liquor volume

	- Investigations

		| Investigation | Initial monitoring |
		|---------------|-------------------|
		| CRP | Daily for first 3 days |
		| Full blood count (CBP/FBC) | Daily for first 3 days |
		| High & low vaginal swabs | Microscopy and culture |
		| MSU | Bacteriology |

	- Antibiotics

		- Indications
			- All women with PPROM unless contraindicated
			- Choice guided by local protocol

		- Benefits
			- Prolong latency
			- Reduce maternal sepsis
			- Reduce neonatal sepsis

	- Chorioamnionitis

		- Diagnosis
			- Primarily clinical
			- May be difficult early

		- Diagnostic criteria
			- Maternal fever
			- Plus ≥2 of
				- WCC >15 ×10⁹/L
				- Maternal tachycardia >100 bpm
				- Fetal tachycardia >160 bpm
				- Uterine tenderness
				- Offensive vaginal discharge
				- CRP >40 mg/L

		- Exclude other infection sources
			- Urinary tract
			- Respiratory tract

		- If diagnosis uncertain
			- Consult
				- Senior obstetrician
				- Maternal-fetal medicine specialist
				- Infectious diseases physician

		- Histological confirmation
			- Placenta and membranes after delivery
			- Acute inflammatory changes

		- Management
			- Do not suppress labour
			- Expedite delivery
			- Administer IV broad-spectrum antibiotics
			- Mode of delivery depends on
				- Clinical condition
				- Expected time to birth

	- Tocolysis

		- Indications
			- Contractions present
			- No evidence of chorioamnionitis

		- Goal
			- Delay birth for approximately 48 hours
			- Allow antenatal corticosteroids

		- Preferred drug
			- Nifedipine

		- Regimen

			| Time | Dose |
			|------|------|
			| Initial | 20 mg orally stat |
			| 30 min later | 20 mg orally |
			| Maximum first hour | 40 mg |
			| Maintenance | 20 mg orally every 3 hours while contractions persist |
			| After 24 hours | Medical review; consider controlled-release nifedipine (Adalat Oros® 2–3 times/day) |

		- Prescribing point
			- Prescribe regularly
			- Not PRN

	- Antenatal corticosteroids

		- Benefits
			- Reduce respiratory distress syndrome
			- Improve neonatal survival
			- Reduce severity of neonatal lung disease
			- Reduce serious neonatal complications

		- Single course

			- Indications
				- Gestation 23+0 to 34+6 weeks
				- Preterm labour
				- High likelihood of delivery within 7 days

			- Regimens

				| Drug | Dose |
				|------|------|
				| Betamethasone | 5.7 mg IM ×2 doses, 24 hours apart (11.4 mg total) |
				| Dexamethasone (if betamethasone unavailable) | 12 mg IM ×2 doses, 24 hours apart |

			- Prediction tools
				- Fetal fibronectin
				- Cervical length

		- Repeat corticosteroids

			- Indications
				- ≤32+6 weeks
				- ≥7 days since previous course
				- Persistent high risk of preterm birth

			- Options
				- Single repeat dose
				- One repeat full course

			- Maximum
				- Up to 3 single repeat doses
				- No further repeat courses after a complete repeat course

	- Magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection

		- Benefits
			- Reduces cerebral palsy
			- Reduces gross motor dysfunction
			- Number needed to treat ≈63

		- Indications
			- Gestation ≥24+0 to <30+0 weeks
			- Birth expected within 24 hours

		- Timing
			- Ideally complete infusion about 4 hours before birth

		- Applies regardless of
			- Singleton or multiple pregnancy
			- Cause of preterm birth
			- Parity
			- Mode of delivery
			- Corticosteroid administration

		- Administration
			- Follow institutional neuroprotection protocol

		- Counselling
			- Multidisciplinary discussion
				- Obstetrician
				- Neonatologist
				- Midwife
				- Others as required

	- Management according to gestational age

		| Gestation | Management |
		|-----------|------------|
		| <23 weeks | Tertiary consultation, counselling, continue antibiotics, expectant vs active management based on maternal wishes and prognosis |
		| 23–34 weeks | Expectant management if stable, antibiotics, corticosteroids, close surveillance until 34 weeks |
		| 34–37 weeks | Active vs expectant management individualized; if GBS positive, active management after 36 completed weeks |

	- PPROM <23 weeks

		- Neonatal outcome depends on
			- Place of birth
			- NICU availability

		- Management considerations
			- Neonatology consultation
			- Shared decision-making with parents
			- Continue antibiotics

		- Deliver if
			- Established labour
			- Chorioamnionitis
			- Significant antepartum haemorrhage
			- Maternal request

		- If expectant management
			- Repeat benzylpenicillin if labour recurs
			- Weekly high vaginal swab
			- CRP twice weekly
			- Full blood count twice weekly

	- PPROM 23–34 weeks

		- Continue antibiotics

		- Deliver if
			- Established labour
			- Chorioamnionitis
			- Significant antepartum haemorrhage
			- Fetal compromise

		- Caesarean section
			- Consider if birth not imminent

		- Expectant management includes
			- Daily medical review
			- Neonatology involvement
			- Education regarding prematurity
			- Repeat benzylpenicillin if labour recurs

		- Maternal observations (twice daily)
			- Temperature
			- Pulse
			- Fetal heart rate
			- Vaginal loss
			- Uterine activity
			- Abdominal pain
			- Uterine tenderness

		- Surveillance

			| Investigation | Frequency |
			|--------------|-----------|
			| CTG | Daily for 3–6 days, then twice weekly if low risk |
			| CRP/FBC | Daily ×3 days, then twice weekly |
			| High vaginal swab | Weekly |

		- Restart CTG if
			- Abdominal pain
			- Uterine tenderness
			- Change in liquor amount or colour
			- Antepartum haemorrhage

		- Laboratory findings suggesting infection
			- Consecutive CRP >20 mg/L
			- Single CRP >40 mg/L

	- PPROM 34–37 weeks

		- Continue antibiotics

		- Deliver if
			- Established labour
			- Chorioamnionitis
			- Significant antepartum haemorrhage
			- Fetal compromise

		- GBS positive
			- Active management after 36 completed weeks

		- Caesarean section
			- Consider if birth not imminent

		- Expectant management
			- Await spontaneous labour until 36 completed weeks
			- Home management may be appropriate in selected women

		- Surveillance
			- Same CTG strategy as 23–34 weeks
			- Restart CTG if maternal or fetal concerns develop

	- Home management

		- Consider after 72 hours of inpatient observation

		- Suitable if
			- Singleton pregnancy
			- Cephalic presentation
			- >23 weeks gestation
			- Reliable access to hospital

		- Continue
			- Daily maternal temperature
			- Twice-weekly outpatient CTG
			- Twice-weekly investigations

		- Return immediately if
			- Reduced fetal movements

	- AMC high-yield points

		- PPROM
			- Membrane rupture before labour and before 37 weeks

		- Sterile speculum examination
			- Preferred assessment
			- Avoid unnecessary digital vaginal examination

		- Maternal transfer
			- Preferred over neonatal retrieval whenever possible

		- Antibiotics
			- Prolong pregnancy
			- Reduce maternal and neonatal infection

		- Nifedipine
			- Preferred tocolytic
			- Used only if no chorioamnionitis
			- Purpose is to gain time for corticosteroids

		- Chorioamnionitis
			- Clinical diagnosis
			- Do not inhibit labour
			- Prompt IV antibiotics and delivery

		- Corticosteroids
			- Window: 23+0–34+6 weeks

		- Magnesium sulfate
			- Neuroprotection at 24+0 to <30+0 weeks
			- Give when birth expected within 24 hours

		- Infection markers
			- CRP >40 mg/L strongly suggests infection

		- CTG
			- Daily initially
			- Then twice weekly if stable
