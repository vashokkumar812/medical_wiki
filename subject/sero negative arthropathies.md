# Psoriatic Arthritis (AMC MCQ High-Yield)

- **Definition**
	- Chronic inflammatory seronegative spondyloarthritis
	- Associated with **psoriasis**
	- Usually **RF and anti-CCP negative**
	- Can occur before, with, or after skin disease

- **Epidemiology**
	- Occurs in **20–30%** of patients with psoriasis
	- Peak age
		- 30–50 years
	- Men and women equally affected

- **Risk Factors**
	- Psoriasis
	- Family history
	- Nail psoriasis
	- Obesity
	- HLA-B27 (especially axial disease)

- **Pathophysiology**
	- Autoimmune inflammation
	- Synovitis
	- Enthesitis (inflammation at tendon insertion)
	- Dactylitis
	- Bone erosion with new bone formation

- **Clinical Features**
	- Peripheral arthritis
		- Asymmetric oligoarthritis (most common)
		- Can become polyarticular
	- Distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint involvement
		- Highly characteristic
	- Dactylitis
		- "Sausage digit"
	- Enthesitis
		- Achilles tendon
		- Plantar fascia
	- Axial disease
		- Sacroiliitis
		- Spondylitis
	- Morning stiffness
		- >30 minutes
		- Improves with activity

- **Skin Features**
	- Well-demarcated erythematous plaques
	- Silvery scales
	- Extensor surfaces
		- Elbows
		- Knees
	- Scalp
	- Umbilicus
	- Gluteal cleft

- **Nail Changes (Highly Tested)**
	- Nail pitting
	- Onycholysis
	- Subungual hyperkeratosis
	- Nail ridging

- **Patterns of Disease**
	- Asymmetric oligoarthritis (most common)
	- Symmetric polyarthritis
	- DIP-predominant arthritis
	- Arthritis mutilans
		 - Severe destructive form
	- Axial spondyloarthritis

- **Investigations**
	- ESR
		- Often raised
	- CRP
		- Often raised
	- Rheumatoid factor
		- Usually negative
	- Anti-CCP
		- Usually negative
	- HLA-B27
		- May be positive in axial disease

- **Imaging**
	- X-ray
		- Joint erosions
		- New bone formation
		- Periostitis
		- **"Pencil-in-cup" deformity**
	- MRI
		- Early sacroiliitis
		- Enthesitis

- **Management**
	- Non-pharmacological
		- Weight loss
		- Exercise
		- Physiotherapy
		- Smoking cessation
	- Mild disease
		- NSAIDs
		- Intra-articular corticosteroid injection
	- Persistent peripheral arthritis
		- **Methotrexate (first-line csDMARD)**
		- Sulfasalazine
		- Leflunomide
	- Moderate to severe disease
		- Biologic DMARDs
			- TNF inhibitors
				- Adalimumab
				- Etanercept
				- Infliximab
			- IL-17 inhibitors
				- Secukinumab
			- IL-12/23 inhibitors
				- Ustekinumab
	- Severe skin disease
		- Coordinate management with dermatology

- **Complications**
	- Joint destruction
	- Permanent deformity
	- Reduced mobility
	- Uveitis
	- Increased cardiovascular risk

- **Differential Diagnosis**
	- Rheumatoid arthritis
		- Symmetrical
		- MCP/PIP joints
		- RF/anti-CCP positive
	- Osteoarthritis
		- Mechanical pain
		- Heberden nodes
	- Reactive arthritis
	- Ankylosing spondylitis
	- Gout

- **AMC Pearls**
	- **Psoriasis + DIP arthritis + nail pitting = Psoriatic arthritis until proven otherwise.**
	- **Dactylitis ("sausage digit") is highly characteristic.**
	- **Enthesitis is a hallmark of seronegative spondyloarthropathies.**
	- **RF and anti-CCP are usually negative.**
	- **Methotrexate is the first-line DMARD for peripheral disease.**
	- **TNF inhibitors are used if conventional DMARDs fail.**
	- **"Pencil-in-cup" deformity on X-ray is classic.**
	- **Unlike rheumatoid arthritis, DIP joints are commonly involved.**

- ankylosing spondylitis
	- definition
		- chronic inflammatory axial spondyloarthritis
		- primarily affects sacroiliac joints and spine

	- epidemiology
		- affects ~1 in 200 individuals
		- diagnosis often delayed
			- average 5–7 years
		- women
			- ~1/3 of patients
		- non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis
			- common early presentation
			- ~50% progress to typical AS

	- important concepts
		- inflammatory markers may be normal
		- X-ray changes are late findings
		- MRI useful in early disease
		- chronic back pain common
			- requires high suspicion for diagnosis

- clinical features
	- inflammatory back pain
		- archetypal feature

	- prevalence
		- ~5% chronic low back pain due to spondyloarthritis
		- inflammatory back pain raises likelihood of SpA to ~14%

	- inflammatory back pain characteristics
		- onset <40 years
		- insidious onset
		- duration >3 months
		- improves with exercise
		- worsens with rest
		- morning stiffness >45 minutes
		- alternating buttock pain
			- highly specific
		- night pain/stiffness
			- wakes patient in second half of night

	- symptoms used to assess activity
		- fatigue
		- spinal pain
		- morning stiffness severity
		- morning stiffness duration

	- mechanical back pain characteristics
		- any age
		- variable onset
		- improves with rest
		- mild short-lived morning stiffness

- spinal mobility
	- may be normal in early disease

	- modified Schober test
		- assesses lumbar flexion
		- serial measures useful for progression
		- may also be abnormal in disc disease

	- lumbar side flexion
		- best measure of overall spinal restriction and activity

	- occiput-to-wall distance
		- normal = zero

	- chest expansion
		- measured at 4th intercostal space
		- abnormal if <5 cm
		- often normal in early disease

- extra-axial manifestations
	- peripheral arthritis
		- asymmetric oligoarthritis
			- ≤4 joints
		- lower limb predominance
		- occurs in up to 50%

	- enthesitis
		- inflammation at tendon/ligament insertion

		- presentations
			- Achilles tendinitis
			- plantar fasciitis
			- intercostal enthesitis
				- chest wall pain

	- dactylitis
		- sausage digit
		- diffuse swelling of finger/toe
		- due to flexor tenosynovitis
		- more common in
			- psoriatic arthritis
			- reactive arthritis

- extra-articular manifestations
	- anterior uveitis
		- ~40%
		- acute painful red eye
		- blurred vision
		- photophobia
		- ophthalmology slit-lamp review mandatory

		- treatment
			- corticosteroid eye drops
			- mydriatic drops

	- inflammatory bowel disease
		- symptomatic IBD in ~10%
		- mucosal inflammation on colonoscopy in ~60%

	- osteoporosis/osteopenia
		- common in chronic disease
		- vertebral fracture risk increased ~7-fold

		- important
			- spinal DEXA may be falsely elevated
				- due to syndesmophytes

	- pulmonary
		- apical pulmonary fibrosis
			- up to 15%

	- cardiac
		- aortic valve incompetence
			- up to 10%

		- pulmonary/cardiac complications
			- usually late
			- often asymptomatic

- juvenile spondyloarthritis
	- ~50% progress to AS in adulthood

	- features
		- asymmetric oligoarthritis
		- severe midfoot pain
			- tarsitis
		- hip involvement

	- predictors of persistent AS
		- HLA-B27 positivity
		- tarsitis
		- hip involvement

- investigations
	- imaging
		- X-ray
			- AP pelvis sufficient
			- evaluates sacroiliac joints

			- AS radiographic criteria
				- bilateral grade 2 sacroiliitis
				- or unilateral grade 3 sacroiliitis

			- sacroiliitis grading
				- grade 1
					- suspicious changes
				- grade 2
					- erosions/sclerosis
					- normal joint space
				- grade 3
					- erosions
					- sclerosis
					- narrowing
					- partial fusion
				- grade 4
					- complete fusion

			- spinal X-ray progression
				- shiny corner
					- Romanus lesion
				- vertebral squaring
				- erosions
				- syndesmophytes
				- bony bridging
				- bamboo spine
					- advanced AS

		- MRI
			- useful in early disease
			- considered if X-rays normal but suspicion high
			- may still be normal in active disease

			- STIR sequences
				- marrow oedema at SI joints/vertebral corners

			- marrow oedema predicts
				- sclerosis
				- fusion

			- chronic sacroiliitis changes better seen on X-ray

		- ultrasonography
			- useful for enthesitis
				- Achilles tendinitis
			- role in sacroiliitis uncertain
			- colour Doppler may help early disease detection

	- laboratory tests
		- ESR/CRP
			- elevated in only 50–70%
			- more likely elevated with
				- peripheral arthritis
				- inflammatory bowel disease

		- HLA-B27
			- positive in 85–90% AS patients
			- present in 5–15% general population
			- only 5% of HLA-B27 positive individuals develop AS

			- role
				- supports diagnosis with clinical features
				- not suitable as screening test

- delay in diagnosis
	- average delay
		- 5–7 years

	- reasons
		- chronic back pain common
		- inflammatory markers may be normal
		- X-ray changes delayed
		- under-recognition in women

	- early features suggesting SpA
		- alternating buttock pain
		- asymmetric peripheral arthritis
		- heel pain
		- uveitis
		- positive family history
		- good NSAID response

	- diagnostic likelihood
		- 1–2 SpA features
			- 35–70% likelihood
		- ≥3 features
			- 80–95% likelihood

- non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis
	- early axial SpA without X-ray sacroiliitis
	- X-ray changes may take >10 years

	- diagnosis
		- MRI sacroiliitis + ≥1 SpA feature
			- diagnostic

		- if MRI normal
			- HLA-B27 + ≥2 SpA features required

	- disease burden
		- similar pain and activity to established AS
		- warrants early treatment

	- ASAS criteria
		- sensitivity
			- 83%
		- specificity
			- 84%

- predictors of poor prognosis
	- juvenile onset
	- hip arthritis
	- persistent active disease
	- smoking
	- poor NSAID response
	- baseline spinal radiographic changes

- management
	- goals
		- minimal morning stiffness
		- stable spinal mobility
		- optimal function

	- education and exercise
		- patient education essential
		- stretching program
		- regular exercise recommended
		- support groups useful

	- NSAIDs
		- first-line therapy
		- regular use may slow radiographic progression
		- assess long-term NSAID risks individually

	- TNF inhibitors
		- indicated if inadequate response to NSAIDs
		- improve quality of life significantly
		- >2/3 patients respond inadequately to NSAIDs

		- PBS criteria
			- active AS
			- inadequate response to exercise + NSAIDs
			- requires radiographic sacroiliitis

		- efficacy
			- all TNF inhibitors similar for axial symptoms
			- choice depends on
				- extra-articular disease
				- patient preference

		- important
			- little evidence for preventing spinal fusion

		- precautions
			- infection risk
			- melanoma risk
			- skin cancer risk
			- withhold before major surgery
				- 1–2 half-lives

	- traditional DMARDs
		- methotrexate
		- sulfasalazine

		- role
			- little effect on spinal disease
			- may help peripheral arthritis

	- monitoring
		- monitor
			- morning stiffness
			- spinal mobility
			- functional status

		- significant deterioration
			- semi-urgent rheumatology review

# DISH
- definition
	- Ligamentous ossification of the spine
- AKA
	- Diffuse Idiopathic Skeletal Hyperostosis
	- Forestier's disease
- Etiology
	- Exact cause unknown  
- associations
	  - **Metabolic disorders**:
	    - Diabetes mellitus  
	    - Obesity  
	    - Hyperinsulinemia  p
- main site
	- **anterior longitudinal ligament**  
- pathogenenesis
	- 🔑 **Non-inflammatory process (key differentiator from AS)**  
- Epidemiology
	- **Older adults**

- Clinical Features
	- Symptoms
		- **Spinal stiffness**
		  - Progressive  
		  - Commonly affects **thoracic spine**
		- **Spinal pain**
		  - absent to moderate  
		- if **Cervical involvement**
		  - Dysphagia  
	- Signs
		- Palpable spinal stiffness  
	- Red Flags (Emergency)
		- Severe dysphagia  
		- Suspected spinal fracture  

- Investigations
	- First-line
		- **X-ray spine**
		- ==diagnostic criteria==
			1. Flowing ossification involving ≥ 4 contiguous vertebrae  
			2. Preservation of intervertebral disc height  
			3. Absence of:
			   - Apophyseal joint ankylosis  
			   - Sacroiliac joint erosion  
	- Gold Standard
		- X-ray is usually sufficient (clinical diagnosis)
	- Most sensitive test
		- X-ray spine  
	- Most specific test
		- X-ray with classical criteria  
	- Test to assess severity
		- Functional mobility assessment  
- Differential Diagnosis
	- [[ankylosing spondylitis]] → inflammatory, SI joint involvement  
	- Osteoarthritis with osteophytes  
	- Spondylosis deformans  

- Management
	- Emergency
		- Fracture → stabilization  
		- Severe dysphagia → consider surgical decompression  
	- First-line treatment
		- **Conservative**
		  - Physiotherapy  
		  - Posture correction  
		  - Exercise  
	- Second-line
		- Surgical intervention (rare)
	- pharmacological management
		- Paracetamol  
		- NSAIDs (if needed)
			- no disease modifications


- Complications
	- Increased risk of **spinal fractures** (due to rigidity)  
	- Dysphagia  

- Prognosis
	- Slowly progressive  
	- Not life-threatening  
	- Can cause disability due to stiffness  
	- Less painful and non-inflammatory compared to AS 

 - Follow-up / Monitoring
	- Functional mobility  
	- Symptom progression
