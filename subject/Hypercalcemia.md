# Calcium Metabolism & Hypercalcaemia

---

## Overview
- Highly regulated cation involved in:
  - Cell death
  - Duration and strength of **cardiac muscle contraction**
  - Muscle contraction (blood vessels, airways, uterus)
  - Coagulation
  - Bone metabolism
  - Neurotransmitter & hormone release  

- Plasma calcium exists in two states:
  - **Free ionized Ca²⁺**  
  - **Protein-bound (mostly albumin, rest to β-globulins, phosphate, citrate)**  

- Ionized Ca²⁺ concentration is **inversely related to pH** → ↑ pH → ↓ ionized Ca²⁺  

---

## Normal Calcium Levels
- **Total Ca²⁺**: 2.2–2.5 mmol/L  
  - 55% bound, 45% ionized  
- **Ionized Ca²⁺**: 1.1–1.3 mmol/L (≈ 50%)  
- **Protein-bound Ca²⁺**: 0.95–1.2 mmol/L (≈ 40%)  
- **Complex Ca²⁺** (phosphate, salts): 0.05 mmol/L (≈ 10%)  

---

## Calcium Metabolism

### Vitamin D
- Group of related sterols  
- **Pathway**:  
  Cholecalciferol (skin) → 25-hydroxycholecalciferol (liver) → 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol (kidney proximal tubules)  
- Controlled by **PTH**  
- Functions: ↑ intestinal absorption, ↑ renal reabsorption, mobilises bone Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻  

---

### Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)
- **Secretion increased by**: [[Hypocalcaemia]], [[Hypomagnesaemia]]  
- **Secretion decreased by**: hypercalcaemia, hypermagnesaemia  
- Functions:
  - Mobilises bone Ca²⁺  
  - ↑ renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption  
  - ↑ renal PO₄³⁻ excretion  
  - ↑ formation of 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol  

---

### Calcitonin
- Antagonist of PTH  
- Secreted by parafollicular thyroid cells in response to:
  - Hypercalcaemia
  - Catecholamines
  - Gastrin  
- Functions:
  - Inhibits bone Ca²⁺ mobilisation  
  - ↑ renal Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ excretion  

---

## Causes of Hypercalcaemia

### By Mechanism
- **Intake**: Ca²⁺, Vit A/D, hypoMg²⁺, hypovolaemia, TPN  
- **Redistribution**: immobilisation, malignancy, hyperparathyroidism, sarcoid, lithium, adrenal insufficiency, endocrine (thyrotoxicosis, acromegaly, phaeochromocytoma)  
- **Output**: urinary losses (thiazides)  

---

### By Frequency

#### Common
- Malignancy (bone mets, humoral hypercalcaemia → PTHrP release)  
  - Lung, breast, prostate, colon, T-cell, MM  
- Post-hypocalcaemic hypercalcaemia (recovery from pancreatitis, ARF, rhabdo)  
- Primary hyperparathyroidism (adenoma)  
- Adrenal insufficiency  
- Prolonged immobilisation  
- Mg²⁺ disorders  
- TPN  
- Hypovolaemia  
- Iatrogenic Ca²⁺  

#### Less Common
- Granulomatous disease (sarcoid, TB, berylliosis, leprosy, histoplasmosis) → ↑ 1,25(OH)₂D  
- Vit A/D intoxication  
- Endocrine: thyrotoxicosis, acromegaly, phaeochromocytoma  
- Chronic lithium therapy  
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism (renal failure)  
- Tertiary hyperparathyroidism (requires parathyroidectomy)  

---

## Clinical Features

**History (mnemonic: “stones, bones, groans, psychic moans”)**  
- GI: [[constipation]], anorexia, N/V  
- Neuro: lethargy, hypotonia, confusion, [[coma]]  
- Renal: polyuria, dehydration, stones  
- CVS: arrhythmias  

**Examination**
- Assess dehydration
- Consciousness level
- Search for cause (malignancy, endocrine signs, etc.)  

---

## Investigations
1. **Confirm cause**:  
   - X-ray, CT, bone scan (malignancy, bony involvement)  
   - ALP (bone turnover)  
   - PTH assay  
2. **Routine labs**:  
   - U&E (renal failure)  
   - Mg²⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, phosphate  
   - Albumin (adjust Ca²⁺: add 0.1 mmol/L per 5 g/L albumin <40)  
3. **Other**:  
   - ECG → shortened QTc, Osborn/J wave  
   - Lipase (pancreatitis)  

---

## Management of Hypercalcaemia

### 1. Quantify severity  

### 2. Increase Ca²⁺ Excretion
- IV N/S (200–300 mL/hr) → promote renal elimination  
- Loop diuretics (↓ reabsorption at Loop of Henle)  
- Steroids (hydrocortisone 100 mg QID):  
  - ↓ Vit D effect, ↓ absorption, ↑ renal elimination  
  - Inhibit osteoclast-activating factor  
  - Useful in **granulomatous disease** (not malignancy)  

### 3. Reduce Ca²⁺ Release
- **Calcitonin** → lowers Ca²⁺ in 24–48 hr  
- **Bisphosphonates** (pamidronate 90 mg IV) → inhibit osteoclasts  
- **Others**: plicamycin, gallium, inorganic phosphate (risk: Ca²⁺ precipitation)  

### 4. Additional Measures
- Stop drugs that ↑ Ca²⁺ (thiazides, Vit A/D, supplements)  
- Restrict intake  
- Correct K⁺, Mg²⁺  
- Mobilise patient  
- Stop [[digoxin]] (arrhythmia risk with hypercalcaemia)  
- **Surgery**: parathyroidectomy if refractory/adenoma
