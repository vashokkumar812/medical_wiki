- Definition
	- Polymyositis
		- Uncommon systemic inflammatory myopathy
		- Characterised by:
			- Symmetrical proximal muscle weakness
			- Muscle wasting
				- Mainly shoulder girdle
				- Pelvic girdle

	- Dermatomyositis
		- Inflammatory myopathy with skin involvement
		- Characterised by:
			- Muscle weakness
			- Characteristic cutaneous manifestations

- Epidemiology
	- Can occur at any age
	- Peak incidence:
			- 40–60 years
	- Female : Male
			- 2 : 1

- Clinical Features

	- Muscle Features
		- Symmetrical proximal muscle weakness
		- Muscle wasting
		- Main complaint:
			- Weakness
		- Difficulty:
			- Climbing stairs
			- Rising from chair
			- Lifting arms
			- Combing hair

	- Muscle Pain
		- Pain and tenderness in ~50%

	- Joint Features
		- Arthralgia
		- Arthritis
			- ~50%
			- May resemble rheumatoid arthritis distribution

	- Oesophageal Involvement
		- Dysphagia
			- ~50%

	- Vascular Features
		- Raynaud phenomenon

	- Malignancy Association
		- Consider underlying malignancy
		- Common associations:
			- Lung cancer
			- Ovarian cancer

- Dermatomyositis – Key Clinical Clue
	- Weakness
	- Joint and muscle pain
	- Violaceous facial rash

- Dermatomyositis Rash

	- General Features
		- Photosensitive rash
		- Erythema resembling sunburn

	- Heliotrope Rash
		- Violet discoloration of eyelids
		- Periorbital oedema

	- Facial Involvement
		- Forehead
		- Cheeks

	- Truncal Rash
		- Macular rash over:
			- Upper chest
			- Back
			- Shoulders

	- Hand Involvement
		- Fingers
		- Nail folds

	- Other Common Sites
		- Knees
		- Elbows

- Differential Diagnosis
	- Statin-induced necrotising myositis
			- Markedly elevated CK

- Diagnosis

	- Muscle Enzymes
		- Creatine kinase (CK)
			- Elevated
		- Aldolase
			- Elevated

	- Autoantibodies
		- Anti-Jo-1 antibody

	- Biopsy
		- Muscle biopsy
		- Skin biopsy in dermatomyositis

	- Electromyography (EMG)
		- Characteristic myopathic pattern

- Management

	- First-Line
		- Corticosteroids

	- Additional Treatments
		- Hydroxychloroquine
			- Especially for skin manifestations
		- Cytotoxic drugs
			- eg methotrexate
			- azathioprine

	- Supportive Care
		- Physiotherapy
		- Occupational therapy
		- Nutritional support

	- Referral
		- Early specialist referral essential
		- Multidisciplinary care recommended

- Monitoring

	- Blood Pressure Monitoring

	- Malignancy Surveillance
		- Increased malignancy risk
		- Age-appropriate cancer screening important

	- Monitor
		- Muscle strength
		- CK levels
		- Treatment adverse effects

- Key Exam Points
	- Symmetrical proximal muscle weakness is hallmark feature
	- Dermatomyositis:
			- Muscle weakness + heliotrope rash
	- Dysphagia suggests oesophageal involvement
	- Anti-Jo-1 associated with inflammatory myopathy
	- Always consider associated malignancy
