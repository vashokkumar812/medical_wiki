

 - Info
	- All STI testing should include:
		- HIV testing
		- Syphilis testing

 - Special Subpopulations
	 -[[Men who have sex with men]]
	 -Sex workers
	 -Pregnant people
	- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people
	- Trans and gender diverse people

 - Avoid Asymptomatic Screening
	- Avoid unless indicated by population-specific guidelines:
		- Hepatitis C
		- Trichomonas
		- Mycoplasma genitalium
		- [[Bacterial vaginosis]]
		- Human papillomavirus (HPV)

	- Important point:
		- MSM and people living with HIV may require HPV vaccination
			- May not have been included in school vaccination programs

 - Diagnosis

	  -Indications for Asymptomatic STI Testing
		- Perform testing in people who:
			- Request STI testing
			- Are at increased risk of STI
				- New sexual partner
				- Living in or travelling to higher prevalence areas
			- Have known exposure to STI
			- Have history of STI within previous 12 months
			- Are partner of:
				- Special subpopulation
				- Any high-risk group



	- HIV testing:
		- HIV antigen/antibody test
		- Repeat if recent exposure
			- 6-week window period for Ag/Ab test

	- Syphilis testing:
		- Syphilis serology
		- If recent exposure:
			- Repeat at 12 weeks
			- Presumptively treat

	  -Hepatitis B Testing

		- Used to establish hepatitis B status

		- Tests:
			- HBsAg
				- Hepatitis B surface antigen
			- Anti-HBs
				- Hepatitis B surface antibody
			- Anti-HBc
				- Hepatitis B core antibody

	  -Gonorrhoea and Chlamydia Testing

		- Urethral first-pass urine (FPU):
			- NAAT
			- Vaginal swab more sensitive than FPU

		- Self-collected vaginal swab:
			- NAAT
			- Specimen of choice

		- Endocervical swab:
			- NAAT
			- Collect if speculum examination indicated

	  -Gonorrhoea Follow-up

		- Positive gonorrhoea NAAT:
			- Recall patient for treatment

		- At same visit:
			- Collect gonorrhoea culture
				- For antibiotic sensitivity testing

	  -Extragenital Testing

		- Pharyngeal and anorectal swabs:
			- Not routinely collected in women

		- Consider in increased-risk women:
			- Sexual contacts of:
				- Gonorrhoea
				- Chlamydia
			- Sex workers

	  -Hepatitis C Testing

		- Perform HCV testing in people with:
			- HIV
			- Current HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) use
			- History of injecting drug use
			- Anal sex with partner with HCV infection
			- History of incarceration
			- Non-professional tattoos or body piercings
			- Receipt of blood products or organs before 1990

	  -Other Infections

		- Trichomonas:
			- Asymptomatic screening only in selected groups/situations

		- Mycoplasma genitalium:
			- Asymptomatic screening not recommended

		- Bacterial vaginosis:
			- Asymptomatic screening not recommended

 - Management

	- Immunise against hepatitis B:
		- If not previously documented

	- Hepatitis B testing not required in:
		- People aged ≤34 years in 2020
		- People who grew up in Australia

	- Discuss HIV PrEP:
		- If clinically appropriate

	- Provide and promote:
		- Condom use during consultation

 - High-Yield AMC Pearls

	- ALL STI screening:
		- HIV + syphilis testing mandatory

	- Best specimen for chlamydia/gonorrhoea in women:
		- Self-collected vaginal swab

	- Gonorrhoea positive NAAT:
		- Always obtain culture for sensitivities

	- Asymptomatic screening NOT routinely recommended for:
		- Mycoplasma genitalium
		- Bacterial vaginosis
		- Trichomonas
		- HPV

	- Hepatitis C screening high-risk groups:
		- HIV
		- PrEP users
		- IVDU
		- Prison history

	- Syphilis recent exposure:
		- Repeat serology at 12 weeks
		- Consider presumptive treatment
