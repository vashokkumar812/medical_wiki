- Rhinitis and rhinosinusitis

- Key points
    - Infective rhinosinusitis rarely causes
        - Orbital complications
        - Intracranial complications
    - Allergic rhinitis management
        - Allergen minimisation
        - Antihistamines
        - ± Nasal steroids
    - Neonatal rhinitis
        - Common
        - Often resolves spontaneously

- Background

    - Definitions
        - Rhinitis
            - Inflammation of nasal mucosa
        - Rhinosinusitis
            - Inflammation of paranasal sinuses
        - Often coexist

    - Rhinitis classification
        - Allergic
            - Seasonal
            - Perennial
        - Non-allergic

    - Rhinosinusitis classification
        - Acute
            - <4 weeks
            - Usually infectious
        - Recurrent acute
            - ≥3 episodes in 6 months with symptom-free intervals
            - Or ≥4 episodes in 12 months
        - Subacute
            - 4–12 weeks
        - Chronic
            - >12 weeks

    - Acute rhinosinusitis
        - Usually viral
        - Secondary bacterial infection possible
            - Especially if symptoms >10 days

    - Viral causes
        - Rhinovirus
        - RSV
        - Influenza
        - Adenovirus

    - Bacterial causes
        - Streptococcus pneumoniae
        - Non-typeable Haemophilus influenzae
        - Moraxella catarrhalis

    - Allergic rhinitis
        - IgE-mediated response to environmental allergens

    - Symptoms of allergic rhinitis
        - Sneezing
        - Watery rhinorrhoea
        - Itching
        - Nasal congestion
        - Ocular symptoms
            - Itchy eyes
            - Watery eyes
            - Red eyes

    - Asthma association
        - Risk factor for asthma development
        - Worsens asthma control
        - Impacts adult asthma prognosis

    - Neonatal rhinitis
        - Common
        - Usually self-resolving

- Assessment

    - Nasal examination

        - Pig nose manoeuvre
            - Tilt head back
            - Elevate nasal tip
            - Illuminate nostril

        - Assess
            - Mucosal surface
            - Turbinate colour/calibre
            - Septal position

        - Allergic rhinitis findings
            - Pale boggy inferior turbinates

        - Infective/medication-induced rhinitis
            - Red oedematous mucosa

- Acute bacterial sinusitis

    - History
        - Preceding viral illness common
        - Nasal discharge >10 days
        - Nasal obstruction
        - Maxillary toothache
        - Unilateral facial pain
        - Headache
        - Fever

    - Examination
        - Inflamed nasal mucosa
        - Acute otitis media commonly present

    - Complications
        - Periorbital cellulitis
        - Orbital cellulitis
        - Cerebral abscess
        - Cavernous sinus thrombosis
        - Meningitis
        - Encephalitis
        - Subdural empyema
        - Epidural empyema

- Chronic rhinosinusitis

    - Usually uncommon <12 years
    - Symptoms >12 weeks

    - Symptoms
        - Nasal obstruction/congestion
        - Nasal discharge
        - Facial pain
        - Postnasal drip
        - Reduced smell
        - Cough
        - Halitosis

    - Risk factors
        - Cigarette smoke exposure
        - Adenoid hypertrophy
        - Allergy
        - Asthma
        - Reflux disease
        - Cystic fibrosis
        - Physical obstruction
        - Mucociliary dysfunction
        - Immunodeficiency
        - Prolonged intranasal decongestant use

- Allergic rhinitis

    - Symptoms
        - Sneezing
        - Itchy nose
        - Sniffing
        - Upward nose rubbing
        - Nasal obstruction
            - Nasal speech
            - Mouth breathing
            - Snoring
        - Itchy throat
        - Frequent throat clearing

    - Triggers
        - Pollen
        - Seasons
        - House dust
        - Mould
        - Mites
        - Animals

    - Seasonal allergic rhinitis
        - Spring/summer
        - Increased pollen counts

    - Perennial allergic rhinitis
        - Persistent symptoms
        - Clear rhinorrhoea
        - Paroxysmal sneezing

    - Associated conditions
        - Asthma
        - Allergic conjunctivitis
        - Eczema

    - Family history
        - Atopy

    - Allergic facies
        - Dennie-Morgan lines
        - Allergic salute
        - Allergic shiners
        - Transverse nasal crease
        - Mouth breathing

    - Examination
        - Clear rhinorrhoea
        - Watery itchy eyes
        - Pale boggy turbinates

- Other causes of rhinitis

    - Obstructive causes

        - Foreign body
            - Unilateral symptoms
            - Brown discharge
            - Foul smell
            - Foreign body may be visible

        - Adenoidal hypertrophy
            - Mouth breathing
            - Snoring

    - Hormonal
        - Menstrual cycle
        - Pregnancy
        - Subclinical hypothyroidism

    - Drug-induced
        - Aspirin
        - NSAIDs
        - Rebound congestion from topical decongestants

    - Environmental irritants
        - Cigarette smoke
        - Pollutants

    - Granulomatous disease
        - External nasal swelling
        - Sinusitis
        - Epistaxis

    - Vasomotor rhinitis
        - Sudden watery rhinorrhoea
        - Triggered by smells/temperature changes

    - Rare causes
        - Anatomical anomalies
        - Immunodeficiency
        - Ciliary dysfunction
        - Cystic fibrosis

- Severity classification of allergic rhinitis

    - Duration
        - Intermittent
            - <4 days/week or <4 weeks
        - Persistent
            - >4 days/week and >4 weeks

    - Severity
        - Mild
            - Normal sleep
            - No activity impairment
            - Normal school/work
        - Moderate-severe
            - Sleep disturbance
            - Daytime tiredness
            - Reduced activities
            - Reduced quality of life

- Investigations

    - Usually clinical diagnosis

- Acute rhinosinusitis investigations

    - Nasal swabs
        - Not useful

    - CT scan
        - Consider if
            - Failed treatment
            - Suspected orbital/intracranial complication
            - Surgery considered

- Allergic rhinitis investigations

    - Positive tests only indicate sensitisation
    - Must correlate clinically

    - Testing may assist allergen minimisation

    - Serum specific IgE
        - Not affected by allergy medications

    - Skin prick testing
        - Affected by allergy medications

- Treatment

- Neonatal rhinitis

    - Avoid
        - Nasal antihistamines
        - Decongestants
        - Steroids
        - Humidifiers

    - Saline drops before feeding

    - Specialist review if
        - Breathing affected
        - Feeding affected

- Acute rhinosinusitis

    - Watchful waiting initially
        - Viral sinusitis most likely

    - Start antibiotics if
        - No improvement
        - Clinical worsening

- Acute bacterial sinusitis

    - First-line
        - Amoxicillin
        - Cefalexin if penicillin allergy

    - If not improving
        - Amoxicillin/clavulanate

    - Systemically unwell/unable to tolerate oral
        - IV amoxicillin/clavulanate
        - Ceftriaxone + metronidazole if penicillin allergy

    - Orbital/intracranial complications
        - Follow local antimicrobial guidelines

    - Additional treatment notes
        - Steroid sprays
        - Decongestants
        - Antihistamines
            - No benefit in acute sinusitis

    - Surgery
        - Rarely needed
        - Unless orbital/intracranial complications

- Chronic rhinosinusitis

    - Intranasal saline
        - Minimum 6 weeks
        - Use before steroid spray

    - Intranasal corticosteroids
        - Minimum 6 weeks
        - Example: mometasone

    - Use with allergen minimisation
    - Assess adherence and spray technique

    - No role for oral/intranasal decongestants

    - ENT referral if persistent symptoms
    - Surgery may be considered

- Allergic rhinitis pharmacotherapy

    - Always combine with allergen minimisation
        - House dust mite
        - Pollens
        - Pet dander
        - Mould

    - Trial each step before escalation
    - Assess adherence and spray technique
    - Use minimal effective regimen
    - No role for decongestants

- Mild allergic rhinitis

    - Step 1
        - Oral antihistamine OR intranasal antihistamine
        - Using both provides no extra benefit

    - Oral antihistamines
        - Better for non-nasal itch
        - Better tolerated in younger children
        - Less sedating preferred
        - Example: cetirizine

    - Intranasal antihistamines
        - Better for local nasal symptoms
        - Faster onset
        - Require good technique
        - Example: azelastine

- Moderate-severe allergic rhinitis

    - Step 2
        - Add intranasal corticosteroid
        - Especially effective for obstruction and ocular symptoms
        - Requires good adherence/technique

    - Step 3
        - Combination intranasal antihistamine + corticosteroid
        - Cease oral antihistamine if used

    - Step 4
        - Add site-specific therapy

        - Ocular symptoms
            - Ketotifen eye drops
            - Olopatadine eye drops

        - Intermittent allergic rhinitis + asthma
            - Consider montelukast

- Allergen immunotherapy

    - Indication
        - Moderate-severe symptoms despite treatment

    - Requires immunology/allergy referral

    - Types
        - SCIT
        - SLIT

    - Duration
        - ≥3 years

    - Not PBS funded
        - Significant cost possible

- Consult paediatric team when
    - Systemically unwell
    - Suspected orbital/intracranial complications

- ENT referral indications
    - Suppurative complications
    - Surgical intervention needed
    - Refractory nasal obstruction
    - Persistent symptoms despite treatment

- Immunology/allergy referral indications
    - Further allergy testing required
    - Severe/uncontrolled allergic rhinitis
    - Considering immunotherapy

- Transfer indications
    - Suspected intracranial involvement
        - Altered consciousness
        - Seizures
        - Focal neurological signs
    - Escalation beyond local capability
