- Definition
	- Heart failure (HF)
		- Clinical syndrome caused by structural and/or functional cardiac abnormality
		- Results in inadequate cardiac output and/or elevated filling pressures

- Classification
	- HFrEF
		- Symptoms/signs of HF
		- LVEF ≤40%
	- HFmrEF
		- LVEF 41–49%
		- Additional evidence required
			- Raised filling pressures
			- Diastolic dysfunction
	- HFpEF
		- Symptoms/signs of HF
		- LVEF ≥50%
		- Plus objective evidence of
			- Structural heart disease
				- LV hypertrophy
				- Left atrial enlargement
			- And/or diastolic dysfunction with raised filling pressures
				- Echo
				- Cardiac catheterisation
				- Elevated BNP/NT-proBNP
				- Exercise testing

- Aetiology
	- Ischaemic heart disease (commonest cause)
		- MI
		- Chronic ischaemia
		- Microvascular disease
		- Hibernating myocardium
	- Hypertension
	- Valvular heart disease
		- Aortic stenosis
		- Aortic regurgitation
		- Mitral regurgitation
		- Rheumatic heart disease
	- Cardiomyopathies
		- Dilated cardiomyopathy
		- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
		- ARVC
		- Peripartum cardiomyopathy
	- Myocarditis
		- Viral
		- Autoimmune
	- Infiltrative disease
		- Amyloidosis
		- Sarcoidosis
		- Haemochromatosis
		- Fabry disease
	- Metabolic/endocrine
		- Diabetes
		- Thyroid disease
		- Phaeochromocytoma
		- Cortisol disorders
	- Toxic
		- Alcohol
		- Anthracyclines
		- Amphetamines
		- Cocaine
		- Clozapine
		- Trastuzumab
		- Radiation
	- High-output states
		- Anaemia
		- Sepsis
		- AV fistula
		- Thyrotoxicosis
	- Arrhythmias
		- AF
		- VT
		- Bradyarrhythmias
		- AV block
	- Congenital heart disease
	- Pericardial disease
		- Constriction
		- Effusion

- Clinical Features
	- Cardiovascular
		- Dyspnoea (most common)
		- Exertional dyspnoea
		- Orthopnoea
		- Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea (PND)
		- Bendopnoea
		- Fatigue
		- Elevated JVP
		- Hepatojugular reflux
		- S3 gallop
		- Laterally displaced apex beat
		- Tachycardia
		- Cardiac murmur

	- Respiratory
		- Nocturnal cough
		- Wheeze (cardiac asthma)
		- Tachypnoea
		- Pulmonary crackles
		- Pleural effusion
		- Cheyne-Stokes respiration

	- Fluid Overload / Congestive Features
		- Weight gain >2 kg/week
		- Peripheral oedemaintestinal / Hepatic Congestion
		- Abdominal bloating
		- Anorexia

	- Neurological / Psychological
		- Palpitations
		- Dizziness
		- Syncope
		- Confusion (elderly)
		- Depression

	- Advanced Heart Failure
		- Weight loss
		- Cardiac cachexia 
	

- Functional Classification (NYHA)
	- Class I
		- No limitation
		- Symptoms absent with ordinary activity
	- Class II
		- Mild limitation
		- Symptoms with ordinary activity
	- Class III
		- Marked limitation
		- Symptoms with less than ordinary activity
	- Class IV
		- Symptoms at rest

- Investigations
	- Initial
		- ECG
		- Chest X-ray
			- Cardiomegaly
			- Pulmonary oedema
			- Pleural effusion
			- Alternative diagnoses
	- Biomarkers
		- BNP
			- Rule out HF
				- <100 ng/L
			- Rule in HF
				- \>400 ng/L
		- NT-proBNP
			- Rule out HF
				- <300 ng/L
			- Rule in HF
				- <50 years
					- \>450 ng/L
				- 50–75 years
					- \>900 ng/L
				- \>75 years
					- \>1800 ng/L
	- Definitive investigation
		- Transthoracic echocardiography
			- Most important test
	- Further investigations
		- Coronary angiography
			- Suspected CAD
			- Refractory angina
			- VT/cardiac arrest
		- CT coronary angiography
			- Low/intermediate CAD probability
		- Cardiac MRI
			- Cardiomyopathy
			- Myocarditis
			- Infiltrative disease
		- PET/Bone scintigraphy
			- Amyloidosis/infiltrative disease
		- Genetic testing
			- DCM + conduction disease

- Acute Decompensated Heart Failure
	- Common precipitants
		- Infection (commonest)
		- ACS/MI
		- AF/arrhythmia
		- Medication non-compliance
		- Excess salt intake
		- Excess fluid intake
	- Cardiac causes
		- Hypertensive crisis
		- Mechanical complications of MI
		- Pericardial tamponade
	- Respiratory causes
		- Pneumonia
		- Pulmonary embolism
		- Hypoxia
	- Systemic causes
		- Anaemia
		- Hyperthyroidism
		- Hypothyroidism
		- Adrenal disorders
	- Other causes
		- Takotsubo cardiomyopathy
		- Phaeochromocytoma

- Acute Management
	- Identify and treat precipitant
	- Monitoring
		- Continuous SpO₂
	- Oxygen
		- If SpO₂ <94%
	- Pulmonary oedema
		- NIV
			- Persistent hypoxia
			- Tachypnoea despite oxygen
	- Congestion
		- IV loop diuretic
			- First-line
			- Furosemide
	- SBP >90 mmHg
		- Consider IV vasodilator
	- Hypoperfusion + SBP <90 mmHg
		- Consider IV inotrope

- Prevention
	- Smoking cessation
	- Avoid excess alcohol
	- Weight reduction
	- Regular exercise
	- Control hypertension
	- Lipid lowering therapy
	- ACEI
		- Established CVD
		- LV systolic dysfunction
	- SGLT2 inhibitor
		- T2DM + CVD

- Chronic HFrEF Treatment
	- Foundation ("Fantastic Four")
		- ACEI / ARB / ARNI
		- Beta blocker
		- MRA
		- SGLT2 inhibitor
	- Diuretics
		- Symptomatic congestion
	- ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan)
		- Replace ACEI/ARB
		- Persistent symptoms
		- ACEI washout
			- ≥36 hours
	- Ivabradine
		- LVEF ≤35%
		- Sinus rhythm
		- HR ≥70 bpm
	- Hydralazine + nitrate
		- ACEI/ARB intolerance
	- Digoxin
		- NYHA III–IV
		- Sinus rhythm

- Device Therapy
	- CRT
		- LVEF ≤35%
		- Sinus rhythm
		- QRS ≥150 ms
	- ICD
		- Secondary prevention
			- Cardiac arrest
			- Sustained VT
		- Primary prevention
			- Post-MI + LVEF ≤30%
			- Ischaemic cardiomyopathy
				- LVEF ≤35%
			- DCM
				- LVEF ≤35%

- Surgical / Advanced Therapy
	- CABG
		- Ischaemic HFrEF
		- LVEF ≤35%
	- Valve intervention
		- SAVR
			- Severe AS/AR
		- TAVI
			- High surgical risk severe AS
		- Mitral valve repair/replacement
			- Severe MR
	- Ventricular assist device (VAD)
	- Heart transplantation

- Drugs That Cause/Worsen HF
	- NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors)
	- Verapamil
	- Diltiazem
	- Flecainide
	- Disopyramide
	- Quinidine
	- Corticosteroids
	- Thiazolidinediones
		- Pioglitazone
		- Rosiglitazone
	- Moxonidine
	- Clozapine
	- Trastuzumab
	- Tyrosine kinase inhibitors
		- Sunitinib
	- Saxagliptin
	- Minoxidil
	- Anthracyclines
	- Cocaine
	- Amphetamines

- Special Situations
	- AF
		- Assess stroke risk
		- Anticoagulate if indicated
		- Rate control target 60–100 bpm
		- Consider AF ablation if LVEF ≤35%
	- Iron deficiency
		- Ferritin <100 μg/L
		- OR ferritin 100–300 μg/L + TSAT <20%
		- Consider IV iron
	- Recovered EF
		- Continue ACEI/ARB/ARNI
		- Continue beta blocker
		- Continue MRA
	- Advanced HF
		- Early palliative care referral

# treatment
- Loop diuretics
	- Role
		- Relieve symptoms of fluid retention
	- Principles
		- Use minimum effective dose
		- Monitor clinically + biochemically
	- Monitor for
		- Volume depletion
		- Electrolyte disturbance

- ACE inhibitors
	- Initiation
		- Start immediately unless contraindicated
	- Benefits
		- Improve symptoms
		- Reduce mortality
		- Slow disease progression
	- Optimisation
		- Aim for maximum tolerated dose
		- Reduce loop diuretic dose if needed to allow ACEi up-titration

- Hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate
	- Indication
		- ACE inhibitor intolerance
	- Regimen
		- High-dose hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate

- Spironolactone
	- Low-dose spironolactone
		- Reduces
			- Mortality
			- Hospitalisation
		- Improves symptoms
	- Used with
		- ACE inhibitor
		- Loop diuretic
		- ± digoxin
	- Particularly useful in severe heart failure

- Beta blockers
	- Carvedilol
		- Non-selective beta blocker
		- Additional alpha-blocking vasodilator effect
	- Indication
		- Mild–moderate heart failure
	- AMC pearl
		- Only beta blocker specifically marketed for HF in Australia

- Digoxin
	- Main indication
		- Heart failure + atrial fibrillation
	- Role
		- Symptom control
		- Rate control in AF

- Anticoagulation
	- Indications in heart failure
		- Atrial fibrillation
		- Intracardiac thrombus
		- Previous systemic thromboembolism

- Other drug therapy
	- Alpha blockers
		- Ineffective
	- Calcium channel blockers
		- Generally not beneficial
	- ARBs
		- Previously under investigation
		- Now commonly used if ACEi intolerant

- Severe refractory heart failure
	- Mechanical support
		- Intra-aortic balloon pump
	- Heart transplantation

- Acute severe heart failure
	- IV vasodilators
		- GTN infusion
		- Sodium nitroprusside
		- Effects
			- ↓ preload
			- ↓ afterload
	- Parenteral inotropes
		- Dopamine
		- Dobutamine
		- Milrinone

- Asymptomatic LV dysfunction
	- ACE inhibitors
		- Prevent progression
		- Improve survival

- Cardiovascular risk factor management
	- Treat associated conditions
		- Hypertension
		- Dyslipidaemia

- AMC MCQ pearls
	- First-line mortality-reducing drugs in HFrEF
		- ACE inhibitors
		- Beta blockers
		- Spironolactone
	- Loop diuretics
		- Improve symptoms
		- No major mortality benefit
	- Digoxin
		- Especially useful in HF + AF
	- Nitrates
		- Reduce preload
	- Nitroprusside
		- Reduces preload + afterload
	- Severe refractory HF
		- Consider transplant/mechanical support
