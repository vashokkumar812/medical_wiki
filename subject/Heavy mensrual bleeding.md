- Key points
	- most common cause of heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) in adolescents:
		- anovulatory cycles
	- important differentials:
		- pregnancy-related bleeding
		- bleeding disorders
	- hormonal + non-hormonal treatments may be combined
	- mild bleeding with normal haemoglobin:
		- usually managed conservatively

- Background

	- Normal menstrual blood loss
		- pad/tampon changes:
			- ≥3-hour intervals
			- rarely overnight
		- <21 pads/tampons per cycle

	- Excessive menstrual flow
		- >7 days duration
		- >80 mL/cycle
		- changing super pad/tampon >every 2 hours
		- symptomatic anaemia
		- lifestyle disturbance

	- Anovulatory uterine bleeding
		- excessive non-cyclic bleeding
		- due to immature hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis
		- occurs without:
			- structural uterine lesions
			- systemic disease

	- Menstrual cycles after menarche
		- commonly irregular/anovulatory initially
		- regular ovulatory cycles establish gradually
		- delayed with increasing age of menarche

- Causes

	- Pregnancy-related
		- miscarriage
		- ectopic pregnancy
		- gestational trophoblastic disease

	- Endocrine
		- anovulatory cycles
			- most common in adolescents
		- thyroid dysfunction
		- PCOS

	- Haematological
		- von Willebrand disease
		- platelet function disorder
		- thrombocytopenia
		- other bleeding disorders

	- Medications
		- hormonal contraception
		- anticoagulants

	- Other
		- trauma
		- infection
		- malignancy
		- structural causes
			- uncommon in adolescence
		- gastrointestinal bleeding

- Assessment

	- History

		- Menstrual history
			- menarche
			- LMP
			- frequency
			- duration
			- flow
			- pain
			- flooding
			- clots >2 cm
			- pad/tampon frequency

		- Symptoms of anaemia
			- dizziness
			- dyspnoea
			- fatigue

		- Bleeding disorder symptoms
			- epistaxis
			- bleeding gums
			- easy bruising

		- Sexual history
			- HEADSS assessment

		- Past medical history
			- coagulopathy
			- platelet disorders
			- haemorrhagic cysts

		- Functional impact
			- school absence
			- sports limitation
			- social impact

		- Factors affecting treatment choice
			- dysmenorrhoea
			- mid-cycle pain
			- luteal symptoms
				- nausea
				- breast tenderness
				- bloating
			- contraception needs

	- Examination
		- vital signs
			- hypotension
			- tachycardia
		- skin
			- pallor
			- petechiae
			- bruising
		- abdomen
			- tenderness
			- pelvic mass
		- vaginal examination
			- rarely indicated
			- senior discussion required
			- perform once only if necessary

- Severity assessment

	- Mild
		- slightly/moderately increased flow
		- normal haemoglobin

	- Moderate
		- frequent/prolonged menses
			- every 1–3 weeks
		- moderate-heavy flow
		- Hb ≥100 g/L

	- Severe
		- Hb <100 g/L
		- haemodynamic instability

- Investigations

	- Consider
		- FBE
		- ferritin
		- coagulation screen
		- TSH
		- urine/blood ßhCG
			- with consent
		- blood group + antibody screen
			- severe bleeding

	- If bleeding disorder suspected
		- PFA-100
		- von Willebrand screen
		- avoid testing:
			- during acute bleeding
			- after recent NSAID use

	- Pelvic ultrasound
		- rarely required
		- consider if:
			- atypical features
			- palpable mass

- Treatment principles
	- treat underlying cause
	- anovulatory bleeding:
		- stabilise endometrium
			- oestrogen:
				- initial haemostasis
			- progestin:
				- prolonged stability
	- consider:
		- iron supplementation
		- menstrual tracking app/calendar
			- Clue®
			- Flo®

- Acute management by severity

	- Mild
		- reassurance + observation
		- if no contraception desired:
			- non-hormonal treatment
				- NSAIDs
				- tranexamic acid during menses

	- Moderate
		- tranexamic acid during menses
		- hormonal therapy:
			- progesterone-only therapy
			- combined OCP

	- Severe
		- exclude:
			- pregnancy
			- bleeding disorder
		- IV access
		- resuscitation
		- tranexamic acid
		- high-dose hormonal therapy
			- eg norethisterone
		- iron infusion if required
		- PRBC transfusion if unstable despite fluids
		- specialist gynaecology review
		- consider admission

- Non-hormonal medications

	- Tranexamic acid
		- 15–25 mg/kg
			- max 1 g
		- 2–3 times daily during heavy bleeding
		- decreases flow by 25–50%

	- NSAIDs
		- mefenamic acid 500 mg tds
		- naproxen 500 mg bd
		- use regularly during menstruation
		- can combine with tranexamic acid

- Hormonal medications

	- Progestins
		- examples:
			- norethisterone
			- medroxyprogesterone acetate
		- mechanism:
			- creates secretory endometrium
		- useful in anovulation

	- Active bleeding treatment

		- Norethisterone
			- 5–10 mg tds for 10 days
			- taper slowly
				- eg reduce by 5 mg/week

		- Medroxyprogesterone acetate
			- 10–20 mg tds for 10 days
			- then:
				- stop
				- or continue 10 mg daily prophylaxis

	- Important notes
		- abrupt cessation precipitates bleeding
		- seek specialist advice if:
			- higher doses needed
			- poor response

- Prophylactic hormonal therapy

	- Norethisterone
		- 5–10 mg daily continuous

	- Medroxyprogesterone acetate
		- 10 mg daily continuous

	- Depot medroxyprogesterone
		- 150 mg IM every 12 weeks

	- Mirena IUD
		- effective 5–8 years

	- Combined OCP
		- decreases flow by ~50%
		- effective for:
			- anovulation
			- irregular menses
		- commonly:
			- ethinylestradiol 30 microg
			- levonorgestrel 150 microg

- Combined OCP use in active bleeding
	- 1 pill every 8 hours until bleeding stops
		- usually within 48 hours
	- then:
		- 1 pill every 12 hours for 3 days
	- then:
		- 1 pill daily continuously
	- continue until outpatient review

- Combined OCP prophylaxis
	- 1 pill daily
	- transition to continuous use after first withdrawal bleed

- Follow up
	- GP/paediatrician review:
		- within 2 weeks for active bleeding resolution
	- after maintenance therapy:
		- reassess after 3–4 months
			- ~3–4 cycles

- Consult gynaecology when
	- severe bleeding
	- haemodynamic instability
	- symptomatic anaemia
	- Hb <100 g/L
	- poor response to tranexamic acid/hormonal therapy

- Consider transfer when
	- escalation beyond local capability
	- unable to control bleeding

- Discharge criteria
	- clinically stable
	- review arranged:
		- within 1 month for mild bleeding
		- within 48 hours if ongoing heavy bleeding
