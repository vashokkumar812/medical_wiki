- Anisocoria
  - Difference in pupil size between eyes
  - May be
    - Physiological
    - Pathological
    - Pharmacological
  - May indicate disease involving
    - Eye
    - Orbit
    - Brain
    - Neck
    - Chest

- Physiological anisocoria
  - Present in ~20% of population
  - Difference usually ≤2 mm
  - Equal in light and dark
  - Normal pupil reactions
  - No ptosis
  - No EOM abnormalities
  - Reassurance only

- Pupillary pathways

  - Parasympathetic pathway (pupil constriction)
    - CN II
    - Pretectal nucleus
    - Bilateral Edinger-Westphal nuclei
    - CN III
    - Ciliary ganglion
    - Sphincter pupillae

  - Sympathetic pathway (pupil dilation)
    - Hypothalamus
    - Brainstem
    - Cervical spinal cord
    - Superior cervical ganglion
    - Internal carotid plexus
    - Dilator pupillae

- Examination of anisocoria

  - Assess
    - Visual acuity
    - Visual fields
    - Pupil size in light
    - Pupil size in dark
    - Direct reflex
    - Consensual reflex
    - Accommodation
    - Ptosis
    - EOMs
    - Pupil shape
    - Heterochromia

  - Perform swinging flashlight test
    - Detect RAPD

- Interpretation

  - Anisocoria greater in light
    - Large pupil abnormal
    - Parasympathetic lesion

  - Anisocoria greater in dark
    - Small pupil abnormal
    - Sympathetic lesion

  - Same anisocoria in light and dark
    - Physiological anisocoria

---

- Large pupil abnormal (anisocoria greater in light)

  - Causes
    - CN III palsy
    - Adie's tonic pupil
    - Pharmacological mydriasis
    - Traumatic mydriasis

- CN III palsy

  - Dilated pupil
  - Marked ptosis
  - Diplopia
  - Ophthalmoplegia
  - Eye "down and out"

  - New-onset CN III palsy
    - Posterior communicating artery aneurysm until proven otherwise

  - Most appropriate investigation
    - Urgent CTA/MRA brain

- Adie's tonic pupil

  - Young women
  - Usually benign
  - May follow viral illness
  - Large pupil
  - Poor light response
  - Better near response
  - Light-near dissociation
  - Slow redilation

- Pharmacological mydriasis

  - Atropine
  - Tropicamide
  - Anticholinergics
  - Plant alkaloids
  - Usually topical exposure

- Traumatic mydriasis

  - Iris sphincter injury
  - Previous trauma
  - Irregular pupil

---

- Small pupil abnormal (anisocoria greater in dark)

  - Causes
    - Horner syndrome
    - Argyll Robertson pupil
    - Pharmacological miosis

---

- Horner syndrome

  - Interruption of sympathetic pathway to eye
  - Lesion anywhere from
    - Hypothalamus
    - Brainstem
    - Cervical spinal cord
    - Superior cervical ganglion
    - Internal carotid artery
    - Cavernous sinus
    - Orbit

  - Classic triad
    - Miosis
    - Mild ptosis
    - Anhidrosis

  - Additional findings
    - Apparant enophthalmos
    - Reverse ptosis of lower lid
    - Hot flushed face
    - Reduced sweating

  - Pupil findings
    - Small pupil
    - Anisocoria greater in dark
    - Dilation lag
    - Normal direct reflex
    - Normal consensual reflex
    - Normal accommodation response

---

- Sympathetic pathway localisation

  - First-order neuron (central)

    - Pathway
      - Hypothalamus
      - Brainstem
      - Cervical spinal cord
      - Synapse at C8–T2

    - Causes
      - Brainstem stroke
      - Lateral medullary syndrome
      - Brainstem tumour
      - Multiple sclerosis
      - Trauma

    - Associated findings
      - Hemiparesis
      - Sensory loss
      - Dysarthria
      - Ataxia
      - Hemibody anhidrosis

    - AMC clue
      - Horner + neurological deficits
        - Central lesion

  - Second-order neuron (preganglionic)

    - Pathway
      - Spinal cord
      - Lung apex
      - Neck
      - Superior cervical ganglion

    - Causes
      - Pancoast tumour
      - Mediastinal mass
      - Neck trauma
      - Chest trauma
      - Thyroid surgery
      - Thoracic surgery
      - Iatrogenic injury

    - Associated findings
      - Shoulder pain
      - Chest pain
      - Neck pain
      - Facial pain
      - Facial and neck anhidrosis

    - AMC clue
      - Horner + smoker + shoulder pain
        - Pancoast tumour

  - Third-order neuron (postganglionic)

    - Pathway
      - Internal carotid artery
      - Cavernous sinus
      - Orbit

    - Causes
      - Internal carotid artery dissection
      - Carotid aneurysm
      - Carotid disease
      - Cavernous sinus lesion
      - Carotid-cavernous fistula
      - Nasopharyngeal carcinoma
      - Migraine
      - Cluster headache
      - ENT infection

    - Associated findings
      - Facial pain
      - Neck pain
      - Headache
      - Eye pain
      - Forehead-only anhidrosis

    - AMC clue
      - Horner + acute neck pain
        - Carotid dissection

---

- Painful Horner syndrome

  - Internal carotid artery dissection until proven otherwise
  - AMC emergency

  - Symptoms
    - Acute neck pain
    - Facial pain
    - Eye pain
    - Headache

  - Most appropriate investigation
    - Urgent CTA neck

---

- Pancoast tumour

  - Superior sulcus tumour
  - Lung apex tumour
  - Strong smoking association

  - Clinical features
    - Shoulder pain
    - Arm pain
    - Hand weakness
    - Hand muscle wasting
    - Sensory loss upper chest
    - Brachial plexus involvement
    - Horner syndrome

  - Brachial plexus findings
    - Weak hand grip
    - Hand muscle atrophy
    - Upper limb weakness
    - Upper limb sensory symptoms

  - Most appropriate investigations
    - Chest X-ray
    - CT chest

  - Prognosis
    - Aggressive tumour
    - High mortality if untreated

---

- Argyll Robertson pupil

  - Small irregular pupils
  - Light-near dissociation
  - Accommodates but does not react
  - Usually bilateral
  - Classically due to neurosyphilis

---

- Pharmacological miosis

  - Pilocarpine
  - Opioids

---

- Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect (RAPD)

  - No anisocoria
  - Afferent pathway lesion
  - Usually optic nerve disease

  - Swinging flashlight test
    - Light moved to affected eye
    - Both pupils paradoxically dilate

  - Causes
    - Optic neuritis
    - Ischaemic optic neuropathy
    - Advanced glaucoma
    - Optic nerve compression
    - Traumatic optic neuropathy
    - Retinal detachment
    - Retinal artery occlusion
    - Retinal vein occlusion
    - Ocular melanoma

---

- Iris causes of anisocoria

  - Trauma
  - Previous ocular surgery
  - Cataract surgery
  - Posterior synechiae
  - Uveitis

- Uveitis

  - Red eye
  - Pain
  - Photophobia
  - Blurred vision
  - Irregular pupil

  - Management
    - Ophthalmology referral

---

- Leukocoria

  - White pupil
  - Retinoblastoma until proven otherwise

  - Management
    - Urgent ophthalmology referral

---

- AMC Red Flags

  - New CN III palsy
    - Posterior communicating artery aneurysm

  - Painful Horner syndrome
    - Internal carotid artery dissection

  - Horner syndrome + brainstem signs
    - Brainstem stroke

  - Horner syndrome + smoker + shoulder pain
    - Pancoast tumour

  - Horner syndrome + hoarseness
    - Chest/neck malignancy

  - RAPD
    - Optic nerve pathology

  - Red painful eye + anisocoria
    - Uveitis

  - Leukocoria in child
    - Retinoblastoma

---

- AMC Rapid Pattern Recognition

  - Anisocoria greater in light
    - Large pupil abnormal

  - Anisocoria greater in dark
    - Small pupil abnormal

  - Large pupil + ptosis + ophthalmoplegia
    - CN III palsy

  - New CN III palsy
    - PCom aneurysm

  - Large pupil + poor light response + near response preserved
    - Adie's pupil

  - Small pupil + mild ptosis + anhidrosis
    - Horner syndrome

  - Horner + neck pain
    - Carotid dissection

  - Horner + smoker + shoulder pain
    - Pancoast tumour

  - Horner + ataxia/dysarthria/hemiparesis
    - Brainstem stroke

  - Small irregular pupils + accommodates but does not react
    - Argyll Robertson pupil

  - RAPD without anisocoria
    - Optic nerve disease

  - White pupil in child
    - Retinoblastoma
