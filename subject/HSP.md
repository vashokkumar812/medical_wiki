- Henoch-Schönlein purpura
	- definition
		- most common childhood vasculitis
		- IgA-mediated small vessel vasculitis

	- epidemiology
		- most common in children
			- 2–8 years

	- key points
		- urinalysis and blood pressure measurement mandatory
		- most cases self-limiting
		- close follow-up essential
		- renal involvement often asymptomatic

	- clinical diagnosis
		- rash plus one or more of
			- arthritis/arthralgia
			- abdominal pain
			- nephritis

	- frequencies
		- arthritis/arthralgia
			- 50–75%
		- abdominal pain
			- ~50%
		- nephritis
			- 25–50%

- history
	- manifestations may evolve over days to weeks
	- order of symptoms variable
	- purpura
		- occurs in all patients
		- presenting complaint in ~75%

	- preceding infection
		- upper respiratory tract infection
			- ~50%
		- usually
			- viral
			- Group A streptococcus

- examination
	- general
		- blood pressure
			- must be measured initially and during follow-up
		- assess for hypertension

	- skin
		- palpable purpura
		- petechiae
		- ecchymoses

		- preceding lesions may include
			- urticarial lesions
			- erythematous lesions
			- maculopapular lesions
			- bullous lesions

		- rash characteristics
			- symmetrical
			- gravity-dependent distribution
			- pressure-dependent areas

		- common locations
			- buttocks
			- lower limbs

		- oedema
			- painful
			- non-pitting
			- subcutaneous

		- oedema locations
			- periorbital
				- especially non-ambulant child
			- hands
			- feet
			- scrotum

	- joints
		- arthralgia ± arthritis
		- usually large joints of lower limbs
		- upper limb involvement rare
		- minimal warmth or effusion

	- abdominal
		- diffuse abdominal pain
		- generalised tenderness
		- bowel obstruction signs
		- peritonism

		- complications
			- intussusception
				- most common complication
			- GI haemorrhage
			- bowel ischaemia
			- bowel necrosis
			- bowel perforation
			- protein-losing enteropathy
			- pancreatitis

	- genital
		- testicular pain
		- orchitis
		- testicular necrosis
		- cord haematoma

		- important
			- exclude testicular torsion

	- respiratory
		- rare
		- respiratory distress
		- diffuse alveolar haemorrhage

	- neurological
		- rare
		- altered mental status
		- labile mood
		- apathy
		- hyperactivity
		- encephalopathy
		- focal neurological signs

		- consider
			- intracranial haemorrhage

	- renal/urinalysis findings
		- microscopic haematuria
		- macroscopic haematuria
		- proteinuria
			- mild to severe

		- renal manifestations
			- nephrotic syndrome
			- acute nephritic syndrome
			- hypertension
			- renal impairment
			- renal failure

- investigations
	- classic presentation
		- urinalysis usually sufficient

	- if hypertension/macroscopic haematuria/significant proteinuria
		- urine microscopy
		- urinary protein-creatinine ratio
			- UPCR
		- UEC
		- albumin

	- if diagnosis unclear or complications suspected
		- FBE
		- UEC
		- albumin
		- blood cultures
		- urine cultures
		- abdominal imaging
		- ASOT
		- anti-DNase B
		- ANA
		- dsDNA
		- ANCA
		- C3
		- C4

	- differentials to exclude
		- ITP
		- leukaemia
		- meningococcal infection

- treatment
	- most cases
		- supportive management only

	- mild pain
		- bed rest
		- elevation of oedematous area
		- paracetamol

		- NSAIDs if not contraindicated
			- ibuprofen
				- 10 mg/kg TDS
				- max 400 mg
			- naproxen
				- 10 mg/kg BD
				- max 500 mg

		- avoid NSAIDs if
			- GI bleeding
			- renal impairment

	- moderate-severe pain
		- steroids may help
			- abdominal pain
			- joint pain
		- may reduce symptom duration
		- do not reduce long-term renal complications

		- oral prednisolone
			- 1–2 mg/kg/day
			- max 60 mg/day

		- IV methylprednisolone
			- 0.8–1.6 mg/kg/day
			- max 1 g/day

		- taper gradually after symptom resolution

- follow-up
	- essential
		- renal involvement often asymptomatic

	- if initial urinalysis normal or microscopic haematuria only
		- clinical review + BP + urinalysis

			- weekly
				- first month

			- fortnightly
				- weeks 5–12

			- single review
				- 6 months
				- 12 months

		- return to weekly review if
			- flare occurs
			- renal involvement develops

		- if normal urinalysis at 12 months
			- no further follow-up needed

- indications for paediatric consultation/admission
	- serious abdominal complications
	- severe debilitating pain
	- severe renal involvement
	- neurological involvement
	- pulmonary involvement
	- if prednisolone considered
	- hypertension
	- proteinuria
	- macroscopic haematuria

- indications for renal specialist referral
	- hypertension
	- abnormal renal function
	- macroscopic haematuria >5 days
	- nephrotic syndrome
	- acute nephritic syndrome
	- persistent proteinuria

	- UPCR thresholds
		- >250 mg/mmol for 4 weeks
		- >100 mg/mmol for 3 months
		- >50 mg/mmol for 6 months

- transfer indication
	- care exceeds local healthcare capability

- discharge
	- appropriate follow-up arranged

- prognosis
	- first episode
			- usually resolves within 4 weeks
			- if no significant renal disease

	- rash
		- often last symptom to resolve

	- joint pain
		- usually resolves within 72 hours

	- uncomplicated abdominal pain
		- resolves within 24–48 hours

	- recurrence
		- 25–35%
		- usually within 4 months
		- recurrent episodes usually milder and shorter

	- renal complications
		- 90% occur within 2 months
		- 97% occur within 6 months
