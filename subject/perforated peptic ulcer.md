# Perforated Peptic Ulcer

- Definition
	- Perforation of a gastric or duodenal ulcer
		- → leakage of gastric or duodenal contents into peritoneal cavity
	- Acute surgical emergency

- Epidemiology
	- Common age group
			- 45–55 years
	- More common in males
	- Duodenal ulcer more common than gastric ulcer

- Risk Factors / History
	- NSAID use
	- May follow heavy meal
	- May occur without previous ulcer history
	- No back pain
			- Important differentiating feature
	- May be painless in patients taking steroids

- Clinical Course

	- Stage 1 – Prostration Phase
		- Sudden severe pain

	- Stage 2 – Reaction Phase
		- Occurs after ~4 hours
		- Temporary symptom improvement
			- Deceptive phase

	- Stage 3 – Peritonitis Phase
		- Occurs after ~4 hours
		- Severe generalized abdominal pain

- Clinical Features
	- Sudden onset severe epigastric pain
	- Initially localized to epigastrium
			- Later becomes generalized
	- Pain usually continuous
			- May transiently lessen

	- Pain Radiation
		- Shoulders
			- Rare
		- Right lower quadrant

	- Associated Symptoms
		- Nausea
		- Vomiting
			- Usually delayed
		- Hiccough
			- Late sign

	- Classic Description
		- Sudden severe abdominal pain
		- Anxious patient
		- Lies still
		- Pale or grey appearance
		- Sweaty
		- Brief transient improvement before worsening

- Signs of Peritonitis
	- Patient lies motionless
			- Movement worsens pain
	- Pale
	- Sweaty
	- Ashen appearance
	- Guarding
	- Board-like rigidity
			- Hallmark sign
	- Maximum tenderness at perforation site
	- No abdominal distension early
	- Retracted abdomen
			- “Shelf” appearance beneath lower chest
	- Reduced or absent bowel sounds
			- Silent abdomen
	- Shifting dullness may be present

- Vital Signs

	- Early
		- Pulse may be normal
		- Blood pressure may be normal
		- Temperature may be normal

	- Later
		- Tachycardia
		- Shock
			- Typically after 3–4 hours
		- Shallow breathing due to pain

- Examination
	- Per rectal examination
			- Pelvic tenderness may be present

- Investigations

	- Chest X-ray
		- Erect film preferred
		- Free air under diaphragm in ~75%

	- Important Technique
		- Patient should remain upright for ~15 minutes before X-ray

	- Contrast Study
		- Limited Gastrografin meal
		- May confirm perforation

	- CT Scan
		- Preferred investigation if available and safe

- Management

	- Initial Management
		- Pain relief
		- IV fluid resuscitation
		- Nasogastric tube
			- Drip and suction
		- Broad-spectrum antibiotics

	- Definitive Management
		- Immediate laparotomy after resuscitation

- Conservative Management

	- Selected Cases Only
		- Late presentation
		- Evidence of sealed perforation on Gastrografin study

- Key Exam Points
	- Sudden severe epigastric pain progressing to generalized pain
	- Temporary deceptive pain relief phase
	- Board-like rigidity is classic hallmark
	- Silent abdomen suggests peritonitis
	- Free air under diaphragm on erect chest X-ray
	- Most patients require urgent surgery
	- Always ask about NSAID use
	- Steroid users may present without pain
