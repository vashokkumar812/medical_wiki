## definition
- **Normal range:** 1.3–2.2 mEq/L  
- **Hypermagnesaemia:** > 2.2 mEq/L  
- **Toxic:** > 4 mEq/L  
- Rare, usually **iatrogenic**  
- Often associated with **↑ K⁺** and **↓ Ca²⁺**

---

## Causes

### 1. Iatrogenic
- Hyperalimentation  
- IV / oral magnesium therapy  
- Laxatives, enemas, antacids (esp. in elderly & renal failure)  

### 2. Renal Failure
- Impaired excretion  

### 3. Other
- Perforated viscus with continued oral intake  
- Tumour lysis syndrome (↑ K⁺, Mg²⁺, PO₄³⁻; ↓ Ca²⁺)  
- Rhabdomyolysis  

---

## Clinical Manifestations

- **Often asymptomatic** until Mg²⁺ > 4 mEq/L  

### Serum Mg²⁺ > 4 mEq/L
- Muscle [[weakness]]  
- Hyporeflexia  
- Nausea, vomiting  
- Hypotension (vasodilation)  

### Serum Mg²⁺ > 10 mEq/L
- [[coma]]  
- Hypoventilation  
- Neuromuscular paralysis  
- Cardiac arrhythmias, [[bradycardia]], death  

---

## Complications
- **Cardiac arrhythmias**  
- **ECG changes (similar to hyperkalaemia):**
  - ↑ PR interval  
  - ↑ QTc  
  - Prolonged QRS  
  - Peaked T waves, flattened P waves  
  - Complete [[AV block]], asystole  

---

## Management

### 1. Stop Magnesium Intake
- Discontinue IV/oral Mg, antacids, laxatives  

### 2. Antagonise Mg²⁺
- **IV Calcium chloride 10% (5–10 mL, repeated if needed)**  
  - Treats life-threatening arrhythmias  
  - Watch for **[[Hypocalcaemia]]** (may worsen symptoms)  

### 3. Remove Magnesium
- **Dialysis** → treatment of choice in severe/renal failure  
- **Forced diuresis** with IV saline + furosemide
