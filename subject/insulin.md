

- indications
	- T1DM
	- T2DM
		- post operative
		- preganancy
		- inefficient OHA

- guide
	- intercurrent illness
		- increased dose
	- pregancy
		- avoid insilin lispo
	- lactation
		- safe

- adverse effects
	- erythema
	- pruritus
	- lipodystrophy
	- lipoatrophy
	- allergic reactions
	- [[hypoglycemia]]
		- warning signs
			- sweating
			- hunger
			- faintness
			- palpitations
			- tremor
			- headache
			- visual disturbance

- decisions
	- switching from bovine to human insulin
		- reduce dose by 10%

- insulin lispro
	- immediately before meals
	- less hypoglycemia risk

- ultra-short acting insulin – site
	- usually abdomen
		- fastest absorption
	- alternative sites
		- thighs
		- upper arms
		- buttocks
	- rotate injection sites

- handling and mixing of insulin
	- gently roll intermediate and long-acting insulin vials/cartridges before use
		- allows resuspension
	- when mixing insulins
		- draw up short-acting (clear) insulin first
			- prevents contamination with long-acting (cloudy) insulin

- storage of insulin
	- store at 2–8°C
		- protect from light
	- do not freeze
	- opened vial
		- store at ≤25°C
		- up to 1 month

- adverse effects and comfort
	- local injection-site reactions
		- resolve spontaneously
	- generalised allergic reactions
		- switching insulin
		- desensitisation
	- warming insulin to room temperature
		- reduces injection discomfort


- treatment

- non pharmacological
	- diet
	- BMI
		- overweight
			- reduce 10%
	- physical activity

		- children and adolescents
			- ≥60 mins/day moderate to severe activity
			- 3 days/week muscle + bone strengthening activity

		- adults
			- 150 mins aerobic activity
			- 2–3 sessions resistance exercise/week
				- total ≥60 mins

	- cigarette
		- 0

	- alcohol consumption
		- no more than 10 standard drinks/week
		- no more than 4 on any one day

	- blood glucose monitoring
		- fasting
			- 4–7 mmol/L
		- postprandial
			- 5–10 mmol/L

	- indications for SMBG (Self-Monitoring of Blood Glucose)
		- T2DM using
			- insulin
			- sulfonylureas
		- non-insulin patients
			- individualise need/frequency
		- pregnancy complicated by diabetes
		- gestational diabetes
		- intercurrent illness with hyperglycaemia
			- sick-day management
		- useful in
			- haemoglobinopathies
			- unreliable HbA1c conditions

- clinical management goals

	- HbA1c
		- target
			- individualised
		- general target
			- ≤7% (53 mmol/mol)

	- lipids
		- Australian CVD risk calculator
		- once therapy started
			- follow Australian guideline targets
		- secondary prevention
			- ≥50% LDL reduction from baseline
			- LDL target <1.4 mmol/L

	- blood pressure
		- lower BP targets may be considered
			- younger patients
			- secondary prevention with high stroke risk
		- diabetes with CKD
			- target <130/80 mmHg
		- general BP target
			- ≤140/90 mmHg

	- urine albumin excretion
		- UACR targets
			- women
				- <3.5 mg/mmol
			- men
				- <2.5 mg/mmol
		- timed overnight collection
			- <20 μg/min
		- spot urine collection
			- <20 mg/L

	- vaccination
		- recommended immunisations
			- influenza
			- pneumococcal
			- dTpa
			- COVID-19
			- RSV
				- age >60 years
		- consider
			- hepatitis B
				- especially if travelling
			- herpes zoster

- type 1 DM

| Feature           | Intensive regimen (Basal–Bolus)                                         | Conventional regimen (Split–Mixed)                          |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Basic principle   | Bolus insulin before meals + basal insulin for background control       | Fixed mixture of short and intermediate/long acting insulin |
| Insulin used      | Short/ultra-short acting (bolus) + intermediate or long acting (basal)  | ~30% short acting + ~70% intermediate/long acting           |
| Timing            | Bolus before each meal; basal once or twice daily (bedtime ± breakfast) | Once or twice daily                                         |
| Dose distribution | Individualised according to meals                                       | If split: 2/3 before breakfast, 1/3 before evening meal     |
| Flexibility       | High                                                                    | Low                                                         |
| Glycaemic control | Near-physiological                                                      | Moderate                                                    |
| Best suited for   | Patients needing tight control and flexible meal timings                | Patients with regular meal patterns                         |
| Memory aid        | BBB – Bolus Before meals + Basal at Bedtime                             | 30–70 → 2/3 AM, 1/3 PM                                      |
