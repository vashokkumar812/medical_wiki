- Definition
	- Benign vascular tumour of infancy
	- Due to proliferation of endothelial cells
	- Appears during the first few weeks of life
	- Not fully developed at birth

- Epidemiology
	- Female predominance
	- Prematurity
	- Low birth weight

- Clinical Features
	- Natural history
		- Appears within first few weeks of life
		- Proliferative phase
			- Rapid growth during first 6 months
		- Plateau phase
			- Growth usually stops by 12 months
		- Involution phase
			- Begins after first year
			- Most resolve by school age
			- Residual changes may include
				- Loose skin
				- Telangiectasia
				- Fibrofatty tissue
				- Scarring

	- Types
		- Superficial
			- Bright red
			- Raised
			- "Strawberry" haemangioma
		- Deep
			- Bluish
			- Soft
			- Compressible swelling
		- Mixed
			- Superficial and deep components

- Common Sites
	- Head
	- Neck
	- Face
	- Trunk
	- Extremities

- Most Infantile Haemangiomas
	- Observation only
	- No treatment required

- High-Risk Haemangiomas (Require Investigation and/or Specialist Referral)

	- Large head or neck
		- Investigate for

			- PHACE syndrome

	- Eyelid / Periorbital
		- Urgent ophthalmology referral
		- Risk
			- Amblyopia
			- Astigmatism
			- Permanent visual loss

	- Nose
		- Cosmetic deformity

	- Lip
		- Feeding difficulty
		- Scarring

	- Face
		- Permanent cosmetic deformity
	- Beard distribution + airway symptoms
		- Suspect subglottic haemangioma

	- Large lumbosacral
		- Investigate for
			- Spinal dysraphism
			- Tethered cord
			- Genitourinary anomalies
		- First-line investigation
			- MRI spine

	- Perineal / Napkin area
		- High risk of
			- Ulceration
			- Pain
			- Infection

	- Female nipple
		- Permanent breast deformity

	- ≥5 cutaneous haemangiomas
		- Liver ultrasound
		- Assess for
			- Hepatic haemangiomas
			- High-output cardiac failure
			- Hypothyroidism

- Indications for Treatment
	- Airway obstruction
	- Visual obstruction
	- Feeding difficulty
	- Hearing obstruction
	- Rapid growth causing deformity
	- Ulceration
	- Bleeding
	- Pain
	- Functional impairment
	- High-output cardiac failure
	- Significant cosmetic risk

- Treatment

	- First-line
		- Oral propranolol
			- First-line for problematic infantile haemangioma
			- Give with feeds
			- Duration
				- Usually until ~12 months
				- May continue up to 24 months

	- Alternative
		- Atenolol
			- If propranolol unsuitable
			- Less bronchospasm and respiratory adverse effects

	- Small superficial lesions
		- Topical timolol 0.5% gel
		- Best for
			- Small
			- Flat
			- Superficial lesions
		- Avoid on
			- Ulcerated lesions

	- Laser therapy
		- Indications
			- Early superficial lesions
			- Residual telangiectasia
			- Cosmetic improvement
		- Not first-line for
			- Large proliferating haemangiomas

	- Surgery
		- Rare during infancy
		- Indications
			- Residual deformity
			- Persistent fibrofatty tissue
			- Failure of medical therapy
			- Selected functional impairment

- Oral Propranolol

	- Before starting
		- Assess
			- Cardiac history
			- Respiratory history
			- Feeding
			- Baseline heart rate
			- Blood pressure

	- During treatment
		- Always give with feeds
		- Temporarily stop if
			- Vomiting
			- Poor feeding
			- Fasting
			- Intercurrent illness
		- Restart
			- Once feeding normally

	- Common adverse effects
		- Cold hands
		- Cold feet
		- Sleep disturbance
		- Mild diarrhoea

	- Serious adverse effects
		- Hypoglycaemia
		- Bradycardia
		- Hypotension
		- Bronchospasm


	- Contraindications
		- Asthma
		- Significant bronchospasm
		- Bradycardia
		- Heart block
		- Severe cardiac disease
		- Hypotension

- Complications

	- Ulceration
		- Management
			- Wound care
			- Dressings
			- Pain control
			- Topical antibiotic if infected
			- Continue specialist review

	- Bleeding
		- First aid
			- Firm pressure for 10 minutes
		- Persistent bleeding
			- Emergency Department assessment


- Differential Diagnosis

	- Congenital haemangioma
		- Fully formed at birth
		- Different natural history

	- Vascular malformation
		- Present at birth
		- Grows with the child
		- Does not involute

	- Port-wine stain
		- Capillary malformation
