# Postrenal Acute Kidney Injury – Clinical Presentation

- **Bladder distension** in prostatism or neurologic disorders  
- **Urine output**: diminished or absent, preceded by incomplete voiding (prostate/bladder disease)  
- **Pain**:  
  - Over bladder → prostatism  
  - Flank pain → stones

# Postrenal Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

## Definition
- AKI due to **obstruction anywhere from the renal pelvis to the urethra**.  

## Key Point
- Must be **bilateral** to cause AKI.  
  - Unilateral obstruction is compensated by the other kidney.  
  - Explains why **kidney donors don’t develop ↑ [[creatinine]]**.  

---

## Common Causes

### 1. Renal Pelvis
- Bilateral renal calculi  

### 2. Ureters
- Bilateral stones  
- Bilateral ureteral disease (e.g., retroperitoneal fibrosis, strictures)  

### 3. Bladder
- Stones  
- Clots  
- [[urology and nephrology cancer]] obstructing both ureteral orifices  

### 4. Prostate
- Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)  
- Prostate cancer  

### 5. Neurologic Causes (Neurogenic Bladder)
- Multiple sclerosis  
- Spinal cord lesions  
- Peripheral neuropathy  
- **Clues**: prior obstructive symptoms → sudden oliguria/anuria  

## Diagnosis

- **[[creatinine]]** rises unless disease is unilateral  
- **[[BUN]]:[[creatinine]] ratio**:  
  - Initially 20:1 (like prerenal AKI)  
  - Later decreases to 10:1  
- **Urinalysis**:  
  - Variable — normal (neurogenic bladder)  
  - Hematuria (stones, [[urology and nephrology cancer]], clots)  
- **Imaging**:  
  - Bilateral hydronephrosis on renal ultrasound or non-contrast CT  
  - Should be done early in all AKI  

- **Post-void residual urine**:  
  - Normally ≤ 50 mL after urination  
  - Large residual volume → obstruction  

---

## Treatment

- **Bladder/prostate obstruction** → Foley catheter insertion  
- **Ureteral/pelvic obstruction** → Nephrostomy tube (percutaneous or transurethral)  
- **Goal**: Prompt relief of obstruction to prevent permanent damage
