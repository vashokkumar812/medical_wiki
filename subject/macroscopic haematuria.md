- Investigations for haematuria
	- Initial assessment
		- Urine tests
		- Blood tests
		- Imaging

- Urine dipstick
	- Rapid screening for haemoglobin
	- False positives
		- Myoglobin
		- Bacteria
		- Povidone
		- Hypochlorite
	- Sensitivity
		- 91–100%
	- Specificity
		- 65–99%
	- False negatives rare
	- Positive dipstick
		- Send urine for microscopy

- Urine MC&S
	- Essential investigation
	- Excludes UTI
	- Send promptly
			- Avoid RBC lysis
	- RBC morphology
			- Differentiates glomerular vs urinary source
	- Helps avoid unnecessary imaging
	- Albumin:creatinine ratio
			- Assesses renal vs post-renal cause

- Urine cytology
	- Detects urothelial cancer
	- Best for
			- High-grade tumours
			- Carcinoma in situ
	- Poor sensitivity for low-grade tumours
	- Normal result does not exclude cancer
	- Atypical cytology
			- Requires cystoscopy
	- Adjunct to cystoscopy
	- Improved sensitivity with
			- 3 mid-morning samples
			- 3 separate voids

- Blood tests
	- Full blood count
			- Infection
			- Blood loss
	- Renal function
			- Creatinine
			- eGFR
	- PSA in men
			- Prostate cancer
			- May also rise in
					- UTI
					- Urinary retention
					- BPH

- Imaging
	- XR-KUB
		- Detects radio-opaque stones
		- Sensitivity ~60%
		- Limited role

	- IVP
		- Contrast with serial X-rays
		- Sensitivity ~50–60%
		- Largely replaced by CT

	- Ultrasound
		- Assesses
				- Renal masses
				- Renal cysts
				- Hydronephrosis
				- Large bladder lesions
		- No radiation
		- No contrast
		- Operator dependent
		- Sensitivity for renal tumours ~40%
		- Limited for ureters/bladder
		- Useful in
				- Pregnancy
				- Children
				- Low-risk patients
		- Often followed by CT

	- CT-KUB
		- Non-contrast CT
		- Gold standard for stones
		- Sensitivity 94–98%
		- Poor for malignancy

	- CT-IVP / CT urogram
		- Preferred test for malignancy
		- Visualises
				- Kidneys
				- Ureters
				- Collecting system
		- Sensitivity 94–100%
		- Specificity ~97%

- Cystoscopy
	- Gold standard for bladder evaluation
	- Visualises
			- Urethra
			- Bladder
	- Flexible cystoscopy
			- Local anaesthetic
	- Rigid cystoscopy
			- General anaesthetic
			- Used for
					- Biopsy
					- Tumour resection
	- Recommended if
			- Age >40 years
			- Malignancy risk factors

- Initial management
	- Most cases settle conservatively
	- Admit if
			- Clot retention
			- Haemodynamic instability
			- Falling haemoglobin
	- Acute clot retention
			- Suprapubic pain
			- Inability to void
	- Management
			- Catheterisation
			- Continuous bladder irrigation
			- Blood transfusion if required
	- Refractory cases
			- Emergency cystoscopy
			- Diathermy

- Specific causes and management
	- Urinary tract infection
		- Empirical antibiotics
		- Adjust according to sensitivities
		- Repeat urine MC&S after treatment

	- Renal/ureteric stones
		- Initial conservative management
		- Stones <5 mm
				- ~98% pass spontaneously
		- Pain relief
				- NSAIDs
				- Opiates if severe
		- Alpha blockers
				- Prazosin
				- Tamsulosin
		- Refer if
				- Sepsis
				- Renal impairment
				- Large stones
				- Recurrent stones

	- Renal/ureteric/bladder tumours
		- Refer to urology if
				- Lesion on CT-IVP
				- Atypical cytology
		- Early disease
				- Transurethral tumour removal

	- Prostatomegaly
		- Alpha blockers
				- First line
		- 5-alpha reductase inhibitors
				- Second line
				- Reduce retention risk
				- Reduce surgery risk
		- Combination therapy
				- Most effective
		- Also reduces prostate-related haematuria
		- Start only on urologist advice

	- Prostate cancer
		- Less common cause of haematuria
		- Investigations
				- PSA
				- Digital rectal examination
		- Abnormal findings
				- Refer for TRUS biopsy

- Paediatric haematuria
	- Key points
		- Most children have isolated asymptomatic microscopic haematuria
		- Usually no urgent tests initially
		- Follow-up repeat urinalysis required
		- Pain/oedema/hypertension/proteinuria
				- Requires investigation

- Background
	- Microscopic haematuria
		- >10 RBC/microlitre
		- Detected only on urinalysis
		- Urine colour normal

	- Persistent microscopic haematuria
		- 3 positive samples
		- ≥1 week apart
		- Not after exercise
		- Not during menstruation

	- Isolated asymptomatic microscopic haematuria
		- No symptoms
		- No proteinuria
		- No other urine abnormalities

	- Macroscopic haematuria
		- Visible blood in urine
		- More commonly bladder/urethral source

	- Red/brown urine not always haematuria
		- Haemoglobin
		- Myoglobin
		- Drugs
		- Foods
		- Requires microscopy

	- Newborns
		- Urate crystals common
		- “Brick dust” nappies
		- Not blood

	- Blood may be non-urinary
		- Vaginal bleeding
		- Rectal fissure

	- Common causes
		- Isolated microscopic haematuria
		- Viral infection
		- UTI
		- Trauma
		- HSP
		- Hypercalciuria
		- Inherited GBM disorders

- Assessment
	- History
		- Previous haematuria
		- UTI symptoms
				- Dysuria
				- Frequency
				- Pain
				- Fever
		- Systemic symptoms
				- Fatigue
				- Oedema
				- Rash
				- Arthralgia
				- Coryza
		- Recent surgery/trauma
				- Consider non-accidental injury
		- Family history
				- Haematuria
				- Renal disease
				- Stones
				- Hearing loss
		- Bleeding disorders
		- Immunodeficiency
		- Medications
				- NSAIDs
				- Rifampicin
				- Metronidazole
				- Nitrofurantoin
				- Cyclophosphamide
		- Foods
				- Beetroot
				- Berries
		- Exercise history

- Examination and investigations
	- Urinalysis
	- Weight
	- Blood pressure
	- Physical examination
			- Eyes
			- Skin
			- Genitalia
			- Joints
			- Oedema
			- Organomegaly

- Red flags
	- Haematuria with
			- Proteinuria
			- Hypertension
			- Oedema
			- Ascites
			- Flank pain
			- Abdominal pain
			- Immunocompromise

- Further investigations

- Microscopic haematuria
	- Isolated asymptomatic microscopic haematuria
		- No other abnormalities present
		- Refer to GP for follow-up
		- Repeat urinalysis
			- 2 additional tests
			- ≥1 week apart
			- Not during menstruation
			- Avoid exercise on testing day
		- No interim investigations required
		- Often resolves spontaneously

	- Isolated persistent microscopic haematuria
		- Urine microscopy
		- Urine protein:creatinine ratio
		- Urine calcium:creatinine ratio
		- Urinalysis of immediate family members

- Macroscopic haematuria
	- Urine microscopy and culture
	- Urine protein:creatinine ratio
	- Urine calcium:creatinine ratio
	- Blood tests
		- FBE
		- UEC
		- [[liver]]
		- Coagulation profile
		- CMP
	- If painless
		- Perform glomerulonephritis screen
	- Renal ultrasound

- When to consult paediatrics
	- Child systemically unwell
	- Haematuria with any red flag
	- Persistent microscopic haematuria after 3 tests
	- Features of nephritis
		- Consider nephrology referral
	- Suspected haemolytic uraemic syndrome
		- Consider nephrology referral
	- Abdominal mass
		- Consider surgical/urology referral

- When to transfer
	- Required care exceeds local hospital capability
