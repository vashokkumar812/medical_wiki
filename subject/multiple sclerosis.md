- definition
	- Multifocal CNS demyelinating disorder

- pathology
	- Autoimmune
		- T-cell mediated
	- Affects:
		- White matter
		- Grey matter
	- Key pathology
		- Inflammatory demyelination ==> Early irreversible axonal injury ==> Progressive CNS damage =>  irreversible disability

- Aetiology
	- Multifactorial
		- Genetic + environmental interaction
	- Environmental risk factors
		- Residence in higher latitude regions during childhood
		- Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)
			- Later exposure ↑ MS risk
			- EBV seronegative → very low risk
		- Smoking
	- Genetics
		- Strongest association:
			- HLA-DRB1

- Epidemiology (Australia)
	- More common in southern Australia
		- Highest prevalence in Tasmania
	- Female predominance
		- ~73%
	- Mean onset age
		- Mid-30s

- clinical features
	- Better prognosis
		- Optic neuritis or isolated sensory symptoms as CIS
		- Initial relapsing-remitting course
		- Long interval to second relapse
		- No disability after 5 years
		- Normal initial MRI
		- Female gender
		- Younger age at onset
		- Complete recovery from first neurological episode
	
	- Poor prognosis
		- Efferent systems affected in CIS
		- Multifocal CIS
		- High relapse rate in first 2–5 years
		- Substantial disability after 5 years
		- Abnormal initial MRI with large lesion loads4

- Disease phases- 
	- Clinically isolated syndrome (CIS)
		- First demyelinating event
		- Initial presentation in ~85%
		- Symptoms evolve over:
			- Days to weeks
		- Common presentations
			- Optic neuritis
			- Brainstem syndrome
			- multifocal motor and sensory deficits

	- Relapsing remitting MS (RRMS)
		- Most common initial disease course
		- Characterised by:
			- Relapses
			- Remissions
		- Relapse definition
			- New neurological symptoms/signs
			- Last >24 hours
			- Due to CNS demyelination
		- Residual deficits persist in up to 40%
		- Mean relapse rate
			- ~0.65/year untreated
		- Pseudorelapse
			- NOT true relapse
			- Due to:
				- Fatigue
				- Fever
				- Heat/infection worsening old symptoms

	- Secondary progressive MS (SPMS)
		- Progressive disability after RRMS
		- Gradual worsening without relapses

	- Primary progressive MS (PPMS)
		- 15–20%
		- Progressive from onset
		- No relapses/remissions
		- Common presentation
			- Slowly progressive spastic paraparesis
		- Poor response to current treatment
		-  PPMS diagnosis
			- Requires:
				- ≥1 year disease progression
				- MRI evidence of dissemination in space
				- Positive CSF oligoclonal bands

- Diagnosis
	- Clinical diagnosis supported by investigations

- Investigations
	- MRI brain/spine
		- Most important investigation
		- Typical lesions:
			- Periventricular white matter (if at right angles to the corpus callosum, these are referred to as ‘Dawson fingers’)
			- Juxtacortical white matter
			- Corpus callosum
			- Optic nerve (with gadolinium enhancement in acute neuritis)
			- Infratentorial structures (pons, cerebellar peduncles and cerebellum)
			- Spinal cord
	- CSF
		- Oligoclonal bands
		- Raised IgG index
	- Evoked potentials
		- Delayed conduction with preserved waveform

- Multiple sclerosis differential diagnoses

	- Neuromyelitis optica (Devic disease)
		- Relapsing CNS demyelinating disease
		- Optic nerve involvement
		- Severe myelopathy
		- MRI:
			- Longitudinally extensive spinal cord lesions
		- Serum aquaporin-4 autoantibodies
		- Different clinical course and prognosis from MS
		- Poor response to MS disease-modifying therapies

	- Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis (ADEM)
		- Monophasic autoimmune demyelinating illness
		- Usually paediatric
		- Also occurs in adults
		- Can resemble acute MS attack
		- More explosive course
		- Altered mental status
		- Possible triggers
			- Recent viral infection
			- Vaccination

	- Other differential diagnoses
		- Migraine
		- Cerebral neoplasms
			- Primary
			- Secondary
		- Nutritional deficiencies
			- Vitamin B12 deficiency
			- Copper deficiency
		- Compressive spinal cord lesions
		- Infections
			- Syphilis
			- HIV
		- Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis
		- Steroid-sensitive relapsing disorders
			- Systemic lupus erythematosus
			- Neurosarcoidosis
		- Recurrent infarcts
		- Paraneoplastic syndromes
		- Psychiatric disease/functional symptoms

- Acute relapse treatment
	- IV methylprednisolone
		- Treatment of choice
		- Use if relapse significantly affects quality of life
	- Effects
		- Hastens recovery
		- Does NOT:
			- Improve ultimate recovery
			- Reduce permanent disability
			- Reduce future relapse frequency
	- Adverse effects
		- Mood changes
		- Psychosis
		- Aseptic hip necrosis
		- Hyperglycaemia
	- Steroid-unresponsive severe relapse
		- Plasma exchange
- Pseudorelapse
	- Often triggered by:
		- Sepsis
		- Fever
	- Treat underlying infection
	- Avoid unnecessary steroids

- Disease modifying treatments (DMTs)
	- Approved for
		- Clinically isolated syndrome
		- RRMS
	- PBS funded mainly for RRMS
	- Goal
		- Reduce relapses
		- Reduce new MRI lesions

	- Suboptimal response to DMT
		- Persistent relapse rate
		- Ongoing MRI activity after ≥6 months therapy
		- Exclude secondary causes
			- Noncompliance
			- Neutralising antibodies to interferon-β
	
	- First-line DMTs
		- Interferon-β
		- Glatiramer acetate
		- Effects
			- ~30% relapse reduction
			- Fewer active MRI lesions
			- Delay CIS → clinically definite MS

	- Second-line DMTs
	
		- Natalizumab
			- Reduces:
				- Disability progression
				- Relapse rate
				- New MRI lesions
			- Major adverse effect
				- Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)
					- JC virus infection
			- Higher PML risk
				- Longer treatment duration
				- Prior immunosuppressant use
				- JC virus antibodies
			- Used for
				- Aggressive disease
				- Poor response to first-line therapy

	- Fingolimod
		- First oral RRMS therapy
		- Mechanism
			- Sphingosine 1-phosphate receptor modulator
			- Causes reversible lymphopenia
		- Effects
			- ~50% lower relapse rate vs placebo
		- Adverse effects
			- First-dose bradycardia
			- Macular oedema
			- LFT abnormalities
			- Increased infection risk
		- Avoid if no prior:
			- Varicella infection
			- Varicella immunisation

	- Third-line/salvage therapy
		- Cyclophosphamide
		- Mitoxantrone
		- High-dose chemotherapy + autologous stem cell transplant
		- Limited by:
			- Poor tolerability
			- Serious adverse effects

	- Symptomatic treatment
	
		- Common symptoms
			- Spasticity
			- Paraesthesias
			- Tremor
			- Erectile dysfunction
			- Depression/anxiety
			- Fatigue
			- Pain
	
		- Spasticity
			- Physiotherapy
			- Muscle stretching
	
		- Cerebellar tremor
			- Wrist weights may help
	
		- Bladder dysfunction
			- Small spastic bladder
				- Anticholinergics
			- Atonic bladder
				- Intermittent catheterisation
			- Pre/post-void bladder scan useful
	
		- Fatigue
			- Amantadine 100 mg morning + midday
			- Exclude depression
	
		- Pain management
			- Amitriptyline
			- Carbamazepine
			- Gabapentin
			- Pregabalin
	
		- Depression/anxiety
			- Counselling
			- Antidepressants
	
	- Pregnancy and MS
		- Pregnancy decreases disease activity
			- Especially third trimester
		- Increased relapse risk
			- First 3 months postpartum
		- Most DMTs
			- Pregnancy category D
		- Exceptions
			- Glatiramer acetate → B1
			- Natalizumab → C
