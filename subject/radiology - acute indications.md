- General principles
    - Radiologist consultation
        - Uncertain imaging modality
        - Urgent imaging requests
    - Non-urgent discussions
        - Business hours only
            - 8:30–17:00 Mon–Fri

    - Imaging request must include
        - Relevant history
        - Clinical suspicion

    - Patient preparation
        - Adequate assessment before imaging
        - Nurse present during imaging
        - Medical escort if unstable

- Upper airway imaging

    - Retropharyngeal abscess
        - Widened prevertebral shadow
        - Normal width
            - ≈ vertebral body width at C4
        - False widening may occur with neck flexion

    - Inhaled foreign body
        - Imaging if
            - Upper airway suspicion
            - Possible sharp foreign body
                - Risk soft tissue perforation

    - Acute epiglottitis
        - Clinical diagnosis only
        - Not radiological diagnosis

- Chest X-ray (CXR)

    - General
        - Lateral CXR rarely indicated
        - Pre-operative CXR not routine

    - Respiratory indications
        - Suspected pneumonia
        - Foreign body aspiration
        - Inspiratory/expiratory films
            - Assess air trapping
        - Trauma
            - Pneumothorax
            - Haemothorax
            - Mediastinal injury
        - Pneumothorax
            - Inspiratory film sufficient
        - Asthma/bronchiolitis
            - Only if
                - Diagnosis unclear
                - Severe disease
                - Poor response
                - Suspected air leak

        - Focal signs ± fever
            - Often mucus plugging/viral illness

    - Cardiac indications
        - Cardiomegaly
        - Heart failure

    - Normal cardiothoracic ratio
        - Children
            - ≤0.5
        - Infants
            - ≤0.6

    - Large thymus (<2 years)
        - Normal finding

    - Innocent murmur
        - No urgent CXR needed

    - Hypertension
        - CXR rarely useful

    - Neonates (<6 weeks)
        - Septic screen
            - CXR unless another focus identified
        - Respiratory distress
            - Exclude cardiac causes

- Limb X-rays and musculoskeletal imaging

    - Comparative/stress views
        - Rarely indicated
    - Complex fractures
        - Discuss with senior/radiology

- Trauma imaging

    - X-ray requirements
        - Include fracture site
        - Include joint above and below

    - Avoid whole limb imaging without discussion

    - Post-reduction
        - Repeat X-ray required

    - Normal X-ray but high suspicion
        - Discuss consultant
        - Treat as fracture if uncertain

    - CT/bone scan indications
        - Stress fractures
        - Toddler fractures

- Non-accidental injury (NAI)

    - Must be reviewed by
        - Registrar
        - Consultant

    - Imaging
        - >2 years
            - Targeted X-rays
        - <3 years
            - Skeletal survey
        - ± Bone scan

    - Red flag fractures
        - Metaphyseal fractures
        - Epiphyseal separation
        - Rib fractures
        - Spine fractures
        - Unexplained skull fracture
        - Intracranial injury

- Joint and bone conditions

    - Painful hip
        - AP pelvis X-ray
        - Frog-leg lateral view
        - May require
            - Ultrasound
            - Bone scan

    - Osteomyelitis
        - Early X-ray often normal
        - MRI/bone scan useful early

    - Septic arthritis
        - Normal imaging does not exclude diagnosis
        - Ultrasound useful for effusion

- Metabolic disorders

    - Rickets
        - Wrist ± knee X-ray

    - Osteogenesis imperfecta
        - Low threshold for imaging

- Pulled elbow
    - No X-ray if classic presentation

- Abdominal X-rays (AXR)

    - Indications
        - Bowel obstruction
            - Dilated loops
        - Perforation
            - Erect AXR
        - Intussusception
            - Exclude complications

    - Foreign body
        - Single AP film
            - Mouth to anus
        - No routine follow-up if asymptomatic

    - Abdominal mass
        - Initial imaging
            - AXR
            - Ultrasound

    - Trauma
        - CT best modality

    - Not indicated for
        - Vague abdominal pain
        - Gastroenteritis
        - Haematemesis
        - Pyloric stenosis
        - Uncomplicated appendicitis
        - Chronic constipation in ED

- Abdominal and pelvic ultrasound

    - Urgent imaging
        - Discuss consultant/surgeon

    - Indications
        - Intussusception
            - Diagnostic modality of choice
        - Pyloric stenosis
            - Highly sensitive
        - Female pelvic pain
            - Full bladder required
        - Renal obstruction
            - Early ultrasound

    - UTI
        - First UTI in <4 years
            - Renal ultrasound
        - MCU occasionally indicated

- Intracranial and skull imaging

    - Skull X-ray indications (well child only)
        - NAI skeletal survey
        - Plagiocephaly
        - Craniosynostosis

- CT brain

    - General
        - Must discuss with consultant
        - Neurosurgery involvement if unstable

    - Indications

        - Head trauma
            - Suspected intracranial injury
            - Clinical deterioration
                - Repeat CT

        - Reduced consciousness
            - After stabilisation

        - Headache red flags
            - Neurological deficit
            - Visual reduction
            - Seizures
            - Behavioural change
            - Enlarging head circumference
            - Raised ICP
            - Increasing headache frequency

        - Seizures
            - Persistent neurological deficit
            - Focal signs
            - Abnormal EEG
            - Refractory seizures
            - Neurocutaneous syndromes

- Skull size abnormalities

    - Macrocephaly
        - Rapid growth
            - Ultrasound/CT

    - Microcephaly
        - Usually pathological
        - CT/MRI outpatient assessment

    - Cranial ultrasound
        - Open fontanelle
        - Neonates/infants
        - Discuss radiologist

- Spinal imaging

    - Advanced imaging
        - Consultant decision

    - Down syndrome
        - Increased C1–C2 instability risk

- Spinal trauma

    - Cervical spine
        - Normal imaging does not clear unconscious patient

    - Thoracolumbar spine
        - May require full spine imaging
        - Neurological deficit
            - CT/MRI
            - Neurosurgical review

- Non-traumatic spinal conditions

    - Scoliosis
        - Entire spine X-ray

    - Cord compression
        - Urgent discussion
        - Neurosurgical involvement

    - Vertebral pathology
        - Imaging modality based on consultant decision
