## Physiology
- **Forms in plasma:**
  - Ionized Ca²⁺ (45–50%) → active form
  - Protein-bound Ca²⁺ (mainly albumin, ~40%)
  - Complexed Ca²⁺ (phosphate, citrate, ~10%)  
- **Normal ranges:**
  - Total Ca²⁺: 2.2–2.5 mmol/L  
  - Ionized Ca²⁺: 1.1–1.3 mmol/L  
- **pH effect:** Alkalosis ↓ ionized Ca²⁺ (↑ protein binding)  
- **Regulators:**
  - **Vitamin D** → ↑ gut absorption, ↑ renal reabsorption, mobilises bone Ca²⁺  
  - **PTH** → ↑ bone resorption, ↑ renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption, ↓ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption, ↑ calcitriol  
  - **Calcitonin** → antagonises PTH, ↑ renal Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ excretion  

---

## Causes of Hypocalcaemia

### 1. Reduced Intake / Absorption
- Low dietary Ca²⁺ or vitamin D  
- Drugs: phenytoin (↑ vitamin D metabolism), PPI, bisphosphonates, SSRIs  

### 2. Redistribution
- Alkalosis (respiratory or metabolic)  
- Citrate toxicity (massive transfusion, RRT)  
- Hyperphosphataemia  
- Pancreatitis, tumour lysis, rhabdomyolysis  
- Hypoparathyroidism  

### 3. Increased Loss
- **Renal/urinary:** loop diuretics, cisplatin, ethylene glycol, protamine  
- **Non-renal:** bleeding, plasmapheresis, citrate removal therapy  

---

## Hypocalcaemia + Acid–Base Disorders
- **Metabolic alkalosis:** citrate toxicity (transfusion, dialysis anticoagulation)  
- **Metabolic acidosis:** renal failure, tumour lysis, rhabdomyolysis, pancreatitis, ethylene glycol poisoning, hydrofluoric acid, sepsis, burns  

---

## Clinical Features
- **Neuromuscular:** perioral numbness, paresthesias, cramps, tetany, laryngospasm, seizures  
- **Signs:**  
  - **Chvostek sign:** tapping facial nerve → spasm  
  - **Trousseau sign:** BP cuff inflation → carpal spasm  
- **Cardiac:** hypotension, arrhythmias (long QT, VT risk), [[heart failure]]  
- **Neuropsychiatric:** irritability, confusion, collapse  

---

## Investigations
- Serum **Ca²⁺** (total & ionized), albumin  
- **Mg²⁺**, **PO₄³⁻**, **PTH**, vitamin D  
- **U&E:** renal function  
- **Lipase:** pancreatitis  
- **CK, urate:** rhabdomyolysis  
- **ECG:** prolonged QT/ST, risk of VT  

---

## Management
- **Treat underlying cause**  
- **Mild / Asymptomatic:** oral Ca²⁺, vitamin D, Mg²⁺ replacement  
- **Severe / Symptomatic (IV calcium indicated):**
  - Symptomatic hypocalcaemia  
  - Ionized Ca²⁺ < 0.8 mmol/L  
  - With **hyperkalaemia**, **hypermagnesaemia**, **Ca²⁺ channel blocker OD**, **massive transfusion**, **high inotrope requirement**  

### IV Calcium Dosing
- **10 mL calcium gluconate** = 2.3 mmol (93 mg elemental Ca²⁺)  
- **10 mL calcium chloride** = 6.8 mmol (272 mg elemental Ca²⁺)
