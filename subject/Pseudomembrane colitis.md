## info
### causes
- antibiotic exposure ==> overgrowth of _C. difficile_  ==> increased toxin production ==> mucosal damage -> inflammation and necrosis
- rarely other organisms
	- staph aureus
	- Salmonella
	- Clostridium perfringens
	- Yersinia
	- Shigella
	- Campylobacter,
	- CMV
	- Entamoeba
	- Listeria

### Risk Factors
  - **Antibiotic exposure – any antimicrobial can incite disease**
    - Likely: beta-lactams, clindamycin
  - **Other**
    - Age >65y
    - Previous GI surgery
    - Renal impairment
    - Prolonged hospitalisation
    - Gastric acid suppression
    - Renal failure
    - Immunocompromised and chemotherapy patients



### Complications
- related to diarrhoea
	  - Hypovolaemia
	  - Electrolyte disturbance
	    - Hypokalaemia
	    - Hypomagnesaemia
- related to intestinal infection
	  - Sepsis and septic shock
	  - Perforation
	  - Toxic megacolon
	  - Bleeding

### **Prevention**
- Avoid excessive antibiotic use → stewardship
- Isolation
- Hand washing with soap and water

## diagnosis
### Clinical Features
  - Diarrhoea 3–9 days post initiation of antibiotics
  - May start weeks after discontinuation of antibiotics
  - Loose stools → toxic megacolon and colonic perforation
  - Profuse, watery/mucoid, foul-smelling stool
  - May contain blood and pseudomembranes
  - Fever
  - Rarely: oligoarthritis and iridocyclitis

### Investigations
  - **General**
    - Leucocytosis
    - Low albumin
    - AXR: mucosal oedema, thumb-printing
    - CT: distension, diffuse and focal thickening of colonic wall
  - **Specific**
    - Faecal leucocytosis
    - Stool cultures
    - Stool assay for C. difficile toxins (takes 2 days)
    - ELISA for toxin A and B (takes 2 hours)
    - PCR (false positives are problematic)
    - Mucosal assessment: proctosigmoidoscopy, flexible sigmoidoscopy, colonoscopy

## Markers of Severity
  - **Clinical**
    - Fever T >38
    - Peritonism
    - Ileus
    - Hemodynamic instability
  - **Investigations**
    - WBC >15 and <20% neutrophils
    - Elevated lactate
    - 50% increase in baseline creatinine
    - Albumin <25
    - Megacolon on imaging

## Management
  - if hypovolaemia
	  - fluid resuscitation
- if septic shock
	- inotropes
  - **Correct metabolic disturbance**
    - Metabolic acidosis from diarrhoea + lactate
    - Hyponatraemia
    - Hypochloraemia

### antibiotics

| Disease Severity                      | Treatment Protocol                                                                                                                   |
| :------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mild to Moderate                      | Metronidazole 400 mg (child: 10 mg/kg up to 400 mg) orally/NG tube, 8-hourly x 10 days. IV metronidazole if oral not tolerated.      |
| Severe                                | Vancomycin 125 mg (child: 3 mg/kg up to 125 mg) orally/NG tube, 6-hourly x 10 days. IV vancomycin is not effective for C. difficile. |
| Complicated (Shock, Ileus, Megacolon) | Vancomycin 125 mg orally/NG tube 6-hourly plus metronidazole 500 mg (child: 12.5 mg/kg up to 500 mg) IV, 8-hourly x 10 days.         |
| Complicated with Ileus                | Above protocol plus Vancomycin retention enema (500 mg in 100 mL 0.9% NaCl) rectally, 6-hourly.                                      |
    -  if severe
	    - Early surgical referral for colectomy
    - if Relapsing disease
	    - multiple antibiotic strategies 
    - if resistant cases
	    - Stool transplant (faecal bacteriotherapy)
	- if Toxic megacolon
		- surgery
