- Breath-Holding Spells

	- Definition
		- Reflex, involuntary episodes of breath holding
		- Usually triggered by crying, pain, fear or frustration
		- Benign and self-limiting

	- Epidemiology
		- Age: 6 months–6 years
		- First episode usually <18 months
		- Resolve spontaneously by 6 years
		- Frequency
			- Usually 1–6 episodes/week
			- Up to 25% have multiple episodes/day

	- Types

		- Cyanotic (Blue) Breath-Holding Spell
			- Most common
			- Trigger
				- Anger
				- Frustration
				- Crying
				- Minor injury
			- Sequence
				- Cry/scream
				- Forced expiration
				- Breath holding
				- Cyanosis (especially lips)
				- May become floppy and briefly lose consciousness

		- Pallid Breath-Holding Spell
			- Less common
			- Trigger
				- Sudden fright
				- Minor injury
			- Mechanism
				- Vagal-mediated bradycardia
			- Features
				- Silent cry (mouth open, no sound)
				- Pallor
				- Brief loss of consciousness
				- May have transient stiffening or urinary/faecal incontinence
				- Can mimic seizure

	- After the Episode
		- Rapid spontaneous recovery
		- May be briefly drowsy or sleep
		- Rarely
			- Muscle twitching
			- Brief anoxic seizure after prolonged episode

	- Management
		- Reassure parents
		- Place child safely in lateral position if unconscious
		- Do not shake child
		- Do not splash water or blow air on face
		- No treatment usually required

	- When to Investigate / Refer
		- First episode with diagnostic uncertainty
		- Episodes without an obvious trigger
		- Prolonged loss of consciousness
		- Frequent or atypical episodes
		- Features suggestive of epilepsy or cardiac disease
		- Significant head injury during event

	- Prognosis
		- Excellent
		- No brain injury or long-term neurological sequelae
		- Most children outgrow episodes by 6 years
