- Major depressive disorder (Major depression)

	- Definition
		- Mood disorder
		- Symptoms persist for ≥2 weeks
		- Represents a change from the patient's previous level of functioning
		- Requires at least one core symptom
			- Depressed mood
			- Anhedonia (loss of interest or pleasure)

	- Epidemiology
		- More common in women
			- Hormonal influences
			- Greater psychosocial stress
			- Possible diagnostic bias
		- Typical age of onset
			- Around 40 years

	- Risk factors
		- Family history
		- Psychosocial stressors
		- Lack of close interpersonal relationships
		- Divorced or separated
		- Learned helplessness
		- Neurotransmitter abnormalities
			- Serotonin
			- Norepinephrine
			- Dopamine

	- Clinical features

		- Core symptoms
			- Depressed mood
				- Most of the day
			- Anhedonia
				- Most of the day

		- Other symptoms
			- Significant weight loss
				- \>5% body weight
			- Insomnia
			- Psychomotor agitation
			- Psychomotor retardation
			- Fatigue
			- Loss of energy
			- Feelings of worthlessness
			- Excessive or inappropriate guilt
			- Reduced concentration
			- Indecisiveness
			- Recurrent thoughts of death
			- Suicidal ideation

	- Physical examination

		- Usually normal

		- May demonstrate
			- Psychomotor retardation
				- Stooped posture
				- Slowed movements
				- Slowed speech
			- Cognitive impairment
				- Poor concentration
				- Forgetfulness

	- Clinical specifiers

		- Psychotic features
			- Delusions
			- Hallucinations
			- Worse prognosis

		- Atypical features
			- Increased appetite
			- Weight gain
			- Hypersomnia

	- Initial assessment
		- Assess suicide risk immediately
		- Assess risk to others
		- Assess severity of depression
		- Assess for psychotic symptoms
		- Screen for substance misuse
		- Exclude bipolar disorder
		- Review medications
		- Screen for medical causes

	- Initial investigations

		- No investigation confirms depression
		- Investigations aim to exclude organic causes


		- Additional investigations if clinically indicated
			- ECG
				- Before TCAs
				- Elderly patients
				- Cardiac disease


	- Most appropriate management

		- First priority
			- Ensure patient safety
			- Suicide risk assessment

		- If high suicide risk, psychosis, severe self-neglect or inability to care for self
			- Urgent psychiatric assessment
			- Consider hospital admission
			- Consider involuntary treatment if necessary

		- Mild depression
			- Psychoeducation
			- Lifestyle modification
			- Psychological therapy
				- Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
				- Interpersonal therapy (IPT)

		- Moderate to severe depression
			- Psychological therapy
			- Plus antidepressant medication

		- Pharmacotherapy

			- First-line
				- SSRI
					- Sertraline
					- Escitalopram
					- Fluoxetine

			- Alternatives
				- SNRI
				- Mirtazapine
				- Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)
				- MAOIs
					- Usually specialist initiated

		- Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)

			- Indications
				- Severe depression
				- High suicide risk
				- Psychotic depression
				- Catatonia
				- Poor oral intake
				- Failure or intolerance of medication
				- Need for rapid response

		- Psychotherapy
			- Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
				- Corrects distorted beliefs about
					- Self
					- World
					- Future
			- Individual psychotherapy
				- Addresses interpersonal conflict
				- Addresses grief and loss

	- Follow-up
		- Review suicide risk regularly
		- Monitor medication response
		- Monitor adverse effects
		- Assess adherence
		- Continue antidepressants for at least 6–12 months after remission
			- Longer if recurrent depression

	- Differential diagnosis
		- Bipolar disorder
		- Persistent depressive disorder
		- Adjustment disorder
		- Bereavement
		- Substance-induced mood disorder
		- Hypothyroidism
		- Parkinson disease
		- Dementia
		- Pseudodementia
		- Brain tumour
		- Stroke
		- Medication-induced depression

	- AMC high-yield pearls
		- Diagnosis requires
			- ≥2 weeks of symptoms
			- Depressed mood or anhedonia
		- Suicide risk assessment is always the first step
		- Exclude bipolar disorder before prescribing antidepressants
		- SSRIs are first-line pharmacotherapy
		- CBT is first-line psychotherapy
		- ECT is the treatment of choice for
			- Severe suicidal depression
			- Psychotic depression
			- Catatonia
			- Severe depression requiring rapid improvement

- Bipolar I disorder

	- Definition
		- Mood disorder characterised by at least one manic episode
		- Mania lasts ≥1 week
			- Or any duration if hospitalisation is required
		- Causes marked impairment in social or occupational functioning
		- May include psychotic features
		- Major depressive episodes commonly occur but are not required for diagnosis

	- Epidemiology
		- Equal prevalence in men and women
		- Mean age of onset
			- ~18 years
		- Strongest genetic association among psychiatric disorders
		- More common in higher socioeconomic groups
		- Common comorbidities
			- Anxiety disorders
			- Alcohol use disorder
			- Substance use disorders

	- Clinical features

		- Mood
			- Elevated mood
			- Expansive mood
			- Irritable mood

		- Behaviour
			- Grandiosity
			- Inflated self-esteem
			- Increased goal-directed activity
			- Excessive involvement in activities
			- Increased sexual activity
			- Risk-taking behaviour

		- Speech and thought
			- Pressured speech
			- Flight of ideas
			- Racing thoughts
			- Distractibility

		- Activity
			- Psychomotor agitation
			- Decreased need for sleep
			- Increased energy

	- Physical examination
		- Usually normal
		- May demonstrate
			- Psychomotor agitation
			- Pressured speech

	- Initial assessment
		- Assess risk to self
		- Assess risk to others
		- Assess capacity
		- Assess for psychosis
		- Assess need for hospital admission
		- Screen for substance use

	- Initial investigations
		- FBC
		- UEC
		- LFT
		- Thyroid function tests
		- Blood glucose
		- Urine drug screen
		- Pregnancy test (if appropriate)
		- ECG before selected medications
		- Brain imaging only if neurological features or suspected organic cause

	- Management

		- Initial management
			- Ensure safety
			- Hospitalisation if severe mania, psychosis, or high-risk behaviour

		- Pharmacological treatment

			- Mood stabilisers
				- Lithium
				- Sodium valproate
				- Carbamazepine

			- Atypical antipsychotics
				- Olanzapine
				- Quetiapine
				- Risperidone

			- Benzodiazepines
				- Short-term management of agitation

		- Long-term management
			- Mood stabiliser maintenance
			- Psychoeducation
			- Psychological therapy
			- Relapse prevention
			- Regular psychiatric follow-up

	- Differential diagnosis
		- Schizophrenia
		- Bipolar II disorder
		- Personality disorders
		- Hyperthyroidism
		- CNS infection
		- Brain tumour
		- Substance-induced mania
		- Medication-induced mania

	- AMC high-yield pearls
		- Mania lasting ≥1 week defines Bipolar I disorder
		- Hospitalisation is often required
		- Lithium is the classic mood stabiliser
		- Always exclude substance- or medical-induced mania

- Persistent depressive disorder (Dysthymia)

	- Definition
		- Chronic depressed mood
		- Present most of the day
		- On most days
		- Duration ≥2 years in adults

	- Epidemiology
		- Frequently associated with
			- Anxiety disorders
			- Substance use disorders
			- Borderline personality disorder

	- Clinical features
		- Persistent low mood
		- Poor appetite
		- Low energy
		- Poor concentration
		- Difficulty making decisions
		- Functional impairment

	- Initial assessment
		- Assess suicide risk
		- Exclude major depressive disorder
		- Screen for bipolar disorder
		- Exclude medical causes

	- Initial investigations
		- Similar to major depressive disorder
			- FBC
			- UEC
			- LFT
			- Thyroid function tests
			- Vitamin B12
			- Folate
			- Blood glucose
			- Other tests if clinically indicated

	- Management
		- Hospitalisation usually unnecessary
		- Psychotherapy
			- Addresses chronic maladaptive beliefs
			- Resolves longstanding interpersonal conflicts
		- Pharmacotherapy
			- SSRIs (first-line)
			- TCAs
			- MAOIs

	- Differential diagnosis
		- Same as major depressive disorder

	- AMC high-yield pearls
		- Duration ≥2 years distinguishes dysthymia from major depression
		- Chronic low-grade depression
		- SSRIs and psychotherapy are first-line

- Cyclothymic disorder

	- Definition
		- Chronic mood disorder
		- Numerous hypomanic symptoms
		- Numerous depressive symptoms
		- Symptoms persist ≥2 years
		- Does not meet criteria for Bipolar I, Bipolar II, or major depressive disorder

	- Epidemiology
		- More common in women
		- Strong family history of bipolar disorder
		- Frequently associated with
			- Borderline personality disorder
			- Alcohol use disorder
			- Substance use disorders
			- Interpersonal and marital difficulties

	- Clinical features
		- Recurrent mood fluctuations
		- Hypomanic symptoms
			- Elevated mood
			- Increased libido
			- Irritability
		- Depressive symptoms
			- Fatigue
			- Low self-esteem
			- Insomnia

	- Initial assessment
		- Confirm chronic fluctuating course
		- Exclude Bipolar I and Bipolar II disorder
		- Exclude substance-induced mood disorder

	- Initial investigations
		- Similar to bipolar disorder
			- Thyroid function tests
			- FBC
			- UEC
			- LFT
			- Urine drug screen
			- Additional investigations if indicated

	- Management
		- Mood stabilisers
			- Lithium
			- Carbamazepine
			- Sodium valproate
		- Psychotherapy
			- Improve insight
			- Develop coping strategies
			- Improve interpersonal functioning

	- Differential diagnosis
		- Bipolar I disorder
		- Bipolar II disorder
		- Personality disorders
		- Seizure disorders
		- Substance-induced mood disorder
		- Medication-induced mood disorder

	- AMC high-yield pearls
		- Mood fluctuations for ≥2 years
		- Never fulfils criteria for mania or major depression
		- Mood stabilisers are the mainstay of treatment

- Major depressive disorder with seasonal pattern (Seasonal affective disorder)

	- Definition
		- Major depressive episodes occurring during winter months
		- Symptoms remit during spring and summer
		- Thought to be related to abnormal melatonin regulation

	- Clinical features
		- Seasonal depression
		- Sleep disturbance
		- Recurrent annual pattern

	- Initial assessment
		- Confirm seasonal relationship
		- Exclude non-seasonal major depression
		- Assess suicide risk

	- Initial investigations
		- Same as major depressive disorder
			- Exclude organic causes of depression

	- Management
		- Bright light therapy (phototherapy)
		- Psychological therapy
		- Antidepressants if moderate to severe

	- AMC high-yield pearls
		- Winter depression with summer remission
		- First-line treatment is phototherapy
### Grief vs Major Depression

| Feature | Grief / Bereavement | Major Depression |
|---------|----------------------|------------------|
| Common symptoms | Sadness, tearfulness, ↓ sleep, ↓ appetite, ↓ interest in the world | Sadness, tearfulness, ↓ sleep, ↓ appetite, ↓ interest in the world |
| Symptom pattern | Wax and wane | Persistent and unremitting |
| Guilt / shame | Less common | Common |
| Suicidal thoughts | Less likely | More common |
| Duration | May last up to 1 year | Usually >1 year if untreated |
| Functional recovery | Usually returns to baseline within ~2 months | Does not return to baseline functioning |
| Treatment | Supportive psychotherapy | Antidepressants ± psychotherapy |

### Postpartum Mood Disorders

| Feature | Postpartum Blues (Baby Blues) | Major Depression with Peripartum Onset | Bipolar Disorder / Brief Psychotic Disorder with Peripartum Onset |
|---------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Onset | Within 2 weeks after delivery | During pregnancy or within 4 weeks postpartum | During pregnancy or within 4 weeks postpartum |
| Symptoms | Sadness, mood lability, tearfulness | Depressed mood, weight changes, sleep disturbance, excessive anxiety | Depression and/or mania with delusions, hallucinations, psychosis, thoughts of harm |
| Feelings toward baby | No negative feelings | May have negative feelings | May have thoughts of harming the baby |
| Treatment | Reassurance, supportive care, self-limiting | Antidepressants ± psychotherapy | Psychiatric emergency; antipsychotics + mood stabiliser (e.g. lithium) ± antidepressants; often requires hospital admission |
