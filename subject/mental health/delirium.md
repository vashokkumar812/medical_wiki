### AMC High-Yield Clues

| Presentation                                                                    | Most Likely Diagnosis               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Acute fluctuating confusion + poor attention + visual hallucinations            | **Delirium**                        |
| Progressive memory loss over years                                              | **Dementia**                        |
| Auditory hallucinations + delusions + symptoms >6 months                        | **Schizophrenia**                   |
| Mutism + posturing + waxy flexibility + negativism                              | **Catatonia**                       |
| Dramatic, seductive behaviour with excessive emotionality and attention-seeking | **Histrionic Personality Disorder** |

  - Definition
    - Acute disturbance of:
      - Attention (**most important feature**)
      - Awareness
      - Cognition
    - Develops over:
      - Hours to days
    - Course:
      - Fluctuating
      - Symptoms often worsen at night ("sundowning")
    - Usually reversible when the underlying cause is treated.


  - Epidemiology
    - Occurs in:
      - \>40% of hospitalized elderly patients
      - Common in ICU patients
      - Common after surgery
    - Medical emergency until proven otherwise.

  - Risk Factors
    - Advanced age
    - Dementia
    - Hospitalization
    - ICU admission
    - Polypharmacy
    - Multiple comorbidities
    - Severe illness
    - Sensory impairment
      - Poor vision
      - Hearing loss
    - Dehydration
    - Malnutrition
    - Alcohol misuse

  - Causes (Etiology)

        - Sepsis
      - Metabolic disorders
        - Hypoglycaemia
        - Hyponatraemia
        - Hypercalcaemia
      - Hepatic encephalopathy
      - Uraemia
      - Hypoxia
      - Endocrine disorders
    - Neurological disorders
      - Stroke
      - Seizure
      - Head trauma
      - CNS infection

  - Management
    - Treat underlying cause (**most important**)
      - Antibiotics for infection
      - Correct electrolytes
      - Treat hypoglycaemia
      - Oxygen for hypoxia
      - Stop offending drugs
    - Non-pharmacological management (**first-line**)
      - Frequent reorientation
      - Clock and calendar
      - Family involvement
      - Adequate hydration
      - Correct sensory impairment
        - Glasses
        - Hearing aids
      - Early mobilization
      - Promote sleep
      - Reduce environmental noise
      - Avoid unnecessary restraints
      - Avoid unnecessary urinary catheters
    - Pharmacological management
      - Indications
        - Severe agitation
        - Risk to self
        - Risk to others
        - Preventing essential medical care
      - Antipsychotics
        - Haloperidol
          - Low dose
          - Monitor QT prolongation
          - Avoid in Parkinson disease
          - Avoid in Lewy body dementia
        - Olanzapine
        - Quetiapine
        - Risperidone
      - Benzodiazepines
        - Avoid routinely
        - Indications
          - Alcohol withdrawal
          - Benzodiazepine withdrawal

  - Complications
    - Falls
    - Aspiration
    - Pressure injuries
    - Functional decline
    - Prolonged hospitalization
    - Increased mortality
    - Long-term cognitive decline

  - Differential Diagnosis

  - High-Yield AMC Pearls
    - Delirium is an acute medical emergency.
    - **Poor attention is the hallmark feature.**
    - Acute onset with fluctuating symptoms strongly suggests delirium.
    - Visual hallucinations are more common than auditory hallucinations.
    - Hypoactive delirium is frequently missed.
    - Infection, medications, dehydration, and electrolyte abnormalities are common causes.
    - EEG classically shows generalized slowing.
    - Non-pharmacological measures are first-line treatment.
    - Antipsychotics are reserved for severe agitation or danger.
    - Benzodiazepines should generally be avoided except in alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal.

  - AMC Exam Tips
    - Elderly patient + acute confusion + fluctuating consciousness + poor attention
      - → **Delirium until proven otherwise**
    - Always search for and treat the underlying medical cause.
    - Think:
      - Infection
      - Drugs
      - Metabolic disturbance
      - Hypoxia
      - Dehydration
    - First-line management
      - Treat the cause
      - Reorientation
      - Supportive care
    - Reserve antipsychotics for severe agitation or safety concerns.
