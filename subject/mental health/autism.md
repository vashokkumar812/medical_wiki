- Definition
	- Lifelong neurodevelopmental disorder
	- Characterised by impairment in:
		- Social interaction
		- Communication
		- Restricted and repetitive behaviours

- Onset
	- Early childhood
	- Typically before 3 years

- Epidemiology
	- Prevalence
		- ~0.08%
		- Broader estimates: 1–2%
	- Male predominance
		- Male : Female ≈ 5 : 1
		- Ratio narrowing
		- Females may mask symptoms

- Etiology / Risk Factors
	- Multifactorial
	- CNS involvement
	- Often no clear cause identified

	- Associated Conditions
		- Phenylketonuria
		- Tuberous sclerosis
		- Fragile X syndrome
		- Congenital rubella syndrome
		- Encephalitis
		- Perinatal hypoxia

	- Risk Modifiers

		- Child Factors
			- Male sex
			- Prematurity
			- Low birth weight
			- Genetic syndromes

		- Environmental Factors
			- Neglect
			- Low stimulation
			- Trauma
			- Maltreatment
			- Lead poisoning
			- Toxin exposure
			- Low socioeconomic status

		- Maternal / Parental Factors
			- Poor maternal health
			- Prenatal alcohol exposure
			- Prenatal drug exposure
			- Parental mental illness
			- Poor parental health

		- Perinatal Factors
			- Birth complications

- Core Clinical Features

	- 1. Social Impairment
		- Poor peer relationships
		- Lack of eye contact
		- Reduced non-verbal communication
		- Poor gestures
		- Impaired joint attention
		- Failure of social reciprocity
		- Deficits in social interaction

	- 2. Communication Impairment
		- Delayed speech
		- Absent speech
		- Echolalia
		- Bizarre speech patterns
		- Receptive communication deficits
		- Expressive communication deficits

	- 3. Behavioural Features
		- Restrictive behaviours
		- Repetitive behaviours
		- Stereotyped movements
		- Stereotypies
		- Restricted interests
		- Insistence on sameness
		- Rigid routines
		- Resistance to change

	- 4. Sensory Features
		- Hyper- or hypo-sensitivity
		- Sensitivity to:
			- Sounds
			- Lights
			- Colours
			- Textures
			- Smells
			- Touch
		- Sensory triggers may cause agitation

- Behavioural & Sensory Characteristics

	- Social Communication
		- Impaired reciprocity
		- Reduced non-verbal communication
		- Impaired interaction
		- Use:
			- Short communication
			- Clear communication
			- Fact-based language

	- Behaviour Patterns
		- Restrictive repetitive behaviours
		- Stereotypies
		- Fixated routines

	- Sensory Sensitivities
		- Hyper- or hypo-sensitive responses
		- Consult parents/carers regarding triggers

- Associated Features / Comorbidities

	- Intellectual disability
		- ~75%
		- ASD + ID worse functional impairment than ID alone

	- Epilepsy / Seizures
		- ~25%
		- Abnormal EEG
		- Abnormal brain morphology

	- Other Common Comorbidities
		- Speech and language disorders
		- ADHD
		- Anxiety disorders
		- Depression
			- More common in older children
		- Specific learning disorders

- Developmental Domains to Assess
	- Social
		- Interaction
		- Joint attention
	- Emotional
		- Emotional regulation
		- Caregiver response
	- Communication
		- Receptive
		- Expressive
	- Cognition / Fine Motor / Self-care
		- Problem solving
		- Play
		- ADLs
	- Gross Motor
		- Posture
		- Gait
		- Milestones

- Red Flags (VERY HIGH-YIELD)

	- 6 Months
		- No social smile

	- 9 Months
		- No vocal interaction

	- 12 Months
		- No pointing
		- No gestures
		- No response to name

	- 16 Months
		- No single words

	- 18–24 Months
		- No 2-word phrases

	- Any Age
		- Developmental regression

- Acute Presentation (VERY HIGH-YIELD)

	- Always Look for Underlying Cause
		- Pain
		- Dietary changes
		- Fluid balance issues
		- Infection
		- Constipation / bowel habit changes

	- Behavioural Concerns
		- Meltdowns
		- Self-injurious behaviour
		- Head banging
		- Biting
		- Aggression

	- These May Indicate
		- Physical illness
		- Anxiety
		- Emotional dysregulation
		- Intolerance of change

- Hospital Considerations (VERY HIGH-YIELD)

	- 10× more likely to require admission
	- Hospital environment may be difficult to process

	- Key Principles
		- Parents/carers guide what works best
		- Maintain low stimulus environment
		- Assume distress = underlying medical issue until proven otherwise
		- Avoid repeated interventions
		- Combine procedures whenever possible

- Assessment

	- History
		- Behaviour often represents change from baseline
		- Use family to interpret behaviour
		- Identify triggers

	- Acute Presentation Questions
		- Has this happened before?
		- How was it managed previously?
		- Any triggers?
		- Is the child otherwise well?

	- Examination
		- Look for pain or illness
		- May have higher pain threshold
		- Examine:
			- Opportunistically
			- Carefully
			- Repeatedly if required
		- Communicate before each examination step

- Investigations
	- Guided by history and examination
	- Group investigations together
		- Minimise distress

- Management

	- Primary Treatment
		- Supportive
		- Multidisciplinary

	- Includes
		- Family counselling
		- Special education programs

	- Environmental Modification (VERY HIGH-YIELD)
		- Low stimulus environment
			- Dim lights
			- Minimal noise
		- Familiar faces
			- Parents/carers
		- Minimise staff changes and room entry
		- Familiar objects
			- Toys
			- Pillows
			- Sheets
		- Music/technology if calming
		- Sensory toys for distraction

	- Procedural Planning
		- Use Child Life / Play Therapy early
		- Explain procedures using:
			- Simple words
			- Visual aids
			- Gestures
		- Demonstrate on parent/carer first
		- Minimise interventions
		- Group investigations/procedures
		- Consider sedation
		- Allow child to sit with parent
		- Use distraction techniques
		- Remove IV cannula under sedation if possible

- Medications

	- For Severe Behavioural Disturbance
		- Atypical antipsychotics may be used for:
			- Severe agitation
			- Self-injurious behaviour

	- For Non-acute Anxiety / Agitation
		- Psychotropic medications may be used

	- Before Prescribing Consider
		- Current medications
			- Drug interactions
		- Contraindications
			- Medical
			- Behavioural
		- Procedure factors
			- Duration
			- Invasiveness

- Screening and Surveillance

	- Case Finding Approach
		- Preferred strategy

	- Routine General Population Screening
		- Not routinely recommended
		- Insufficient evidence of benefit

	- Autism Screening (1.5–5 years, no concerns)
		- Generally not recommended
		- Only if concerns raised

	- Speech and Language Delay Screening (<5 years)
		- Not recommended routinely

	- Active Surveillance
		- At all visits from 0–5 years
		- Opportunistic detection after 5 years

	- Focus On
		- School difficulties
		- Behaviour issues
		- Learning concerns

	- Tools
		- PEDS
		- ASQ

	- If Concern Present
		- Hearing assessment
		- Vision assessment
		- Specialist referral

	- Important Principles
		- Do not wait if red flags present
		- Early detection improves outcomes
		- Enables earlier intervention/support

- Developmental Delay Definitions

	- Developmental Delay
		- Delay in ≥1 developmental domain

	- Global Developmental Delay
		- Delay in ≥2 domains

	- Developmental Disability
		- Functional impairment

	- Developmental Vulnerability
		- Increased risk due to:
			- Child factors
			- Environmental factors

- Differentiation

	- Autism vs Hearing Loss

		- Autism
			- Poor joint attention
			- Poor reciprocity

		- Hearing Loss
			- Good eye contact
			- Good gestures

	- Other Differentials
		- Intellectual disability alone
		- Hearing impairment
		- Environmental deprivation
		- Selective mutism
		- Rett syndrome
		- Isolated speech delay
			- Normal social interaction and motor development
			- Consider hearing loss

- Developmental Context

	- Early Infancy
		- Secure attachment:
			- Enhances neurological development
			- Improves social/emotional development

	- Play at 12 Months
		- Develops:
			- Communication
			- Cognition
			- Social skills

	- Language Development
		- Supports all developmental domains

	- Parent Role
		- Encourage:
			- Play
			- Interaction
			- Shared attention

- When Concerns Identified
	- Perform comprehensive developmental assessment
	- May involve:
		- Allied health
		- Specialists
	- Confirm developmental delay
	- Initiate early intervention
	- Subthreshold concerns may still qualify for support programs

- Early Intervention
	- Improves long-term outcomes
	- Structured evidence-based programs
	- Funded support available
		- NDIS early childhood approach

- Follow-up and Referral

	- Always
		- Search for underlying cause of distress

	- If No Medical Cause
		- Consider mental health referral

	- Consult Paediatric Team When
		- Initial strategies fail
		- Cause unclear
		- Admission required after sedation
		- Further investigations required

	- Transfer When
		- Care exceeds local capability

	- Discharge Criteria
		- Returned to baseline
		- Cause identified and managed
		- Follow-up arranged
			- GP
			- Outpatient services

- Practical Consultation Considerations
	- Reduce stress during consultation
	- Flexible appointments
	- Support carers

- Terminology
	- Avoid:
		- High-functioning
		- Low-functioning
	- Focus on:
		- Functional impairment
		- Comorbidities

- Ethics (IMPORTANT)
	- Children with ASD / ID / developmental disabilities:
		- Have equal healthcare rights
		- Must be treated with:
			- Dignity
			- Respect
			- Understanding

- Course and Prognosis
	- Chronic lifelong condition
	- ~30% achieve partial independence
	- Most have persistent disability

	- Poor Prognostic Factors
		- Intellectual disability
		- Lack of functional speech

- Epidemiology of Developmental Vulnerability
	- Developmental vulnerability at age 5
		- ~20%
	- Disability prevalence (0–14 years)
		- 7.4%
	- Intellectual disability
		- 4.3%
	- Speech/sensory disability
		- 3.2%
