- Delusional disorder

	- Definition
		- Persistent delusions
		- Duration ≥1 month
		- Delusions are non-bizarre
			- Situations that could occur in real life
				- Being poisoned
				- Being followed
				- Being deceived by spouse
		- Does not meet criteria for schizophrenia
		- Functioning generally preserved outside the delusion

	- Epidemiology
		- Mean age of onset
			- ~40 years
		- More common in women
		- Most patients are
			- Married
			- Employed
		- Associated with
			- Low socioeconomic status
			- Recent immigration

	- Risk factors
		- Low socioeconomic status
		- Recent immigration
		- Organic brain disease
			- Limbic system lesions
			- Basal ganglia pathology

	- Clinical features

		- Core feature
			- Non-bizarre delusions lasting ≥1 month

		- Hallucinations
			- Absent or minimal

		- Thought process
			- Logical
			- Coherent

		- Functioning
			- Generally preserved
			- Patients are reliable except regarding their delusional beliefs

	- Types
		- Erotomanic
		- Jealous
		- Grandiose
		- Somatic
		- Mixed
		- Unspecified

	- Initial assessment
		- Assess risk to self
		- Assess risk to others
		- Assess insight
		- Assess impact of delusion on functioning
		- Exclude medical and substance-induced causes

	- Initial investigations
		- FBC
		- UEC
		- LFT
		- Thyroid function tests
		- Blood glucose
		- Vitamin B12 and folate
		- Urine drug screen
		- Brain CT/MRI if neurological signs or suspected organic cause

	- Management

		- Initial management
			- Usually outpatient management
			- Hospitalisation if necessary to exclude organic causes or if safety concerns exist

		- Pharmacotherapy
			- Antipsychotic medication
			- Response often limited

		- Psychotherapy
			- Individual supportive psychotherapy
			- Build therapeutic alliance
			- Gradually improve insight
			- Help patient recognise the impact of delusions on daily life

	- Differential diagnosis
		- Schizophrenia
		- Schizoaffective disorder
		- Mood disorders with psychotic features
		- Substance-induced psychosis
		- Neurocognitive disorders
		- Organic brain disease

	- AMC high-yield pearls
		- Delusions are non-bizarre
		- Duration ≥1 month
		- No prominent hallucinations
		- Functioning relatively preserved
		- Antipsychotics are first-line treatment, although response is often incomplete



| Feature | Schizophrenia | Brief Psychotic Disorder | Schizophreniform Disorder | Schizoaffective Disorder |
|---------|---------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Definition** | Chronic psychotic disorder with impaired thought, perception, behaviour and reality testing | Acute psychotic disorder with complete recovery | Schizophrenia-like illness of intermediate duration | Psychotic disorder with concurrent mood disorder plus independent psychosis |
| **Duration** | ≥6 months | >1 day to <1 month | >1 month to <6 months | ≥6 months overall; psychosis persists ≥2 weeks without mood symptoms |
| **Typical onset** | Men: 15–25 years (earlier than women) | Any age, often after severe stress | Young adults | Young adulthood |
| **Trigger** | Usually none | Often follows severe psychological stress | Usually none | Mood disorder (mania or depression) plus psychosis |
| **Core psychotic symptoms** | Hallucinations (usually auditory), bizarre delusions, disorganised speech, disorganised/catatonic behaviour, negative symptoms | Hallucinations, delusions, disorganised speech, grossly disorganised/catatonic behaviour | Same as schizophrenia | Schizophrenia symptoms plus mood episodes |
| **Negative symptoms** | Common | Usually absent | Present | May occur |
| **Mood symptoms** | May occur briefly but not predominant | Usually absent | Usually absent | Major depressive or manic episodes are prominent |
| **Psychosis without mood symptoms** | Yes | Yes | Yes | **Required:** hallucinations or delusions for ≥2 weeks without mood symptoms |
| **Functional impairment** | Marked social/occupational dysfunction | Temporary | Social/occupational dysfunction; most recover baseline function | Variable impairment |
| **Return to baseline** | Usually poor/incomplete | Complete recovery | Most return to baseline | Better than schizophrenia but worse than mood disorders |
| **Physical examination** | Usually normal; may have psychomotor abnormalities, saccadic eye movements | Usually normal | Usually normal | Usually normal |
| **Brain imaging** | CT: enlarged lateral/third ventricles, cortical atrophy; MRI: enlarged ventricles; PET: frontal hypoactivity, basal ganglia hyperactivity | Usually normal | Usually normal | Usually normal |
| **Risk factors** | Strong genetic predisposition, dopamine excess, serotonin abnormalities, low socioeconomic status | Psychological stress, personality disorders, low socioeconomic status | Suicide risk after psychotic episode resolves | History of mood disorder |
| **Initial investigations** | FBC, UEC, LFT, TFT, B12/folate, glucose, urine drug screen, HIV/syphilis if indicated, CT/MRI if neurological signs | Same work-up to exclude organic and substance-induced causes | Same as schizophrenia | Same as schizophrenia plus assessment of mood disorder |
| **Initial management** | Assess risk; hospitalise if unsafe or severely psychotic | Assess risk; hospitalise if acutely psychotic | Assess need for hospitalisation | Assess risk; hospitalise if severe psychosis or mania/depression |
| **Most appropriate treatment** | Antipsychotics (first-line); clozapine if treatment-resistant; supportive psychotherapy | Short-term antipsychotics ± benzodiazepines | Antipsychotics for 3–6 months ± psychotherapy | Treat mood disorder (antidepressants or mood stabilisers) + antipsychotics |
| **Prognosis** | Chronic illness with relapses | Excellent; complete recovery within 1 month | Generally good; many recover completely | Intermediate prognosis (better than schizophrenia, worse than mood disorders) |
| **Important differentials** | Substance-induced psychosis, temporal lobe epilepsy, mood disorders, HIV, brain tumour, stroke, personality disorders, malingering | Substance-induced psychosis, delirium | Schizophrenia, brief psychotic disorder, mood disorders | Bipolar disorder with psychotic features, major depression with psychotic features, schizophrenia |

### AMC High-Yield Duration Rule

| Disorder | Duration |
|----------|----------|
| Brief psychotic disorder | **>1 day to <1 month** |
| Schizophreniform disorder | **>1 month to <6 months** |
| Schizophrenia | **≥6 months** |
| Schizoaffective disorder | **Mood disorder + schizophrenia + ≥2 weeks of psychosis without mood symptoms** |
