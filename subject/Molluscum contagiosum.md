- pathophysiology
	- Spread
		- Direct skin-to-skin contact
		- Fomites
			- E.g. towels
		- Auto-inoculation
			- Scratching
			- Touching lesions
- diagnosis
	- clinical diagnosis
	- appearance
		- Firm
		- Pearly
		- Dome-shaped papules
		- Central umbilication
			- Pit
	- size
		- Usually 1–3 mm
		- Can grow to 1–2 cm

	- site
		- Can occur anywhere except
			- Palms
			- Soles
	- number of lesions
		- Few lesions
		- Many lesions
	- Children with pre-existing eczema
		- More extensive molluscum
		- Longer lasting molluscum

	- Distribution
		- Anywhere on body

		- Common areas
			- Trunk
			- Axillae
			- Antecubital fossae
			- Popliteal fossae
			- Groin

		- Anogenital lesions in children
			- Usually due to autoinoculation


- Management
	- avoid
		- School/day care Exclusion
		- Isolated
		- Restricted in activities
	- Reduce spread to siblings
		- Avoid sharing towels and bathing together
	- Resolution
		- Occurs when immune response develops
		- May take 3 months to 3 years
	- Treat associated eczema
	- Inflamed lesions
		- Antibiotics often not needed
		- antibiotics if
			- Purulent exudate
			- Abscess
			- Extending erythema
	- Treatment of lesions
		- Most children
			- Do not require treatment
	
		- Consider treatment if
			- Quality of life affected
			- Immunocompromised
	
		- Physical treatments
			- Cryotherapy
			- Curettage
	
			- Not routinely recommended in children
	
			- May cause scarring
	
		- Refer dermatology
			- cantharidin treatment
	
				- Topical blistering agent
	
				- When used appropriately
					- Should not cause scarring
	
	- Consider paediatric consultation when
		- Diagnosis uncertain
		- Severe eczema
