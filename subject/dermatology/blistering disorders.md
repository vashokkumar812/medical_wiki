- **Classification**
	- Immunobullous
		- Pemphigus vulgaris
		- Bullous pemphigoid
		- Dermatitis herpetiformis
	- Non-immunobullous
		- Bullous impetigo
		- HSV/VZV
		- Drug eruptions
		- Porphyria cutanea tarda

- **Clinical clues**
	- Fragile blisters/erosions
		- Pemphigus vulgaris
	- Tense blisters
		- Bullous pemphigoid
	- Grouped itchy vesicles
		- Dermatitis herpetiformis
	- Honey-coloured crusts
		- Bullous impetigo

- **Diagnosis**
	- Skin biopsy
		- Histopathology
	- Direct immunofluorescence
		- Important for autoimmune blistering disease

# Bullous Pemphigoid

- **Definition**
	- Autoimmune subepidermal blistering disease
	- Most common autoimmune blistering disorder

- **Pathophysiology**
	- IgG autoantibodies against basement membrane
	- Complement activation

- **Epidemiology**
	- Usually elderly
	- Both sexes equally affected
	- Rare mucosal involvement

- **Clinical features**
	- Tense pruritic bullae
	- Flexural predominance
	- Blisters on normal or erythematous skin
	- Significant itch common

- **Important presentation**
	- May initially present as persistent urticaria before bullae develop

- **Drug triggers**
	- Frusemide
	- NSAIDs
	- Captopril
	- Penicillamine
	- Antibiotics

- **Diagnosis**
	- Skin biopsy from blister edge
		- Subepidermal blister
	- Direct immunofluorescence
		- Linear IgG/complement along basement membrane

- **Treatment**
	- Potent topical corticosteroids
	- Oral prednisolone if extensive
	- Doxycycline/minocycline + nicotinamide possible
	- Immunosuppressants for severe disease
		- Azathioprine
		- Methotrexate
		- Mycophenolate

- **Steroid considerations**
	- Calcium + vitamin D supplementation
	- Bone mineral density monitoring

- **Course**
	- Often remits within years
	- Long-term treatment commonly required



# Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (PCT)

- **Definition**
	- Most common porphyria
	- Deficiency of uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase

- **Associations/Risk factors**
	- Alcohol
	- Hepatitis C
	- HIV
	- Oestrogens
	- Iron overload
	- Haemochromatosis

- **Pathophysiology**
	- Accumulation of photoactive porphyrins
	- Photosensitivity → oxidative skin damage

- **Clinical features**
	- Photosensitive blistering
	- Fragile skin
	- Dorsum of hands commonly affected
	- Slow-healing erosions/blisters
	- Scarring
	- Milia
	- Hyperpigmentation
	- Hypertrichosis
		- Temples/cheeks
	- Dark “tea/port wine” urine

- **Histology**
	- Subepidermal blister
	- Minimal inflammation
	- Festooning of dermal papillae

- **Investigations**
	- Urine porphyrins ↑
	- Faecal isocoproporphyrin ↑
	- Iron studies/ferritin
	- LFTs
	- Hepatitis/HIV screening
	- Haemochromatosis testing if indicated

- **Treatment**
	- Avoid sun exposure
	- Reflective sunscreen
		- Zinc oxide/titanium dioxide
	- Avoid alcohol/oestrogens/hepatotoxins
	- Venesection
		- First-line
	- Low-dose hydroxychloroquine/chloroquine

- **Important differential**
	- Pseudoporphyria
		- NSAIDs
		- Tetracyclines
		- Frusemide
