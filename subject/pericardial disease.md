# Pericarditis

- Definition
	- Important cause of chest pain
	- Inflammation of pericardium

- Common symptoms
	- Pleuritic chest pain
	- Sharp retrosternal pain
	- Pain worse with inspiration
	- Pain worse lying flat
	- Relief sitting forward
	- Restricted breathing due to pain
	- May radiate to neck or arm

- Epidemiology
	- ~5% chest pain presentations to ED without MI

- Important history
	- Cardiovascular disease
	- Connective tissue disease
	- Autoimmune disease
	- Renal impairment
	- Diabetes
	- Hypertension
	- Dyslipidaemia
	- Family history cardiac disease
	- Smoking
	- Recent viral illness
			- URTI
			- Gastroenteritis

- Examination
	- Measure BP in both arms
	- Assess for:
			- Shock
			- Diaphoresis
			- Clamminess
			- Tachycardia
			- Hypotension
			- Pulsus paradoxus
			- Elevated JVP
			- Heart sound changes
			- Lung sound changes
			- Peripheral oedema

- Pericardial friction rub
	- Seen in ~35%
	- High-pitched scratching/“velcro-like”
	- Best heard:
			- Left lower sternal border
			- In expiration
			- Leaning forward

- Differential diagnoses
	- Pulmonary embolism
	- Aortic dissection
	- Pneumothorax
	- Pneumonia
	- Acute MI

- Important point
	- If diagnosis unclear
			- Send to ED

- Investigations
	- ECG
	- Blood tests
	- Troponin
	- CRP
	- FBC
	- Chest X-ray
	- Echocardiography

# ECG in pericarditis

- Typical ECG findings
	- Diffuse ST elevation
	- Diffuse PR depression
	- ST elevation with upward concavity

- Differences from STEMI
	- Not confined to coronary territory
	- Usually no reciprocal ST depression

- ECG tamponade findings
	- Low voltage QRS
	- Tachycardia
	- Electrical alternans

- Electrical alternans
	- Alternating QRS amplitude
	- Due to swinging heart in pericardial fluid

# Blood tests

- CRP
	- Often elevated

- Leucocytosis
	- Mild elevation common

- Troponin
	- Usually normal
	- May be elevated in ~30%
	- Epicardial inflammation can elevate troponin

# Chest X-ray

- Uses
	- Exclude pneumothorax
	- Detect large effusion

- Large pericardial effusion findings
	- Globular cardiac silhouette
	- Cardiothoracic ratio >0.5

- Important point
	- Small/moderate effusions may have normal CXR

# Causes of pericarditis/pericardial effusion

- Idiopathic
	- Most common
	- Diagnosis of exclusion

- Viral
	- Most common infectious cause
	- Usually preceded by respiratory/GI illness

- HIV pericarditis
	- Consider in high-risk patients

- Bacterial
	- Often with high fever
	- Can occur with pneumonia

- Tuberculosis
	- Consider exposure/demographic risk

- Autoimmune
	- RA
	- SLE

- Uraemic
	- Chronic renal failure
	- Peri-dialysis periods

- Neoplastic
	- Lung cancer
	- Breast cancer
	- Haematological malignancy

- Post-procedural
	- Cardiac surgery
	- Thoracic surgery

- Post-MI
	- Weeks after MI
	- More common after large anterior infarcts

- Radiation

- Drugs

- Rare causes
	- Fungal
	- Parasitic

# Investigation according to cause

- Bacterial
	- Blood cultures
	- Pericardial fluid cultures

- Tuberculosis
	- Chest X-ray
	- Tuberculin test
	- Quantiferon Gold
	- Sputum cultures

- Autoimmune
	- Targeted autoimmune tests

- Neoplastic
	- Pericardial fluid cytology
	- Pericardial biopsy

- Uraemic
	- Urea
	- Creatinine

# Treatment of acute pericarditis

- Main goals
	- Pain relief
	- Reduce inflammation

- NSAIDs
	- First-line
	- Lowest effective dose
	- Shortest duration possible

- Example regimen
	- Ibuprofen 400 mg TDS with food for 1 week

- PPI indication
	- High GI risk patients
			- Age >65
			- Previous peptic ulcer
			- Aspirin use
			- Steroid use
			- Anticoagulants

- Colchicine
	- Commonly used first episode
	- Prevents recurrence
	- Usually with NSAIDs
	- Generally well tolerated

- Colchicine side effects
	- Mainly GI
	- High-dose prolonged use
			- Irreversible toxicity

- Example regimen
	- Colchicine 0.5 mg BD for 3 months

- Steroids
	- Controversial

- Prednisone indications
	- NSAID refractory
	- Uraemic pericarditis
	- Connective tissue disease

- Prednisone regimen
	- 1 mg/kg/day
	- Taper over 2–4 weeks

- Important point
	- Continue NSAIDs + colchicine + PPI

# Lifestyle advice

- Avoid strenuous activity until symptoms resolve
- Competitive athletes
	- Restrict activity for at least 3 months after symptom resolution

# Pericardial effusion

- Seen in ~60% acute pericarditis

- Symptoms/signs
	- Dyspnoea
	- Tachycardia
	- Elevated JVP

# Cardiac tamponade

- Occurs in ~5%

- Definition
	- Pericardial pressure impairs diastolic filling

- Important point
	- Depends on rate of accumulation
	- Not just total volume

- Beck triad
	- Hypotension
	- Elevated JVP
	- Muffled heart sounds

- Other features
	- Tachypnoea
	- Tachycardia
	- AF
	- Kussmaul sign
	- Positive hepatojugular reflux
	- Weak peripheral pulses
	- Peripheral oedema
	- Cyanosis

# Pulsus paradoxus

- Definition
	- >10 mmHg inspiratory fall in systolic BP

- Measurement
	- Inflate cuff above systolic pressure
	- Deflate slowly
	- Initial Korotkoff sounds only during expiration
	- Continue deflating until heard throughout inspiration and expiration
	- Difference >10 mmHg
			- Positive pulsus paradoxus

# Echocardiography

- Confirms
	- Effusion
	- Constriction
	- Tamponade
	- 

- Repeat echo important
	- Detect recurrence
	- Prevent recurrent tamponade

# Pericardiocentesis

- Indications
	- Symptomatic moderate/large effusion
	- Failed medical therapy
	- Diagnostic purposes

- Diagnostic uses
	- Cytology
	- TB testing
	- Bacterial cultures

# Important AMC MCQ points

- Pericarditis pain
	- Worse lying flat
	- Better leaning forward

- ECG hallmark
	- Diffuse ST elevation + PR depression

- Different from STEMI
	- No territorial pattern
	- No reciprocal depression

- Friction rub
	- Left lower sternal border
	- Leaning forward
	- Expiration

- First-line treatment
	- NSAID + colchicine

- Cardiac tamponade
	- Beck triad
	- Pulsus paradoxus

- Troponin
	- May be elevated even without myocarditis

- Viral/idiopathic
	- Most common causes

- Echo
	- Best test for effusion/tamponade

- Pericardiocentesis
	- Urgent in tamponade
