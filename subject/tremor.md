# Parkinsonism (AMC MCQ High-Yield)

- **Definition**
	- Clinical syndrome characterized by:
		- Bradykinesia (**required**)
		- Plus at least one of:
			- Resting tremor
			- Rigidity
			- Postural instability (usually later)

- **Causes**
	- Idiopathic Parkinson disease (most common)
	- Drug-induced
		- Antipsychotics (e.g. haloperidol, risperidone)
		- Metoclopramide
		- Prochlorperazine
	- Vascular parkinsonism
	- Normal pressure hydrocephalus
	- Atypical parkinsonian syndromes
		- Multiple system atrophy (MSA)
		- Progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP)
		- Corticobasal degeneration (CBD)
		- Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB)

- **Pathophysiology**
	- Degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta
	- ↓ Dopamine in the striatum
	- Lewy bodies (α-synuclein) in Parkinson disease

- **Clinical Features**
	- Motor
		- Bradykinesia
		- Resting "pill-rolling" tremor (4–6 Hz)
		- Cogwheel rigidity
		- Postural instability (late)
		- Shuffling gait
		- Reduced arm swing
		- Festination
		- Freezing of gait
		- Masked facies
		- Hypophonia
		- Micrographia
	- Non-motor
		- Constipation
		- Anosmia
		- REM sleep behaviour disorder
		- Depression
		- Anxiety
		- Orthostatic hypotension
		- Urinary dysfunction
		- Cognitive impairment
		- Dementia (late)

- **Diagnosis**
	- Clinical diagnosis
	- Good response to levodopa supports Parkinson disease
	- MRI brain
		- Usually normal
		- Used to exclude other causes
	- DaTscan
		- Occasionally useful if diagnosis uncertain
		- Not routinely required

- **Management**
	- Non-pharmacological
		- Regular exercise
		- Physiotherapy
		- Occupational therapy
		- Speech therapy
	- Medications
		- Levodopa + carbidopa/benserazide
			- Most effective
			- Preferred in older patients
		- Dopamine agonists
			- Pramipexole
			- Ropinirole
			- More common in younger patients
		- MAO-B inhibitors
			- Selegiline
			- Rasagiline
		- COMT inhibitors
			- Entacapone
			- Used with levodopa for wearing-off
		- Amantadine
			- Useful for dyskinesias
		- Anticholinergics
			- Benztropine
			- Mainly for troublesome tremor in younger patients
	- Advanced disease
		- Deep brain stimulation
			- Medication-responsive disease
			- Significant motor fluctuations
			- No severe dementia

- **Complications**
	- Falls
	- Aspiration pneumonia
	- Dementia
	- Depression
	- Psychosis
	- Levodopa-induced dyskinesias
	- Wearing-off phenomenon
	- On-off fluctuations

- **Drug Adverse Effects**
	- Levodopa
		- Nausea
		- Orthostatic hypotension
		- Dyskinesias
		- Hallucinations
		- Motor fluctuations
	- Dopamine agonists
		- Hallucinations
		- Excessive daytime sleepiness
		- Impulse control disorders
			- Gambling
			- Hypersexuality
			- Compulsive shopping
	- Anticholinergics
		- Confusion
		- Urinary retention
		- Dry mouth
		- Constipation

- **Differentials**
	- Essential tremor
		- Action tremor
		- Improves with alcohol
		- No bradykinesia
	- Drug-induced parkinsonism
		- Symmetrical symptoms
		- Dopamine-blocking medications
	- Progressive supranuclear palsy
		- Early falls
		- Vertical gaze palsy
	- Multiple system atrophy
		- Early autonomic failure
	- Dementia with Lewy bodies
		- Dementia before or within 1 year of parkinsonism
		- Visual hallucinations

- **AMC Pearls**
	- Bradykinesia is **essential** for diagnosing parkinsonism.
	- Resting tremor improves with voluntary movement.
	- Levodopa is the most effective symptomatic treatment.
	- Dopamine agonists commonly cause impulse control disorders.
	- Parkinson disease is associated with **Lewy bodies (α-synuclein)**.
	- Avoid dopamine-blocking drugs (e.g. metoclopramide, haloperidol) in Parkinson disease.
	- Parkinson disease = asymmetric onset + good levodopa response.
	- Early falls, early autonomic failure, poor levodopa response, or early dementia suggest **atypical parkinsonism** rather than idiopathic Parkinson disease.

- Rapid AMC differentiation ⭐
	- Rest tremor + bradykinesia + rigidity
		- Parkinson disease
	- Action tremor + bilateral + alcohol improves
		- Essential tremor
	- Intention tremor + ataxia
		- Cerebellar lesion
	- Irregular + task-specific
		- Dystonic tremor
	- Variable + distractible
		- Functional tremor
	- Symmetric postural tremor + medication history
		- Drug-induced tremor
