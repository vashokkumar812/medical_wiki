## risk factors

| Condition               | Risk Factors                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| BPPV                    | Increasing age, head trauma, prolonged bed rest, osteoporosis, migraine, inner ear disorders |
| Ménière’s disease       | Family history, autoimmune disease, viral infections, allergies, migraine                    |
| Vestibular neuritis     | Recent viral infection (URTI), herpes simplex virus reactivation                             |
| Labyrinthitis           | Viral infections, bacterial otitis media, meningitis, cholesteatoma                          |
| Perilymph fistula       | Head trauma, barotrauma, heavy lifting, scuba diving                                         |
| Ototoxic vestibulopathy | Aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, \- Salicylates, Antihypertensives drugs                     |

- Evaluation



	- Key History Clues
		- Hearing loss present
			- Labyrinthitis
			- Ménière disease
		- Hearing loss absent
			- BPPV
			- Vestibular neuronitis


		- Medication causes
			- Frusemide
			- Salicylates
			- Antihypertensives
		- Psychogenic dizziness
			- Internal spinning sensation
			- Feeling detached from body
			- Spinning inside the head rather than environment

	- Ear Examination
		- Otoscopy
			- Vesicles
				- Herpes zoster oticus
			- Retraction pockets
				- Cholesteatoma
		- Tragus pressure or Valsalva (Hennebert sign)
			- Vertigo
				- Perilymphatic fistula

	- Bedside Examination


		- Romberg Test
			- Assesses
				- Proprioception
				- Vestibular function
				- Cerebellar function
			- Positive test
				- Stable with eyes open
				- Unstable with eyes closed

		- Fukuda-Unterberger Test
			- Marches on spot with eyes closed
			- Positive test
				- Deviates from midline
				- Usually toward side of vestibular hypofunction

		- Orthostatic Blood Pressure
			- Systolic drop >20 mmHg on standing
			- Suggests
				- Dehydration
				- Autonomic dysfunction

- Investigations
	- Check capillary blood glucose in all patients
	- Blood tests
		- Not routinely required
	- MRI
		- Investigation of choice
		- Multiple sclerosis
		- Cerebral infarction
		- Cerebellopontine angle tumour
	- CT
		- Cerebellar haemorrhage
		- Petrous bone pathology
		- Trauma-related vertigo

- Red Flags Requiring Urgent Neuroimaging
	- Very sudden onset vertigo (seconds)
	- Persistent vertigo not triggered by position
	- New occipital headache
	- Deafness without typical Ménière history
	- Acute vertigo with normal head impulse test
	- Severe gait ataxia
	- Truncal ataxia
	- Central neurological signs



- Treatment
	- Avoid benzodiazepines
		- Addictive
		- Not routinely recommended
