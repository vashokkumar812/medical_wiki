- Acute otitis externa (AOE)
	- Inflammatory condition of the outer ear
- epidemiology
	- Tropical climates
- Common organisms
	- Bacterial
		- Pseudomonas aeruginosa
		- Staphylococcus aureus
	- Fungal
		- Aspergillus
			- black spore
			- white hyphae
		- Candida
			- off white sebaceous debris
- prognosis
	- Usually improves within 72 hours with:
		- Topical antibiotics
		- Ear cleaning
	- Untreated/complex cases
		- Necrotising otitis externa (NOE)
			- Especially in diabetics
- Diagnosis
	- Acute tragal tenderness
		- Pathognomonic

- Swab indication
	- Empirical treatment failure
	- Need pathogen identification
- Differentials
	- Acute/chronic otitis media
		- Middle ear effusion
		- Dull/yellow TM
		- Bulging TM
		- Immobile TM
		- EAC inflammation absent if TM intact
	- Mastoiditis
		- Complication of AOM
		- Mastoid tenderness/erythema/fluctuance
		- Pinna protrusion
		- Bulging posterior EAC wall
	- Furunculosis
		- “Pimple-like” fluctuant erythematous nodule
	- Perichondritis
		- Significant pinna inflammation
		- Lobule sparing
		- EAC not involved
	- Neoplasm
		- Cholesteatoma
		- SCC
		- BCC
		- Melanoma
		- Clues
			- Refractory symptoms
			- Constitutional symptoms
			- Facial nerve palsy
	- Contact dermatitis
		- Trigger:
			- Metals
			- Plastics
			- Soap
			- Topical antibiotics
		- Pruritus predominant
		- Improves after trigger removal
	- Ramsay Hunt syndrome
		- otalgia + lmn palsy + vesicles
		- Loss of taste
	- Eczema
		- Pruritus predominant
		- Crusting/exudation
		- Minimal oedema
	- Seborrhoeic dermatitis
		- Scaly erythematous plaques
		- Scalp/pinna/face involvement
		- No EAC inflammation
	- Psoriasis
		- Bilateral erythematous scaly plaques
		- Sharply demarcated
		- Pruritus predominant
	- TMJ disorder
		- No EAC inflammation
		- TMJ pain/crepitus
		- History:
			- Bruxism
			- Trauma
			- Dental procedure
			- Gum chewing
- treatment
	- Systemic antibiotics
		- avoid in uncomplicated
		- Indications
			- Infection extending beyond EAC
				- Periauricular cellulitis
			- High-risk populations
				- Diabetes mellitus
				- Immunocompromised patients
		- Infectious diseases consultation
			- Important due to:
				- Limited anti-pseudomonal options
				- Increasing antibiotic resistance
	- Drug delivery
		- Ear drop administration
			- Affected ear facing upward
			- Fill EAC completely
			- Ideally administered by another person
			- Maintain position for 5 minutes
			- Tragal pumping may help displace air
		- Microsuction
		- Tissue spear
		- Multiple ear toilet sessions
			- Often needed with antibiotics
		- Irrigation
			- Strongly discouraged
				- Especially:
					- Suspected AOM
					- Diabetics
					- Immunocompromised patients
				- Increased NOE risk
	- Wick insertion
		- Needed if severe EAC oedema prevents drop penetration
		- Pope wick
			- Expandable rectangular sponge
			- Recommended size:
				- 15 mm
		- Function
			- Allows antibiotic penetration
			- Expands for better canal contact
		- Lateral wick end
			- Must remain visible
		- Reassess/remove wick
			- After 72 hours
				- Improvement expected by then
	- Analgesia
		- First-line
			- Paracetamol
			- NSAIDs
		- Opioids
			- if severe pain/procedure
		- Topical analgesics
			- Discouraged
		- Steroid component in drops
			- Helps reduce pain via anti-inflammatory effect
- Complications
	- Necrotising otitis externa (NOE)
		- Major complication of AOE
		- Mainly in:
			- Diabetics
			- Immunocompromised patients
		- Can progress to:
			- Skull base osteomyelitis
			- Cranial nerve palsies
			- Meningitis
			- Brain abscess
		- Findings
			- Cortical bone erosion
			- Granulation tissue in EAC
		- Management
			- Hospital admission
			- ENT + infectious diseases involvement
			- Prolonged targeted antibiotics
				- ≥4–6 weeks
