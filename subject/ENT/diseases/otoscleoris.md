Definition
	- Sclerosis of bone surrounding the inner ear
	- Abnormal bony remodeling of the otic capsule
	- Commonly causes conductive hearing loss
- Pathology
	- Primarily affects the footplate of the stapes
	- May later involve cochlea
		- → sensorineural hearing loss
- Epidemiology
	- Most common cause of conductive hearing loss with normal tympanic membrane in adults
	- More common in females 20-30
- Etiology
	- Strong family history
	- Usually autosomal dominant inheritance
		- Variable penetrance
- Clinical Features
	- Progressive conductive hearing loss
	- May be:
		- Unilateral
		- Bilateral
	- Hearing loss may worsen rapidly during pregnancy
	- Initially affects lower frequencies → later progresses to broader frequency loss
	- Paracusis Willisii
		- Hearing better in noisy environments
	- Tinnitus may occur
	- Usually normal tympanic membrane on examination
- Associations
	- Ménière’s disease syndrome
- Investigations
	- Audiometry
		- Conductive hearing loss
		- May show mild sensorineural component

	- Impedance Audiometry
		- Reduced stapedial compliance
		- Abnormal acoustic reflexes

	- Tuning Fork Tests
		- Rinne negative in affected ear
		- Weber lateralizes to affected ear

	- CT temporal bone
		- Occasionally used in atypical cases

- Management

	- Referral
		- Refer to ENT specialist

	- More Effective Treatment
		- Stapedectomy
			- Removal of stapes
			- Vein grafting with insertion of prosthesis

	- Less Effective Treatment
		- Hearing aids
