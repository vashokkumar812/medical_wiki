# Ear 

* Ears  
  * Anatomy  
    * Outer ear  
    * parts  
      * Pinna, external auditory canal and tympanic membrane.  
    * functions  
      * Ceruminous glands produce cerumen (ear wax).  
      * Cerumen and desquamated epithelial cells migrate outwards naturally.  
    * Middle ear  
      * Tympanic cavity, tympanic membrane's inner layer, ossicles and mastoid air cells.  
      * Ossicles: malleus, incus and stapes.  
      * Eustachian tube connects middle ear to nasopharynx.  
      * Chorda tympani (facial nerve branch): carries taste sensation from anterior two-thirds of tongue.  
    * Inner ear (labyrinth)  
      * Vestibular system: balance; semicircular canals detect head movement.  
      * Cochlea: hearing; converts sound vibrations into nerve impulses.  
      * Signals travel through vestibular and cochlear divisions of cranial nerve VIII.

* Examination  
  * Inspect the pinna and surrounding skin.  
  * Examine the mastoid region.  
  * Perform otoscopy.  
  * Perform bedside hearing tests when appropriate.  
    * Rinne test  
    * Weber test  
  * Arrange formal audiometry.  
  * Assess cranial nerves and neurological function when indicated.  
  * Escalate urgently for sudden hearing loss, mastoid swelling, facial weakness or concerning neurological features.  
* Ear examination  
  * Components  
    * Inspection and palpation.  
    * Otoscopy.  
    * Tuning fork tests.  
    * Hearing assessment.  
    * Peripheral vestibular examination.  
    * Lower cranial nerve examination when indicated.  
  * External ear: inspection  
    * Assess pinna position, size and shape.  
    * Look for scars, swelling, inflammation and discharge.  
    * Identify deformities  
      * Accessory auricle: additional cartilage separate from pinna.  
      * Cauliflower ear: deformity due to recurrent trauma and haematomas.  
      * Bat ear: prominent protrusion of the pinna.  
    * Gouty tophi  
      * Firm, pale, non-tender nodules containing urate deposits in auricular cartilage.  
  * Otitis externa  
    * Acute localised  
      * Infection of outer one-third of external auditory canal.  
      * Involves hair follicles in cartilaginous canal.  
      * Furunculosis, usually caused by Staphylococcus aureus.  
    * Acute diffuse (swimmer's ear)  
      * Predisposing factors: moisture, heat, humidity, loss of cerumen and increased canal pH.  
      * Common organism: Pseudomonas aeruginosa.  
      * Symptoms: itching progressing to pain, worsened by pinna movement.  
      * Findings: canal erythema, oedema and possible white, clumpy discharge.  
    * Chronic  
      * Usually due to repeated local irritation or persistent discharge from middle ear infection.  
      * Triggers: cotton swabs, ear picks and other foreign bodies.  
      * Itching predominates over pain.  
      * Findings: scaly, erythematous dermatitis.  
      * Differentiate from psoriasis, atopic dermatitis and seborrhoeic dermatitis.  
    * Malignant (invasive)  
      * Aggressive infection, particularly in elderly patients with diabetes or immunocompromise.  
      * Usually caused by Pseudomonas aeruginosa.  
      * Severe, deep otalgia; swollen, erythematous canal and discharge.  
      * May progress to skull-base osteomyelitis, meningitis and intracranial infection.  
      * Requires urgent specialist assessment.  
  * External ear: palpation  
    * Palpate pinna for swelling, tenderness and nodules.  
    * Gently move the pinna.  
    * Pain on pinna movement suggests external auditory canal inflammation, particularly otitis externa.  
  * Otoscopy  
    * Indications  
      * Ear symptoms.  
      * Upper respiratory tract infection when ear pathology is suspected.  
      * Dizziness, facial weakness or head injury.  
      * Examine both ears.  
    * Technique  
      * Select a comfortable-sized speculum.  
      * Ask the patient to turn the head slightly.  
      * Pull pinna up, out and back in adults to straighten the canal.  
      * Stabilise the otoscope hand against the patient's cheek.  
      * Insert the speculum gently, directing it forward and downward.  
      * Examine the external canal, then the tympanic membrane.  
    * External auditory canal  
      * Normal: no inflammation or tenderness.  
      * Cerumen: white/yellow, translucent and shiny; may be moist, hard or impacted.  
      * Impacted cerumen may obscure the tympanic membrane.  
      * Blood or clear watery fluid after head injury: consider skull-base fracture and CSF leak.  
      * Vesicles around the canal: consider herpes zoster.  
    * Normal tympanic membrane  
      * Pearly grey, ovoid and semitransparent.  
      * Pars flaccida: upper one-fifth.  
      * Pars tensa: lower four-fifths.  
      * Handle of malleus may be visible.  
      * Cone of light is usually visible but is not a sensitive or specific indicator of disease.  
    * Abnormal tympanic membrane  
      * Hyperaemia: may occur in otitis media.  
      * Bulging: suggests middle ear effusion; with acute inflammation, supports acute otitis media.  
      * Retraction: suggests negative middle ear pressure, often due to Eustachian tube dysfunction.  
      * Perforation: document site and appearance.  
    * Pneumatic otoscopy  
      * Use a snug-fitting speculum and rubber insufflation bulb.  
      * Gently increase canal pressure.  
      * Normal: tympanic membrane moves promptly.  
      * Reduced or absent mobility: suggests middle ear effusion.  
  * Hearing assessment  
    * Whisper test  
      * Stand behind the patient to prevent lip-reading.  
      * Test one ear at a time.  
      * Whisper numbers or words, e.g. "mark" or "park".  
      * Mask the opposite ear by gently rubbing the tragus or using an appropriate masking technique.  
      * Abnormal result: arrange formal audiometry.  
    * Rinne test (512 Hz tuning fork)  
      * Place vibrating fork on mastoid process (bone conduction, BC).  
      * When sound is no longer heard, move fork beside the auditory canal (air conduction, AC).  
      * Normal or sensorineural loss: AC \> BC (Rinne positive).  
      * Conductive loss: BC ≥ AC (Rinne negative).  
    * Weber test (512 Hz tuning fork)  
      * Place a vibrating fork at the centre of the forehead.  
      * Normal: sound heard centrally or equally in both ears.  
      * Conductive hearing loss: sound lateralises to the affected ear.  
      * Sensorineural hearing loss: sound lateralises to the better ear.  
    * Formal assessment  
      * Audiometry is indicated when hearing loss is suspected or screening is abnormal.
