# First Trimester Bleeding

- etiology
	- miscarriage
	- ectopic pregnancy
	- Other Causes
		- Implantation bleeding
		- Cervical ectropion, polyp, carcinoma
		- infection - vagina, cervix, uterus
		- Gestational trophoblastic disease (GTD)
			- Consider if markedly elevated β-hCG
			- Consider if suggestive ultrasound findings

- Assessment
	- Haemodynamically Unstable Patient
		- Immediate hospital transfer

		- Important causes
			- Ruptured ectopic pregnancy
			- Incomplete miscarriage with cervical shock
				- Products of conception lodged in cervical os
				- Parasympathetic stimulation
				- Bradycardia
				- Hypotension
			- Massive haemorrhage secondary to miscarriage
		- Management
			- Speculum examination
			- Remove products from cervical os
			- May require
				- Suction curettage
				- Laparoscopy
				- Laparotomy


	- Symptoms suggesting significant blood loss
		- Syncope
		- Chest pain
		- Shortness of breath

	- Symptoms suggesting intraperitoneal bleeding
		- Shoulder tip pain

	- Risk Factors for Ectopic Pregnancy
		- Current IUD use
		- Progesterone-only pill (minipill)
		- Assisted reproduction
		- Previous pelvic infection
		- Previous sexually transmitted infection
		- Previous tubal surgery
		- Previous ectopic pregnancy

	- Risk Factors for Miscarriage
		- Poorly controlled diabetes
		- Thyroid disease
	- if abnormal bleeding outside pregnancy
		- cervical cause

- Examination
	- Uterine Size
		- Fundus palpable above symphysis pubis at approximately 12 weeks
		- Earlier enlargement cause
			- Multiple pregnancy
			- Gestational trophoblastic disease
			- Fibroids
			- Ovarian cyst
	- Speculum Examination
		- Remove tissue from cervical os
		- Send tissue for histopathology
	- Bimanual Examination
	- Pelvic tenderness or cervical motion tenderness
		- Requires urgent specialist assessment
- Investigations
	- β-hCG
		- Normal Pregnancy
			- Increases by ≥50% every 48 hours
			- Up to 6–7 weeks gestation
	
		- Falling β-hCG
			- Indicates non-viable pregnancy
				- Failed intrauterine pregnancy
				- Involuting ectopic pregnancy
		- Plateauing or Slow Rise
			- <50% increase in 48 hours
			- Suggests
				- Ectopic pregnancy
				- Non-viable intrauterine pregnancy
- Transvaginal Ultrasound
	- Preferred imaging modality
	- Gestational Sac
		- Usually visible from 4 weeks + 3 days
	- Assess for
		- Intrauterine pregnancy
		- Ectopic pregnancy
		- Retained products of conception
	- Non-Viable Pregnancy Criteria
		- Mean sac diameter >25 mm
			- No live fetus visible
		- Crown-rump length >7 mm
			- No fetal cardiac activity
			- Confirm after ≥30 seconds observation
		- If uncertain
			- Repeat scan in 1 week
			- Obtain second opinion
	- Ectopic Pregnancy Findings
		- Adnexal mass
			- Most common finding
			- Present in >88%
		- Free pelvic fluid
- Discriminatory Zone
	- Definition
		- β-hCG level above which an intrauterine gestational sac should be visible on TVS
	- Usually
		- 1500–2000 IU/L
	- Interpretation
		- β-hCG <2000 IU/L
			- Repeat TVS and β-hCG in 48–72 hours
		- β-hCG >2000 IU/L
			- No intrauterine pregnancy
			- Adnexal mass and/or free fluid present
				- High probability of ectopic pregnancy
		- β-hCG >2000 IU/L
			- No intrauterine pregnancy
			- No abnormal findings
				- Repeat TVS and β-hCG in 48–72 hours
		- Declining or suboptimally rising β-hCG
			- Non-viable pregnancy
				- Ectopic pregnancy
				- Failed intrauterine pregnancy

- Pregnancy of Unknown Location (PUL)
	- Definition
		- No intrauterine pregnancy seen
		- No ectopic pregnancy seen
		- No retained products seen

	- Possible Outcomes
		- Intrauterine pregnancy
		- Ectopic pregnancy
		- Failed PUL

	- Management
		- Serial β-hCG measurements
		- Repeat TVS
		- Reassess immediately if symptoms worsen

	- Refer for specialist assessment if
		- Plateauing β-hCG
		- New symptoms
		- Worsening symptoms

# Miscarriage

- Definition
	- Pregnancy loss before 20 weeks
	- Or fetal weight <400 g

- Types

	- Threatened Miscarriage
		- Vaginal bleeding before 20 weeks
		- Viable pregnancy remains

	- Inevitable Miscarriage
		- Products of conception expected to pass imminently

	- Incomplete Miscarriage
		- Retained products of conception present

	- Missed Miscarriage
		- Non-viable intrauterine pregnancy on ultrasound
		- No vaginal bleeding

	- Septic Miscarriage
		- Miscarriage with infection

	- Recurrent Miscarriage
		- ≥3 consecutive miscarriages

	- Complete Miscarriage
		- Complete expulsion of products of conception

- Threatened Miscarriage

	- Management
		- Expectant

	- Risks
		- 2.6-fold increased risk of later miscarriage
		- Increased risk of
			- Preterm labour
			- Intrauterine growth restriction

	- Progesterone
		- Insufficient evidence for routine use

- Incomplete / Inevitable / Missed Miscarriage

	- Management Options
		- Expectant
		- Medical
		- Surgical

	- Expectant Management

		- Incomplete miscarriage
			- 60% complete within 2 weeks
			- 90% complete within 6–8 weeks

		- Follow-up
			- Review at 1–2 weeks

			- If pain and bleeding resolved
				- Repeat β-hCG at 3 weeks

			- Persistent positive β-hCG
				- Serial β-hCG
				- Repeat ultrasound

			- No bleeding or pain after 7–14 days
				- Repeat ultrasound
				- Reconsider treatment options

	- Medical Management

		- Misoprostol
			- Prostaglandin E1 analogue

		- Routes
			- Oral
			- Vaginal

		- Should be performed by experienced unit

	- Surgical Management

		- Indications
			- Haemorrhage
			- Sepsis
			- Patient preference
			- Contraindication to medical therapy

		- Complications
			- Anaesthetic complications
			- Haemorrhage
			- Uterine perforation
			- Retained products of conception
			- Endometritis

# Ectopic Pregnancy

- Location

	- Fallopian tube
		- 95%

	- Other sites
		- Cervix
		- Ovary
		- Abdominal cavity
		- Caesarean scar

- Heterotopic Pregnancy
	- Coexisting intrauterine and ectopic pregnancy

- Management

	- Surgery

		- Indications
			- Haemodynamic instability
			- Ruptured ectopic pregnancy
			- Failed methotrexate therapy
			- Contraindication to methotrexate
			- Inability to comply with follow-up
			- Patient preference

		- Preferred approach
			- Laparoscopic surgery

		- Procedures
			- Salpingectomy
			- Salpingostomy

		- Salpingostomy
			- Weekly β-hCG until negative
			- Risk of retained trophoblastic tissue

	- Methotrexate

		- Suitable if
			- Haemodynamically stable
			- Reliable follow-up
			- β-hCG <5000 IU/L
			- Adnexal mass <3.5 cm
			- No fetal cardiac activity

		- Dose
			- 50 mg/m² IM

		- Outcomes
			- Approximately 85% success
			- Approximately 14% require second dose
			- Up to 15% require surgery

	- Future Fertility
		- Similar after
			- Methotrexate
			- Salpingostomy
			- Salpingectomy

# RhD-Negative Women

- Anti-D (RhIg)

	- Dose
		- 250 IU

	- Indications
		- Miscarriage
		- Ectopic pregnancy
		- Termination of pregnancy
		- Chorionic villus sampling

	- Timing
		- Within 72 hours
		- May still provide benefit up to 9–10 days later
