- asynptomatic bacteriuria
	- Definition
		- significant bacteriuria in the absence of signs and symptoms of urinary tract infection (UTI)
	- Etiology
		- origin
			- Usually originates from normal gastrointestinal flora
		- Risk factors
			- Physiological
				- Increasing age
				- Female sex
				- Reduced mobility
			- Neurological disorders
				- Alzheimer disease
				- Parkinson disease
				- Stroke
			- Metabolic disease
				- Diabetes mellitus
					- Neurogenic bladder
					- Microangiopathy
					- Impaired immunity from hyperglycaemia
			- Hepatobiliary disease
				- Primary biliary cirrhosis
			- Urinary tract abnormalities
				- Renal calculi
				- Prostatic enlargement
				- High post-void residual (PVR)
					- \> 180 ml
				- Urinary obstruction/stasis
			- Indwelling urinary catheter
			- Urinary incontinence
			- Chronic constipation
				- Urinary retention
				- Pelvic floor neuropathy
				- Faecal incontinence and perineal soiling
			- Estrogen therapy (association observed)
			- Functional impairmen
	
	- Most Common
		- Organism
			- Escherichia coli
		- Other organisms
			- Klebsiella pneumoniae
			- Proteus mirabilis
			- Enterococcus faecalis
			- Coagulase-negative Staphylococci
			- Group B Streptococcus
		- Institutionalised patients
			- Proteus mirabilis commonly isolated
		- Long-term catheter users
			- Frequently polymicrobial
			- Pseudomonas aeruginosa
			- Morganella morganii
			- Providencia stuartii
	
	- Clinical Features
		- Symptoms
			- No urinary symptoms
				- No dysuria
				- No frequency
				- No urgency
				- No suprapubic pain
			- Often discovered incidentally on urine testing
			- Cognitive impairment limits reliable history
			- Language barriers
			- Multiple comorbidities causing nonspecific symptoms
				- Lethargy
				- Weakness
				- Poor appetite
			- Long-term care residents often investigated because of nonspecific changes
				- Irritability
				- Aggression
				- "Not being themselves"
		- Signs
			- No systemic signs attributable to UTI
			- Fever and leukocytosis less common than in younger adults
	
	- Investigations
		- Urine culture
			- Gold standard
		- Urine dipstick
			- Poor diagnostic test for ASB
			- Useful mainly as a rule-out test
				- Negative nitrite + negative leukocyte esterase → infection unlikely
			- Positive leukocyte esterase correlates poorly with bacteriuria
		- Pyuria
			- Neither sensitive nor specific
			- Common without bacteriuria
			- Particularly unreliable in catheterised patients
		- Urinary cytokines (experimental)
			- IL-6 may have diagnostic value
			- Not routinely available
	
	- Diagnostic Criteria
		- No signs or symptoms of UTI
		- Urine specimen collected to minimise contamination
		- Men
			- One voided specimen
			- Single organism ≥100,000 CFU/mL
		- Women
			- Two consecutive voided specimens
			- Same organism ≥100,000 CFU/mL
		- Catheterised specimen
			- Single organism ≥100 CFU/mL
	
	- Complications
		- No proven increase in mortality
		- No proven increase in long-term renal impairment
		- No proven increase in hypertension
		- Associated with:
			- Acute cystitis in some postmenopausal women
			- Urosepsis in some diabetic patients
		- Causal relationship remains uncertain
	
	- Management
		- Routine antibiotic treatment NOT recommended
		- Antibiotics reduce positive urine cultures
		- Risks of treatment
			- Adverse drug effects
			- Polypharmacy interactions
			- Antimicrobial resistance
			- Treatment failure
			- Clostridioides difficile diarrhoea
	
		- Who Should NOT Be Treated
			- Elderly community dwellers with ASB
			- Institutionalised elderly patients
			- Diabetic women
			- Patients with spinal cord injury
			- Patients with indwelling urinary catheters
			- Any patient without symptoms of UTI
	
		- Indications for Treatment
			- Symptomatic bacteriuria/UTI
				- Acute cystitis symptoms
				- Acute pyelonephritis
				- Bacteraemia with same organism
				- Catheter trauma/obstruction with infection
			- Prior to urological procedures expected to cause mucosal bleeding
			- Persistent bacteriuria after:
				- Clean intermittent catheterisation
				- Genitourinary manipulation
				- Instrumentation with expected mucosal bleeding
			- Pregnancy	
	- Prevention
		- Avoid unnecessary long-term urinary catheters
		- Sterile catheter insertion technique
		- Good catheter care
			- Early blockage detection
			- Regular maintenance
		- Prevent constipation
			- Oral laxatives when appropriate
		- Staff and physician education regarding ASB vs symptomatic UTI
		- Potential Preventive Measures (Evidence Emerging)
			- Intravaginal oestriol
				- Increases Lactobacillus colonisation
				- May reduce uropathogen colonisation
			- Cranberry products
				- May reduce bacteriuria
				- May reduce recurrent UTI risk
				- Optimal dose/duration unclear
			- Methenamine hippurate
				- May reduce bacteriuria
				- May reduce symptomatic UTI
	
	- Prognosis
		- Often persistent without causing symptoms
		- May spontaneously resolve or recur
		- Some evidence suggests colonising E. coli strains may protect against symptomatic UTI


- Urinary Tract Infection (UTI) — AMC High-Yield

	- Definition
		- Infection of the urinary tract
		- Classified as
			- Lower UTI (cystitis)
			- Upper UTI (pyelonephritis)
			- Complicated UTI
			- Catheter-associated UTI (CAUTI)
			- Asymptomatic bacteriuria

- Common Organisms

	- Escherichia coli (≈80–90%) ⭐ Most common
	- Klebsiella
	- Proteus mirabilis
	- Enterococcus
	- Staphylococcus saprophyticus
			- Young sexually active women
	- Pseudomonas
			- Catheters
			- Structural urinary disease

- Risk Factors

	- Female sex
	- Sexual activity
	- Pregnancy
	- Diabetes
	- Urinary obstruction
	- Renal calculi
	- Catheterisation
	- Neurogenic bladder
	- BPH
	- Vesicoureteric reflux
	- Immunosuppression

- Clinical Features

	- Acute uncomplicated cystitis
		- Dysuria
		- Frequency
		- Urgency
		- Suprapubic pain
		- Haematuria
		- No fever
		- No flank pain

	- Acute pyelonephritis
		- Fever
		- Rigors
		- Flank pain
		- CVA tenderness
		- Nausea/vomiting
		- Dysuria ±

	- Urosepsis
		- Fever/hypothermia
		- Hypotension
		- Tachycardia
		- Confusion
		- Septic shock

- Initial Investigations

	- Urine dipstick
		- Leukocyte esterase
		- Nitrites

	- Midstream urine (MSU)
		- Microscopy
		- Culture
		- Sensitivity (MCS)

	- Pregnancy test
			- Women of reproductive age

	- If pyelonephritis/systemically unwell
		- FBC
		- UEC
		- CRP
		- Blood cultures (before antibiotics)

- Imaging

	- Not required for uncomplicated cystitis

	- Renal ultrasound / CT KUB if
		- Suspected obstruction
		- Stone
		- Abscess
		- Persistent fever >48–72 hours
		- Recurrent pyelonephritis
		- Male UTI
		- Complicated infection

---

- Acute Uncomplicated Cystitis (Non-pregnant Female)

	- First-line antibiotics (AMC favourites)

		- ⭐ Nitrofurantoin 100 mg BD for 5 days
			- Avoid if eGFR <30
			- Not for pyelonephritis

		- ⭐ Trimethoprim 300 mg nocte for 3 days
			- Only if local resistance low/susceptible

		- Cefalexin
			- Alternative

	- Supportive
		- Oral fluids
		- Simple analgesia

---

- Acute Pyelonephritis

	- Admit if
		- Septic
		- Pregnant
		- Unable to tolerate oral therapy
		- Immunocompromised
		- Obstruction
		- Severe illness

	- Initial treatment
		- IV fluids
		- Blood cultures
		- Urine culture

	- Antibiotics
		- IV Ceftriaxone ⭐
		- OR IV Gentamicin
		- Tailor to cultures

	- Step-down
		- Oral antibiotics
		- Total duration 7–14 days

---

- Complicated UTI

	- Causes
		- Male
		- Pregnancy
		- Catheter
		- Stone
		- Obstruction
		- Diabetes
		- CKD
		- Structural abnormality

	- Management
		- Urine culture before antibiotics
		- Broad-spectrum antibiotics initially
		- Correct underlying cause
		- Relieve obstruction if present

---

- Catheter-Associated UTI (CAUTI)

	- Diagnose only if
		- Symptoms present
		- Positive urine culture

	- Management
		- Replace/remove catheter ⭐
		- Send urine from new catheter
		- Antibiotics if symptomatic

---

- Asymptomatic Bacteriuria

	- Treat ONLY if
		- Pregnancy ⭐
		- Before invasive urological procedures ⭐

	- Do NOT treat
		- Elderly
		- Diabetics
		- Catheterised patients
		- Nursing home residents

---

- UTI in Pregnancy

	- Always perform urine culture

	- Treat
		- Asymptomatic bacteriuria
		- Acute cystitis
		- Pyelonephritis

	- Safe antibiotics
		- ⭐ Cefalexin
		- ⭐ Nitrofurantoin
			- Avoid near term (38–42 weeks) and in G6PD deficiency
		- ⭐ Amoxicillin (if susceptible)

	- Avoid
		- Fluoroquinolones
		- Tetracyclines

	- Pyelonephritis
		- Admit
		- IV Ceftriaxone ⭐

	- Repeat urine culture
		- 1–2 weeks after treatment (test of cure)

---

- UTI in Men

	- Consider complicated UTI
	- Send urine culture
	- Assess for prostatitis
	- Longer antibiotic course
			- Usually 7–14 days

---

- Recurrent UTI

	- Definition
		- ≥2 UTIs in 6 months
		- OR
		- ≥3 UTIs/year

	- Management
		- Behavioural measures
		- Post-coital voiding
		- Adequate hydration
		- Vaginal oestrogen (postmenopausal)
		- Low-dose prophylactic antibiotics if indicated

---

- Acute Bacterial Prostatitis

	- Features
		- Fever
		- Dysuria
		- Pelvic/perineal pain
		- Tender boggy prostate

	- Investigations
		- Urine culture
		- Blood cultures if septic

	- Avoid
		- Vigorous prostate massage

	- Antibiotics
		- Ciprofloxacin ⭐
		- Trimethoprim
		- IV therapy if septic

---

- Drug of Choice Summary (AMC High Yield)

| Condition | Drug of Choice |
|------------|----------------|
| Uncomplicated cystitis | ⭐ Nitrofurantoin |
| Alternative uncomplicated cystitis | Trimethoprim |
| Pregnancy cystitis | ⭐ Cefalexin or Nitrofurantoin* |
| Pregnancy pyelonephritis | ⭐ IV Ceftriaxone |
| Acute pyelonephritis | ⭐ IV Ceftriaxone |
| Severe Gram-negative UTI | Gentamicin +/− Ceftriaxone |
| Acute bacterial prostatitis | ⭐ Ciprofloxacin |
| Catheter-associated UTI | Replace catheter + culture-directed antibiotics |
| Asymptomatic bacteriuria (pregnancy) | ⭐ Treat (Cefalexin/Nitrofurantoin) |
| Asymptomatic bacteriuria (non-pregnant) | ❌ No treatment |

> *Avoid nitrofurantoin near term (38–42 weeks) and in G6PD deficiency.

---

- AMC Pearls ⭐

	- E. coli = most common cause.
	- Nitrofurantoin does **not** treat pyelonephritis (poor renal tissue penetration).
	- Always culture urine before antibiotics in complicated UTI or pyelonephritis.
	- Treat asymptomatic bacteriuria **only** in pregnancy or before urological procedures.
	- All UTIs in men are considered complicated until proven otherwise.
	- Pyelonephritis in pregnancy requires admission and IV antibiotics.
	- Replace catheter before collecting urine in suspected catheter-associated UTI.
