- Tuberculosis (TB) Testing — AMC High-Yield

	- Types
		- Active TB
			- Symptomatic
			- Infectious (pulmonary TB)
			- Chest X-ray usually abnormal
			- Sputum microscopy/culture may be positive
			- Requires multidrug anti-TB therapy
		- Latent TB Infection (LTBI)
			- Asymptomatic
			- Not infectious
			- Chest X-ray usually normal
			- Diagnosed by immune response (TST/IGRA)
			- Treated to prevent reactivation

---

- When to Suspect TB

	- Exposure risk
		- Elderly (especially migrants)
		- Aboriginal Australians
		- Torres Strait Islanders
		- Migrants/refugees from endemic countries
			- India ⭐
			- China
			- Sub-Saharan Africa
		- Healthcare workers from endemic countries
		- Household contact of TB patient

	- Reactivation risk
		- Recent infection (<2 years) ⭐
		- HIV infection ⭐ (highest risk)
		- Immunosuppressive therapy
			- Corticosteroids
			- TNF-α inhibitors
			- Chemotherapy
		- Organ transplantation

---

- Clinical Features Suggesting Active TB

	- Cough (>2–3 weeks)
	- Fever
	- Night sweats
	- Weight loss
	- Haemoptysis
	- Extrapulmonary symptoms
			- Lymphadenopathy
			- Pleural effusion
			- Bone/joint pain
			- Meningitis
			- Genitourinary disease

---

- Investigation of Suspected Active TB

	- Chest X-ray
		- First imaging investigation
		- Typical findings
			- Upper lobe infiltrates
			- Cavitation ⭐
			- Fibrosis
		- HIV patients
			- May have atypical or even normal CXR
		- Cannot reliably distinguish active from latent TB

	- Sputum examination
		- Collect 3 early-morning specimens ⭐
		- Refrigerate if delayed
		- Gastric aspirate in children
		- Induced sputum with hypertonic saline if needed
		- Bronchoscopy rarely required

---

- Sputum Tests

	- Acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smear
		- Rapid (<24 hours)
		- Ziehl-Neelsen stain
		- Detects acid-fast bacilli
		- Approximately 50% sensitivity
		- Positive smear requires confirmation

	- Culture ⭐ (Gold standard)
		- Most sensitive investigation
		- Confirms diagnosis
		- Allows drug susceptibility testing
		- Liquid culture faster than solid media

	- PCR (Xpert MTB/RIF) ⭐
		- Detects MTB DNA
		- Rapid diagnosis
		- Detects rifampicin resistance simultaneously
		- High sensitivity in smear-positive disease
		- Lower sensitivity in smear-negative disease

---

- Drug Susceptibility Testing

	- Performed on cultured isolate
	- First-line drugs tested
		- Isoniazid
		- Rifampicin
		- Ethambutol
		- Pyrazinamide
		- Streptomycin
	- MDR-TB
		- Resistant to
			- Isoniazid
			- Rifampicin ⭐
	- Resistant TB requires prolonged therapy

---

- Diagnosis of Latent TB

	- Immune-based tests
		- Mantoux (Tuberculin Skin Test; TST)
		- Interferon-Gamma Release Assay (IGRA)

	- Become positive
		- Approximately 4–6 weeks after infection

---

- Mantoux Test (TST)

	- Intradermal tuberculin injection
	- Measures delayed hypersensitivity reaction
	- Read at
		- 48–72 hours ⭐
	- Positive result
		- Suggests previous TB exposure
	- False positives
		- BCG vaccination
		- Environmental mycobacteria
	- Preferred in
		- Children <2 years ⭐

---

- Interferon-Gamma Release Assay (IGRA)

	- Blood test
	- Measures IFN-γ released by sensitised T lymphocytes
	- Examples
		- Quantiferon Gold
		- T-SPOT
	- Advantages
		- Not affected by BCG vaccination ⭐
		- One visit only
		- More specific than TST
		- Preferred in immunosuppressed patients
	- Limitations
		- Does not distinguish active from latent TB
		- Negative result does not exclude active TB

---

- TST vs IGRA (High-Yield)

	- TST
		- Affected by BCG
		- False positives from environmental mycobacteria
		- Two visits required
		- Preferred in children <2 years

	- IGRA
		- Not affected by BCG ⭐
		- One visit only
		- Better specificity
		- Preferred after BCG vaccination
		- Preferred in immunosuppressed adults

---

- Interpretation of TST / IGRA

	- Positive
		- Previous TB infection
		- Current latent TB
		- Active TB possible
		- Always exclude active disease with
			- Chest X-ray
			- Sputum microscopy/culture if symptomatic

	- Negative
		- Does NOT exclude active TB ⭐

---

- Extrapulmonary TB

	- Diagnosis
		- Tissue biopsy
		- Microscopy
		- Culture ⭐
		- Histopathology

	- Histology
		- Caseating granulomas ⭐
		- Necrotising granulomas
		- Acid-fast bacilli

---

- Additional Tests Before Treatment

	- HIV testing ⭐
	- Liver function tests
	- Hepatitis B serology
	- Hepatitis C serology
	- Full blood count
	- Drug susceptibility testing
	- Consider Vitamin D level

---

- Not Recommended

	- TB serology ❌
		- Poor sensitivity
		- Poor specificity

---

- AMC Diagnostic Approach

	- Suspected active pulmonary TB
		- Chest X-ray
		- 3 early-morning sputum samples
			- AFB smear
			- Culture ⭐
			- PCR (Xpert MTB/RIF)

	- Positive TST/IGRA
		- Exclude active TB
			- Chest X-ray
			- Sputum if symptomatic

	- BCG-vaccinated patient
		- Prefer IGRA ⭐

	- Child <2 years
		- Prefer Mantoux ⭐

	- Immunosuppressed patient
		- Prefer IGRA

	- Suspected extrapulmonary TB
		- Biopsy
		- Histology
		- Culture

---

- AMC Pearls ⭐

	- Culture = Gold standard for active TB
	- PCR (Xpert MTB/RIF) = Rapid diagnosis + rifampicin resistance
	- 3 early-morning sputum specimens increase diagnostic yield
	- TST affected by BCG; IGRA is not
	- TST/IGRA cannot differentiate active from latent TB
	- Positive TST/IGRA → Always exclude active TB before treating LTBI
	- Negative TST/IGRA does NOT exclude active TB
	- MDR-TB = Resistance to isoniazid + rifampicin
	- HIV is the strongest risk factor for TB reactivation
	- Greatest risk of reactivation = First 2 years after infection
