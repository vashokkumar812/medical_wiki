- Common clinical complaint
	
- Most cases due to:
	
	- GERD
		
	- Gastritis
		
	- Peptic ulcer disease
		
- Rare but important:
	
	- Oesophageal cancer
		
	- Gastric cancer
		
	- MALT lymphoma
            
- **Epidemiology**
    
    - Gastric cancer
        
        - ↓ incidence (↓ H. pylori)
            
    - Oesophageal cancer
        
        - ↑ incidence
            
    - Still less common than colorectal cancer
        
    - High mortality:
        
        - Often diagnosed late
            
- **Risk factors**
    
    - Oesophageal cancer
        
        - Acid reflux
            
        - Alcohol
            
        - Smoking
            
        - Geographic factors
            
    - Gastric cancer / MALT lymphoma
        
        - **Helicobacter pylori infection**
            

---

- **Clinical challenge**
    
    - Differentiate:
        
        - Benign dyspepsia
            
        - Serious pathology
            
    - No effective population screening
        
    - → Early diagnosis critical
        

---

- **Alarm features (indications for endoscopy)**
    
    - Age >40 with new symptoms
        
    - Dysphagia
        
    - Weight loss
        
    - Early satiety
        
    - Iron deficiency anaemia
        
    - Palpable epigastric mass
        
    - Liver enlargement
        
    - Poor response to PPI
        

---

- **Investigation strategy**
    
    - **With alarm features**
        
        - → **Urgent upper GI endoscopy**
            
    - **Without alarm features (<40 years)**
        
        - Trial:
            
            - **PPI therapy**
                
        - If persistent:
            
            - → Endoscopy
                
    - **Suspected gastritis/duodenitis**
        
        - Test:
            
            - **H. pylori (non-invasive)**
                
        - Treat:
            
            - Eradication therapy
                
        - Reassess
            

---

- **Other investigations**
    
    - Endoscopy
        
        - Best initial test
            
    - CT scan
        
        - Useful for:
            
            - Linitis plastica
                
            - Pancreatic disease
                
    - Ultrasound
        
        - Biliary disease
            

---

- **Differential diagnoses**
    
    - Gallstones → Ultrasound
        
    - Cardiac disease → Stress ECG
        
    - Liver disease → LFTs
        
    - Pancreatic disease → CT
        

---

- **Key takeaway**
    
    - **Alarm features = immediate endoscopy**
        
    - **No alarm features = treat first, investigate if no response**




# gastric carcinoma
- Gastric cancer
	- Major cause of global cancer morbidity and mortality
	- 2nd most frequently diagnosed cancer worldwide (historical data)
	- Accounts for
		- 9.9% of new cancers
		- 12.1% of cancer deaths
	- More than 50% of cases occur in
		- China
		- Japan
	- Much lower incidence in Western countries
	- Poor prognosis overall

- Risk factors
	- Increasing age
	- Male sex
	- Helicobacter pylori infection
	- Smoking
	- Pernicious anaemia
	- Chronic atrophic gastritis
	- Family history
	- Diet high in
		- Salted foods
		- Smoked foods
	- Diet low in
		- Fruits
		- Vegetables

- Curative treatment
	- Surgical resection remains the mainstay
	- Overall 5-year survival
		- 15–35%
	- Locoregional recurrence after curative surgery
		- ~87%
	- All resectable cases should be managed by a multidisciplinary team

- Surgical principles
	- Aim
		- Complete tumour resection with negative margins
	- Recommended margin
		- ≥5 cm

- Total gastrectomy
	- Standard treatment for
		- Proximal two-thirds gastric cancers
		- Extensive distal cancers
	- Reconstruction
		- Roux-en-Y oesophagojejunostomy
	- Benefits
		- Reduces bile reflux
	- Splenectomy
		- May be indicated for fundal lesions

- Distal (subtotal) gastrectomy
	- Used for
		- Antral cancers
		- Pyloric cancers
	- Advantages over total gastrectomy
		- Better anastomotic healing
		- Residual stomach acts as reservoir
		- Larger meal tolerance

- Proximal gastrectomy
	- Generally avoided
	- Associated with severe alkaline bile reflux

- Lymphadenectomy
	- D1
		- Limited lymph node dissection
	- D2
		- Extended lymph node dissection
	- Japanese centres favour D2 dissections
	- D2 associated with
		- Higher morbidity
		- Higher mortality
		- Longer hospital stay
	- In specialised centres
		- Spleen-preserving and pancreas-preserving D2 dissections may improve long-term survival

- Endoscopic approaches
	- Endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR)
		- Useful for dysplasia
		- Useful for carcinoma in situ
		- Role in invasive cancer remains limited

- Post-gastrectomy complications
	- Early satiety
		- Due to loss of gastric reservoir
	- Early dumping syndrome
		- Postprandial abdominal pain
		- Diarrhoea
		- Due to rapid hyperosmolar gastric emptying
	- Late dumping syndrome
		- Reactive hypoglycaemia
		- Sweating
		- Tremor
		- Weakness
	- Diarrhoea
		- Bacterial overgrowth
		- Malabsorption
		- Steatorrhoea
	- Bile reflux
		- Epigastric pain
		- Heartburn
		- Regurgitation

- Nutritional deficiencies after gastrectomy
	- Vitamin B12 deficiency
		- Loss of intrinsic factor
		- Lifelong monitoring required
		- IM B12 every 3 months
	- Iron deficiency
	- Calcium deficiency
	- Vitamin D deficiency
	- Metabolic bone disease

- Adjuvant treatment
	- Postoperative chemoradiotherapy
		- Improves overall survival
		- Increased acute toxicity
	- Perioperative chemotherapy
		- Preoperative + postoperative chemotherapy
		- Improves 5-year survival
		- Example regimen
			- ECF
				- Epirubicin
				- Cisplatin
				- Fluorouracil

- Palliative treatment
	- Goals
		- Symptom control
		- Survival prolongation
		- Quality of life improvement

- Management of bleeding
	- Severe haemodynamic instability
		- Surgery
		- Angiographic embolisation
	- Less severe bleeding
		- External beam radiotherapy
		- Often effective

- Gastric outlet obstruction
	- Palliative gastrojejunostomy
	- Palliative distal gastrectomy
	- Endoluminal stenting
		- Less invasive option

- Metastatic disease
	- Consider palliative chemotherapy
	- Benefits
		- Improved survival
		- Symptom control
			- Pain
			- Nausea
			- Fatigue
			- Appetite loss

- Common chemotherapy regimens
	- ECF
		- Epirubicin
		- Cisplatin
		- Fluorouracil
	- ECX
		- Epirubicin
		- Cisplatin
		- Capecitabine
	- FAM
		- Fluorouracil
		- Adriamycin
		- Mitomycin
	- ELF
		- Etoposide
		- Leucovorin
		- Fluorouracil

- Newer agents
	- Taxanes
		- Paclitaxel
		- Docetaxel
	- Oxaliplatin
	- Irinotecan
	- Targeted therapies
		- Bevacizumab
		- Cetuximab
		- Erlotinib

- AMC MCQ Pearls
	- Most important risk factor tested = Helicobacter pylori
	- Curative treatment = surgical resection
	- Proximal gastric cancer → total gastrectomy
	- Distal/antral gastric cancer → distal subtotal gastrectomy
	- Early dumping = diarrhoea + abdominal pain after meals
	- Late dumping = reactive hypoglycaemia after meals
	- Lifelong vitamin B12 replacement required after total gastrectomy
	- Roux-en-Y reconstruction reduces bile reflux
	- Gastric cancer commonly presents late and carries poor prognosis
	- Multidisciplinary management is standard of care
