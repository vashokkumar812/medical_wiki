- Nasal anatomy
	- Structural thirds
		- Upper third
			- Nasal bones
			- Frontal bone
		- Middle third
			- Septal cartilage
			- Upper lateral cartilages
		- Lower third
			- Fibrofatty tissue
			- Lower lateral/alar cartilages

	- Neurovascular supply
		- Nerve supply
			- Trigeminal nerve
				- Ophthalmic division (V1)
				- Maxillary division (V2)

		- Blood supply
			- Internal carotid system
			- External carotid system

		- Venous drainage
			- Facial vein
				- Anterior
			- Pterygoid plexus
			- Pharyngeal plexus
				- Posterior

		- Clinical significance
			- Valveless venous system
			- Risk of retrograde intracranial infection

- Important point
	- Soft tissue oedema develops within hours
	- May obscure fracture

- Investigations
	- Generally not required
		- X-ray

		- Blood tests

		- CT scan

- Cartilage deformity
	- Features
		- Nasal bones remain midline
		- Septal cartilage deformity present

	- Management
		- Not reducible acutely
		- Exclude septal haematoma
		- Persistent obstruction >1 month
			- ENT referral
			- Septoplasty

- Uncomplicated nasal fracture
	- Management
		- Closed reduction

	- Timing
		- After swelling subsides
		- Within 10–14 days

	- Referral
		- ENT
		- Maxillofacial surgery

	- Anaesthesia
		- Local anaesthesia
		- General anaesthesia
		- Similar success rates

	- Local anaesthetic technique
		- Lignocaine + [[adrenaline]]
		- Injection sites
			- Root of nose
			- Lateral nasal bones

	- Remote settings
		- Skilled GP may perform closed reduction under local anaesthesia

	- Severe injuries
		- Immediate ED referral
			- Gross deformity
			- Compound fracture

- Paediatric nasal fractures
	- Characteristics
		- Incomplete ossification
		- Higher cartilage proportion
		- Greenstick fractures

	- Reduction timing
		- 3–5 days after injury

	- Requires early ENT referral

	- Frail/dementia patients
		- Minor cosmetic deformity
			- Consider no intervention

- Complications of nasal trauma
	- Lacerations
		- Management
			- Irrigation
			- Debridement
			- Closure

		- Sutures
			- 6-0 non-absorbable monofilament

		- Technique
			- Small bites

			- Cartilage involvement
			- Mucosal involvement
			- Deep lacerations

		- Alternative
			- Tissue adhesive

		- Other
			- Update tetanus status

	- Epistaxis
		- First aid
			- Sit upright
			- Lean forward
			- Pinch nostrils
			- Maintain pressure 10–20 min

		- Avoid
			- Tilting head backward

		- Adjuncts
			- Vasoconstrictor
				- Example
					- Cophenylcaine

		- ED referral if
			- Persistent bleeding
			- Haemodynamic instability
			- Anticoagulant use

- Nasal packing
	- Technique
		- Pack horizontally along nasal floor
		- Never pack superiorly
			- Risk intracranial placement in skull base fracture

	- Commercial packs
		- Merocel
			- Expands with saline
			- Used for anterior packing
		- Rapid Rhino
			- Balloon pack
			- Inflated with air
			- More comfortable

	- Alternatives
		- Vaseline ribbon gauze
		- Tranexamic acid pledgets

	- Posterior epistaxis
		- Foley catheter insertion
		- Inflate balloon in nasopharynx
		- Pack anterior cavity

	- Antibiotics required for all packing
		- Examples
			- Amoxicillin
			- Cephalexin
		- Purpose
			- Prevent toxic shock syndrome

	- Pack duration
		- 3–5 days
		- ENT admission/referral required

- Septal haematoma
	- Incidence
		- ~2% of nasal injuries

	- Pathophysiology
		- Blood between
			- Septal cartilage
			- Mucoperichondrium
		- Leads to
			- Cartilage ischaemia
			- Saddle nose deformity

	- Examination findings
		- Bilateral septal swelling
		- Nasal obstruction
		- Boggy swelling on palpation
		- Septal deviation is firm

	- Management
		- ENT emergency
		- Urgent drainage
		- IV antibiotics
		- ED referral

- Septal abscess
	- Delayed complication

	- Symptoms
		- Progressive nasal obstruction
		- Severe pain
		- Fever
		- [[headache]]
		- Purulent discharge

	- Risks
		- Meningitis
		- Cavernous sinus thrombosis
		- Intracranial abscess

	- Management
		- IV antibiotics
		- Urgent drainage
		- Hospital referral

- CSF rhinorrhoea
	- Cause
		- Cribriform plate fracture

	- Presentation
		- Clear watery rhinorrhoea
		- Positional
		- Worse on leaning forward

	- Onset
		- 2 days to 3 months after trauma

	- Investigation
		- Beta-2 transferrin
			- Specific for CSF

	- Management
		- Emergency referral
		- Hospital transfer with
			- ENT
			- Neurosurgery

- Key AMC clinical pearls
	- Always exclude septal haematoma
	- X-rays not useful
	- CT only if facial fracture suspected
	- Closed reduction within 10–14 days
	- Paediatric reduction within 3–5 days
	- CSF rhinorrhoea
			- Beta-2 transferrin test
	- All nasal packing requires antibiotics
