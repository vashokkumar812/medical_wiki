- Red flag features
    - Age <6 months
    - Bilious vomiting
    - Haematemesis
    - Diarrhoea >10 days
    - Severe abdominal pain
    - Refusal to walk
    - Significant GI/surgical history
        - Short gut
        - Hirschsprung disease
        - Ileostomy
    - Complex comorbidities
        - Renal disease
        - Cardiac disease
    - Post-organ transplant
    - Immunocompromised state
    - Re-presentation/failure of standard therapy

- clinical features
    - Vomiting may precede diarrhoea by 24–48 hours
    - Vomiting without diarrhoea
        - Consider alternative causes
	- Blood or mucus in stool
		- Suggests significant inflammation
			- Bacterial infection
			- IBD
	- Examination
	    - Examination may be normal
	    - Generalised abdominal tenderness common

- Investigations
	- Uncomplicated gastroenteritis
		- Usually no investigations
	- Blood glucose/ketones/electrolytes
	    - All children needing IV fluids
	        - Check electrolytes/glucose before fluids
	        - Repeat within 24 hours if IV fluids continue
	    - Investigate if
	        - Severe dehydration
	        - Profuse/prolonged losses
	        - Altered conscious state
	        - Hypertonic fluid intake
	            - Sports drinks
	            - Polyjoule
	            - NG feeds
	        - Prolonged hypotonic intake
	            - Diluted formula
	            - Water-only rehydration
	        - Complex medical/surgical disease
	        - Diuretic use
	        - Risk accelerated starvation/ketosis
	    - Ketotic hypoglycaemia
	        - May occur with vomiting
	        - Especially younger children
	- Stool testing
	    - Not routinely recommended
	    - Consider if
	        - Bloody diarrhoea in unwell child/young infant
	        - Returned traveller with symptoms >10 days
	        - Immunocompromised patient with fever
- Treatment
    - Usually self-limiting
    - viral or bacterial cause : 
	    - mostly no treatment difference
    - Main management
        - Encourage fluids
        - Monitor hydration
    - Failure of initial treatment
        - Reconsider diagnosis/management
	- Oral rehydration
	
	    - Use oral rehydration solution (ORS)
	        - Gastrolyte
	        - Hydralyte
	        - Pedialyte
	
	    - Mild dehydration refusing ORS
	        - Flavoured ice blocks
	        - Diluted apple juice
	
	    - Stop feed fortification
	        - Extra formula scoops
	        - Poly-Joule
	
	    - Breastfeeding
	        - Continue breastfeeding
	        - More frequent feeds
	        - Supplement ORS if needed
	
	- Escalation of rehydration
	
	    - If oral rehydration fails
	        - Rapid NG rehydration
	        - Slow NG rehydration
	
	    - NG intolerance
	        - IV rehydration if clinically dehydrated
	
	    - Older children
	        - IV fluids may be preferred if NG unsuitable
	
	- Medications
	
	- Ondansetron
	    - Supports hydration therapy
	    - Use in >6 months with persistent nausea/vomiting
	
	- Additional medication guidance
	    - Other antiemetics
	        - Not routinely recommended
	        - Side effects common
	
	    - Response to antiemetics
	        - Does not confirm diagnosis
	
	    - Not recommended
	        - Antispasmodics
	        - Antidiarrhoeals
	
	    - Probiotics
	        - Insufficient evidence
	
	    - Antibiotics
	        - Reserved for selected cases
	            - Enteritis-associated sepsis
	            - Salmonella Typhi
	            - Non-typhoidal Salmonella
	                - <3 months
	                - Immunocompromised
	                - Sepsis
	            - Severe Clostridioides difficile infection
	
	- Ongoing management
	    - Resume normal diet after rehydration
	
	    - Lactose restriction
	        - Not routinely advised
	        - Consider only if stool frequency worsens
	
	    - Diarrhoea may persist after rehydration

- Discharge criteria
    - Vomiting reduced
    - Adequate oral intake tolerated

- Consult paediatrics when
    - Failed initial rehydration
    - Significant ongoing losses
    - Complex comorbidities
    - Electrolyte abnormalities
    - Unclear diagnosis
    - Red flag features

- Transfer indications
    - Severe electrolyte disturbance
    - Shock requiring ≥40 mL/kg fluid boluses
