- Child involved in high-energy trauma
	- Assess mechanism
		- MVC with rollover/ejection/lateral impact/fatality
		- Pedestrian vs car
		- Fall >3 m
		- Crush injury
		→ High suspicion for pelvic fracture

- Primary survey
	- Assess haemodynamic status
		- Tachycardia
		- Hypotension
		- Prolonged CRT
		- Reduced GCS
		→ Suggests haemorrhagic shock

	- Examine pelvis
		- Bruising/deformity/asymmetry
		- Pelvic tenderness
		- Blood at urethral meatus
		- Rectal/vaginal bleeding
		- Scrotal bruising

- Suspected unstable pelvic fracture?
	- Yes
		- Immediate management
			- Manual pelvic stabilisation
			- Apply pelvic binder urgently
				- Position at greater trochanters
				- NOT iliac crests
			- Begin trauma resuscitation
			- Apply binder before intubation if required
			- Avoid repeated pelvic compression (“springing”)

		- Additional adjunct
			- Tie feet in figure-of-eight position

		- Imaging
			- AP pelvic X-ray
			- FAST
				- Limited utility in children
			- CT
				- Only after initial stabilisation
				- If ongoing instability

		- Persistent haemodynamic instability despite binder/resuscitation?
			- Yes
				- Urgent angiography and/or operating theatre
				- Transfer to major trauma centre

	- No
		- Continue trauma assessment
		- Evaluate for associated injuries

- Secondary survey
	- Look for associated injuries
		- Abdominal
		- GU
		- Spinal
		- Thoracic
		- Intracranial

- Pelvic binder removal
	- Only after orthopaedic consultation
	- Remove in monitored resuscitation-capable area
	- Clinical deterioration after removal?
		- Reapply binder
		- Repeat imaging

- Complications of prolonged binder use
	- Pressure sores
	- Skin abrasions

- Transfer indications
	- Major trauma
	- Persistent haemodynamic instability
	- Requirement for specialist trauma/orthopaedic care
	- Care beyond local capability
