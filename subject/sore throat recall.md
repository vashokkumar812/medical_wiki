**

Q85. Child having fever, rash on face chest and back, rash is rough with B/L conjunctivitis, with red throat?

1. Scarlet fever
    

**
![[Pasted image 20260617015356.png]]

- Sore throat
  - Most common cause
    - Viral pharyngitis
  - Most common bacterial cause
    - Group A Streptococcus (GAS)
  - GAS
    - Uncommon <4 years
    - Usually self-limiting
    - Difficult to distinguish clinically from viral infection
    - Empiric antibiotics not routinely indicated
  - High-risk ARF patients
    - Treat any sore throat with antibiotics
    - Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander
    - Previous ARF/RHD
    - Living in high ARF prevalence communities

- GAS complications
  - Non-suppurative
    - Acute rheumatic fever
    - Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
  - Suppurative
    - Peritonsillar abscess (Quinsy)
    - Retropharyngeal abscess
    - Parapharyngeal abscess


- Viral symptoms
    - Cough
    - Coryza
    - Conjunctivitis
    - Oral ulcers
    - Diarrhoea
    - Viral rash

- Examination
  - Hepatosplenomegaly
    - EBV
    - CMV
- Scarlet fever features
	- Fever + sore throat + pharyngitis  
	- Diffuse blanching erythematous "sandpaper" rash  
	- Starts neck/chest → spreads to trunk & limbs  
	- More prominent in skin folds  
	- Pastia lines  
	- Linear petechiae in flexural creases  
	- Flushed face with circumoral pallor  
	- Strawberry tongue  
	- Initially white → later bright red


- Investigations
  - Not routinely required
  - Throat swab
    - Not routinely recommended
  - Streptococcal serology
    - No role
  - Hepatosplenomegaly
    - FBE
    - EBV serology
    - ± Monospot
  - Suspected abscess
    - Appropriate imaging

- Treatment
  - Supportive
    - Simple analgesia
    - Hydration
    - Corticosteroid if severe pain not responding to analgesia
  - Antibiotics
    - High-risk ARF patients
    - Confirmed/highly suspected GAS when indicated
    - Not routine for all sore throats

- Peritonsillar abscess (Quinsy)
  - Features
    - Fever
    - Severe sore throat
    - Odynophagia
    - Dysphagia
    - Drooling
    - Hot potato voice
    - Trismus
    - Peritonsillar swelling
    - Uvula deviation
  - Management
    - ENT referral
    - Drainage
    - IV antibiotics

- Retropharyngeal/parapharyngeal abscess
  - Features
    - Fever
    - Neck pain/swelling
    - Dysphagia
    - Torticollis
    - Limited neck extension
    - Retropharyngeal bulge
  - Investigation
    - CT neck with IV contrast (preferred)
    - Lateral neck X-ray may assist but does not exclude
  - Management
    - ENT referral
    - IV antibiotics

- Epiglottitis / bacterial tracheitis
  - Features
    - Abrupt onset
    - Toxic appearance
    - High fever
    - Stridor
    - Drooling
    - Dysphagia
    - Odynophagia
    - Hot potato voice
    - Tripod position
    - Neck extension
  - Risk factor
    - Unimmunised against HiB
  - Management
    - Minimal handling
    - Do not examine throat
    - Early anaesthetics + ENT + ICU
    - Secure airway first
    - IV antibiotics
