- Neonatal Sepsis: Antimicrobial Guidelines

	- Key Principles
		- Antibiotics should be administered within 1 hour of decision to treat
		- Early-onset sepsis (EOS)
			- <48 hours of age
		- Late-onset sepsis (LOS)
			- >48 hours of age
		- Empiric therapy initially
		- Tailor antibiotics according to microbiology results
		- Avoid unnecessary, prolonged, or broad-spectrum antibiotic use
			- Reduces antimicrobial resistance
			- Reduces neonatal morbidity and mortality

	- Epidemiology
		- Neonatal sepsis
			- Significant cause of morbidity
			- Mortality up to 20% in high-income countries
		- Mortality inversely related to gestational age
			- Higher risk in preterm infants

	- Early-Onset Sepsis (EOS)
		- Definition
			- <48 hours of age
		- Source
			- Maternal genital tract organisms
		- Common pathogens
			- Group B Streptococcus (GBS)
			- Escherichia coli
		- GBS
			- More common in term infants
		- E. coli
			- More common in preterm infants

	- Late-Onset Sepsis (LOS)
		- Definition
			- >48 hours of age
		- Source
			- Community-acquired
			- Hospital-acquired
		- Pathogens differ from EOS

	- High-Risk Groups
		- Preterm infants
		- Very low birth weight (VLBW) infants

	- Important Pathogens
		- Group B Streptococcus
		- Escherichia coli
		- Herpes simplex virus (HSV)
			- Consider in all septic neonates
			- Very low threshold for empiric aciclovir
		- Listeria monocytogenes
			- Rare
			- Serious neonatal infection
		- Candida species
			- Important in selected patient groups

	- Investigations
		- FBE
		- CRP or procalcitonin

		- Blood cultures
			- Peripheral culture
			- Culture from all central venous access lumens
			- Minimum volume
				- ≥0.5 mL
			- Preferred volume
				- 1 mL if weight <1.5 kg
				- 1.5 mL if weight 1.5–5 kg
			- Minimise contamination

		- Lumbar puncture
			- Particularly important in LOS
			- Meningitis in EOS uncommon
				- Approximately 1–2%
			- Perform if high suspicion
				- Maternal chorioamnionitis
				- PPROM
				- Neurological signs in infant
				- Positive blood cultures

		- Chest X-ray
			- If respiratory infection suspected
			- Not required if clinical picture clearly consistent with bronchiolitis

	- Treatment Principles
		- Do not delay antibiotics while awaiting investigations
		- Commence therapy within 1 hour of decision to treat
		- Use empiric antimicrobial regimen initially
		- Modify treatment according to culture results

	- Central Lines
		- Strongly consider central line removal in septic infants

	- Severe / Fulminant Sepsis
		- Urgent infectious diseases consultation
		- Consider broadening antimicrobial coverage

| Infection | Likely Organisms | Initial IV Antibiotics | Duration / Comments |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **EOS (<48 h)**<br>Normal CSF / meningitis not suspected | GBS, *E. coli*, *Listeria monocytogenes* | **Benzylpenicillin + Gentamicin** | Stop after 36 h if cultures negative and infant well. Add Aciclovir if HSV suspected. Consider MRSA/ESBL coverage if colonised. |
| **EOS (<48 h)**<br>Meningitis suspected / abnormal CSF | GBS, *E. coli*, *Listeria monocytogenes* | **Benzylpenicillin + 3rd-generation cephalosporin** | Treat according to culture results. |
| **LOS (>48 h)**<br>Normal CSF / meningitis not suspected | GBS, *E. coli*, other Gram-negatives, *H. influenzae*, *S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*, *S. aureus*, GAS | **Cefazolin + Gentamicin** | Stop after 36 h if cultures negative. Add Benzylpenicillin if Listeria suspected. Use Vancomycin instead of Cefazolin if MRSA risk. Add Aciclovir if HSV suspected. Add Liposomal Amphotericin if fungal infection suspected. |
| **LOS (>48 h)**<br>Meningitis suspected / abnormal CSF | Above organisms | **Benzylpenicillin + 3rd-generation cephalosporin** | Add Vancomycin if MRSA risk. |

| Infection | Organisms | Initial IV Antibiotics | Duration / Comments |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Intra-abdominal infection (NEC)** | Gram-negative coliforms, anaerobes, Enterococcus spp. | **Amoxicillin + Gentamicin + Metronidazole** | 7–10 days. Switch to Amoxicillin-clavulanate at 72 h if improving. Switch to Piperacillin-tazobactam if no improvement. |
| **UTI (uncomplicated)** | *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, *K. oxytoca*, other Gram-negatives, Enterococcus spp. | **Benzylpenicillin + Gentamicin** | 3 days IV then 4 days oral |
| **UTI (bacteraemic)** | Same | **Benzylpenicillin + Gentamicin** | 7 days IV then 3 days oral. Early renal ultrasound. |
| **Pneumonia** | *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae* | **3rd-generation cephalosporin** | 7 days |
| **Ventilator-associated pneumonia** | *S. aureus*, GAS, Gram-negatives | **Cefazolin + Gentamicin** | Pre-antibiotic BAL recommended. Use Vancomycin instead of Cefazolin if critically unwell. |
| **Atypical pneumonia** | *Ureaplasma*, *Chlamydia trachomatis* | **Azithromycin** | 3 days |
| **Cellulitis / Post-operative wound infection** | GAS, *S. aureus* | **Cefazolin** | 5–7 days; IV-to-oral switch after 3 days if improving |

| Prophylaxis Type | Indication | Antibiotic | Comments |
|------------|------------|------------|------------|
| **Medical – Fungal prophylaxis** | All infants <1500 g | **Nystatin** | Continue until weight ≥1500 g |
| **Medical – Fungal prophylaxis** | Older infants with multiple risk factors for invasive fungal infection | **Nystatin** | Consider if prolonged broad-spectrum antibiotics or other fungal risk factors |
| **Medical – UTI prophylaxis** | Recurrent / selected UTI risk | **Trimethoprim** | Cease if nil by mouth. Discuss ongoing need with Nephrology/Urology. |
| **Surgical prophylaxis** | Most surgical procedures | **Cefazolin** | Give within 1 hour before surgery. Post-operative antibiotics usually not required (maximum 24 h). |
| **Surgical prophylaxis** | Intra-abdominal surgery, pelvic surgery, deep wound debridement, ischaemic limb, risk of bowel lumen entry, open fracture | **Cefazolin + Metronidazole** | Give within 1 hour before surgery. |

- Third-generation cephalosporins
	- Cefotaxime
	- Ceftriaxone
	- Avoid ceftriaxone in neonates <41 weeks gestation
		- Especially if jaundiced
		- Especially if receiving calcium-containing solutions
			- TPN

- HSV risk factors
	- Maternal HSV
	- Recent HSV exposure
		- Incubation period up to 12 days
	- Abnormal neurological status
	- Seizures
	- Vesicular rash
	- Corneal ulcer
	- Keratitis
	- Hepatitis

- MRSA risk factors
	- Fulminant infection
		- Inotrope requirement
	- Known infant colonisation
	- Known maternal colonisation
	- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander child
	- Pacific Islander child

- ESBL risk factors
	- Known infant colonisation
	- Known maternal colonisation
		- Check maternal microbiology around delivery

- Fungal infection risk factors
	- Fulminant infection
		- Inotrope requirement
	- Weight <1500 g and deteriorating despite antibiotics
	- ≥7 days of antibiotics with deterioration
	- TPN
	- Systemic steroids
	- Thrombocytopenia

- Listeria risk factors
	- Disseminated abscesses
	- Granulomas
	- Pustular skin rash
	- Maternal listeriosis

- Uncomplicated meningitis
	- Absence of
		- Ventriculitis
		- Cerebritis
		- Hydrocephalus
		- Brain abscess
		- Cerebral infarction
		- Cerebral venous thrombosis
		- Arterial stroke
		- Subdural effusion
		- Empyema

- Uncomplicated UTI
	- Fever present
	- No vomiting
	- No urological abnormalities
