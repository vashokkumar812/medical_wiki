# Takayasu Arteritis

- **Definition**
	- Chronic granulomatous large-vessel vasculitis
- pathology
	- Primarily affects:
		- Aorta
		- Major branches of the aorta
		- Pulmonary arteries (occasionally)
	- Leads to:
		- Arterial wall thickening
		- Stenosis
		- Occlusion
		- Aneurysm formation
	- Also known as:
		- **Pulseless disease**

- **Epidemiology**
	- < 40 asian females

- **Clinical Features**

	- **Systemic (Early inflammatory phase)**
		- Fever
		- Fatigue
		- Malaise
		- Weight loss
		- Night sweats
		- Arthralgia
		- Myalgia

	- **Vascular (Late occlusive phase)**
		- Upper limb claudication
		- Limb fatigue with exertion
		- Reduced or absent peripheral pulses
		- Blood pressure discrepancy between arms
		- Bruits over affected arteries
			- Carotid
			- Subclavian
			- Aortic

	- **Neurological**
		- Headache
		- Dizziness
		- Syncope
		- Visual disturbances
		- Transient ischaemic attack (TIA)
		- Stroke

	- **Cardiovascular**
		- Hypertension
			- Often secondary to renal artery stenosis
		- Aortic regurgitation
		- Heart failure
		- Coronary artery involvement

	- **Renal**
		- Renal artery stenosis
		- Renovascular hypertension


- **Investigations**

	- **Blood Tests**
		- Elevated ESR
		- Elevated CRP
		- Normocytic normochromic anaemia

	- **Imaging**
		- CT angiography (CTA)
			- Vessel wall thickening
			- Stenosis
			- Occlusion
			- Aneurysm

		- MR angiography (MRA)
			- Assessment of vessel wall inflammation and luminal changes

		- Doppler ultrasonography
			- Carotid and subclavian artery assessment

		- FDG-PET
			- Detects active vascular inflammation

	- **Conventional Angiography**
		- Used when endovascular intervention is planned

- **Classification Criteria (ACR)**
	- Diagnosis supported by ≥3 of:
		- Age at onset <40 years
		- Claudication of extremities
		- Decreased brachial artery pulse
		- Systolic BP difference >10 mmHg between arms
		- Bruit over subclavian arteries or aorta
		- Arteriographic abnormalities

- **Differential Diagnosis**
	- Giant cell arteritis
	- Atherosclerotic disease
	- Fibromuscular dysplasia
	- Coarctation of the aorta
	- Other vasculitides

- **Management**

	- **Medical**
		- High-dose corticosteroids
			- Prednisolone

		- Steroid-sparing immunosuppressive agents
			- Methotrexate
			- Azathioprine
			- Mycophenolate mofetil

		- Biologic therapy for refractory disease
			- Tocilizumab
			- TNF-α inhibitors

	- **Management of complications**
		- Antihypertensive therapy
		- Treatment of heart failure
		- Cardiovascular risk factor modification

	- **Surgical / Endovascular**
		- Angioplasty
		- Stenting
		- Bypass surgery
		- Aneurysm repair
		- Preferably performed during inactive disease

- **Complications**
	- Renovascular hypertension
	- Stroke
	- Transient ischaemic attack (TIA)
	- Aortic regurgitation
	- Heart failure
	- Coronary artery disease
	- Aortic aneurysm
	- Chronic kidney disease

- **Diagnosis Pattern**
	- Young woman
		- Constitutional symptoms
		- Upper limb claudication
		- Reduced or absent pulses
		- Unequal blood pressure between arms
		- Large-vessel stenosis on angiography





# Buerger Disease (Thromboangiitis Obliterans)

- **Definition**
	- Non-atherosclerotic, segmental inflammatory vaso-occlusive disease 
- Affects:
	- Small arteries
	- Medium arteries
	- Veins
- Primarily involves:
	- Distal upper limbs
	- Distal lower limbs
- Strongly associated with:
	- Tobacco use

- Epidemiology
	- tobacco exposure
	- < 45 years
	- males

- Clinical Features
  - Distal extremity pain
  - Claudication
    - Foot
    - Calf
    - Hand
  - Rest pain
  - Cold sensitivity
  - Raynaud phenomenon
  - Migratory superficial thrombophlebitis

	- Digital Ischaemia
	  - Finger/toe pain
	  - Ulcers
	  - Gangrene
	
	- Examination
	  - Cool cyanotic extremities
	  - Digital ulcers
	  - Digital gangrene
	  - Reduced/absent distal pulses
	  - Raynaud signs
- **Investigations**

	- **Blood Tests**
		- Usually normal inflammatory markers
		- No specific diagnostic laboratory test

	- **Tests to Exclude Other Causes**
		- ANA
		- ANCA
		- Antiphospholipid antibodies
		- Diabetes screening
		- Hypercoagulable workup

	- **Doppler Ultrasound**
		- Distal arterial occlusions

	- **Angiography**
		- Segmental arterial occlusions
		- Corkscrew collateral vessels
		- Distal vessel involvement
		- Absence of atherosclerosis

- **Diagnostic Criteria**
	- Age <45–50 years
	- Current or recent tobacco use
	- Distal extremity ischaemia
		- Claudication
		- Rest pain
		- Ulcers
		- Gangrene
	- Exclusion of:
		- Autoimmune disease
		- Diabetes mellitus
		- Hypercoagulable states
		- Proximal embolic source



- **Management**

	- **Definitive Treatment**
		- Complete smoking cessation
	- **Medical**
		- Analgesia
		- Vasodilators
			- Calcium channel blockers if Raynaud phenomenon present
		- Antiplatelet therapy
			- May be used for symptom management
	- **Advanced/Refractory Disease**
		- Intravenous prostaglandin analogues
			- Iloprost

	- **Surgical**
		- Limited role due to distal vessel involvement
		- Debridement of ulcers
		- Amputation
			- For irreversible gangrene

- **Complications**
	- Chronic limb ischaemia
	- Recurrent ulcers
	- Digital gangrene
	- Amputation
	- Functional disability


# Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN)

- **Definition**
	- Systemic necrotising vasculitis
	- Affects:
		- Medium-sized muscular arteries
	- Causes:
		- Transmural inflammation
		- Fibrinoid necrosis
		- Aneurysm formation
		- Vessel stenosis and occlusion
	- Characteristically:
		- Spares arterioles, capillaries, and venules
		- No glomerulonephritis
		- ANCA-negative

- **Epidemiology**
	- Usually affects:
		- Middle-aged adults
	- Slight male predominance
	- Associated with:
		- Hepatitis B infection
		- Less commonly Hepatitis C

- **Pathophysiology**
	- Immune complex-mediated vascular injury
	- Segmental transmural necrotising inflammation
	- Leads to:
		- Thrombosis
		- Organ ischaemia
		- Microaneurysm formation

- **Clinical Features**

	- **Constitutional**
		- Fever
		- Weight loss
		- Malaise
		- Fatigue

	- **Skin**
		- Livedo reticularis
		- Purpura
		- Nodules
		- Ulcers
		- Digital infarcts

	- **Neurological**
		- Mononeuritis multiplex
		- Peripheral neuropathy
		- Foot drop
		- Wrist drop

	- **Renal**
		- Hypertension
		- Renal infarction
		- Renal insufficiency
		- No glomerulonephritis

	- **Gastrointestinal**
		- Abdominal pain
			- Especially postprandial
		- Mesenteric ischaemia
		- Gastrointestinal bleeding
		- Bowel perforation

	- **Musculoskeletal**
		- Myalgia
		- Arthralgia

	- **Cardiac**
		- Coronary arteritis
		- Myocardial infarction
		- Heart failure

- **Examination Findings**
	- Hypertension
	- Skin nodules
	- Livedo reticularis
	- Peripheral neuropathy
	- Signs of organ ischaemia

- **Investigations**

	- **Blood Tests**
		- Elevated ESR
		- Elevated CRP
		- Normocytic anaemia
		- Hepatitis B serology

	- **Autoimmune Tests**
		- ANCA negative

	- **Angiography**
		- Multiple microaneurysms
			- Renal arteries
			- Mesenteric arteries
		- Segmental stenoses

	- **Biopsy**
		- Gold standard
		- Transmural necrotising vasculitis
		- Fibrinoid necrosis

- **Differential Diagnosis**
	- Microscopic polyangiitis
	- Granulomatosis with polyangiitis
	- Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis
	- Takayasu arteritis
	- Systemic lupus erythematosus

- **Management**

	- **Mild to Moderate Disease**
		- Corticosteroids
			- Prednisolone

	- **Severe Disease**
		- Corticosteroids
		- Cyclophosphamide

	- **Hepatitis B-associated PAN**
		- Antiviral therapy
		- Short-course corticosteroids
		- Plasma exchange

- **Complications**
	- Renal failure
	- Severe hypertension
	- Mesenteric ischaemia
	- Bowel infarction
	- Myocardial infarction
	- Stroke
	- Peripheral neuropathy

- **Key Diagnostic Features**
	- Medium-vessel vasculitis
	- ANCA-negative
	- Mononeuritis multiplex
	- Hypertension
	- Abdominal pain
	- Hepatitis B association
	- Renal involvement without glomerulonephritis
	- Microaneurysms on angiography


# kawasaki disease


- Major complication
	- Coronary artery aneurysm (CAA)

- Epidemiology
  - <5 years
  - ethnicities
    - Japanese ethnicity
    - Korean ethnicity
    - Chinese ethnicity
  - Second most common childhood vasculitis
    - After IgA vasculitis

- Key Facts
  - No diagnostic test exists
  - Diagnosis is clinical
  - Clinical features may appear sequentially over several days
  - Untreated disease
    - develop CAA or self limiting in 3 weeks
  - Early IVIg significantly reduces coronary complications

- Complete Kawasaki Disease Diagnostic Criteria
  - Fever ≥4 days
    - Day 1 = first day of fever
  - Plus ≥4 principal clinical features
  - Features do NOT need to occur simultaneously
  - No alternative diagnosis

- Principal Clinical Features

  - Bilateral conjunctival injection
    - Bilateral
    - Non-exudative
    - Non-purulent
    - Painless
    - Peri-limbal sparing

  - Oral and mucosal changes
    - Strawberry tongue
    - Erythematous tongue
    - Prominent papillae
    - Erythematous lips
    - Fissured lips
    - Cracked lips
    - Haemorrhagic lips
    - Erythematous oropharynx
    - Not typically exudative
    - Not typically ulcerative

  - Cervical lymphadenopathy
    - Usually unilateral
    - Cluster of anterior cervical nodes
    - ≥1.5 cm
    - Tender

  - Rash
    - Polymorphous
    - Erythematous
    - Trunk
    - Perineum
    - Extremities
    - Perineal desquamation may occur
    - Not vesicular
    - Not bullous
    - Not haemorrhagic

  - Extremity changes
    - Palmar erythema
    - Plantar erythema
    - Painful oedema of hands and feet
    - Periungual desquamation
      - Usually 2–3 weeks after onset

- Mnemonic
  - CRASH and Burn
    - C
      - Conjunctivitis
    - R
      - Rash
    - A
      - Adenopathy
    - S
      - Strawberry tongue
    - H
      - Hand and foot changes
    - Burn
      - Fever

- Other Clinical Manifestations

  - General
    - Extreme irritability
      - Highly characteristic

  - Cardiovascular
    - Coronary artery dilatation
    - Coronary artery aneurysm
    - Myocarditis
    - Pericarditis
    - Valvular regurgitation
    - Left ventricular dysfunction
    - Shock
      - Kawasaki Disease Shock Syndrome (KDSS)
    - Aortic root enlargement
    - Axillary artery aneurysm
    - Peripheral gangrene

  - Neurological
    - Aseptic meningitis
    - Sensorineural hearing loss
    - Cranial nerve palsy

  - Gastrointestinal
    - Abdominal pain
    - Vomiting
    - Diarrhoea
    - Hepatitis
    - Jaundice
    - Gallbladder hydrops
    - Pancreatitis

  - Musculoskeletal
    - Arthralgia
    - Arthritis

  - Genitourinary
    - Sterile pyuria
    - Dysuria
    - Urethritis
    - Meatitis
    - Bilirubinuria
    - Elevated urobilinogen

  - Ophthalmological
    - Anterior uveitis
      - Often asymptomatic
      - Requires slit-lamp examination

  - Respiratory
    - Peribronchial infiltrates
    - Interstitial infiltrates
    - Pulmonary nodules

  - Skin
    - Groin desquamation
    - BCG scar erythema
    - BCG scar induration

  - ENT
    - Retropharyngeal phlegmon

- Incomplete Kawasaki Disease

  - Fever
  - <4 principal clinical features
  - Requires supportive laboratory findings and/or echocardiographic abnormalities

- High-Risk Groups
  - Infants <12 months
  - Adolescents

- Clinical Clues
  - Prolonged unexplained fever
  - Fewer classical signs
  - Higher risk of coronary complications

- Infants <12 months
  - Echocardiogram recommended even if inflammatory markers are normal

- Echocardiographic Findings Supporting Incomplete KD

  - Coronary artery aneurysm
    - Z-score ≥2.5

  - OR ≥3 of

    - Left ventricular dysfunction
      - EF <55%
      - Fractional shortening <28%

    - Mitral regurgitation
      - Mild or greater

    - Pericardial effusion
      - Small or greater

    - Coronary artery dilatation
      - LAD or RCA Z-score 2–2.5

- Coronary Artery Aneurysm Risk Factors

  - Age ≤6 months

  - Age 6–12 months
    - Coronary Z-score ≥2

  - Coronary artery Z-score ≥2.5

  - Delayed IVIg
    - ≥10 days after fever onset

  - IVIg resistance

  - Kawasaki Disease Shock Syndrome

  - Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS)

  - Platelets ≥450 × 10⁹/L

  - CRP ≥130 mg/L

  - East Asian ethnicity
  - South Asian ethnicity
  - Mixed Asian ethnicity

  - Anaemia

  - Hypoalbuminaemia

  - Hyponatraemia

- Investigations

- Initial Investigations


  - Serum storage before IVIg

- Typical Laboratory Findings

  - Elevated CRP
  - Elevated ESR
  - Neutrophilia
  - Normocytic anaemia
  - Thrombocytosis
    - Usually after first week
  - Hypoalbuminaemia
  - Elevated transaminases
  - Sterile pyuria

- Important Investigation Notes

  - Do not repeat ESR after IVIg
    - Falsely elevated

  - Positive respiratory viral PCR
    - Does NOT exclude KD

- Additional Investigations

  - If systemic JIA suspected
    - Ferritin

  - If MAS suspected
    - Ferritin
    - Fibrinogen
    - Triglycerides

  - If PIMS-TS / MIS-C suspected
    - Ferritin
    - Troponin
    - LDH
    - CK
    - NT-proBNP
    - Coagulation studies
    - D-dimer

- Echocardiogram
  - Most important investigation
  - May be normal early in disease
  - Normal echocardiogram does NOT exclude Kawasaki disease
  - Echocardiography should NOT delay treatment

- Timing of Echocardiography
  - Baseline echocardiogram
    - At presentation
  - Follow-up echocardiogram
    - 4–6 weeks after diagnosis

- Earlier Repeat Echocardiography
  - High-risk patients
  - Baseline coronary artery aneurysm
  - Persistent systemic inflammation
  - Under guidance of paediatric cardiologist

- Initial Management
  - Hospital admission
  - Paediatric review
  - Baseline investigations
  - ECG
  - Echocardiogram

- First-Line Treatment

  - Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIg)
    - Most appropriate treatment
    - Dose
      - 2 g/kg IV once
    - Single infusion
    - Usually infused over 8–12 hours
    - Incremental infusion rates
    - Early administration improves coronary outcomes

  - Aspirin
    - 5 mg/kg orally daily
    - Used to reduce thrombosis risk
    - Continue until cardiology review
      - Usually ~6 weeks after disease onset

- Aspirin Notes
  - Higher doses do not improve coronary outcomes
  - Low-dose aspirin has minimal Reye syndrome risk
  - Avoid NSAIDs
    - Especially ibuprofen
    - May reduce cardioprotective effects

- High-Risk Kawasaki Disease

  - Risk factors
    - Coronary artery abnormalities
    - Age ≤6 months
    - Delayed treatment
    - IVIg resistance
    - Markedly elevated inflammatory markers

  - Additional treatment
    - Corticosteroids

      - IV methylprednisolone

        OR

      - Oral prednisolone

      - Example regimen
        - 2 mg/kg/day
        - Maximum 50 mg
        - Taper over 2–4 weeks

- IVIg Resistance

  - Definition
    - Fever ≥38°C
    - Present ≥36 hours after completion of first IVIg infusion

  - Management
    - Reassess diagnosis
    - Exclude alternative causes
    - Consult Kawasaki disease specialists
      - Infectious diseases
      - Rheumatology
      - Immunology

  - Treatment options
    - Second IVIg dose
    - Infliximab
    - Corticosteroids

- Refractory Kawasaki Disease

  - Persistent inflammation despite standard treatment

  - Specialist consultation required

  - Treatment options
    - Infliximab
    - Anakinra
    - Etanercept
    - Cyclosporine
    - Cyclophosphamide
    - Plasmapheresis

- Coronary Artery Aneurysm Management

  - Cardiology involvement required
  - Antithrombotic therapy may be required

- Resolving Kawasaki Disease

  - Previous illness suggestive of KD
  - No fever
  - Normal inflammatory markers

  - Management
    - Start aspirin
    - Arrange echocardiogram
    - Cardiology follow-up

- Consultation
  - Consult paediatric team whenever Kawasaki disease suspected

- Transfer Indications
  - Cardiac involvement
  - Echocardiography unavailable locally
  - Requires care beyond local hospital capability

- Discharge Criteria
  - Afebrile ≥36 hours after treatment
  - Clinically well
  - Aspirin supplied
  - Follow-up arranged
  - Repeat echocardiogram arranged
  - Consider repeat CRP
    - Especially high-risk patients
  - Family educated regarding recurrence of fever or symptoms

- Kawasaki Disease Shock Syndrome (KDSS)

  - Severe Kawasaki disease

  - Features
    - Hypotension
    - Poor perfusion
    - Myocardial dysfunction

  - Management consideration
    - Slower IVIg infusion rate

- IVIg Timing

  - Give as early as possible when clinically safe

  - Local protocols may allow delay until daytime staffing if
    - Haemodynamically stable
    - ≤7 days of fever

  - IVIg still indicated >10 days after fever onset if
    - Persistent fever
    - Elevated inflammatory markers
    - Coronary abnormalities

- IVIg Adverse Effects

  - Headache

  - Fever during infusion
    - May reflect active Kawasaki disease
    - Not necessarily transfusion reaction

  - Haemolytic anaemia
    - Rare (~1%)
    - Dose dependent
    - More common in non-O blood groups

- Managing IVIg Infusion Symptoms

  - Consider paracetamol
    - 30 minutes before infusion
    - Continue during infusion if required

- Vaccination After IVIg

  - Delay live vaccines for 11 months

    - MMR
    - MMRV
    - Varicella
    - Rotavirus

  - If high measles exposure risk
    - Vaccinate immediately
    - Repeat vaccination after 11 months

- Differential Diagnoses

  - Group A Streptococcal Infection
    - Pharyngitis
    - Scarlet fever
    - Acute rheumatic fever
    - Retropharyngeal abscess
    - Parapharyngeal abscess

    - Consider Kawasaki disease if
      - No improvement after 24–48 hours of appropriate antibiotics

  - Viral Infections
    - Adenovirus
    - Enterovirus
    - EBV
    - CMV
    - HHV-6
    - Parvovirus B19
    - Influenza
    - SARS-CoV

    - Positive viral PCR does NOT exclude Kawasaki disease

    - Adenovirus
      - Exudative conjunctivitis
      - Exudative pharyngitis

    - Kawasaki disease
      - Non-exudative conjunctivitis
      - Limbal sparing

  - Measles
    - Cough
    - Coryza
    - Exudative conjunctivitis
    - Cephalocaudal rash

  - Bacterial Cervical Lymphadenitis
    - Usually responds to antibiotics

    - Kawasaki disease
      - Often unilateral
      - Cluster of enlarged nodes
      - Poor antibiotic response

  - Viral Meningitis
    - Consider Kawasaki disease in infant with
      - Prolonged fever
      - Extreme irritability
      - Aseptic meningitis

  - Urinary Tract Infection
    - Kawasaki disease typically causes
      - Sterile pyuria
      - Nitrite negative urine
      - Leukocyte esterase ≤1+
      - Bilirubinuria
      - Elevated urobilinogen

  - Toxic Shock Syndrome
    - Hypotension
    - Multiorgan dysfunction
    - Rapid progression to shock

    - Kawasaki Disease Shock Syndrome
      - Occurs in ~5%
      - Increased coronary aneurysm risk

  - Malignancy
    - Anaemia
    - Recurrent infections
    - Bruising
    - Weight loss
    - Night sweats
    - Generalised lymphadenopathy

  - PIMS-TS / MIS-C
    - Prominent gastrointestinal symptoms
      - Abdominal pain
      - Vomiting
      - Diarrhoea
    - Headache
    - COVID-19 infection 2–6 weeks earlier

  - Systemic Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (Still Disease)
    - >7 days fever
    - Quotidian fever pattern
    - Well between fever spikes
    - Salmon-pink rash
    - Arthralgia
    - Arthritis

  - Drug Hypersensitivity Reactions
    - Stevens-Johnson syndrome
    - Toxic epidermal necrolysis

    - Features
      - Ulcerated mucosa
      - Medication trigger
      - Eye involvement
      - Genital involvement
