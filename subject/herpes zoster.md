- Aetiology
	- Reactivation of varicella zoster virus
	- Virus remains latent in dorsal root ganglion after chickenpox

- Associated factors
	- Increasing age
	- Immunosuppression
	- Malignancy
		- Leukaemia
		- Lymphoma
	- Radiotherapy
	- Spinal 
		- tumours
		- Local spinal disease

- Epidemiology
	- Can occur at any age
	- More common >50 years


- Clinical features
	- Prodrome
		- Radicular pain
		- Burning pain
		- Hyperaesthesia
		- Tingling
		- Fever occasionally
	- Rash
		- Unilateral
		- Dermatomal
		- Vesicular eruption
		- Patchy distribution
		- One or two contiguous dermatomes
		- Erythematous papules
		- Vesicles
		- Crusting after 7–10 days
		- Scab separation after 10–14 days
		- Residual depigmentation possible
	- Regional lymphadenopathy

- Distribution
	- Thoracic dermatomes
		- Most common
	- Trigeminal dermatomes
		- Common
	- Usually does not cross midline
	- Follows distribution of previous varicella eruption
		- Face and trunk commonly affected

- Cranial nerve involvement
	- Trigeminal nerve
		- Ophthalmic division
			- Most common cranial nerve involvement
			- Nasociliary branch involvement
				- Vesicles on tip of nose
					- Hutchinson sign
						- ![[Pasted image 20260521125542.png]]
				- Increased risk of ocular involvement
			- Eye involvement
				- Conjunctivitis
				- Keratitis
				- Uveitis
		- Maxillary and mandibular divisions
			- Oral lesions
			- Palatal lesions
			- Pharyngeal lesions
	- Facial nerve
		- [[Herpes zoster oticus]]

- Complications
	- Postherpetic neuralgia
	- Secondary bacterial infection
	- Ophthalmic complications
		- Keratitis
		- Corneal ulceration
		- Vision loss
	- Meningoencephalitis
	- Disseminated zoster
			- Immunocompromised patients

- Differential diagnoses
	- Herpes simplex
		- Recurrent lesions
		- Not dermatomal
	- Contact dermatitis
		- Pruritic rash
		- No neuralgia
	- Cellulitis
		- Diffuse erythema
		- No vesicles initially
	- Impetigo
		- Honey-coloured crusts
	- Trigeminal neuralgia
		- Facial pain without rash

- Investigations
	- Usually clinical diagnosis
	- if doubtful
		- PCR from vesicle fluid
	- HIV testing if severe/recurrent disease

- Management
	- Antivirals
		- Aciclovir
		- Valaciclovir
		- Famciclovir
	- Most effective if started within 72 hours
	- Analgesia
		- NSAIDs
		- Neuropathic pain agents
	- Ophthalmology referral
			- Ophthalmic involvement
	- Corticosteroids
			- Selected severe cases
	- Ramsay Hunt syndrome
			- Antivirals + corticosteroids

- Prevention
	- Varicella vaccination
	- Zoster vaccination
			- Older adults
			- Immunocompromised risk groups

# herpes zoster opticus / ramsay hunt syndrome
- Definition
	- Reactivation of varicella zoster virus in geniculate ganglion
	- Causes herpes zoster involving facial nerve

- Classic triad
	- Otalgia
	- Vesicular rash in ear canal
	- Ipsilateral LMN facial nerve palsy

- Clinical features
	- Severe ear pain
	- Vesicles
			- External auditory canal
			- Pinna
	- Facial weakness
	- Hearing loss
	- Vertigo
	- Tinnitus
	- Hyperacusis
	- Altered taste

- Cranial nerves involved
	- CN VII
			- Facial nerve
	- CN VIII
			- Vestibulocochlear nerve
	- May involve
			- CN V
			- CN VI
			- CN IX
			- CN X

- Differential diagnoses
	- Bell palsy
		- Facial palsy without vesicular rash
		- Usually isolated CN VII involvement
	- Malignant otitis externa
		- Elderly diabetic/immunocompromised patient
		- Severe otalgia
		- Otorrhoea
		- Granulation tissue in ear canal
		- Skull base osteomyelitis
	- Malignant transformation of pleomorphic adenoma
		- Longstanding parotid mass
		- Progressive facial nerve palsy
	- Otitis externa
		- Ear pain and discharge
		- No facial paralysis
	- Stroke
		- UMN facial weakness
		- Forehead sparing

- Investigations
	- Usually clinical diagnosis
	- If diagnosis uncertain
		- PCR from vesicle fluid
	- Audiometry
		- Hearing loss assessment
	- MRI brain/parotid
		- Atypical presentation
		- Persistent facial palsy
		- Suspected malignancy
	- Blood glucose
			- Assess diabetes risk

- Management
	- Start treatment within 72 hours
	- Antivirals
		- Aciclovir
			- 800 mg five times daily
		- Valaciclovir
			- 1 g three times daily
		- Famciclovir
			- 250 mg three times daily
		- Duration
			- 7–10 days
	- Corticosteroids
			- High-dose prednisolone
	- Analgesia
	- Neuropathic pain management
			- Gabapentin
	- Eye care
		- Artificial tears
		- Lubricating ointment
		- Eye taping at night
		- Ophthalmology referral if incomplete eye closure

- Complications
	- Permanent facial weakness
	- Postherpetic neuralgia
	- Synkinesis
	- Exposure keratitis
	- Hearing loss
	- Vertigo

- Prognosis
	- Worse prognosis than Bell palsy
	- Poor prognostic factors
		- Elderly age
		- Diabetes mellitus
		- Severe initial facial paralysis
	- Recovery may be incomplete

- Prevention
	- Herpes zoster vaccination
		- Reduces incidence of shingles
		- Reduces postherpetic neuralgia
	- Recommended in older adults
