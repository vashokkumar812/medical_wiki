- exercise
	- regular exercise improves
		- pain
		- fatigue
		- sleep disturbance

	- aerobic exercise
		- best evidence
		- improves physical capacity
		- reduces symptoms

	- principles
		- start slowly
		- gradual increase
		- reassure deconditioned patients
		- avoid overexertion initially

	- practical approach
		- begin with few minutes several times weekly
		- progressively increase tolerance

	- hydrotherapy
		- useful starting option
		- warm water reduces symptoms
		- buoyancy decreases weight-bearing
		- water resistance provides gentle exercise

- other physical therapies
	- yoga
	- qi gong
	- tai chi

	- benefits
		- symptom improvement
		- improved physical functioning
		- improved self-efficacy

	- limitations of evidence
		- studies generally small
		- often unblinded

	- advantages
		- minimal serious adverse effects
		- self-administered

- medications
	- no PBS-listed medication specifically for fibromyalgia in Australia

- analgesics
	- paracetamol
		- EULAR recommends use
		- recommendation mainly expert opinion
		- evidence limited

	- paracetamol + tramadol
		- modest pain improvement
			- ~18%

	- tramadol
		- some benefit in fibromyalgia
		- benefit may relate to
			- serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibition
			- rather than opioid effect

	- opioids
		- generally not recommended
		- reduced opioid receptor availability in fibromyalgia
		- poor evidence of efficacy

	- NSAIDs
		- evidence limited
		- commonly used
		- many patients perceive better effect than paracetamol

- antidepressants as pain modulators
	- mechanism
		- increase serotonin and noradrenaline in descending inhibitory pain pathways

	- useful agents
		- low-dose TCAs
		- SNRIs

	- benefits
		- pain reduction
		- symptom improvement
		- independent of mood effects

	- important drugs
		- amitriptyline
		- duloxetine
		- milnacipran

	- milnacipran
		- approved by TGA for fibromyalgia
		- not currently available in Australia

- membrane stabilisers
	- pregabalin
	- gabapentin

	- mechanism
		- bind voltage-dependent calcium channels
		- reduce calcium influx into sensitised spinal neurons

	- benefits
		- reduced pain
		- improved sleep
		- improved quality of life
