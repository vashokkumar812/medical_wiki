# Aortic aneurysm
- Definition
	- arterial dilation >1.5× normal vessel diameter

- Normal aortic diameter (>50 yrs)
	- women
		- ~1.5 cm
	- men
		- ~1.7 cm

- Epidemiology/risk factors
	- men 
	- \> 65 yrs
	- smoking
	- [[SHTN|hypertension]]
	- family history

- Causes
	- atherosclerotic degeneration
		- [[SHTN|hypertension]]
		- hypercholesterolaemia
	- connective tissue degeneration
	- inflammatory
		- arteritis
	- traumatic
		- pseudoaneurysm
	- infective
		- mycotic aneurysm
	- post-dissection degeneration

- Location
	- 90–95% infrarenal
	- iliac extension common
	- suprarenal extension uncommon

- Growth
	- <5.5 cm
		- grows ~3 mm/year
	- larger aneurysms
		- faster expansion
	- rupture risk increases sharply with size

- Types
	- by wall involvement
		- true aneurysm
			- all 3 vessel wall layers intact
		- false aneurysm/pseudoaneurysm
			- deficient in ≥1 layer
	- by shape
		- fusiform
			- circumferential/bulbous
			- usually true aneurysm
		- saccular
			- focal lateral outpouching
			- often pseudoaneurysm

- Complications
	- rupture
		- contained
		- free rupture
	- thrombosis
	- distal embolisation
	- branch vessel ischaemia
		- renal
		- spinal
		- pancreatic
	- rhabdomyolysis
	- death

- Clinical presentation
	- asymptomatic
		- incidental imaging/exam finding
	- rupture presentation
		- severe abdominal/back pain
		- shock/circulatory collapse
		- pulsatile abdominal mass
			- palpable ≥4–5 cm
	- atypical presentations
		- renal colic mimic
		- sciatica-like pain
		- chronic back pain

- Screening
	- men ≥65 yrs
		- ultrasound screening reduces mortality
	- Australia
		- no national screening program
	- consider screening in
		- smokers
		- family history
		- vascular disease

- Investigations
	- imaging
		- ultrasound
			- first-line for
				- screening
				- surveillance
				- unstable patients
			- measures maximal AP diameter
		- CT angiography
			- morphology assessment
			- endoleak assessment
			- EVAR planning
		- AXR
			- not routinely recommended
			- may show calcification

- Management
	- risk factor modification
		- smoking cessation
		- BP control
		- lipid control
	- medications
		- statin
		- antiplatelet
		- antihypertensive
	- tender AAA
		- vascular emergency

- Surveillance criteria
	- <2 cm
		- normal
	- 2–5.5 cm
		- ultrasound surveillance
	- >5.5 cm
		- operative repair if fit

- Surveillance intervals
	- 3.0–3.9 cm
		- every 24 months
	- 4.0–4.5 cm
		- every 12 months
	- 4.6–5.0 cm
		- every 6 months
	- >5.0 cm
		- every 3 months

- Referral indications
	- elective repair
		- men >5.5 cm
		- women >5.0 cm
	- urgent referral
		- growth >1 cm/year
		- symptomatic AAA
			- pain
			- tenderness
			- embolisation
	- thoracic/thoracoabdominal aneurysm
		- >6 cm

- Ruptured AAA
	- permissive hypotension
		- target SBP ~90 mmHg
	- analgesia
		- fentanyl
		- morphine
	- IDC
	- arterial line
		- if no theatre delay
	- urgent vascular referral
	- immediate transfer to OR
	- contained rupture
		- still requires urgent repair

- Repair options
	- open repair
		- gold standard
		- longer recovery
		- higher morbidity
	- EVAR
		- minimally invasive
		- lower short-term mortality
		- faster recovery
		- suitable in ~50% unruptured AAA
		- lifelong surveillance required
			- endoleaks
			- graft/device failure
		- sometimes require re-intervention within 6 years

- Prognosis
	- asymptomatic AAA
		- >6 cm
			- 5-year survival ~20%
			- ~50% rupture within 1 year
		- elective repair mortality ~5%
	- ruptured AAA
		- fatal if untreated
		- operative mortality up to 50%
		- poorer outcomes with
			- older age
			- comorbidities
			- hypotension

- Patient advice
	- smoking cessation essential
	- optimise BP/lipid control
	- adhere to surveillance
	- seek urgent care for
		- sudden abdominal pain
		- back pain
		- collapse/syncope
	- physical activity usually encouraged
	- avoid extreme heavy straining with large aneurysm
	- travel usually acceptable if stable and monitored

# aortic dissection

![[Pasted image 20260529062227.png]]


- definition
	-  tear occurs in the inner wall of the aorta
- risk factors
	- [[SHTN|hypertension]]
	- pre-existing [[aortic aneurysm]]
	- Marfan syndrome
	- inflammatory vasculitis
		- [[giant cell arteritis]]
		- syphilitic aortitis
		- Takayasu arteritis
	- bicuspid aortic valve
	- coarctation of aorta
	- iatrogenic trauma

- Complications
	- myocardial infarction
		- dissection proximal to coronary arteries
		- inferior [[ischemic heart disease]] + widened mediastinum suggestive
		- diagnosis:
			- transoesophageal echocardiography
		- avoid
			- thrombolysis
			- aspirin
	- aortic regurgitation
	- [[Cardiac Tamponade]]
	- pleural effusion
	- intestinal ischaemia
	- renal ischaemia
		- oliguria
		- haematuria

- Symptoms/signs
	- pain
		- sudden onset
		- severe
		- sharp/tearing
		- radiates to back
			- retrosternal
			- interscapular
			- lower back
		- migratory
		- opioid resistant
	- pulse deficits
		- unequal radial pulses
		- absent femoral pulses
	- BP discrepancy between arms
	- if aortic regurgitation develops
		- diastolic murmur

- Investigations
	- impaired renal function
		- TEE
	- otherwise
		- contrast CT scan
			- investigation of choice
	- chest X-ray
		- widened mediastinum

- Management
	- medical
		- analgesia
			- morphine
		- reduce shear stress
			- beta-blockers
				- first-line
			- calcium channel blockers
				- if beta-blockers contraindicated
		- vasodilation
			- sodium nitroprusside
				- only with beta-blocker
	- definitive
		- surgical repair
		- endovascular stent graft
