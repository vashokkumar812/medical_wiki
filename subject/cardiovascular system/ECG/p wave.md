
## Definition
- First positive deflection on ECG.
- Represents 
	- atrial depolarisation.
- Normal duration:
	- < 120 ms (3 small squares).
## Normal P-wave characteristics
- Morphology
	- Smooth contour.
	- Monophasic in lead II.
	- Biphasic in V1.
- Axis
	- Normal P-wave axis: 0° to +75°.
	- Upright in leads I and II.
	- Inverted in aVR.
- Duration
	- < 120 ms.
- Amplitude
	- Limb leads: < 2.5 mm.
	- Precordial leads: < 1.5 mm.
- Best leads for assessing P-wave abnormalities
	- Inferior leads: II, III, aVF.
	- V1.

## Atrial depolarisation
- Right atrium depolarises before left atrium.
- P wave represents the combined electrical activity of both atria.
- First 1/3 → Right atrial activation.
- Middle 1/3 → Combined atrial activation.
- Final 1/3 → Left atrial activation.
- Lead II
	- Right and left atrial waveforms move in the same direction.
	- Produces a monophasic P wave.
- Lead V1
	- Right and left atrial waveforms move in opposite directions.
	- Produces a biphasic P wave.
	- Initial positive deflection → Right atrial activation.
	- Terminal negative deflection → Left atrial activation.

## Right atrial enlargement (P pulmonale)
- Lead II
	- Tall, peaked P wave.
	- Amplitude > 2.5 mm.
	- Duration usually normal (< 120 ms).
- Lead V1
	- Initial positive deflection > 1.5 mm.
- Causes
	- Pulmonary hypertension.
	- Cor pulmonale.
	- Chronic respiratory disease.

- Left atrial enlargement (P mitrale)
	- Lead II
		- Broad, notched (bifid) P wave.
		- Duration > 120 ms.
		- Notching may or may not be present.
	- Lead V1
		- Terminal negative deflection:
			- Duration > 40 ms.
			- Depth > 1 mm.
	- Causes
		- Mitral stenosis.

- Biatrial enlargement
	- ECG criteria for both right and left atrial enlargement.

- Common P-wave abnormalities
	- P pulmonale
		- Tall, peaked P waves.
		- Suggests right atrial enlargement.
	- P mitrale
		- Broad, notched P waves.
		- Suggests left atrial enlargement.
	- Inverted P waves
		- Suggest non-sinus origin.
		- Inverted P waves in inferior leads (II, III, aVF).
		- PR interval < 120 ms
			- Suggests AV junctional origin.
		- PR interval ≥ 120 ms
			- Suggests ectopic atrial rhythm.
	- Variable P-wave morphology
		- Multiple different P-wave morphologies.
		- Suggests multiple ectopic pacemakers.
		- ≥ 3 different P-wave morphologies
			- Multifocal atrial rhythm.
		- ≥ 3 different P-wave morphologies with heart rate ≥ 100 bpm
			- Multifocal atrial tachycardia (MAT).

- Important ECG correlations
	- P pulmonale → Right atrial enlargement.
	- P mitrale → Left atrial enlargement.
	- Inverted P waves → Ectopic atrial or junctional rhythm.
	- Variable P waves → Multifocal atrial rhythm.
