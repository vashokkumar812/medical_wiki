

## Definition
  - Any negative deflection that precedes an R wave
- Represents normal left-to-right depolarisation of the interventricular septum
- Normal septal Q waves
  - Typically seen in left-sided leads:
    - I
    - aVL
    - V5
    - V6

## Q Waves in Different Leads

- Small Q waves are normal in most leads
- Deeper Q waves (>2 mm) may be seen as a normal variant in:
  - Lead III
  - Lead aVR
- Normally absent in right-sided leads:
  - V1
  - V2
  - V3

## Pathological Q Waves

- Criteria:
  - Width >40 ms (1 mm)
  - Depth >2 mm
  - Depth >25% of QRS complex
  - Presence in leads V1–V3
- Significance:
  - Usually indicate current or previous myocardial infarction

## Differential Diagnosis

- Myocardial infarction
- Cardiomyopathies
  - Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)
  - Infiltrative myocardial disease
- Cardiac rotation
  - Extreme clockwise rotation
  - Extreme counter-clockwise rotation
- Lead placement errors
  - Example: Upper limb leads placed on lower limbs

## Loss of Normal Q Waves

- Absence of small septal Q waves in V5–V6 is abnormal
- Most commonly associated with:
  - Left bundle branch block (LBBB)

## ECG Examples

- Inferior Q waves with STEMI
  - Leads II, III, aVF
  - ST elevation due to acute MI
- Inferior Q waves with old inferior AMI
  - Leads II, III, aVF
  - T-wave inversion due to previous MI
- Lateral Q waves with STEMI
  - Leads I, aVL
  - ST elevation due to acute MI
- Anterior Q waves with STEMI
  - Leads V1–V4
  - ST elevation due to acute MI
- Anterior Q waves post recent MI
  - Leads V1–V4
  - T-wave inversion due to recent MI
