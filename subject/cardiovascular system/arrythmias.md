## diagnosis 
### Clinical Features

- **Atrial fibrillation (AF) / supraventricular tachycardia ([arrythmias](arrythmias.md))** — ==most common==
- [[chest pain 1]]
- [Ventricular fibrillation](ventricular%20fibrillation.md) (VF) arrest — *least common*

### Investigations

#### Resting ECG

- Short PR interval (< 0.12s)  
- T wave abnormalities  
- Dominant R in V1 and V2  
- May have inferior Q waves but not diagnostic of MI  

#### **ECG Types:**
- **Type A:** tall R in V1, **left axis deviation (LAD)** → left posterior septal pathway  
- **Type B:** deep S in V1, **LAD** → right lateral or postero-septal pathway  
- **Type C:** tall R in V1, **inferior axis (~90°)** → left lateral pathway  

---

#### Symptomatic ECG

- **AV re-entrant tachycardia (AVRT)** or **Atrial fibrillation (AF)**  

**AVRT:**
- The re-entry impulse usually travels **down the AV node** and **back up the accessory pathway**  
- Delta wave **not present**  
- Occasionally, the re-entry impulse may pass **down the accessory pathway** and **up the AV node** → **wide QRS tachycardia + delta wave**

**AF:**
- Rapid, irregular QRS complexes with variable QRS duration  
- Very rapid ventricular response → **cardiogenic shock → [ventricular fibrillation](ventricular%20fibrillation.md) (VF)**  

---

- Drugs that shorten refractory period → ❌ **Digoxin**

# Supraventricular  tachycardia

- Sinus rhythm  
- Narrow QRS complexes  
- Rate: 150–200/min  

## etiology
- injury or dysfunction of the upper thoracic (especially T4 and T5) and cervical spine in the absence of organic heart disease
### triggers
- [[anxiety disorders]]
- cigaratte smoking

## treatment

- **If unstable**
  - Synchronized DC cardioversion (70–120 J)

- **If stable**
  - Step 1: Vagal manoeuvres
    - Carotid sinus massage  
      - Only if young, stable, no carotid bruit, no history of TIA/CVA  
    - Valsalva maneuver
  - Step 2: [[adenosine]]
    - Administer via large proximal vein + oxygen
    - Dose:
      - 6 mg rapid IV bolus (over 2–5 sec)
      - **If** no response → 12 mg IV after 1–2 min
      - **If** still no response → further 12 mg IV
    - Effects:
      - Causes temporary high-grade [[AV block]]
      - Warn patient → unpleasant sensation
      - Some may refuse future doses due to prior unpleasant experience
    - **If** patient distressed → small dose IV [[midazolam]] (1.5 mg)  
      - Reduces recall of unpleasant symptoms  
      - No adverse outcomes reported
    - Side effect: may rarely cause cardiac arrest
  - Step 3: Verapamil (alternative)
    - 2.5–5 mg IV bolus
    - In pregnancy → avoid verapamil (teratogenic)  
      - Use IV beta blockers instead
    - Evidence: outcomes similar to [[adenosine]]
    - But:
      - ECG misinterpretation is common
      - **If** broad complex arrhythmia → verapamil contraindicated
      - Therefore → [[adenosine]] generally safer
