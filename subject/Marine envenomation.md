- Marine envenomation
	- Major types
		- Jellyfish stings
		- Penetrating marine injuries

	- Severe envenomation
		- Cardiac collapse
		- Respiratory failure
		- Paralysis
		- Death

	- High risk
		- Children
			- Smaller body size

	- Prevention
		- Full-body Lycra suit
			- Protects against jellyfish stings

	- Jellyfish stings
		- Pathophysiology
			- Nematocyst discharge injects venom
			- More tentacle contact
				- More venom

		- Severity depends on
			- Number of nematocysts
			- Venom potency/species

		- Usually tropical Australia

		- Clinical presentations
			- Immediate linear/tentacle sting
			- Delayed Irukandji syndrome
				- severe systemic symptoms

		- Clinical features
			- Local
				- Sharp burning pain
				- Erythema/wheals
				- Swelling
				- Linear whip-like lesions
				- Blistering
				- Skin necrosis
				- Frosted ladder pattern
					- Suggests box jellyfish

			- Systemic
				- Nausea/vomiting
				- Sweating
				- Headache
				- Restlessness/agitation
				- Impending doom
				- Collapse
				- Respiratory/cardiac arrest

		- Dangerous jellyfish
			- Box jellyfish
				- Severe immediate pain
				- Whip-like lesions
				- Rapid hypotension/cardiac collapse
				- Death within minutes

				- Management
					- Vinegar
						- 4–6% acetic acid
						- Inhibits nematocysts
					- Remove tentacles
					- Avoid fresh water
					- CPR if collapse
					- Ice/oral analgesia/IV opioids
					- Antivenom for severe envenomation

			- Irukandji jellyfish
				- Minimal local sting
				- Delayed symptoms
					- 5–40 min

				- Syndrome
					- Severe generalized pain
						- Abdomen/back/chest
					- Sweating
					- Agitation
					- Tachycardia/hypertension
					- Dyspnoea

				- Complications
					- Myocardial injury
					- Pulmonary oedema
					- Heart failure
					- Intracranial haemorrhage

				- Management
					- Vinegar
					- Admission
					- Opioids
					- Benzodiazepines
					- Antiemetics
					- ECG/troponin/cardiac monitoring

			- Bluebottle ([[Physalia]])
				- Immediate severe local pain
				- Usually benign

				- First aid
					- Remove tentacles
					- Seawater wash
					- Hot water immersion
						- ~45°C × 20 min
					- Avoid vinegar

		- Tentacle clues
			- Long visible tentacles
				- Box jellyfish
			- Blue jellyfish washed ashore
				- Bluebottle

		- Regional management
			- Tropical Australia
				- Prioritise life support
				- Vinegar if species uncertain
				- Cold pack for analgesia
				- Delayed severe symptoms
					- Suspect Irukandji

			- Non-tropical Australia
				- Pain relief focus
				- Hot water immersion
				- Cold pack if persistent pain/no hot water
				- Ambulance if
					- Generalised pain
					- Large sting area
					- Eye involvement

	- Penetrating marine injuries
		- Causes
			- Stonefish
			- Bullrout
			- Catfish
			- Stingray
			- Sea urchins
			- Crown of thorns
			- Sponges
			- Blue-ringed octopus

		- Mechanism
			- Venomous sharp/sheath-covered spines
			- Venom heat sensitive

		- Clinical features
			- Severe pain
				- Disproportionate to wound
			- Swelling/oedema
			- Bluish discoloration
			- Delayed healing
			- Secondary infection
			- Dermonecrosis

		- Stingray injuries
			- Usually ankle
			- Tail spine causes:
				- Gash injury
				- Haemorrhage
				- Superinfection
			- Thoracoabdominal trauma
				- Potentially fatal
			- Do not remove embedded spine prehospital

		- Sea urchins
			- Retained spines major problem
			- Persistent pain/infection
			- X-ray/US may help

		- Sponge injuries
			- Pain
			- Paraesthesia
			- Itching/numbness
			- Fire sponges
				- Delayed erythema/desquamation

		- Blue-ringed octopus
			- Tetrodotoxin
			- Rapid paralysis/respiratory failure
			- Treat with
				- Pressure immobilisation
				- CPR
				- Ventilation

		- Management
			- Irrigation/cleaning/debridement
			- Hot water immersion
				- ~45°C
				- Up to 90 min
			- Analgesia
				- Simple analgesics
				- Lignocaine/regional block
				- IV morphine/fentanyl

			- Stonefish antivenom
				- Severe uncontrolled pain
				- Slow IV infusion

		- Secondary infection
			- Common organisms
				- [[Vibrio]]
					- Marine
				- [[Aeromonas]]
					- Freshwater

			- Management
				- Swab
				- Antibiotics if infected
				- Follow-up for high-risk wounds
