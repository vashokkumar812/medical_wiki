# Erythema Multiforme (EM)

- Definition
  - Acute cutaneous delayed hypersensitivity reaction
  - Characterised by typical target (iris) lesions


- Epidemiology
  - Most common in young adultz

- Clinical Features

  - Skin Lesions
    - Appear 48–72 hours after onset of illness
    - common site
      - Arms
      - Legs
    - Typical target lesion:
      - Central dusky/violaceous area
      - May contain vesicle or bulla
      - Surrounding concentric erythematous rings

  - Mucosal Involvement
    - Oral mucosa
    - Ocular mucosa
    - Genital mucosa

- Classification

  - Erythema Multiforme Minor
    - Minimal or no mucosal involvement
    - Heals within 2–3 weeks

  - Erythema Multiforme Major
    - Severe mucosal involvement
    - Heals within 4–6 weeks

  - Erythema Multiforme Majus (Fuchs Syndrome)
    - Predominantly mucosal disease
    - Minimal or absent skin lesions
    - Risk of missed ocular involvement

- Causes

  - Infectious

    - Most common
      - Herpes simplex virus (HSV-1, HSV-2)

    - Second most common
      - Mycoplasma pneumoniae

    - Other infections
      - Adenovirus
      - Measles
      - Mycobacterium species
      - Yersinia
      - Treponema pallidum

  - Drug-Induced

    - Penicillins
    - Cephalosporins
    - Macrolides
    - Tetracyclines
    - Antituberculosis drugs

  - Other Causes

    - Autoimmune disease
    - Collagen vascular disease
    - Malignancy

  - Idiopathic
    - >50% of cases have no identifiable cause

- Differential Diagnosis

  - Stevens–Johnson Syndrome (SJS)

    - Macular or atypical target lesions
    - Rapid progression to desquamation/epidermal detachment
    - More extensive mucosal involvement

  - Erythema Multiforme

    - Typical target lesions
    - <10% epidermal detachment
    - Usually infection-related

- Diagnosis

  - Primarily clinical

  - Investigations when indicated

    - Recurrent disease
      - HSV serology

    - Suspected Mycoplasma infection
      - Respiratory swab
      - Chest X-ray

    - Uncertain diagnosis
      - Skin biopsy

- Histopathology
  - Perivascular mononuclear cell infiltrate
  - Vacuolisation of basal cell layer
  - Dermal oedema
  - Lichenoid interface dermatitis
  - Numerous necrotic keratinocytes
  - Epidermal changes mainly in centre of target lesion
  - Subepidermal bullae formation

- Management
  - Treat underlying infection
  - Discontinue causative medication
  - Skin Care
    - Soap-free washes
    - Emollients
  - Symptomatic Treatment
    - Oral antihistamines for pruritus
  - Oral Mucosal Care
    - Lignocaine 2% mouth rinse
    - White soft paraffin for lips
  - HSV-Associated Disease
    - Oral aciclovir effective for acute disease
    - Also reduces recurrence
- Indications for Hospitalisation
  - Severe oral involvement causing:
    - Reduced oral intake
    - Dehydration
    - Electrolyte disturbance
    - Malnutrition

- Severe Disease

  - Prednisone
    - Continue until clinical improvement

- Ocular Involvement

  - Findings
    - Eye injection
    - Oedema

  - Complications
    - Keratitis
    - Visual impairment

  - Management
    - Ophthalmology referral if suspected

- Complications

  - Dehydration
  - Electrolyte abnormalities
  - Malnutrition
  - Keratitis
  - Visual impairment
  - Skin scarring
  - Urethral strictures
  - Vaginal strictures

- Prognosis

  - Usually self-limiting
  - Low morbidity
  - Most patients recover completely

- Prevention of Recurrence

  - Eliminate precipitating factor where possible

  - Recurrent HSV-Induced EM
    - Indication
      - ≥6 episodes/year

    - Antiviral prophylaxis for 6 months
      - Valaciclovir OR Aciclovir

    - Dermatology referral if prophylaxis ineffective
