## Definition
- Giant cell arteritis
	- granulomatous vasculitis of medium- and large-sized arteries, especially the extracranial branches of the carotid artery
- AKA
- Temporal arteritis

## association
- PMR precedes GCA

## Etiology
- No definite cause

## epidemiology
- women
- Age >50 years
- PMR typical age
	- 60–70 years
- Rare <50 years

## pathology
- most commonly affected arteries
	- Superficial temporal artery
	- Ophthalmic artery
	- Vertebral artery
	- Aorta and its major branches

## Clinical features
- Polymyalgia rheumatica
	- Proximal muscle symmetrical painful stiffness
		- sites affected
			- Shoulder girdle
			- Pelvic girdle
			- Cervical spine
		- Early morning stiffness
			- \>45 minutes
	- Systemic features
		- Malaise
		- Fever
		- Anorexia
		- Weight loss
	- Signs may be absent later in day
- Temporal arteritis
	- unilateral throbbing headache
	- Diplopia
	- Jaw claudication
	- Temporal artery
		- tenderness
		- Loss of pulsation
## Investigations
- ESR raised
	- PMR
		- \>40 or normal
	- GCA
		- \>50
- CRP elevated
- Mild normocytic normochromic anaemia
- diagnostic test
	- Giant cell arterities
		- Temporal artery biopsy
			- ~5 cm
	- for Polymyalgia rheumatica
		- No specific test

## Treatment
- if Giant cell arteries with visual symptoms
	- IV prednisolone
	- then oral prednisolone
	- else
		- Prednisolone + aspirin
		- tapper based on ESR / CRP
- Polymyalgia rheumatica
	- Prednisolone
- other
	- Azathioprine
	- Methotrexate

## Complications
- Irreversible blindness
- Stroke

 ## Prognosis
- Relapses common
