
- Pathophysiology
	- Panic + breath holding
	- Water aspiration
		- Laryngospasm
		- Asphyxia
	- Loss of consciousness
		- Water enters lungs
	- Pulmonary water
		- V/Q mismatch
		- Hypoxaemia
	- Severe hypoxia
		- Metabolic acidosis
		- Cerebral oedema
		- Acute tubular necrosis
	- Hypothermia
		- Common
		- Neuroprotective effect
			- Especially children

- Important differences from other cardiac arrest
	- Respiratory arrest occurs before cardiac arrest
	- Severe metabolic acidosis common

- Management
	- Immediate CPR
		- Mainstay
	- Continue resuscitation
		- >30 min if required
		- Especially if hypothermic
	- Successfully resuscitated patients
		- 100% oxygen
		- Transfer to hospital

- Assess for aspiration
	- Historical clues
		- Prolonged submersion
		- Choking/inhalation
		- Loss of consciousness
		- Apnoea
		- CPR required

	- Clinical features
		- Cough
		- Dyspnoea
		- Retrosternal discomfort
		- Cyanosis
		- Tachycardia
		- Tachypnoea
		- Wheeze/crackles
		- Pink frothy sputum
		- Reduced consciousness
		- Low oxygen saturation
	- Aspiration may occur despite initially normal chest X-ray
	- If aspiration suspected
		- 100% oxygen
		- Hospital transfer
