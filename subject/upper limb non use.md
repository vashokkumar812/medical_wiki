## Key Points
- Most common cause is trauma (eg pulled elbow)
- Give analgesia early
- Fever or systemic features are red flags
- Consider child abuse if history unclear or findings concerning

---

## Background
Most common causes:
- Pulled elbow
- Upper limb fractures
- Clavicle fractures

Toddlers may present with limb non-use without witnessed trauma.

Consider:
- Infection
- Child abuse

Neonates and children with underlying conditions (bleeding disorders, haemoglobinopathies) require careful review.

---

## Assessment

### History
- Trauma history and mechanism
- History of arm “pull”
- Refusal to move arm without injury
- Pain present or absent
- Location of symptoms
- Acute or subacute onset
- Duration ≥7 days
- Systemic features:
  - Fever
  - Night sweats
  - Malaise
  - Rash
  - Pallor
  - Bruising
- Neurological symptoms:
  - Headache
  - Seizures
  - Paraesthesia
  - Weakness
- Constitutional symptoms:
  - Weight loss
  - Lethargy
  - Anorexia
- Past medical conditions:
  - Bleeding disorders
  - Sickle cell disease

---

### Examination
- General appearance (well/unwell, distress)
- Fever
- Fully expose upper limb

#### Look – Feel – Move (compare both sides)

**Look**
- Resting position
- Spontaneous movement
- Swelling
- Deformity
- Open wounds
- Erythema
- Bruising
- Rash

**Feel**
- Palpate clavicle to fingers
- Tenderness
- Temperature change
- Swelling
- Joint effusion

**Move**
- Active movement (encourage with toy/parent)
- Passive movement
- Assess limitation/asymmetry

- Neurovascular assessment
- Consider systemic examination if no trauma history

---

## Differential Diagnosis

### Trauma

**Pulled elbow**
- Often no history of pull
- Elbow extended, forearm pronated
- Distress with elbow movement
- Pain with supination

**Upper limb fracture**
- Pain and reluctance to move
- Swelling, tenderness, deformity

**Clavicle fracture**
- Fall onto outstretched hand
- Pain/swelling along clavicle

**Soft tissue injury**
- History of injury

---

### Infection

**Septic arthritis**
- Fever
- Severe localised pain
- Hot, swollen, immobile joint

**Osteomyelitis**
- May lack systemic features
- Subacute limb non-use
- Poorly localised pain

---

### Malignancy
- Leukaemia
- Bone or soft tissue malignancy
- Solid organ tumour

Features:
- Fever
- Weight loss
- Lethargy
- Fatigue
- Night sweats
- Nocturnal pain
- Pallor
- Bruising
- Hepatosplenomegaly or abdominal mass

---

### Pain Syndrome

**Complex regional pain syndrome**
- Allodynia
- Hyperalgesia
- Swelling
- Skin colour change
- Dry mottled skin
- Hyperhidrosis
- Consider psychosocial trigger

---

### Neurological

**Brachial plexus neuropathy**
- Birth trauma or fractures
- Reduced arm movement
- Muscle wasting
- Altered sensation

**Stroke**
- Focal weakness
- Speech/visual disturbance
- Ataxia

---

### Rheumatological / Immunological

**Juvenile idiopathic arthritis**
- Fever
- Rash
- Oligo/polyarthritis
- Enthesitis
- Psoriasis
- Haematuria
- Hepatosplenomegaly

**Reactive arthritis**
- Recent infection
- Onset 7–14 days later
- Oligo/monoarthritis

---

### Child Abuse
- No adequate injury history
- Mechanism inconsistent with development

---

## Special Considerations
- Minor trauma → haemarthrosis in bleeding disorders
- Sickle cell disease → vaso-occlusive crisis
- Neonates → brachial plexus injury ± clavicle/humerus fracture after birth trauma

---

## Management

### Investigations
- No investigations if high suspicion of pulled elbow
- X-ray of suspected area
- X-ray may be normal early in septic arthritis or osteomyelitis
- Blood tests:
  - FBE
  - CRP
  - ESR
- Raised inflammatory markers suggest infection/inflammation
- FBE/film may suggest malignancy
- Ultrasound for joint effusion
- MRI or bone scan after specialist consultation

---

### Treatment
- Management based on working diagnosis
- Early analgesia:
  - Paracetamol ± NSAID
- Escalating analgesia → reassess
- Child-led activity restriction (pulled elbow/soft tissue injury)
- Splint suspected fractures
- Septic arthritis = orthopaedic emergency
- Specialist consultation for specific diagnoses

---

## Consult Paediatrics When
- Systemically unwell
- Suspected septic arthritis
- Features suggesting malignancy
- Concerns for child abuse
- Symptoms ≥7 days
- Persistent or progressive symptoms without diagnosis

---

## Transfer
- Required care exceeds local service capability

---

## Discharge Criteria
- Cause identified and managed

OR

- Child using arm more comfortably
- High-risk diagnoses considered
- Clear follow-up plan
- Caregivers understand when to seek review
