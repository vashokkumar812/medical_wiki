###  first **exclude**

| Feature | LVH | HOCM |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Primary cause | systemic hypertension, aortic stenosis, physiological change in strength-based athletes, Fabry disease and cardiac amyloidosis | Genetic cardiomyopathy |
| Hypertrophy pattern | Concentric | Asymmetric septal |
| LVOT obstruction | Absent | Present (dynamic) |
| SAM of mitral valve | Absent | Present |
| LV cavity size | Normal / enlarged | Small |
| Murmur with Valsalva/standing | No change | Increases |
| Family history | Usually absent | Often present |
| Sudden cardiac death risk | Low | High |
|  |  |  |



- Info

	- Risk Factors for Sudden Cardiac Death (SCD)
		- family history of SCD
		- unexplained syncope
		- documented non-sustained ventricular tachycardia
		- maximal LV wall thickness ≥30 mm

	- Screening
		- first-degree relatives
			- do
				- ECG
				- physical examination
				- echocardiography

		- frequency
			- age 10–20 years
				- every 12 months
			- age 20–30 years
				- every 2 years
			- age >30 years
				- every 3 years

		- if no pathogenic mutation identified
			- remove from surveillance

	- Prognosis
		- better prognosis
			- >100% age-predicted and gender-predicted metabolic equivalent achieved

		- poor prognosis
			- hyperintense myocardial areas on late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) MRI sequence

	- Symptoms / Clinical Features
		- exertional syncope
			- collapse during exercise due to arrhythmia

		- mitral regurgitation
		- ventricular diastolic dysfunction
		- ventricular systolic dysfunction

		- murmur
			- ejection systolic murmur
				- resembles aortic stenosis
				- no radiation
				- worsens with Valsalva manoeuvre
					- suggests HOCM

	- Diagnosis

		- Echocardiography
			- repeat every 5 years

			- assess
				- LV systolic function
					- usually normal
				- LV hypertrophy pattern
				- LVOT obstruction
					- at rest
					- with Valsalva manoeuvre
				- mitral regurgitation
					- due to systolic anterior motion (SAM) of mitral valve
				- subaortic septal hypertrophy
					- pushes mitral valve
					- mimics aortic stenosis

		- Exercise Stress Echo
			- detects exercise-induced LVOT obstruction

		- ECG
			- large QRS voltages
			- ST segment repolarisation abnormalities
			- T wave abnormalities

			- differentiation from athlete’s heart
				- may be difficult in young athletes

		- 24-Hour Holter Monitoring
			- detects subclinical arrhythmias

		- Cardiac MRI (CMRI)
			- indications
				- ventricular volumetric assessment
				- myocardial fibrosis assessment

- Management

	- Lifestyle Modifications
		- allowed
			- moderate-intensity exercise
				- jogging
				- cycling
				- swimming
				- golf

		- avoid
			- high-intensity sports
				- squash
				- football

		- adolescents
			- refer to paediatric cardiologist

		- any adolescent with
			- cardiovascular symptoms
			- systolic murmur
			- family history of cardiomyopathy
			- family history of SCD
				- GP should perform
					- ECG
					- echocardiography

	- Pharmacological Treatment
		- if symptomatic
			- beta blockers
				- metoprolol
				- atenolol
			- verapamil

	- Surgery / Procedures
		- if symptomatic + peak LVOT gradient ≥50 mmHg
			- septal reduction therapy

		- if ≥1 risk factor for SCD
			- implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD)

	- Avoid
		- no routine infective endocarditis prophylaxis
		- avoid digoxin
			- may worsen LVOT obstruction
			- may decrease ejection fraction
