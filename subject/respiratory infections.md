# bronchiolitis

- Definition

	- Usually affects infants <12 months
	- Most commonly caused by respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)

	- Self-limiting illness



- Epidemiology
	- Resolution
		- Usually within 7–10 days
	- Cough may persist for several weeks


- Risk factors for severe disease
	- Prematurity (<37 weeks)
	- Age <10 weeks
	- Chronic lung disease
	- Congenital heart disease
	- Chronic neurological disease
	- Immunodeficiency
	- Growth restriction
	- Trisomy 21
	- Indigenous ethnicity
	- Exposure to cigarette smoke

- Clinical features
	- Coryzal prodrome
		- Rhinorrhoea
		- Nasal congestion
		- Mild fever
	- Followed by
		- Cough
		- Tachypnoea
		- Increased work of breathing
		- Difficulty feeding
		- Wheeze
		- Fine crackles
		- Irritability
		- Apnoea
			- Especially in young infants

- Examination
	- Tachypnoea
	- Nasal flaring
	- Chest recession
	- Accessory muscle use
	- Bilateral wheeze
	- Fine inspiratory crackles
	- Hypoxia
	- Dehydration
	- Apnoea in severe disease

- Severity assessment
	- Mild
		- Mild work of breathing
		- Feeding well
		- SpO₂ ≥90%
	- Moderate
		- Increased work of breathing
		- Reduced oral intake
		- Mild dehydration
	- Severe
		- Persistent hypoxia
		- Marked respiratory distress
		- Exhaustion
		- Poor feeding
		- Apnoea
		- Cyanosis

- Diagnosis
	- Clinical diagnosis
	- Based on
		- Age
		- Typical history
		- Physical examination

- Investigations
	- Usually not required
	- Chest X-ray
		- Not routinely indicated
		- Does not distinguish viral from bacterial infection
		- May lead to unnecessary antibiotic use
	- Blood tests
		- Not indicated
			- FBE
			- Blood cultures
			- Blood gas
	- Viral testing
		- Not routinely indicated
		- Does not alter management
	- Investigate only if
		- Clinical deterioration
		- Diagnostic uncertainty
		- Suspected alternative diagnosis

- Management
	- Supportive care
		- Minimal handling
		- Maintain oxygenation
		- Maintain hydration
		- Continue comfort oral feeds if tolerated

	- Oxygen therapy
		- Target SpO₂
			- ≥90%
			- Higher targets only if underlying cardiopulmonary disease
		- Brief desaturations <90%
			- Common
			- Do not require oxygen
		- Persistent SpO₂ <90%
			- Low-flow nasal prong oxygen
		- Persistent SpO₂ <90% after 2 hours of low-flow oxygen
			- Escalate to high-flow nasal prong (HFNP)
		- HFNP
			- Not indicated if SpO₂ consistently ≥90%
		- Wean oxygen
			- Use lowest oxygen required
			- Trial off every 6 hours
			- Restart if persistent SpO₂ <90%
		- Stop continuous saturation monitoring
			- After 2 hours off supplemental oxygen
			- Continue routine clinical observations

	- Hydration
		- Continue oral feeding if possible
		- Nasogastric hydration
			- Preferred if oral intake inadequate
			- Safe during respiratory support
			- Start bolus feeds at two-thirds maintenance
		- Intravenous fluids
			- Only if NG feeding not possible
			- Use isotonic fluid with glucose

	- Medications
		- Recommended
			- Paracetamol
			- Ibuprofen if appropriate age
		- Chloral hydrate
			- May be considered for severe agitation with respiratory distress
			- Usually <2 years and <15 kg
			- Requires close monitoring
		- Not recommended
			- Salbutamol
			- Other beta-2 agonists
			- Corticosteroids
				- Oral
				- IV
				- IM
				- Nebulised
			- Nebulised adrenaline
			- Nebulised hypertonic saline
			- Antibiotics
			- Antivirals
			- Caffeine
			- Routine nebulised therapies

	- Nasal suction
		- Gentle superficial suction may help feeding
		- Nasal saline drops before feeds may be useful
		- Deep suction not recommended

	- Chest physiotherapy
		- Not indicated


# Croup
- Viral inflammation
	- Larynx
	- Trachea
- Most common cause
	- Parainfluenza virus
- Common age
	- 6 months–6 years
- Often worse at night

- Assessment
    - Principles
        - Focused examination only
        - Avoid upsetting child
        - Keep child with caregiver
        - Oximetry/BP
            - Not required if distressing
        - Throat examination
            - Rarely required

- History

    - Risk factors for severe croup
        - Previous severe croup
        - Pre-existing upper airway narrowing
        - Reduced airway tone
            - Trisomy 21
            - Neuromuscular disorders
        - Young age
            - <6 months uncommon
            - <3 months rare
                - Consider alternative diagnosis

- Examination

    - Barking cough
    - Stridor
    - Hoarse voice/cry
    - ± Widespread wheeze
    - Increased work of breathing
    - ± Fever
    - No toxic appearance

- Differential diagnosis
    - Anaphylaxis
    - Inhaled foreign body
    - Bacterial infection
    - Retropharyngeal abscess
    - Peritonsillar abscess
    - Bacterial tracheitis
    - Epiglottitis
    - Airway burns/trauma

- Investigations
    - Clinical diagnosis
    - No routine
        - Swabs
        - X-rays
        - Blood tests
    - Investigate only if alternative diagnosis suspected

- General measures
    - Minimise distress
    - Keep child with carers
    - Quiet environment
    - Moderate lighting
    - Allow position of comfort

    - Oxygen
        - Usually not required
        - If required
            - Treat as severe airway obstruction

- Medication

    - Mild croup
        - Barking cough only
            - No steroids required
        - If stridor/risk factors present
            - Dexamethasone
            - Or prednisolone

    - Moderate croup
        - Dexamethasone
            - Or prednisolone
        - Consider nebulised adrenaline if worsening

    - Severe croup
        - Senior review
        - High acuity area
        - Nebulised adrenaline
        - Dexamethasone

    - Life-threatening croup
        - Resuscitation area
        - Immediate senior staff involvement
        - Nebulised adrenaline
        - 100% oxygen via non-rebreather mask
        - Prepare for intubation
            - Experienced clinician
            - Consider smaller/croup ETT
        - Dexamethasone
        - Repeat adrenaline until airway secured

- Disposition

    - Discharge criteria
        - No stridor at rest
        - Observed ≥3 hours after adrenaline

    - Observe >3 hours if
        - Overnight presentation
        - Limited healthcare access
        - Recurrent stridor during same illness
        - Risk factors for severe croup

- Consult paediatrics if
    - Severe airway obstruction
    - Diagnostic uncertainty
    - Severe risk factors
    - Age <6 months
    - No improvement after adrenaline

- Transfer indications
    - No improvement after adrenaline
    - Repeated adrenaline needed
    - Higher level care required

- Parent advice
    - Return if stridor at rest recurs despite steroids

- Severity assessment
    - Loud stridor does not equal severe obstruction
    - Soft stridor with worsening signs
        - Suggests imminent airway obstruction

| Feature | Mild | Moderate | Severe | Life-threatening |
|----------|--------|------------|------------|------------------|
| Appearance | Normal | Normal | Pale | Pale/mottled/cyanosed |
| Behaviour | Alert | Alert ± mild agitation | Agitated or drowsy | Confused/drowsy/agitated ± drooling |
| Stridor | None or when upset | Intermittent at rest | Persistent at rest/biphasic | Biphasic or soft |
| RR | Normal | Increased | Marked ↑ or ↓ | Abnormal, exhaustion |
| Accessory use | None/minimal | Intercostal/subcostal + tracheal tug | Abdominal breathing + marked recession | Severe sternal recession, poor effort |
| SpO₂ | Normal | Normal | Late hypoxia | Late hypoxia |

# acute bronchitis
- definition
	- acute cough
		- usually <14 days initially
		- may persist up to 3 weeks
- exclude other causes
	- asthma
	- COPD
	- pneumonia

- cause
	- usually viral

- clinical features
	- cough
		- may persist up to 3 weeks
	- sputum production
	- rhinitis
	- sore throat
	- wheeze
		- usually settles after infection resolves
		- persistent/recurrent wheeze
			- perform spirometry
	- chest discomfort
	- mild dyspnoea

- investigations
	- usually clinical diagnosis
	- chest X-ray indicated if
		- heart rate >100/min
		- respiratory rate >24/min
		- temperature >38°C
		- night sweats
		- focal chest signs

- treatment
	- reassurance
	- supportive care
		- fluids
		- rest
		- antipyretics/analgesics if required

	- antibiotics
		- usually not recommended
		- consider only if
			- very unwell
			- older patient (>55 years)
			- significant comorbidities
			- smoker
		- before prescribing
			- discuss with patient
			- exclude pneumonia

	- not routinely effective
		- bronchodilators
		- cough suppressants
