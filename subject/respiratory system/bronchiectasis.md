## Bronchiectasis

- Chronic obstructive lung disease characterised by:
  - Permanent dilatation of airways
  - Increased sputum production
  - Recurrent infective exacerbations
  - Impaired quality of life

## Aetiology

- Pulmonary infections
  - Tuberculosis
- Cystic fibrosis
- Primary ciliary dyskinesia
- Rheumatic diseases
- Autoimmune diseases
- Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA)
- Young syndrome

## Pathogenesis

- Vicious cycle hypothesis:
  - Airway distortion
    - Impaired mucociliary clearance
      - Persistent bacterial colonisation
        - Chronic airway inflammation
          - Progressive airway destruction
            - Further airway dilatation

## Clinical Features

- Primary symptoms
  - Chronic cough
  - Increased sputum production over months to years
- Other symptoms
  - Dyspnoea
  - Occasional haemoptysis
  - Dry cough
  - Pleuritic chest pain
- Recurrent infective exacerbations
  - Repeated episodes requiring antibiotic treatment

## Diagnosis and Investigations

- Diagnosis involves:
  - Radiological confirmation
  - Assessment of disease severity
  - Investigation of underlying causes and associated diseases

### Imaging

- Chest X-ray
  - Initial imaging modality
  - Helps exclude other causes of respiratory symptoms
- High-resolution CT (HRCT) chest
  - Imaging modality of choice
  - Characteristic findings:
    - Dilated airways larger than accompanying blood vessels
    - Tram-track appearance (parallel lines)
    - Signet-ring appearance (end-on ring shadows)
    - Lack of normal airway tapering
    - Tree-in-bud appearance
      - Peripheral irregular short linear branching markings
      - Represents small airway involvement
    - Mucopurulent plugs or debris
    - Post-obstructive air trapping
  - Persistent tree-in-bud changes suggest nontuberculous mycobacterial infection

### Laboratory Investigations

- Full blood examination
- Biochemistry
- C-reactive protein (CRP)
- Aspergillus serology
- Autoimmune screen
- Immunoglobulin levels
  - IgG
  - IgA
  - IgM
  - IgE
- Sputum microscopy and culture
  - Bacteria
  - Mycobacteria
  - Nontuberculous mycobacteria

### Additional Investigations

- Sweat test
- Extended CFTR gene mutation testing
  - Consider in all children
  - Consider in selected adults:
    - Age <50 years
    - Bowel obstruction
    - Heat prostration
    - Pancreatitis
    - Pre-existing liver disease
    - Male infertility

### Functional Assessment

- Spirometry
  - May be normal
  - Obstructive pattern
  - Restrictive pattern
  - Mixed obstructive and restrictive pattern
  - Obstruction demonstrated by reduced FEV1
  - FVC may also be reduced
- Diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO)
- Six-minute walk test

## Referral

- Newly diagnosed patients should be referred to a multidisciplinary team:
  - Respiratory specialist
  - Physiotherapist
  - Dietitian
- Flexible bronchoscopy may be considered if:
  - Suspected mycobacterial infection
  - Suspected focal obstructive lesion
- Rheumatic or autoimmune disease
  - Refer to the respective specialty

## Treatment

- Main objectives:
  - Investigate and manage underlying causes
  - Improve sputum clearance
  - Reduce infective exacerbations
  - Improve quality of life

### Non-pharmacological Management

- Chest physiotherapy
  - Improves sputum clearance
  - Reduces recurrent exacerbations
- Airway clearance techniques
  - Bubble-positive expiratory pressure (Bubble PEP)
  - Positive expiratory pressure (PEP) therapy
  - Active cycle of breathing technique
  - ELTGOL (slow expiration with glottis opened in lateral posture)
    - Twice daily
    - Improves sputum clearance
    - Reduces exacerbations and cough impact
- Avoid lung irritants
  - Smoking
  - Vaping
  - Occupational chemicals
- Optimise nutritional status
  - Low BMI associated with:
    - Increased exacerbations
    - Chronic Pseudomonas aeruginosa colonisation
    - Poorer outcomes
- Pulmonary rehabilitation
  - Improves exercise capacity
  - Improves health-related quality of life
- Immunisation
  - COVID-19
  - Influenza
  - Pneumococcal vaccination

### Pharmacological Management

- Acute exacerbations
  - Oral antibiotics for 14 days
  - Obtain sputum culture and sensitivity
  - Tailor antibiotics according to organism and sensitivity
  - New isolation of Pseudomonas aeruginosa
    - Offer eradication antibiotic treatment
    - May require up to 3 months of antibiotics
  - Hypoxia or failure to respond to oral antibiotics
    - Refer to emergency department
    - Intravenous antibiotics
    - Supplemental oxygen as required

### Long-term Antibiotic Therapy

- Consider long-term macrolides in patients with ≥3 exacerbations/year
  - Azithromycin
  - Erythromycin
- Benefits:
  - Reduces frequency of infective exacerbations
  - Generally well tolerated

### Mucolytics

- Consider long-term mucolytics (≥3 months) in patients with:
  - Poor quality of life
  - Difficulty expectorating sputum
  - Failure of standard airway clearance techniques

### Inhaled Medications

- Inhaled corticosteroids
  - Not routinely indicated
  - Use only if underlying asthma or COPD
- Long-acting bronchodilators
  - Not routinely indicated
  - Use if underlying asthma or COPD
- Bronchodilators, inhaled mucoactive medications and inhaled antibiotics
  - May be used before physiotherapy
  - Improve tolerability and pulmonary drug deposition

### Inhaled Antibiotics

- Benefits:
  - Reduce bacterial load
  - Small reduction in exacerbation frequency
  - No clinically significant improvement in quality of life demonstrated
- Liposomal ciprofloxacin
  - ORBIT-4:
    - Longer median time to first infective exacerbation compared with placebo
  - ORBIT-3:
    - Benefit was not demonstrated
- Ciprofloxacin dry powder inhalation
  - RESPIRE 1:
    - Reduced exacerbation frequency in patients with frequent exacerbations and positive sputum pathogens

## Key Management Principles

- Identify and manage underlying causes
- Maintain effective airway clearance
- Treat acute exacerbations with appropriate antibiotics
- Reduce recurrent exacerbations
- Optimise nutritional status
- Provide pulmonary rehabilitation and vaccinations
- Improve long-term respiratory function and quality of life
