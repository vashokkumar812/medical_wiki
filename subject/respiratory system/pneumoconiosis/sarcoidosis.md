Multisystem granulomatous disease
  - Characterised by non-caseating granulomas
  - Most commonly affects
    - Lungs
    - Skin
    - Eyes
    - Lymph nodes

- Histopathology
  - Non-caseating granulomas
  - Non-necrotising granulomas
  - Epithelioid histiocytes
  - Multinucleated giant cells
  - Sparse lymphocytic infiltrate
  - "Naked granulomas"

- Histological Differential Diagnosis
  - Tuberculosis
  - Other granulomatous infections
  - Foreign body reaction
  - Drug reaction
  - Lymphoma
  - Lymphomatoid granulomatosis
  - ANCA-associated vasculitis
  - Rheumatoid nodules
  - Inflammatory bowel disease

- Clinical Features

  - Constitutional
    - Fever
    - Night sweats
    - Weight loss
    - Fatigue

  - Pulmonary
    - Cough
    - Dyspnoea
    - Reticulonodular infiltrates
    - Upper lobe involvement

  - Cutaneous
    - Infiltrated plaques
    - Maculopapular eruption
    - Subcutaneous nodules
    - Scar sarcoidosis
    - Lupus pernio

  - Ophthalmic
    - Uveitis

- Cutaneous Manifestations

  - Specific Lesions
    - Histology shows granulomas

    - Lupus pernio
      - Pathognomonic

    - Infiltrated plaques

    - Maculopapular lesions

    - Subcutaneous nodules

    - Scar sarcoidosis

  - Non-specific Lesions

    - Erythema nodosum
      - Most common non-specific lesion

    - Calcinosis cutis

    - Sweet syndrome

    - Nail clubbing

    - Erythema multiforme

- Initial Investigation

  - Skin biopsy
    - Punch biopsy of lesion

- Most Appropriate Investigation

  - Biopsy demonstrating non-caseating granulomas

- Systemic Work-up

  - Chest X-ray
  - Pulmonary function tests
  - ECG
  - Ophthalmology assessment
  - Urinalysis

  - Blood tests
    - FBE
    - Calcium
    - Electrolytes
    - Urea
    - Creatinine
    - LFTs
    - ACE level

  - Exclude tuberculosis
    - Tuberculin skin test
    - Interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA)

- Chest X-ray Findings
  - Bilateral reticulonodular infiltrates
  - Pulmonary sarcoidosis

- HRCT Chest Findings
  - Reticulonodular interstitial changes
  - Upper lobe predominance
  - Right middle lobe involvement
  - Lingular involvement

- Laboratory Findings
  - Elevated ACE level
  - Hypercalcaemia may occur
  - Normal FBC possible
  - Normal renal and liver function possible

- Initial Treatment

  - Limited cutaneous disease
    - Ultra-potent topical corticosteroids
    - Intralesional triamcinolone

- Most Appropriate Treatment

  - Localised disease
    - Intralesional corticosteroids

  - Extensive cutaneous disease
    - Hydroxychloroquine

- Severe Disease

  - Oral corticosteroids

- Refractory Disease

  - Methotrexate
  - Chloroquine
  - Hydroxychloroquine
  - Tetracyclines
  - Infliximab

- Additional Treatment

  - Laser therapy
    - Resistant disfiguring lesions

- Prognosis

  - Up to two-thirds undergo spontaneous remission
