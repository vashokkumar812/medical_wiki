

- Common sites
	- Knee
	- Hip
	- Hand
		- DIP (Heberden nodes)
		- PIP (Bouchard nodes)
		- First CMC joint
	- Cervical spine
	- Lumbar spine

- Clinical features
	- Joint pain
		- Worse with activity
		- Relieved by rest
	- Morning stiffness
		- <30 minutes
	- Reduced joint mobility
	- Crepitus
	- Bony enlargement
	- Mild joint swelling
	- Functional limitation

- Examination
	- Reduced ROM
	- Crepitus
	- Bony tenderness
	- Osteophytes
	- Mild effusion
	- Muscle wasting (advanced disease)

	- Diagnosis
		- Primarily clinical
			- History + examination


	- Initial investigations
		- Usually none required if classical presentation
		- Weight-bearing X-ray (if diagnosis uncertain or surgical planning)
			- Joint space narrowing
			- Osteophytes
			- Subchondral sclerosis
			- Subchondral cysts
		- Blood tests
			- Only if alternative diagnosis suspected
- treatment

	- Non-pharmacological management (first-line)


		- Weight loss


		- Exercise


		- Physiotherapy
		- Occupational therapy
		- Not routinely recommended
			- Therapeutic ultrasound
			- Electromagnetic therapy
			- Knee braces/orthoses (routine use)
			- Magnetic bracelets

	- Pharmacological management

		- First-line
			- Regular paracetamol

		- Topical therapy
			- Topical NSAIDs
			- Capsaicin

		- Oral NSAIDs
		

		- Intra-articular corticosteroid
			- Acute flare
			- Short-term relief (~4 weeks)

		- Viscosupplementation
			- Knee OA only
			- Hyaluronic acid injections

		- Opioids


		- not useful Supplements
			- Glucosamine
			- Chondroitin
			- Vitamins/herbal therapies


	- Acute flare management
		- Rest
		- Ice packs
		- Optimise analgesia
		- Topical NSAID
		- Short course oral NSAID
		- Intra-articular corticosteroid if indicated
		- Physiotherapy

	- Surgical indications
		- Persistent severe pain
		- Significant functional limitation
		- Failure of optimal conservative therapy
		- Refer for orthopaedic assessment
			- Total hip replacement
			- Total knee replacement

	- Follow-up
		- Review every 6 months

- Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)
	- Indications ⭐⭐⭐
		- Major depression
			- Resistant to antidepressants/mood stabilisers
			- High suicide risk ⭐
			- Antidepressants contraindicated
				- Pregnancy ⭐
				- Poor drug tolerance
			- Previous good response to ECT
	- Adverse effects
		- Transient memory loss ⭐
			- Worsens during treatment
			- Resolves over weeks
		- Anaesthetic complications
		- Transient ↑ intracranial pressure
	- Contraindications
		- No absolute contraindications
		- Relative
			- Raised ICP
			- Space-occupying intracranial lesion ⭐

- Mood stabilisers

	- Lithium ⭐⭐⭐
		- Uses
			- Bipolar disorder
				- First-line maintenance ⭐
				- Prophylaxis of mood episodes
			- Schizoaffective disorder
			- Augmentation in resistant depression
		- Therapeutic level
			- Target
				- ~1.0 mmol/L
			- Therapeutic
				- 0.6–1.2 mmol/L
			- Toxicity
				- >1.5 mmol/L ⭐
		- Adverse effects
			- Dose-related
				- Fine tremor ⭐
				- GI upset
				- Headache
			- Dermatological
				- Acne
			- Weight gain
			- Cardiac
				- ECG changes
			- Endocrine
				- Hypothyroidism ⭐
			- Renal
				- Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus ⭐
				- Polyuria
				- Chronic nephrotoxicity
			- Haematological
				- Leukocytosis
			- Pregnancy
				- Ebstein anomaly ⭐
		- Toxicity
			- Predisposed by ⭐
				- Dehydration
				- Hyponatraemia
			- Increased by
				- ACE inhibitors
				- NSAIDs
				- Thiazides
				- Loop diuretics
		- Monitoring ⭐
			- Serum lithium
			- Renal function
			- Thyroid function
			- Pregnancy test before initiation

	- Sodium valproate (Divalproex) ⭐
		- Uses
			- Rapid-cycling bipolar ⭐
			- Acute mania
			- When lithium unsuitable
		- Notes
			- Loading dose possible
			- Similar onset to lithium
			- Prophylactic benefit less established
		- Adverse effects
			- Sedation
			- Cognitive impairment
			- Tremor
			- GI upset
			- Hepatotoxicity ⭐
			- Weight gain
			- Alopecia
			- Teratogenicity
				- Neural tube defects (spina bifida) ⭐⭐⭐

	- Carbamazepine
		- Uses
			- Second-line bipolar disorder
		- Adverse effects
			- Agranulocytosis ⭐
			- Hepatotoxicity
			- Sedation

	- Lamotrigine ⭐
		- Uses
			- Bipolar depression ⭐
		- Adverse effect
			- Stevens–Johnson syndrome ⭐⭐⭐

- Anxiolytics

	- Benzodiazepines ⭐
		- MOA
			- Enhance GABA-A activity
		- Uses
			- Acute anxiety
			- Panic attacks
			- Alcohol withdrawal
			- Status epilepticus
		- Adverse effects
			- Sedation
			- Memory impairment
			- Confusion
			- Falls
			- Respiratory depression
				- Especially with alcohol/opioids
		- Important points ⭐
			- Avoid alcohol/sedatives
			- Dependence potential
			- Lower dose in elderly
			- Abrupt withdrawal
				- Seizures ⭐

	- Buspirone ⭐
		- MOA
			- Partial 5-HT1A agonist
		- Uses
			- Generalised anxiety disorder ⭐
			- Social phobia
		- Advantages
			- No sedation ⭐
			- No cognitive impairment
			- No dependence
			- No withdrawal syndrome ⭐
			- No additive effect with sedatives
		- Limitations
			- Delayed onset
				- ~1 week
		- Adverse effects
			- Headache
			- Dizziness
