- definition
	- bacterial infection of the bladder and urinary tract
	- most commonly caused by Escherichia coli

- classification
	- lower UTI (cystitis)
		- bladder infection
	- upper UTI (pyelonephritis)
		- kidney infection
		- associated with systemic illness

- recurrent UTI
	- ≥2 episodes of pyelonephritis
	- 1 episode of pyelonephritis + ≥1 episode of cystitis
	- ≥3 episodes of cystitis

- atypical UTI
	- seriously ill
	- septicaemia
	- poor response to antibiotics after 48 hours
	- organism other than E. coli
	- raised creatinine
	- poor urine flow
	- abdominal or bladder mass

- risk factors
	- infancy
	- vesicoureteric reflux
	- congenital urinary tract abnormalities
	- abnormal urinary stream
	- urinary catheterisation
	- renal stones
	- constipation
	- bladder dysfunction
	- neurogenic bladder
	- spina bifida

- symptoms

	- cystitis
		- dysuria
		- frequency
		- urgency
		- suprapubic pain
		- usually no systemic symptoms
	
	- pyelonephritis
		- fever
		- rigors
		- vomiting
		- malaise
		- loin pain
		- costovertebral angle tenderness

- urine collection
	- toilet-trained child
		- midstream urine
	- pre-continent child
		- clean catch
		- quick wee method
	- infant <6 months
		- suprapubic aspiration
			- gold standard
		- catheter specimen
	- bag urine
		- not recommended for culture

- investigations
	- urinalysis
		- leukocyte esterase
		- nitrites
	- urine microscopy
		- pyuria
		- bacteria
		- ==epithelial cells suggest contamination==
	- urine culture
		- confirms diagnosis
	- lumbar puncture
		- if meningitis suspected
		- febrile infant <4 weeks
	- renal ultrasound
		- atypical UTI
		- recurrent UTI in infants <6 months
		- no antenatal renal imaging
	- STI testing
		- sexually active adolescents

- dipstick interpretation
	- leukocyte esterase positive + nitrite positive
		- UTI likely
	- nitrite positive only
		- UTI likely
	- leukocyte esterase positive only
		- possible UTI
	- both negative
		- UTI unlikely
		- less reliable in infants <3 months

- send urine culture if
	- age <3 months
	- positive dipstick
	- suspected pyelonephritis
	- recurrent UTI
	- no improvement after 24–48 hours
	- strong clinical suspicion despite negative dipstick

- diagnosis
	- positive urine culture
	- pyuria on microscopy

- treatment
	- cystitis
		- oral antibiotics
		- 3–5 days
		- cefalexin commonly used
	- pyelonephritis
		- oral antibiotics if well
		- 7–10 days
		- cefalexin commonly used
	- seriously unwell
		- admit
		- IV antibiotics
		- gentamicin commonly used initially
		- switch to oral when improving

- admit if
	- age <3 months
	- sepsis
	- seriously unwell
	- unable to tolerate oral antibiotics
	- significant urinary tract abnormality

- follow-up
	- renal ultrasound
		- recurrent UTI
		- atypical UTI
	- specialist referral
		- recurrent UTI
		- renal tract abnormalities
	- routine antibiotic prophylaxis
		- generally not recommended
