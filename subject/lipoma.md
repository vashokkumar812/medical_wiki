* Definition  
  * Common benign tumour  
* Pathogenesis  
  * Composition  
    * Mature fat cells  
  * Location  
    * Subcutaneous tissue  
* Clinical features  
  * Soft consistency  
    * May be fluctuant  
    * Soft to rubbery  
  * Well-defined  
  * Lobulated  
  * Painless  
  * May be  
    * Single  
    * Multiple  
  * Common sites  
    * Limbs  
      * Especially arms  
    * Trunk  
    * Can occur at any site  
* Investigations  
  * Imaging  
    * Ultrasound  
      * Assesses depth  
    * CT / MRI  
      * For large or deep lipomas  
      * Helps diagnosis and delineation  
* Management  
  * General  
    * Reassurance about the benign nature  
  * Indications for surgery  
    * Cosmetic reasons  
    * Discomfort due to pressure  
  * Surgical excision  
    * Many lipomas  
      * Enucleated using a gloved finger  
* Important considerations/pitfalls  
  * May be deeper than expected  
  * May be adjacent to important structures  
    * Large nerves  
    * Blood vessels  
  * Some tethered  
    * By fibrous bands  
  * Risk of recurrence  
    * Especially if the excision is incomplete  
* Surgical care  
  * Ensure good haemostasis  
  * Layered closure  
    * Include subcutaneous tissue  
  * Prevent complications  
    * Haematoma  
    * Infection  
    * Scar dimpling  
* Cautions  
  * Lipomas on the back  
    * Difficult to shell out  
  * Size \> 5 cm  
    * Consider specialist referral
