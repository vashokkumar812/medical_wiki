- Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)
	- More commonly:
		- Lower abdominal pain
		- Altered bowel habit

	- Features
		- Bloating
		- Incomplete evacuation
		- Variable stool frequency/consistency

- GORD
	- Common cause of recurrent epigastric pain

	- Typical symptoms
		- Retrosternal burning
		- Regurgitation
		- Waterbrash

	- Associated with:
		- Asthma

	- Alarm symptoms requiring endoscopy
		- Dysphagia
		- Odynophagia
		- GI bleeding
		- Weight loss

	- Endoscopy indications
		- Alarm symptoms
		- Age >55 years
		- Diagnostic uncertainty
		- Treatment failure

	- PPIs
		- First-line treatment
		- Better than H2 blockers

	- Step-down therapy
		- Start with standard-dose PPI
		- Continue 4–8 weeks
		- Then reduce/withdraw if possible

	- Relapse
		- Common after stopping PPIs

- Helicobacter pylori
	- Prevalence in Australia
		- ~15–40%

	- Higher prevalence
		- Elderly
		- Lower socioeconomic groups
		- Indigenous communities
		- Immigrants from endemic regions

	- Testing
		- Urea breath test highly accurate
		- Stop antibiotics ≥4 weeks before testing
		- PPIs interfere with testing

	- Stool antigen testing
		- Requires proper specimen handling

	- Treatment principles
		- Compliance important
		- Warn regarding side effects
		- Previous clarithromycin use
			- Higher resistance risk

	- Eradication failure
		- Repeat treatment 10–14 days

	- Serology
		- Poor negative predictive value after treatment
		- Not useful to confirm eradication

	- H. pylori and GORD
		- Relationship controversial
		- Routine testing in GORD not recommended

- Gallstone disease

	- Often asymptomatic

- Biliary colic

	- Usually:
		- Afebrile
		- No systemic symptoms

	- Typical duration
		- 30 minutes–2 hours

- Pancreatitis
	- Severe epigastric pain lasting hours–days

	- Common causes
		- Gallstones
		- Alcohol

	- Diagnosis
		- Lipase >5× ULN

	- Imaging/lipase
		- May occasionally be nondiagnostic in chronic pancreatitis

	- Management
		- Hospital admission
		- Fluids
		- Analgesia

		- Anorexia
		- Jaundice
		- Insidious pain

	- Investigations

		- ERCP
		- MRI
