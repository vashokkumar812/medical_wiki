- Definition
	- Chronic inflammatory condition of the natal cleft in Sacrococcygeal region

- Etymology
	- Pilus
		- Hair
	- Nidus
		- Nest

- Epidemiology

	- Typical Patient Profile
		- Male
		- Age 15–40 years
		- Overweight
		- Hairy skin
		- Deep natal cleft
		- Poor hygiene
		- Sedentary lifestyle

	- Important Facts
		- Rare in children
		- Not congenital
		- Male : Female ≈ 2 : 1

- Pathogenesis
	- Primary pit formation
		- → debris accumulation
		- → hair penetration
		- → foreign body reaction
		- → sinus formation
		- → track extension ==> Depends on hair follicle orientation ==> Acute abscess ==> Chronic discharging sinus

- Pathology

	- Sinus Characteristics
		- Branching cavities
		- Lined by:
			- Granulation tissue
			- Macrophages
			- Foreign body giant cells

	- Hair present in:
		- 50–75%

- classification
	- Asymptomatic Midline Pits
		- clinical features
			- Small pits in natal cleft
			- No pain
			- No discharge

		- Management
			- Reassurance
			- Hygiene advice
	
		- prognosis
			- Often resolves by middle age

	- Acute Pilonidal Abscess
		- clinical features
			- Severe pain
			- Inability to sit
			- Tender swelling
			- Erythema
			- Fluctuant mass
	

		- Management of Acute Abscess
			- Treatment Options
				- Needle aspiration
				- Incision and drainage
					- Position
						- in general
							- Lateral position with buttock elevated
						- If midline abscess:
							- Prone position
					- Steps
						- Lightly shave area
						- Inject 1% lignocaine with adrenaline at thinnest skin point
						- Make small Vertical incision or Cruciate incision 1cm off midline
						- Open gently with artery forceps
						- Apply gauze dressing
						- Home Care
						- Simple dressing
						- Self-care possible
					- Avoid
						- Midline incision
						- Deroofing
						- Irrigate cavity
						- Pack cavity


					- Follow-Up After Abscess Drainage
						- Once swelling resolves
							- Primary pit may become visible
						- Prognosis
							- 40% have no recurrence
						- Patient Options
							- Observation
							- Surgical consultation
						- Hygiene Measures
							- Regular showers
							- Remove loose hairs
							- Weekly inspection
							- Tweezers removal of hair
			
						- Optional
							- Gauze between buttocks daily for 1 month
								- Aerates cleft
									- Drainage with curettage
		- Antibiotics
			- Not curative
			- Used only if:
				- Cellulitis
				- Immunocompromised patient
				- Systemic illness
			- Antibiotic Coverage
				- Should include anaerobes
			- Example
				- Flucloxacillin
					- If cellulitis present

	- Chronic Pilonidal Sinus
	
		- clinical features
			- Intermittent pain
			- Swelling
			- Chronic discharge
			- Recurrent abscesses
			- Midline pits
			- Sinus tract
	
	
		- Surgical Treatment
			- Goals
				- Remove sinus
				- Flatten natal cleft
				- Move scar away from midline
			- Minor Procedures
				- Pit Picking
					- Used for small sinus disease
				- Procedure
					- Remove pit using:
						- No.11 blade
						- Skin biopsy punch
				- Steps
					- Excise primary pit
					- Excise superficial abscess scar
					- Clean sinus tract
					- Close pit with single suture
					- Leave abscess opening to heal
				- Advantages
					- Quick recovery
					- Day surgery
					- Rapid return to work
				- Recurrence
					- ≈15%
				- Important Point
					- Procedure repeatable
			- Flap Procedures
				- Preferred for complex disease
				- Principles
					- Remove midline pit
					- Flatten natal cleft
					- Off-midline closure
				- Benefits
					- Better aeration
					- Easier hygiene
					- Reduced recurrence
				- types
					- Karydakis Flap
						- Procedure
							- Complete excision to sacral fascia
							- Lateralised skin flap closure
						- Features
							- Vertical lateral scar
							- Flattened cleft
						- Recurrence
							- ≈1–5%
					- Bascom Cleft Lift
						- Principles
							- Remove hair pits
							- Clean sinus cavity
							- Lateralise scar
						- Steps
							- Mini incision
								- <5 mm
							- Remove hair follicles
							- Debride cavity
							- Mobilise skin
								- Lateralise closure
					
						- Healing
							- ≈3–4 weeks
					
						- Recurrence
							- ≈10–15%
					
					- Limberg Flap
					
						- Procedure
							- Rhomboid excision
							- Transposition flap
							- Z-shaped scar
					
						- Advantages
							- Low infection rate
							- Good healing
					
						- Disadvantages
							- Larger scar
							- Longer surgery
		
			- Modern Minimally Invasive Option
	
		- EPSiT
			- Endoscopic Pilonidal Sinus Treatment
	
		- Technique
			- Uses fistuloscope
	
		- Steps
			- Endoscopic sinus exploration
			- Identify hair and debris
			- Endoscopic debridement
	
		- Advantages
			- Minimal tissue damage
			- Healing within 2–3 weeks
			- Early return to work
	
		- Recurrence
			- ≈5%
	
		- Limitation
			- Limited long-term evidence

- Management of Complex Disease
	- Features
		- Multiple pits
		- Multiple secondary openings
		- Thickened tracks
	- Traditional Wide Excision
		- Not ideal
	- Reason
		- High recurrence
			- 22–41%
	- Preferred Approach
		- Off-midline closure
		- Flattening natal cleft
	- Best Surgical Options
		- Karydakis flap
		- Bascom cleft lift
	- Recurrence
		- ≈1–4%
	- Return to Work
		- 2–4 weeks
	- Secondary Prevention
		- Hygiene
			- Most important preventive measure
			- Daily washing
			- Keep natal cleft dry
		- Hair Removal
			- Best Evidence
				- Laser depilation
			- Avoid
				- Shaving
				- Hair removal creams
	
			- Reason
				- Skin trauma
				- Increased recurrence
- Decision Algorithm in Primary Care
	- Asymptomatic pit
		- Reassure
		- Hygiene advice
	- First abscess
		- Incision and drainage
	- Recurrent abscess or chronic sinus
		- Surgical treatment
	- Complex disease
		- Specialist referral
