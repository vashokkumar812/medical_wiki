		- Major risk

# subclinical hypothyroidism
- Subclinical hypothyroidism
	- Definition
		- ↑TSH with normal FT4

	- Symptoms
		- Often absent
		- Nonspecific if present
			- Fatigue
			- Weight gain
			- Constipation
			- Dry skin

	- Important principle
		- Mild TSH abnormalities often transient
		- Small TSH deviations
			- Unclear long-term significance

- RACGP position
	- Avoid routine thyroid screening
		- Asymptomatic non-pregnant adults

	- Avoid routine treatment
		- TSH 4–10 mIU/L
		- Normal FT4

	- Delay testing during acute illness

- Do NOT
	- Routine thyroid ultrasound
			- If no palpable neck mass
	- Repeat TPO antibodies once positive
	- Routine treatment of asymptomatic subclinical hypothyroidism
	- Use liothyronine/T3 therapy
	- Measure FT3 or reverse T3 routinely

- Causes/TSH influencers
	- Concurrent illness
	- Medications
	- Supplements
	- Age
	- Gender
	- Ethnicity
	- Iodine status
	- Circadian variation
	- Autoantibodies
	- Heterophile antibodies

	- Drug causes
		- Amiodarone
		- Lithium
		- Interferon
		- Tyrosine kinase inhibitors
		- Immune checkpoint inhibitors
		- Iodine excess
			- Contrast
			- Kelp tablets

- Investigation approach
	- If ↑TSH
		- Check FT4

	- If TSH 4–10 + normal FT4
		- Repeat TFTs
			- In 2–3 months
		- Check TPO antibodies

	- Positive TPO antibodies
		- ↑Risk progression to overt hypothyroidism
		- Monitor
			- 3 months
			- 6 months
			- Then annually

	- Retest earlier if
		- Symptoms worsen
		- New symptoms develop
		- But wait ≥6 weeks after prior test

- Treatment indications
	- Consider levothyroxine if
		- TSH >10 mIU/L
			- On 2 tests
			- 3 months apart
		- AND symptoms present

	- Consider treatment for TSH 4–10 if
		- Symptoms suggestive of hypothyroidism
		- Positive TPO antibodies
		- Elevated cardiovascular risk
		- Previous iodine therapy
		- Previous thyroid surgery

	- Initial management option
		- Trial of therapy

- Levothyroxine
	- Benefits
		- Limited evidence in mild disease
		- No proven mortality benefit
		- No clear cardiovascular outcome benefit

	- If symptoms persist despite normal TSH
		- Consider alternative diagnosis
		- Consider stopping therapy

- Liothyronine/T3
	- No evidence of benefit
	- Not recommended
			- Alone or combination therapy

- Harms of overtreatment
	- Suppressed TSH may cause
		- Atrial fibrillation
		- Osteoporosis
		- Fractures

- Monitoring without treatment
	- Appropriate for many patients
			- TSH 4–10
			- Asymptomatic

- Pregnancy
	- Different thresholds apply
	- Requires specialist-guided management

- Ultrasound
	- Indicated for
		- Palpable goitre
		- Thyroid nodules

	- Not routine for hypothyroidism
	- Overuse risks
		- Incidental nodules
		- Overdiagnosis of thyroid cancer

- High-yield points
	- Most patients with subclinical hypothyroidism do not benefit from treatment unless
			- TSH >10
			- Or TPO antibodies positive
	- Repeat mild abnormalities before diagnosing disease
	- Positive TPO antibodies alone ≠ treatment indication
	- Routine FT3/reverse T3 testing not useful

			- TSH <2.5

		- Monitor
			- Every 4 weeks

## hypothyroidism in children
- Hypothyroidism (Paediatric)

- Mild TSH Elevation
  - TSH <10 U/L
  - Common
  - Often normalises spontaneously

- Initial Investigations
  - TSH
  - Free T4 (fT4)
  - Anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies (anti-TPO)
  - Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies

- Positive Thyroid Antibodies
  - Normal TSH or mildly elevated TSH
  - No referral required
  - Repeat TFTs in 3 months

- Refer to Paediatrics/Endocrinology
  - TSH >20 U/L
  - OR fT4 below reference range
  - OR TSH 10–20 U/L on 2 occasions
    - Even if fT4 normal

- Central (Secondary) Hypothyroidism
  - Low fT4
  - Normal or low TSH
  - Suspect if
    - Pituitary disease
    - Hypothalamic disease
    - Structural brain lesion

- Most Appropriate Investigation
  - TFTs
    - TSH + fT4

- Additional Investigations
  - Anti-TPO antibodies
  - Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies

- Most Appropriate Treatment
  - Levothyroxine
    - If overt hypothyroidism

# Thyroid investigation approach

- When to check TSH
	- Suspected hypo/hyperthyroidism
	- Goitre/nodule
	- Monitor levothyroxine
		- Recheck ≥4–6 weeks after dose change
		- Annual once stable
	- Pregnancy
		- Preconception
		- Early first trimester
		- Especially if risk factors/on thyroxine

- TSH principles
	- Most sensitive screening test
	- Normal TSH usually excludes thyroid dysfunction
		- Except central hypothyroidism/pituitary disease
	- Symptoms of hypothyroidism
		- Often nonspecific
	- Hyperthyroidism
		- Better symptom-biochemistry correlation

- Elevated TSH approach
	- Check
		- FT4

	- Interpretation
		- ↑TSH + low FT4
			- Primary hypothyroidism
		- ↑TSH + normal FT4
			- Subclinical/mild hypothyroidism

	- Mild TSH elevation
		- Often transient
		- Repeat TFTs
			- 4–6 weeks or 1–3 months

	- Pregnancy
		- TSH above trimester range or >4
			- Urgent review
			- Specialist referral
			- Start levothyroxine

	- Antibodies
		- TPO/Tg antibodies
			- Common even in euthyroid patients
		- Positive antibodies
			- ↑Risk progression to hypothyroidism
		- Do not serially recheck antibodies

	- Imaging
		- Ultrasound/scintigraphy NOT routine in uncomplicated hypothyroidism
		- Ultrasound only if
			- Palpable nodule/goitre

- Suppressed TSH approach
	- Check
		- FT4
		- FT3

	- Interpretation
		- ↓TSH + ↑FT4/FT3
			- Hyperthyroidism
		- Mildly ↓TSH + normal hormones
			- Subclinical hyperthyroidism
			- Non-thyroidal illness
			- Drug effect

	- TRAb/TSI
		- Positive
			- Graves disease likely

	- Thyroid scintigraphy
		- Indications
			- TRAb negative
			- Diagnostic uncertainty
			- Suppressed TSH with nodule

		- Findings
			- Graves
				- Diffuse uptake
			- Toxic adenoma
				- Focal hot uptake
			- Thyroiditis
				- Low/absent uptake

	- Ultrasound
		- Usually not useful for cause of hyperthyroidism

- When FT4/FT3 needed
	- Suspected pituitary/hypothalamic disease
	- Discordant TFTs
	- Suspected assay interference
			- Biotin
			- Heterophile antibodies
	- Rapidly changing thyroid function
	- Monitoring antithyroid drugs
	- Central hypothyroidism
			- Use FT4
			- Do NOT use TSH for monitoring

- Reverse T3
	- No role in routine assessment

# Thyroid ultrasound

- Indications
	- Thyroid nodule
	- Goitre
	- Incidental thyroid lesion
	- Thyroid cancer staging/follow-up

- NOT indicated for
	- Routine hypothyroidism
	- Positive thyroid antibodies alone
	- Neck pain without thyroid lesion

- Ultrasound goals
	- Risk stratification
	- Determine biopsy need
	- Follow-up planning

- Suspicious ultrasound features
	- Irregular margins
	- Microcalcifications
	- Hypoechoic
	- Solid lesion
	- Intranodular vascularity
	- Absent halo

|---|---|---|---|
| TR1 | Benign | No | None |
| TR2 | <2% | No | Usually none |
| TR3 | ~5% | ≥2.5 cm | 1, 3, 5 years |
| TR4 | 5–20% | ≥1.5 cm | 1, 2, 3, 5 years |
| TR5 | >20% | ≥1 cm | Annual ×5 years |

	- ≥20% increase in 2 dimensions
	- Minimum 2 mm increase

- Indications
	- Suspicious nodules

	- Benign
	- Atypia

	- Malignant

	- Benign FNAB

- First step
	- Check TSH

- If low TSH
	- Scintigraphy first
		- Hot nodule

		- Non-hot nodule

- Normal TSH usually excludes thyroid disease
- Repeat mild TSH abnormalities before extensive workup

- Ultrasound not routine for uncomplicated hypothyroidism

- Hot nodules rarely malignant

# Thyroid nodule
	- Definition
		- Discrete thyroid lesion distinct from surrounding tissue

	- Epidemiology
		- Palpable in ~7%
		- US detects nodules in up to 50%
		- ~5% malignant

	- Types
		- Solitary nodule
			- Higher malignancy risk
		- Dominant nodule in multinodular goitre

	- Benign causes
		- Multinodular goitre
		- Colloid nodule
		- Follicular adenoma
		- Thyroid cyst
		- Hashimoto thyroiditis
		- Subacute thyroiditis

	- Malignant causes
		- Papillary carcinoma
		- Follicular carcinoma
		- Medullary carcinoma
		- Anaplastic carcinoma
		- Thyroid lymphoma
		- Metastasis

	- Symptoms
		- Often asymptomatic

		- Compression symptoms
			- Dysphagia
			- Dyspnoea
			- Hoarseness
				- Suggests RLN involvement/malignancy

		- Pain
			- Subacute thyroiditis
			- Haemorrhagic cyst
			- Anaplastic carcinoma

		- Thyroid dysfunction symptoms
			- Hyperthyroidism
			- Hypothyroidism

	- Red flags for malignancy
		- Age <20 or >70
		- Male
		- Size >4 cm
		- Rapid growth
		- Prior neck irradiation
		- Family history thyroid cancer/MEN2/FAP/Cowden syndrome
		- Hard/fixed nodule
		- Cervical lymphadenopathy
		- Hoarseness

	- Examination
		- Inspect
			- Visible goitre
			- Scars
			- Vein distension
			- Swelling moves with swallowing

		- Palpate
			- Size
			- Consistency
			- Tenderness
			- Mobility
			- Solitary vs multinodular
			- Cervical nodes

		- Auscultation
			- Bruit
				- Graves disease

		- Pemberton sign
			- Facial plethora/stridor on arm elevation
			- Suggests retrosternal extension

		- Assess for
			- Hyperthyroidism signs
			- Hypothyroidism signs
			- Thyroid eye disease

	- Differential diagnosis of neck lump
		- Lymph node
		- Salivary gland lesion
		- Thyroglossal cyst
		- Lipoma
		- Sebaceous cyst

	- Investigations
		- TSH
			- Initial test for all patients

		- If TSH normal
			- Usually no further TFTs needed

		- If TSH high
			- Free T4
			- TPO antibodies
				- Suggest Hashimoto thyroiditis

		- If TSH low
			- Free T4/T3
			- TRAb
				- Differentiate Graves vs toxic multinodular goitre

		- Thyroid ultrasound
			- Most patients
			- Assesses:
				- Number
				- Size
				- Suspicious features

		- FNAB
			- Most nodules requiring evaluation

		- Thyroid scintigraphy
			- Indicated if low TSH/toxic nodule
			- FNAB may not be needed in hot nodules

		- CT/MRI
			- Selective use
			- Retrosternal extension/compression

	- High-yield points
		- Most thyroid nodules benign
		- Solitary nodules more suspicious
		- TSH = first investigation
		- Low TSH → radionuclide scan
		- Hoarseness + hard fixed nodule + nodes
			- Strongly suggest malignancy
- Thyroid ultrasound
	- Routine investigation for palpable nodules
	- Detects
		- Additional impalpable nodules
		- Suspicious features
	- Incidental nodules
		- Same malignancy risk as palpable nodules of same size

	- Suspicious ultrasound features
		- Irregular margins
		- Microcalcifications
		- Hypoechogenicity
		- Absent halo
		- Predominantly solid
		- Intranodular vascularity

	- Important point
		- Benign US appearance does NOT exclude malignancy
		- FNAB still usually required

- FNAB
	- Indications
		- Nearly all palpable/symptomatic nodules
		- Nodules >1 cm
		- Nodules <1 cm with suspicious features

	- Usually not needed
		- Toxic/hot nodule with suppressed TSH

	- FNAB reporting
		- Nondiagnostic/insufficient
		- Benign
		- Atypical
		- Malignant

	- Follicular neoplasm
		- Cytology cannot distinguish
			- Adenoma vs carcinoma
		- Requires surgical excision

	- Accuracy
		- Sensitivity
			- 65–98%
		- Specificity
			- 72–100%
		- Overall accuracy
			- ~95%

	- Important points
		- US-guided FNAB more accurate
		- Atypical cytology
			- ~30% malignant
			- Usually surgery indicated
		- Repeat FNAB often diagnostic if initial inadequate

- CT/MRI
	- Not routine
	- Indications
		- Retrosternal extension
		- Tracheal compression
		- Extent of malignancy
		- Nodal disease assessment

- Thyroid scintigraphy
	- Main indication
		- Suppressed TSH/hyperthyroidism

	- Hot nodule
		- Very low malignancy risk
		- FNAB usually unnecessary

	- Uses
		- Toxic nodules
		- Ectopic thyroid tissue
		- Occult hyperfunctioning tissue

- Serum calcium
	- Routine assessment
	- Excludes coexisting hyperparathyroidism

- Follow-up of benign nodules
	- Clinical + ultrasound surveillance
	- Usually repeat at
		- 6–12 months
		- OR 2–3 years

	- If growth occurs
		- Repeat FNAB

	- Important point
		- Many benign nodules enlarge slowly

- Indications for surgery
	- Malignant FNAB
	- Suspicious/atypical FNAB
	- Compression symptoms
			- Dysphagia
			- Tracheal compression
	- Hyperthyroidism
	- Cosmetic reasons
	- Repeated nondiagnostic FNAB
	- Growth during observation

- Surgical procedures
	- Hemithyroidectomy
		- Solitary hot nodule
		- Atypical solitary nodule
		- Unilateral dominant nodule
		- Small differentiated cancer <1 cm

	- Total thyroidectomy
		- Most thyroid cancers
		- Bilateral multinodular goitre
		- Graves disease with eye disease
		- Failed/contraindicated medical therapy
		- Compression symptoms
		- Large cosmetic goitre

- Thyroid surgery complications
	- Haemorrhage
			- Rare but potentially fatal
	- RLN injury
			- Hoarseness/voice change
	- Superior laryngeal nerve injury
			- Voice fatigue
			- Poor projection
	- Hypocalcaemia
	- Hypothyroidism

	- Re-do surgery
		- Higher complication risk

- Other treatments
	- Radioactive iodine
		- Toxic nodules/toxic multinodular goitre
		- Avoid if compressive symptoms

	- Percutaneous ethanol injection
		- Benign cystic nodules
		- Reduces cyst recurrence
		- Not for solid/toxic nodules

- High-yield points
	- US suspicious features
			- Microcalcification + irregular margins
	- Suppressed TSH
			- Do scintigraphy
	- Hot nodules rarely malignant
	- Follicular lesions require excision
	- US-guided FNAB preferred

- Thyroid nodules
	- Definition
		- Discrete thyroid lesion distinct from surrounding parenchyma

	- Epidemiology
		- Palpable
			- 4–7%
		- Ultrasound detected
			- Up to 67%
		- Malignancy risk
			- ~7–15%

- Types
	- Non-neoplastic
		- Hyperplastic
		- Colloid
		- Inflammatory
		- Thyroid cysts
			- Nearly always benign

	- Neoplastic
		- Benign
			- Follicular adenoma
		- Malignant
			- Papillary
			- Follicular
			- Medullary
			- Anaplastic
			- Lymphoma
			- Metastasis

- Clinical presentation
	- Neck lump
	- Incidental imaging finding
	- Compressive symptoms
		- Dyspnoea
		- Dysphagia
		- Globus sensation

	- Suspicious symptoms
		- Dysphonia
		- Rapid growth
		- Dysphagia
		- Suggest invasion/malignancy

- Malignancy risk factors
	- Childhood head/neck irradiation
	- MEN2
			- Medullary carcinoma
	- Familial non-medullary thyroid cancer
	- Familial syndromes
			- FAP
			- Cowden syndrome
			- Gardner syndrome
			- Carney complex

- Examination
	- Assess
		- Size
		- Consistency
		- Mobility
		- Cervical lymph nodes

	- Thyroid nodules
		- Move upward on swallowing

	- Suspicious findings
		- Firm/fixed nodule
		- Cervical lymphadenopathy
		- Hoarseness

- Initial investigations
	- TSH
			- First test in all patients

	- If low TSH
		- Suggests hyperfunctioning nodule
		- Very low malignancy risk
		- Perform radionuclide scan
			- Usually Tc-99m in Australia

	- If normal/high TSH
		- Ultrasound risk stratification

- Do NOT routinely order
	- Thyroglobulin
			- Not sensitive/specific
	- Calcitonin
			- Only if medullary carcinoma suspected
	- Routine radionuclide scan
			- Unless low TSH

- Ultrasound
	- Imaging of choice
	- Detects nodules 1–3 mm
	- Assess
		- Size
		- Composition
		- Suspicious features
		- Cervical nodes

	- High-risk ultrasound features
		- Microcalcifications
		- Irregular margins
		- Taller-than-wide shape
		- Hypoechoic
		- Extrathyroidal extension

- ATA ultrasound classification

| Risk category | Malignancy risk | Features | FNAB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benign | <1% | Pure cystic | No |
| Very low suspicion | <3% | Spongiform/partially cystic | Consider if ≥2 cm |
| Low suspicion | 5–10% | Isoechoic/hyperechoic solid or partially cystic | ≥1.5 cm |
| Intermediate suspicion | 10–20% | Smooth hypoechoic solid | ≥1 cm |
| High suspicion | >70–90% | Hypoechoic + microcalcification/irregular margins/taller-than-wide/extrathyroidal extension | ≥1 cm |

- FNAB
	- Diagnostic accuracy
			- ~95%

	- Indications
		- Based on ultrasound risk + size

	- Bethesda system
		- Nondiagnostic
		- Benign
		- AUS/FLUS
		- Follicular neoplasm
		- Suspicious
		- Malignant

	- Management
		- Benign
			- Follow-up ultrasound
		- Nondiagnostic
			- Repeat US-guided FNAB
		- Indeterminate/suspicious/malignant
			- Refer endocrinologist/thyroid surgeon

- Follow-up
	- High suspicion nodules
			- Repeat US 6–12 months
	- Low/intermediate suspicion
			- Repeat US 12–24 months
	- Very low-risk nodules <1 cm
			- Usually no routine follow-up

- Incidentalomas
	- Definition
			- Incidental asymptomatic non-palpable nodules
	- Same malignancy risk as palpable nodules
	- CT/MRI incidental nodules
			- Require ultrasound assessment
	- FDG-PET focal uptake
			- Malignancy risk ~35%
			- FNAB if >1 cm

- Paediatric nodules
	- Higher malignancy risk
			- ~26%
	- FNAB based on
			- Ultrasound + clinical context
			- Not size alone
	- Indeterminate cytology
			- Surgical referral preferred

- Pregnancy
	- Check TSH first
	- If normal/high TSH
			- FNAB indicated
	- Thyroid cancer prognosis
			- Usually excellent
	- Surgery timing
			- Individualised specialist decision

- High-yield points
	- TSH is first investigation
	- Low TSH → radionuclide scan
	- Ultrasound is imaging modality of choice
	- FNAB decision depends on ultrasound risk + size
	- Hot nodules rarely malignant
	- Pure cystic nodules usually benign
	- Suspicious nodules require specialist referral
