- Definition
	- Dilated superficial veins
	- Usually affect the legs

- Epidemiology
	- Pregnancy may worsen disease

- Etiology (Causes)
	- Genetic predisposition
	- Previous DVT

- Classification
	- Vein size classification
		- Telangiectasia
			- <1 mm
		- Reticular veins
			- 1–3 mm
		- Varicose veins
			- \>3 mm
	- CEAP Classification
	
		- C = Clinical
			- C0
				- No visible or palpable venous disease
			- C1
				- Telangiectasia
				- Reticular veins
			- C2
				- Varicose veins
			- C3
				- Oedema
			- C4a
				- Pigmentation
				- Venous eczema
			- C4b
				- Lipodermatosclerosis
				- Atrophie blanche
			- C5
				- Healed venous ulcer
			- C6
				- Active venous ulcer
	
		- E = Etiology
			- Ec
				- Congenital
			- Ep
				- Primary
			- Es
				- Secondary (post-thrombotic)
			- En
				- No identifiable cause
	
		- A = Anatomy
			- As
				- Superficial veins
			- Ad
				- Deep veins
			- Ap
				- Perforator veins
			- An
				- No venous location identified
	
		- P = Pathophysiology
			- Pr
				- Reflux
			- Po
				- Obstruction
			- Pr,o
				- Reflux and obstruction
			- Pn
				- No identifiable pathophysiology

- Most Common
	- Site
		- Legs
	- Investigation
		- Duplex ultrasound
	- Cause of non-saphenous reflux
		- ~10% of venous disease
	- Recurrence after conventional surgery
		- 25–50% within 5 years

- Clinical Features
	- Symptoms
		- Cosmetic appearance
		- Ache
		- Heaviness
		- Cramps
		- Restlessness
		- Ankle swelling
		- Skin pigmentation
		- Eczema
		- Ulceration
		- Superficial thrombophlebitis
		- Haemorrhage
		- Venous disease pain
			- Worse on standing
			- Relieved by walking/elevation
		- Venous claudication
			- "Bursting pain" on walking
		- Non-saphenous reflux
			- Tortuous veins
			- Aching pain
	- Signs
		- Oedema
			- Usually spares metatarsals
			- May be pitting or non-pitting
		- Severe chronic venous disease
			- Lipodermatosclerosis
			- Venous ulceration
	- Examination
		- Examine standing
		-  if atypical veins
		- Consider AV malformation
		- Trendelenburg test
			- Largely obsolete

- Differential Diagnoses
	- Arterial claudication
		- Worse on exertion

- Investigations
	- Duplex ultrasound
		- Investigation of choice
		- Assess
			- Deep vein patency and competence
			- Superficial vein patency and competence
			- Incompetent perforators and junctions
			- Saphenopopliteal junction anatomy
			- Vein diameter

- Complications
	- Venous eczema
	- Venous ulceration
	- Superficial thrombophlebitis
	- Bleeding
	- Chronic venous insufficiency
	- Recurrence
		- Neovascularisation
		- Incomplete or inadequate surgery
		- Re-endothelialisation
		- Pelvic reflux source
		- New reflux in other saphenous veins
		- New varices may develop in ~10% within first year

- Management
	- Reassurance
		- Uncomplicated/asymptomatic veins
	- Compression stockings
		- If palpable pulses or ABI >0.6
			- Class 2 below-knee stockings safe
			- Benefits
				- Symptom relief
	- Endovenous therapy
		- types
			- UGFS
			- EVLT
			- RFA
			- Mechanochemical ablation
			- Adhesive ablation
		- choice
			- First line
				- endo venous ablation
			- if not suitable
				- UGFS
			- if not suitable
				- surgery
		- Endovenous thermal ablation
			- Methods
				- EVLT
				- RFA
		- Ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy (UGFS)
			- Advantages
				- Quick
				- No anaesthesia
				- Repeatable
				- Cheapest
				- Faster recovery
			- Disadvantages
				- Highest technical failure
				- More superficial phlebitis
				- Higher recurrence than EVLT/RFA/surgery
	
		- Endovenous adhesive ablation
	
		- Mechanochemical ablation
			
	
		- Avoid endovenous therapy if veins are
			- >2 cm diameter
				- Increased DVT risk
			- Very tortuous
			- Very superficial
			- Thrombophlebitic
	
		- Tributary vein treatment
			- Ambulatory phlebectomy
				- Not suitable
					- Knee
					- Shin
					- Foot
					- GSV/SSV
		- UGFS
			- Suitable in all locations
			- Less artery/nerve injury due to ultrasound guidance
	
		- Recurrent varicose veins after surgery
			- Usually treated with UGFS
			- Preferred in popliteal fossa
				- Reduced nerve injury
			- Catheter-directed sclerotherapy
				- Useful for large recurrent veins
	
		- Non-saphenous reflux
			- Treatment
				- UGFS
	- Surgery
	- Refer if
		- Venous eczema
		- Venous ulcer
		- Thrombophlebitis
		- Bleeding
		- Severe pain/discomfort
		- Chronic venous insufficiency


- Prognosis
	- EVLT/RFA vs surgery
		- Similar recurrence rates
		- Fewer complications
		- Less pain
		- Quicker recovery




- Non-Saphenous Reflux

	- Causes ~10% of venous disease

	- Common Sites
		- Buttock
		- Perineum
		- Posterolateral thigh
		- Posterior distal thigh
		- Knee

	- Features
		- Tortuous veins
		- Complex tributaries
		- Aching pain

	- Treatment
		- Ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy

- Pelvic Vein Reflux

	- Suspect when reflux arises from
		- Gluteal veins
		- Vaginal veins
		- Pudendal veins

	- Investigate for
		- Ovarian vein incompetence

- Rare Causes of Venous Reflux

	- Iliac vein thrombosis
	- Retroperitoneal tumours
	- Nutcracker syndrome
		- Compression of left renal vein
	- May-Thurner syndrome
		- Right common iliac artery compresses left common iliac vein
