### **Children >3 months corrected age**
![[Pasted image 20260601185820.png]]



- Definition
	- Fever
		- Temperature >38°C

- Important Principles
	- Appearance of child is the best predictor of severity
	- Degree of fever does not predict serious illness
	- Rapidity of onset does not predict serious illness
	- Response to antipyretics does not predict serious illness
	- Febrile seizures do not predict serious illness

- etiology
	- Common Causes
		- Viral infections	
	- Serious Bacterial Infections (SBI)
		- Urinary tract infection
			- Most common SBI without focus
		- Pneumonia
		- Meningitis
		- Osteomyelitis
		- Septic arthritis
		- Skin and soft tissue infection
		- Mastoiditis
		- Bacteraemia
		- Sepsis
	- Occult Bacteraemia
		- Incidence <1% in healthy immunised children
		- Reduced after pneumococcal vaccination

- clinical features
	- Prolonged Fever
		- Fever ≥5 days
			- Assess for Kawasaki disease
			- Assess for PIMS-TS/MIS-C if history of COVID-19
		- Other causes
			- Inflammatory disorders
			- Immune-mediated disorders
			- Malignancy
	- Features Suggesting Serious Illness
	
		- Colour
			- Pallor
			- Mottling
			- Cyanosis
	
		- Activity
			- Lethargy
			- Reduced activity
			- Poor response to social cues
			- Difficult to wake
			- Does not stay awake
			- Weak cry
			- High-pitched cry
			- Continuous cry
	
		- Respiratory
			- Grunting
			- Tachypnoea
			- Increased work of breathing
			- Hypoxia
	
		- Circulation and Hydration
			- Poor feeding
			- Dry mucous membranes
			- Persistent tachycardia
			- Capillary refill ≥3 seconds
			- Reduced skin turgor
			- Reduced urine output
	
		- Neurological
			- Bulging fontanelle
			- Excessive irritability
			- Neck stiffness
			- Focal neurological signs
			- Focal seizure
			- Complex seizure
			- Prolonged seizure
	
		- Other
			- Non-blanching rash
			- Fever ≥5 days
			- Limb swelling
			- Joint swelling
			- Non-weight bearing
			- Not using an extremity



- High-Risk Groups
	- Age <6 months
	- Incomplete immunisation
	- Prematurity
	- Immunosuppression
	- Oncology patients
	- Central venous line
	- Chronic lung disease
	- Congenital heart disease
	- Previous invasive bacterial infection
	- Aboriginal children
	- Torres Strait Islander children
	- Pacific Islander children
	- Māori children
	- Multiple healthcare presentations

- variants
	- Teething does not cause fever
	- Post-vaccination fever
		- Usually starts within 24 hours
		- Usually lasts 2–3 days
		- Do not attribute fever solely to vaccination in an unwell child

- management
	- Any seriously unwell febrile child
		- Manage as sepsis
	- Do not assume otitis media or URTI is sole source in young infants
	- Complete investigations before antibiotics if stable
	- Lower threshold for investigation in high-risk groups
	- If no obvious focus
		- Urine testing required
	- Obtain adequate blood culture volume when indicated
	- Neonates ≤28 Days Corrected Age
		- Treat as high risk
		- Assess urgently for sepsis
		- Discuss with senior clinician
		- Investigations
			- FBE
			- CRP
			- Blood culture
			- Urine culture
			- Lumbar puncture
			- ± Chest X-ray
		- Management
			- Empiric IV antibiotics
			- Do not delay antibiotics if investigations delayed
			- Admit to hospital
	- Infants <3 Months
		- Low threshold for investigation
		- Low threshold for treatment
		- Hypothermia or temperature instability may indicate SBI
- Consultation with Paediatric Team
	- Unwell child
	- Septic shock
	- Neonate <28 days with fever
	- High-risk child
	- Need advice regarding empiric therapy
	- Prolonged fever without diagnosis
	- Barriers to follow-up
- Transfer to Tertiary Centre
	- Requires care beyond local hospital capability
- Discharge Criteria
	- Infants 29 Days–3 Months
		- Well appearing
		- Normal investigations
		- Reviewed by senior clinician
		- Follow-up arranged within 12–24 hours
	- Children >3 Months
		- Well appearing
		- Follow-up arranged
	- Parents should be advised
		- Return immediately if child deteriorates
