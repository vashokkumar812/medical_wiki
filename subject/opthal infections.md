# Orbital and Periorbital Cellulitis

## Key Points
- Orbital cellulitis is an emergency with serious complications including intracranial infection, cavernous sinus thrombosis, and vision loss.  
- Urgent imaging and surgical consultation (ENT and ophthalmology) should be considered for any child with suspected orbital cellulitis.  
- Periorbital cellulitis in a well child can often be treated with oral antibiotics if follow-up is assured.  

---

## Background
- Periorbital and orbital cellulitis are distinct clinical diseases, though have overlapping clinical features and can be difficult to differentiate.

### Orbital Cellulitis
- Infection within the orbit (post-septal, structures posterior to the orbital septum).  
- Surgical emergency with major complications:  
  - Vision loss  
  - Abscess formation  
  - Venous sinus thrombosis  
  - Intracranial infection (subdural empyema, meningitis)  
- Majority (>80%) of cases relate to local sinus disease.

### Periorbital Cellulitis
- Infection of the eyelids and surrounding skin not involving the orbit (pre-septal, structures anterior to the orbital septum).  
- The globe is not involved in either infection.

---

## Assessment

### Typical Presentation
- Unilateral eyelid swelling and erythema  
- Unilateral eye pain or tenderness  
- Consider *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* and *Chlamydia trachomatis* infections in neonatal presentation (send PCR swabs).  
  See also: **Acute Red Eye**

### Red Flags Concerning for Orbital Cellulitis
- Painful or restricted eye movements  
- Visual impairment:  
  - Reduced acuity  
  - Relative afferent pupil defect  
  - Diplopia  
- Proptosis  
- Severe headache or other features of intracranial involvement  

### Differential Diagnosis
- Bilateral findings and/or painless swelling in a well child are more likely due to **allergic reaction**.

## Management

> **Note:** Antimicrobial recommendations may vary according to local susceptibility patterns — refer to local guidelines (including advice on community-acquired MRSA).

If inadequate *Haemophilus influenzae* type B (Hib) vaccination, treat as **severe periorbital cellulitis**.

---

### Orbital Cellulitis
- **Admission**
  - Keep fasted until need for surgery clarified  
  - Seek **ENT and Ophthalmology** advice urgently  
  - Consider **urgent contrast-enhanced CT** of orbits, sinuses ± brain  
- **Investigations**
  - FBE and blood culture  
  - **Do not perform lumbar puncture** (risk of raised ICP)  
- **Treatment**
  - IV antibiotics (see below)  
  - Treat underlying sinus disease (e.g., nasal decongestants, steroids – guided by ENT)  

---

### Periorbital Cellulitis

#### Severe
- Manage as per **orbital cellulitis**  
- Inpatient investigations and IV antibiotics  

#### Moderate
- Inpatient management or **Hospital in the Home (HITH)** if available  
- Consider blood culture if febrile and unwell  
- IV antibiotics → switch to oral once improving  
- If not improving or worsening in 24–48 hr, treat as **severe** periorbital cellulitis  

#### Mild
- Oral antibiotics  
- Outpatient follow-up required

## Summary of Antibiotic Treatment

| Condition | Intravenous Therapy | Oral Therapy | Total Duration |
|------------|--------------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Orbital cellulitis** | 3rd generation cephalosporin:<br>- Cefotaxime 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 6-hourly **or**<br>- Ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg (max 4 g) IV daily<br>If suspected MRSA: add **Vancomycin** | Amoxicillin-clavulanic acid 22.5 mg/kg (max 875 mg) oral BD | 10–14 days (IV for 3–4 days, then oral) |
| **Severe periorbital cellulitis** | Same as orbital cellulitis | Same as orbital cellulitis | — |
| **Moderate periorbital cellulitis** | Cefazolin 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV 8-hourly **or**<br>Ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg (max 2 g) IV daily (HITH)<br>If suspected MRSA: Clindamycin 15 mg/kg (max 600 mg) IV 8-hourly | When improving, switch to:<br>Cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) oral TDS **or**<br>Cefuroxime (10–15 mg/kg oral BD) | 7–10 days |
| **Mild periorbital cellulitis** | — | Cefalexin 20 mg/kg (max 750 mg) oral TDS **or**<br>Cefuroxime (10–15 mg/kg oral BD) | 7–10 days |

If suspected **MRSA**, use:
- Clindamycin 15 mg/kg (max 600 mg) oral TDS  
  **or**
- Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (8/40 mg/mL) 4/20 mg/kg (max 320/1600 mg) oral BD

## Consultation and Transfer Guidelines

**Consult local paediatric team if:**
- Orbital cellulitis suspected  
- Moderate–severe periorbital cellulitis  
- No improvement after 24–48 hr of therapy  

**Consider transfer when:**
- Severe periorbital or orbital cellulitis  
- Suspected intracranial involvement (altered consciousness, seizures, focal signs)  
- Care required above local hospital capacity  

---

## Discharge Criteria

### Mild Periorbital Cellulitis
- Oral antibiotic course prescribed  
- Follow-up assured  

### Moderate Periorbital Cellulitis
- After 24–48 hr IV antibiotics and improvement  
- Able to tolerate oral antibiotics  
- Follow-up assured
