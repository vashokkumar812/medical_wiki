# megaesophagus
- Megaoesophagus

	- Definition
		- End-stage manifestation of achalasia cardia
		- Results from severe oesophageal dilatation due to impaired oesophageal emptying

	- Epidemiology
		- Achalasia incidence
			- Approximately 1 per 100,000 population
		- Equal male and female distribution
		- Can occur at any age
		- Onset before adolescence is uncommon

	- Pathophysiology
		- Disordered oesophageal peristalsis
		- Failure of lower oesophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation
		- Progressive decompensation of oesophageal muscle
		- Leads to:
			- Oesophageal dilatation
			- Food retention
			- Megaoesophagus

	- Other causes of megaoesophagus
		- Oesophageal obstruction
			- Polyp
			- Leiomyoma
		- Secondary oesophagitis
		- Tight gastric band

	- Clinical presentation
		- Chronic cough
			- Especially nocturnal cough
		- Dysphagia
		- Regurgitation of undigested food
		- Reflux symptoms
		- Aspiration episodes
		- Recurrent chest infections

	- Important complications
		- Aspiration pneumonia
		- Severe oesophageal dilatation
		- Rare sudden death reported

	- Diagnostic clues

		- History
			- Chronic cough
			- Dysphagia
			- Reflux
			- Oesophagitis history

		- Examination
			- May be unremarkable
			- Signs of aspiration if present

		- Chest X-ray
			- Key initial investigation
			- Dilated mediastinum
			- Air-fluid level within oesophagus
			- Suggests megaoesophagus/achalasia

	- Case findings

		- Initial chest X-ray
			- Dilated mediastinum
			- Air-fluid level at level of sternal notch

		- Nasogastric tube
			- Inserted into oesophagus
			- Aspirated:
				- Thick fluid
				- Old partially masticated food
			- Subsequently repositioned into stomach

		- Upper GI endoscopy
			- Confirmed megaoesophagus
			- Large pool of thick fluid and food debris
			- Oesophageal wall
				- Oedematous
				- Friable
			- Tight lower oesophageal sphincter identified

	- Management

		- Initial
			- Nasogastric decompression
			- Removal of retained food and fluid

		- Endoscopic management
			- Oesophageal washout
			- Balloon dilatation
			- Botulinum toxin (Botox) injection into LES

		- Follow-up
			- Repeat chest X-ray
				- Resolution of air-fluid level
				- Residual gaseous distension may persist
				- Usually clinically insignificant

	- Treatment options

		- Medical/endoscopic
			- Botulinum toxin injection
				- Effective symptom relief
				- Can be repeated after 6–12 months
				- Subsequent responses may be reduced
				- Possible cause:
					- Neutralising antibodies against toxin

			- Pneumatic dilatation
				- Success rate:
					- Approximately 70–90%
				- High relapse rate

		- Surgical
			- Open surgery
			- Laparoscopic surgery
				- e.g. Heller myotomy



# achalsia cardia

- Achalasia cardia – AMC MCQ

	- Definition
		- Primary oesophageal motility disorder
		- Failure of **lower oesophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation**
		- Loss of oesophageal peristalsis
		- Degeneration of myenteric (Auerbach) plexus neurons

---

- Clinical features
	- Progressive dysphagia ⭐
		- Solids **and liquids from onset**
	- Regurgitation of undigested food ⭐
		- Especially at night
	- Nocturnal cough
	- Aspiration
	- Chest pain
	- Weight loss
	- Halitosis
	- Food sticking in chest
	- Long history of symptoms

---

- Examination
	- Usually normal
	- Weight loss (late disease)
	- Signs of aspiration if complicated

---

- Initial investigations
	- FBC
	- U&E
	- Chest X-ray
		- May show dilated oesophagus
		- Air-fluid level
		- Absent gastric air bubble

---

- Investigation of choice (IOC)
	- Upper GI endoscopy ⭐
		- Exclude pseudoachalasia (malignancy)
		- Exclude oesophageal cancer
		- May show retained food and dilated oesophagus

---

- Definitive investigation (Gold standard)
	- Oesophageal manometry ⭐⭐⭐
		- Increased LES resting pressure
		- Incomplete LES relaxation
		- Absent peristalsis

---

- Supportive investigation
	- Barium swallow
		- Bird-beak appearance ⭐
		- Smooth tapering distal oesophagus
		- Dilated oesophagus
		- Delayed contrast emptying

---

- Differential diagnosis
	- Oesophageal carcinoma
		- Progressive dysphagia
		- Solids → liquids
		- Significant weight loss
	- Peptic stricture
	- Diffuse oesophageal spasm
	- Scleroderma
		- Low LES pressure
		- Severe reflux

---

- Management

	- Initial
		- IV fluids if dehydrated
		- Aspiration precautions
		- Nutritional support
		- Treat aspiration pneumonia if present

	- Medical (poor surgical candidates)
		- Nifedipine
		- Glyceryl trinitrate
		- Temporary symptom relief only

	- Endoscopic
		- Pneumatic balloon dilatation ⭐
		- Botulinum toxin injection
			- Elderly
			- Frail
			- High operative risk

	- Definitive treatment ⭐
		- Laparoscopic Heller myotomy
			- Usually combined with partial fundoplication
		- POEM (Peroral endoscopic myotomy)
			- Increasingly used
			- Similar efficacy

---

- Complications
	- Aspiration pneumonia ⭐
	- Megaoesophagus
	- Oesophageal candidiasis
	- Malnutrition
	- Increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma

---

- AMC Pearls ⭐
	- Dysphagia to **solids and liquids from the beginning** → Achalasia
	- Dysphagia **solids then liquids** → Oesophageal carcinoma
	- Bird-beak appearance → Barium swallow
	- Gold standard diagnosis → Oesophageal manometry
	- Always perform endoscopy first to exclude malignancy
	- Definitive treatment → Heller myotomy or POEM
