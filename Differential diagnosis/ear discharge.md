

  * Onset and duration  
    * When did the discharge start?  
    * Is it persistent or intermittent?  
    * Is this the first episode or have you experienced it previously?  
  * Character of discharge  
    * Watery  
    * Mucoid  
    * Purulent  
      * Is it associated with ear pain?  
      * Did the ear pain improve after the discharge started?  
        * Consider acute otitis media with tympanic membrane perforation.  
    * Blood-stained  
      * Did it occur following ear or head trauma?  
        * Consider ear canal injury or temporal bone trauma.  
  * Quantity  
    * Is the discharge scanty or profuse?  
  * Smell  
    * Is there an offensive or unpleasant smell?  
  * Associated symptoms  
    * Ear pain  
    * Hearing loss  
    * Fever  
    * Tinnitus  
    * Vertigo or imbalance  
    * Itching  
    * Swelling around the ear  
  * Preceding events  
    * Recent upper respiratory tract infection  
    * Water exposure or swimming  
    * Ear instrumentation or cotton bud use  
    * Ear or head trauma  
    * Previous ear surgery or grommet insertion  
  * Previous ENT history  
    * Previous ear infections  
    * Recurrent ear discharge  
    * Tympanic membrane perforation  
    * Chronic suppurative otitis media  
    * Cholesteatoma  
    * Previous ear surgery  
  * Previous treatment  
    * Have you used ear drops or oral antibiotics?  
    * Did the discharge improve with treatment?  
    * Has the discharge persisted despite treatment?  
    * Have you had an ear swab or culture?  
  * Relevant medical history  
    * Diabetes mellitus  
    * Immunosuppression  
    * Previous meningitis or significant infection  
  * Diagnostic clues  
    * Itching, erythema and discharge  
      * Consider otitis externa.  
    * Ear pain followed by purulent discharge and pain relief  
      * Consider acute otitis media with tympanic membrane perforation.  
    * Chronic, scanty, offensive discharge  
      * Consider cholesteatoma or chronic suppurative otitis media.  
    * Blood-stained discharge after trauma  
      * Consider ear canal injury or temporal bone trauma.  
    * Clear, watery discharge following head trauma  
      * Consider cerebrospinal fluid otorrhoea.  
  * Red flags  
    * Severe or persistent ear pain  
    * Fever with headache or neck stiffness  
    * Facial weakness or other cranial nerve abnormalities  
    * Clear, watery discharge after head trauma  
    * Severe ear pain in a patient with diabetes or immunosuppression  
    * Significant or sudden hearing loss  
  * Closing the history  
    * Summarise the findings.  
    * Ask whether the patient has any additional concerns.  
    * Explain the need for otoscopy and further assessment.  
    * Thank the patient.
