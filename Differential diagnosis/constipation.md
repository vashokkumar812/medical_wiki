## Definition  
- rome 4 criteria : 2 or more for atleast 12 weeks
	- infrequent passage of stools < 3 / week
	- passage of lumpy or hard stools at least 25% of time
	- straining at least 25% of time
	- sensation of inomplete evacuation atleast 25% of time
	- use of manual maoeuvres atleast 25% of time
	- sensation of anorectal block atleast 25% of time

* Pregnancy: 
	* common, especially in later stages.  
* Slow-transit constipation
	* delayed colonic transit; more common in young women.  
  * Evacuation difficulty

    * Need for manual evacuation (digitation)  
      * Pelvic floor dysfunction, anorectal disease (e.g. fissure, stricture).  

## Red flag  
* Recent change in bowel habits, especially with weight loss or rectal bleeding  
  * Consider colorectal malignancy.  


# paediatric constipation
- Constipation (Paediatric)

	- Definition
		- Usually functional
		- Clinical diagnosis
		- No routine abdominal X-ray or rectal examination

	- Epidemiology
		- Common
		- Typical periods
			- Introduction of solids
			- Toilet training
			- School entry

	- Pathophysiology
		- Painful defaecation
			→ stool withholding
			→ harder stools
			→ painful defaecation (vicious cycle)

	- History
		- Duration
		- Stool frequency
		- Stool consistency (Bristol chart)
		- Painful defaecation
		- Blood
		- Mucus
		- Stool withholding behaviours
		- Toilet refusal
		- Faecal incontinence (encopresis)
		- Urinary symptoms
		- Diet
			- Fibre
			- Fluid
			- Excess cow's milk
		- Previous laxative use
		- Family history
			- Coeliac disease
			- Hypothyroidism

	- Rome IV criteria (Functional constipation)
		- ≥2 criteria
			- Infants
				- ≥1 month
			- Older children
				- ≥2 months
		- ≤2 stools/week
		- Stool withholding
		- Painful/hard stools
		- Large stools
		- Large rectal faecal mass
		- Toilet-trained children
			- ≥1 episode/week faecal incontinence

	- Red flags
		- Age <6 weeks
		- Delayed meconium (>24 hours)
		- Ribbon stools
		- Weight loss/poor growth
		- Persistent vomiting
		- Abdominal mass (not faeces)

	- Organic causes

		- Medical
			- Coeliac disease
			- Hypothyroidism
			- Hypercalcaemia
			- Slow-transit constipation
			- Neurological disorders

		- Surgical
			- Hirschsprung disease
			- Meconium ileus
			- Anorectal malformation
			- Spinal cord abnormalities

	- Examination
		- Abdomen
			- Palpable faeces
		- Perianal examination
			- Fissure
			- Anal position
			- Anal wink/tone
		- Spine
			- Occult dysraphism
		- Neurological examination
			- Lower limbs
			- Gait
		- Faecal impaction
			- Lower abdominal mass
			- Overflow soiling

	- Investigations
		- Not routinely required
		- Consider if
			- Red flags
			- Persistent constipation despite adequate treatment
		- Targeted investigations
			- Coeliac serology
			- TFTs
			- Calcium
			- Further investigations if Hirschsprung or neurological disorder suspected

	- Management

		- Behavioural
			- Footstool
				- Knees above hips
			- Lean forward
			- Toilet sitting
				- 5 minutes
				- Three times daily
				- After meals
			- Positive reinforcement
			- Stool diary
			- Regular exercise
			- Delay toilet training until painless stools

		- Dietary
			- Reduce excessive cow's milk
			- Normal fluid intake
			- Fibre alone is **not** adequate treatment

		- Medical

			- Goals
				- One soft stool daily
				- Continue treatment for months
				- Titrate dose

			- First-line maintenance

| Age | First-line |
|------|------------|
| <1 month | Coloxyl drops |
| 1–12 months | Macrogol or Lactulose |
| >1 year | Macrogol ± Paraffin oil |
