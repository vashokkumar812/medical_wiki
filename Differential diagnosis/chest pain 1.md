
# child with chest pain
- Definition
	- Chest pain in children is a common presentation
	- Most cases are benign, self-limiting, or have no identifiable cause
	- Serious cardiac or respiratory causes are rare

- Etiology
	- Musculoskeletal
		- Costochondritis
		- Muscle strain
		- Chest wall pain
	- Precordial catch syndrome
	- Respiratory
		- Viral respiratory infection
		- Pneumonia
		- Asthma
		- Pneumothorax
	- Gastrointestinal
		- Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD)
		- Oesophagitis
		- Biliary disease
		- Foreign body ingestion
	- Psychological
		- Anxiety
		- Panic attacks
	- Cardiac (rare)
		- Myocarditis
		- Pericarditis
		- Arrhythmia
		- Cardiomyopathy
		- Myocardial ischaemia
	- Vascular
		- Pulmonary embolism

- Pathogenesis
	- Depends on underlying cause
	- Musculoskeletal:
		- Chest wall inflammation or strain
	- Respiratory:
		- Pleural irritation
		- Airway inflammation
	- Gastrointestinal:
		- Oesophageal irritation or spasm
	- Cardiac:
		- Myocardial or pericardial inflammation
		- Reduced cardiac perfusion
	- Psychological:
		- Hyperventilation
		- Somatic symptom manifestation

- Most common
	- Most common cause = musculoskeletal chest pain
	- Most common identifiable causes:
		- Musculoskeletal pain/costochondritis
		- Precordial catch syndrome
		- Respiratory infection
		- Asthma
		- Gastrointestinal causes
		- Anxiety
	- Cardiac causes account for approximately 1% of presentations

- Epidemiology
	- Common paediatric presentation
	- Majority of cases are non-cardiac
	- Serious cardiac disease is uncommon in previously healthy children

- Clinical features
	- Chest pain
		- Sharp
		- Dull
		- Pleuritic
		- Positional
		- Exertional
	- Associated symptoms may include:
		- Dyspnoea
		- Palpitations
		- Dizziness
		- Syncope
		- Fever
		- Cough
		- Epigastric pain
		- Vomiting
		- Anxiety
	- Important history:
		- Onset
		- Duration
		- Site
		- Radiation
		- Aggravating and relieving factors
		- Recent illness
		- Recent vaccination
		- Trauma
		- Family history

- Examination
	- General appearance
	- Vital signs
		- Heart rate
		- Respiratory rate
		- Blood pressure
		- Oxygen saturation
	- Signs of cardiorespiratory compromise:
		- Tachypnoea
		- Increased work of breathing
		- Hypoxia
		- Abnormal pulse
		- Abnormal blood pressure
		- Distended neck veins
		- Muffled heart sounds
		- Reduced chest expansion
		- Absent breath sounds
		- Altered conscious state
	- Chest wall tenderness may suggest musculoskeletal cause

- Diagnosis
	- Primarily clinical
	- Aim is to identify or exclude serious cardiac, respiratory, or gastrointestinal causes
	- Thorough history and examination are the most important diagnostic tools

- Red flags
	- First episode severe chest pain
	- Pain radiating to arm or back
	- Exertional pain
	- Syncope
	- Dizziness
	- Congenital or acquired heart disease
	- Family history of sudden cardiac death
	- Family history of arrhythmia
	- History of Kawasaki disease
	- Connective tissue disease
	- Longstanding diabetes
	- Pleuritic chest pain
	- Haemoptysis
	- Fever
	- Chronic respiratory disease
	- Recent surgery
	- Immobility
	- Hypercoagulable state
	- Central venous catheter
	- Recurrent vomiting
	- Dysphagia
	- Odynophagia
	- Food impaction
	- Suspected foreign body ingestion
	- Suspected button battery ingestion

- Investigations
	- Initial
		- Usually none required if:
			- Well child
			- Normal examination
			- No risk factors
	- Most appropriate
		- Clinical assessment (history + examination)
	- If red flags present:
		- ECG
		- Chest X-ray
		- Blood tests
	- Consider after senior review:
		- Echocardiography
		- CT pulmonary angiography
		- Other specialist investigations

- Treatment
	- Initial
		- Reassurance
		- Simple analgesia
			- Paracetamol
			- Ibuprofen
	- Most appropriate
		- Treat underlying cause
			- Costochondritis → NSAIDs
			- Asthma → bronchodilators
			- Pneumonia → antibiotics if indicated
			- GORD → acid suppression
			- Myocarditis/pericarditis → specialist management
			- Pulmonary embolism → anticoagulation
	- Early senior involvement if:
		- Trauma
		- Critically unwell child
		- Cardiorespiratory compromise

- Prognosis
	- Excellent in most children
	- Majority have benign self-limiting causes
	- Serious cardiac disease is rare
	- Safe discharge if:
		- Symptoms improved or resolved
		- Normal vital signs
		- No serious cause identified
		- Appropriate follow-up arranged
