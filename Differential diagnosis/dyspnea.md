

  - **Opening questions**
    - "Have you been experiencing any difficulty breathing or shortness of breath?"
    - "Can you describe what you feel when you become breathless?"
    - "When did you first notice the breathlessness?"
    - "Is the breathlessness getting worse?"

  - **Onset**
    - "When did the breathlessness start?"
    - "Did it start suddenly or gradually?"

    - Sudden onset
      - Cardiac
        - Acute myocardial infarction
          - May cause sudden deterioration in left ventricular function.
        - Acute pulmonary oedema
          - Sudden severe cardiac dyspnoea.
        - Acute mitral regurgitation
          - May occur following rupture of chordae tendineae.
          - May occur following papillary muscle infarction.
      - Vascular
        - Pulmonary embolism
          - May cause acute cardiac-type dyspnoea.

    - Gradual onset
      - Cardiac
        - Chronic heart failure
          - Typically chronic and progressive.
          - Initially occurs with exertion.
          - May progress to breathlessness at rest.
        - Left ventricular dysfunction
          - May result from myocardial ischaemia, previous myocardial infarction or hypertrophy.

  - **Exertional dyspnoea**
    - "Do you experience breathlessness when walking or doing physical activities?"
    - "Does climbing stairs make you breathless?"
    - "How far can you walk before becoming breathless?"
    - "Has the amount of physical activity required to make you breathless decreased?"

    - Breathlessness during exertion
      - Cardiac
        - Left ventricular failure
          - Left ventricular output fails to increase adequately during exercise.
          - Increased left ventricular end-diastolic pressure.
          - Increased pulmonary venous pressure.
          - Fluid leakage into pulmonary interstitial tissues.
          - Reduced lung compliance.
        - Myocardial ischaemia
          - Temporary or permanent reduction in myocardial blood supply.
          - May impair left ventricular function.
        - Previous myocardial infarction
          - Myocardial damage may reduce left ventricular contractility.
        - [[Left ventricular hypertrophy]]
          - associated with hypertension.

    - Breathlessness occurring with progressively less exertion
      - Cardiac
        - Progressive left ventricular dysfunction.
        - Chronic heart failure.

  - **Dyspnoea at rest**
    - "Do you experience breathlessness even when you are sitting or resting?"
    - "Has the breathlessness progressed from occurring during exercise to occurring at rest?"

    - Breathlessness at rest
      - Cardiac
        - Severe chronic cardiac failure
          - Cardiac dyspnoea may progress from exertional symptoms to breathlessness at rest.
        - Acute pulmonary oedema
          - May cause sudden severe breathlessness.
        - Acute myocardial infarction
          - May cause sudden deterioration in left ventricular function.
        - Acute mitral regurgitation
          - May follow rupture of chordae tendineae or papillary muscle infarction.

  - **Orthopnoea**
    - "Do you become breathless when lying flat?"
    - "Do you need to sit up to breathe comfortably?"
    - "How many pillows do you use while sleeping?"
    - "Can you lie completely flat without becoming breathless?"
    - "Does your breathing improve when you sit upright?"

    - Breathlessness on lying supine
      - Cardiac
        - Left ventricular failure
          - Orthopnoea is suggestive of left ventricular failure.
          - In the upright position, interstitial oedema is redistributed.
          - Redistribution improves the overall oxygenation of the lungs.
          - Lying supine worsens breathlessness.
          - Patients may require multiple pillows to sleep.
          - Severe orthopnoea may force patients to sleep sitting upright in a chair.

    - Absence of orthopnoea
      - Left ventricular failure
        - Makes left ventricular failure less likely as the cause of dyspnoea.

  - Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea (PND)
    - "Do you wake up suddenly at night feeling breathless?"
    - "Do you have to sit up or get out of bed gasping for breath?"
    - "Does your breathing improve after sitting or standing up?"

    - Sudden awakening from sleep with severe breathlessness
      - Cardiac
        - Left ventricular failure
          - Severe dyspnoea waking the patient from sleep.
          - Patient is forced to get up gasping for breath.
          - Sudden failure of left ventricular output.
          - Acute increase in pulmonary venous and capillary pressures.
          - Fluid transudation into pulmonary interstitial tissues.
          - Increased work of breathing.
          - May be precipitated by resorption of peripheral oedema during the night while supine.
        - Acute pulmonary oedema
          - May cause acute severe cardiac dyspnoea.

  - Sudden episodes of acute cardiac dyspnoea
    - "Have you experienced sudden episodes of severe breathlessness?"
    - "Did the breathlessness develop suddenly while you were resting or sleeping?"

    - Sudden severe breathlessness
      - Cardiac
        - Myocardial infarction
          - May cause sudden deterioration in left ventricular function.
        - Acute pulmonary oedema
          - Causes sudden severe dyspnoea.
        - Acute mitral regurgitation
          - May result from rupture of chordae tendineae.
          - May result from papillary muscle infarction.
      - Vascular
        - Pulmonary embolism
          - May cause acute cardiac-type dyspnoea.

  - **Nature and sensation of breathlessness**
    - "How would you describe your breathing difficulty?"
    - "Do you feel that you cannot take a big enough breath?"
    - "Do you feel unable to fill your lungs completely?"
    - "Do you find yourself taking frequent deep breaths or sighing?"

    - Inability to take a satisfying deep breath
      - Psychological
        - Anxiety
          - Sensation of inability to take a sufficiently deep breath.
          - Feeling that the lungs cannot be filled satisfactorily.
          - Deep breathing.
          - Breathing punctuated by frequent sighs.

  - **Previous cardiac history**
    - "Have you ever had a heart attack?"
      - Cardiac
        - Previous myocardial infarction
          - Myocardial damage.
          - Reduced left ventricular contractility.
          - Increased likelihood of cardiac dyspnoea.

    - "Have you been diagnosed with any heart disease?"
      - Cardiac
        - Previous myocardial ischaemia
          - May impair left ventricular function.
        - Previous myocardial infarction
          - May reduce left ventricular contractility.
        - Previous cardiac failure
          - May cause chronic exertional dyspnoea.

    - "Do you have hypertension?"
      - Cardiac
        - Hypertensive heart disease
          - May cause left ventricular hypertrophy.
          - May impair left ventricular function.

    - "Do you consume alcohol heavily?"
      - Cardiac
        - Alcoholic cardiomyopathy
          - May cause impaired left ventricular function.
          - May present with cardiac dyspnoea.

  - **Important symptom patterns**
    - Chronic breathlessness occurring with exertion
      - Cardiac
        - Left ventricular failure.
        - Impaired left ventricular function.

    - Progressive breathlessness developing from exertion to rest
      - Cardiac
        - Worsening left ventricular dysfunction.
        - Chronic cardiac failure.

    - Breathlessness when lying supine
      - Cardiac
        - Orthopnoea.
        - Left ventricular failure.

    - Sudden awakening from sleep gasping for breath
      - Cardiac
        - Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea.
        - Left ventricular failure.

    - Sudden severe breathlessness
      - Cardiac
        - Myocardial infarction.
        - Acute pulmonary oedema.
        - Acute mitral regurgitation.
      - Vascular
        - Pulmonary embolism.

    - Breathlessness with previous myocardial infarctions
      - Cardiac
        - Reduced left ventricular contractility.

    - Breathlessness with hypertension
      - Cardiac
        - Hypertensive heart disease.
        - Left ventricular hypertrophy.

    - Breathlessness with heavy alcohol consumption
      - Cardiac
        - Alcoholic cardiomyopathy.

    - Inability to take a satisfying deep breath with frequent sighing
      - Psychological
        - Anxiety-related dyspnoea.
