# assessement of people with suspected ACS
- Suspected ACS
	- Acute chest pain
		- ACS minority of cases
			- STEMI <5%
			- NSTEMI 10%
			- UA 5–10%
			- Other cardiac 20%
			- Non-cardiac 50
- ACS assessment
	- History + examination
	- ECG
	- Troponin

- Main initial goal
	- Risk stratification
	- Not necessarily definitive diagnosis

- Must identify
	- ACOMI
			- STEMI
			- STEMI equivalents
	- NSTEMI
	- High-risk UA
	- CAD without confirmed ACS

- Initial assessment
	- Vital signs
		- BP
		- HR
		- RR
		- SpO₂
	- ECG within 10 min
	- Troponin if ACS likely
	- Identify urgent reperfusion candidates
- Continuous cardiac monitoring
	- ECG ischaemia
	- High-risk MI

- Repeat ECG
	- Every 15 min if:
		- Symptomatic
		- Haemodynamically unstable
	- Repeat if:
		- Symptoms recur
		- Symptoms change
		- Clinical deterioration

# initial ECG assessment
- ECG in suspected ACS
	- Perform within 10 min
	- Expert interpretation required
- Repeat ECG
	- Serial ECGs if ACS suspected
	- Repeat if:
		- Symptoms persist
		- Symptoms recur
		- Clinical condition changes
- Continuous monitoring
	- High-risk patients only
	- Not required if:
		- Symptoms resolved
		- Non-ischaemic ECG
		- Normal troponin
- ACOMI ECG findings
	- STEMI
	- STEMI equivalents
- STE causes (not always MI)
	- Pericarditis
	- LVH
	- LV aneurysm
	- LBBB
	- Ventricular pacing
	- Takotsubo
	- Brugada
	- Early repolarisation
	- PE
	- Hyperkalaemia
	- Hypothermia
	- Raised ICP
	- Rule
		- Ischaemic symptoms + STE
			→ Assume ACOMI until proven otherwise

- Difficult ECGs
	- LBBB
	- Ventricular pacing
	- LVH

- Modified Sgarbossa criteria
	- Use in:
		- LBBB
		- Paced rhythm
	- Positive
		- STE >25% of preceding S-wave depth
	- Specificity ~99%
	- Sensitivity ~80%

- LVH
	- Compare with previous ECG
	- If uncertain
			- Serial ECGs
			- Continuous monitoring
			- Expert review

- STE localisation

	- Inferior
		- II, III, aVF
		- Reciprocal: I, aVL

	- Septal
		- V1–V2

	- Anteroseptal
		- V1–V4

	- Anterolateral
		- I, aVL, V3–V6
		- Reciprocal: II, III, aVF

	- Lateral
		- I, aVL, V5–V6
		- Reciprocal: II, III, aVF

	- High lateral
		- I, aVL (±V2)
		- Reciprocal: III (±II, aVF)

	- Posterior
		- V7–V9
		- Reciprocal: V1–V3 STD

	- Right ventricular
		- Right-sided V3–V6

- High-risk ECG patterns

	- Wellens syndrome
		- Deep/biphasic anterior T-wave inversion
		- Critical LAD stenosis
		- Pain often resolved
		- Avoid stress testing
		- Consider angiography
		- Pain recurrence → T waves may pseudonormalise

	- Diffuse STD + STE in aVR
		- Left main disease
		- Triple vessel disease
		- Global ischaemia
		- Type 2 MI

	- Hyperacute T waves
		- Broad
		- Symmetrical
		- Disproportionately large
		- Earliest evolving MI sign
		- Differentiate from hyperkalaemia

- Other ischaemic ECG findings

	- ST depression
		- ≥0.5 mm
		- ≥2 contiguous leads
		- Horizontal/downsloping
		- More extensive = more severe ischaemia
		- First exclude reciprocal STEMI change

	- T-wave abnormalities
		- Dynamic inversion
		- Dynamic flattening
		- New inversion vs old ECG
		- Suggest ischaemia

- Computer ECG interpretation
	- Not reliable alone
	- Common errors
			- LVH mistaken for MI
			- LBBB mistaken for MI
			- Early repolarisation mistaken for STEMI
			- Pericarditis mistaken for STEMI
	- Clinical interpretation essential

- Continuous ECG monitoring indications
	- Cardiogenic shock
	- Haemodynamic instability
	- Recurrent/ongoing chest pain
	- Cardiac arrest
	- Dynamic ST-T changes
	- Ventricular tachycardia
	- High-grade AV block
	- Mechanical MI complications
			- New systolic murmur
	- Acute heart failure

- Key exam pearls
	- Normal ECG ≠ exclude ACS
	- Serial ECGs if suspicion remains
	- Do not wait for troponin before treating clear ACOMI
	- Maintain identical lead placement for serial ECGs
	- Wellens = critical LAD
	- STE aVR + diffuse STD = left main/triple vessel disease
	- Hyperacute T waves = very early MI

- Biomarker of choice
	- High-sensitivity troponin
		- hs-cTnI
		- hs-cTnT
	- Preferred for ACS diagnosis
	- Earlier detection of myocardial injury
	- Faster rule-in/rule-out

- Troponin threshold
	- Myocardial injury
		- cTn > assay-specific 99th percentile

- High-sensitivity troponin (hs-cTn)
	- Units
			- ng/L
	- Detects ≥50% healthy population
	- Sex-specific cut-offs
	- Rapid assessment pathways
	- Serial testing
			- 0 hr
			- 3 hr

- Contemporary troponin
	- Units
			- µg/L
	- No sex-specific cut-offs
	- Serial testing
			- 0 hr
			- 6–8 hr
	- Cannot use rapid rule-out pathways

- Sex-specific considerations
	- Women
			- Lower normal troponin values
			- Use female-specific cut-offs
	- Non-sex-specific cut-offs
			- Risk underdiagnosing MI in women

- Transgender patients
	- Use lower female cut-off if uncertain

- Point-of-care (POC) troponin
	- Contemporary POC
			- Serial testing 6–8 hr
	- POC hs-cTn
			- Emerging rapid assessment tool

- Troponin interpretation
	- Always interpret with:
			- Clinical picture
			- ECG findings

- Serial troponins
	- Determine pattern
			- Rising
			- Falling
			- Stable

- Stable troponin elevation
	- Chronic myocardial injury
	- CKD
	- Delayed MI presentation (plateau phase)

- Dynamic troponin change
	- Suggests acute myocardial injury
	- Assess for myocardial ischaemia

- Elevated troponin ≠ MI
	- Indicates myocardial injury only

- Acute non-MI causes of elevated troponin
	- Acute heart failure
	- Pulmonary embolism
	- Aortic dissection
	- Myocarditis
	- Sepsis
	- Critical illness

- Troponin kinetics
	- Early MI may have normal troponin
	- Troponin rise may lag behind coronary occlusion
	- Repeat testing if:
			- Ongoing symptoms
			- Recurrent symptoms
			- High ACS suspicion

- Assay principles
	- Different assays ≠ interchangeable
	- Use same assay for serial testing
	- Never apply one assay's reference range to another

- Troponin I vs Troponin T

	- Similar accuracy for MI

	- Troponin T more likely elevated in
			- CKD
			- Myopathy
			- Myositis

- Renal disease
	- Chronic troponin elevation common
			- Especially hs-cTnT
	- Diagnose MI using
			- Serial change
			- Not baseline elevation

- False positive troponin
	- Macrotroponin
	- Heterophile antibodies
		- Rheumatoid arthritis
		- EBV
		- CMV

- False negative troponin
	- Severe haemolysis
	- Biotin interference

- If troponin doesn't fit clinical picture
	- Consult laboratory

- Other biomarkers
	- CK-MB
			- No role in MI diagnosis
			- No role in reinfarction diagnosis

- High-yield exam pearls
	- Troponin >99th percentile = myocardial injury
	- Elevated troponin alone ≠ MI
	- Need:
			- Symptoms and/or
			- ECG evidence and/or
			- Dynamic troponin change
	- Early MI may have normal troponin
	- CKD commonly causes chronically elevated troponin
	- Use serial troponins for diagnosis
	- hs-cTn preferred over contemporary troponin

# risk assessment
- Risk assessment in suspected ACS
	- Purpose
		- Identify MI
		- Identify 30-day MACE risk
		- Reduce unnecessary admissions/testing
	- Structured pathways
		- Missed MI/MACE <1%

- High-risk patients
	- Suspected ACS + ischaemic ECG
	- High-risk clinical features
	→ Inpatient evaluation

- Clinical decision pathways (CDP)
	- hs-cTn based
			- Preferred
	- Clinical score based
			- EDACS
			- HEART

- Three-tier risk stratification

	- High risk
		- 30-day MACE >50–70%
		- Admission required
		- Further evaluation required

	- Intermediate risk
		- 30-day MACE 2–22%
		- Further evaluation required
		- Serial troponins required

	- Low risk
		- 30-day MACE <1%
		- No further testing for MI required

- High-risk ACS features
	- Ongoing ischaemic symptoms
	- Recurrent ischaemic symptoms
	- New ischaemic ECG changes
	- Elevated troponin consistent with MI
	- Strong ACS suspicion

- NSTEMI
	- Elevated troponin
	- No STEMI criteria

- UA
	- Troponin ≤99th percentile
	- Symptoms suggestive of ACS

- If high suspicion despite normal initial troponin
	- Repeat serial troponins
	- Late troponin rise possible

- hs-cTn pathways
	- Preferred over contemporary troponin
	- More rapid
	- More accurate
	- More low-risk patients identified

- Typical hs-cTn stratification
	- Low risk
			- 50–65%
	- Intermediate risk
			- 20–30%
	- High risk
			- 15–25%

- hs-cTn protocols
	- Single troponin
	- 0/1-hour pathway
	- 0/2-hour pathway
	- High-STEACS pathway

- Single hs-cTn strategy

	- Requirements
		- Symptom onset ≥2 hr
		- Non-ischaemic ECG

	- Benefits
		- Safely rules out MI
		- Classifies 20–50% as low risk

	- Do NOT use if
		- Symptom onset <2 hr
		→ Serial testing required

- 0/1-hour pathway
	- Uses:
			- Baseline hs-cTn
			- 1-hour delta
	- Effective
	- Often impractical due to lab delays

- 0/2-hour pathway
	- Uses:
			- Baseline hs-cTn
			- 2-hour delta
	- Most practical pathway
	- Preferred in many hospitals

- Intermediate-risk group
	- MACE risk 2–22%
	- Requires further testing
	- If serial troponins normal
			- 30-day MACE ≤2%

- Low-risk group
	- MACE <1%
	- No further MI testing required

- High-risk group
	- Elevated troponin
	- Dynamic troponin change
	- Ischaemic ECG
	- Ongoing symptoms
	→ Admit

- High-STEACS algorithm
	- Validated hs-cTn strategy
	- Safe
	- Effective

- Clinical score pathways

	- Used mainly with contemporary troponin

	- EDACS
		- Emergency Department Assessment of Chest Pain Score

	- HEART score
		- History
		- ECG
		- Age
		- Risk factors
		- Troponin

- Clinical scores vs hs-cTn pathways
	- Less efficient
	- Identify fewer low-risk patients
	- More additional testing
	- No outcome advantage

- Women
	- Use sex-specific hs-cTn cut-offs
	- ACS often underestimated
	- Higher misdiagnosis rates

- Older adults
	- Use standard hs-cTn thresholds
	- Troponin naturally higher with age
	- Less likely classified low risk
	- Reduced specificity if:
			- Age ≥65
			- CKD
			- Comorbidities

- First Nations peoples
	- Single hs-cTn safe for low-risk identification
	- Investigate for CAD even if ACS excluded
	- Be cautious using:
			- HEART
			- IMPACT

- Renal impairment
	- Use hs-cTn pathways
	- Chronic elevation common
	- Fewer classified low risk
	- Safety similar to non-CKD populations

- Key exam pearls

	- High risk
		- MACE >50–70%
		- Admit

	- Intermediate risk
		- MACE 2–22%
		- Further testing

	- Low risk
		- MACE <1%
		- No further MI testing

	- Symptom onset <2 hr
		- Never use single troponin
		- Serial troponins mandatory

	- hs-cTn preferred over contemporary troponin

	- HEART + EDACS
		- Mainly for contemporary troponin pathways

	- Ongoing symptoms or new ECG changes
		→ High-risk regardless of initial troponin

- Initial treatment in suspected ACS

	- Aspirin
		- Give loading dose
			- 300 mg
		- Maintenance
			- 100 mg daily
		- Reduces
			- Vascular death
			- MI
			- Stroke

- Oxygen

	- Do NOT give routinely

	- Give if
		- SpO₂ <90%

	- SpO₂ 90–92%
		- Benefit uncertain

	- COPD
		- Target SpO₂ 88–92%

	- Excess oxygen
		- No mortality benefit
		- ↑ Recurrent MI
		- ↑ Revascularisation
		- Possible ↑ mortality

- Nitrates

	- GTN
		- Symptom relief only
		- No prognostic benefit

	- IV GTN
		- Better symptom relief than SL GTN

	- Contraindications
		- Hypotension
		- Right ventricular infarction
		- Recent PDE-5 inhibitor use
			- Sildenafil
			- Tadalafil
			- Vardenafil

- Opioids

	- Use if pain persists
	- Titrate individually

	- Fentanyl advantages
		- Rapid onset
		- Short duration
		- Less histamine release

	- Morphine and fentanyl
		- ↑ Platelet reactivity
		- ↓ P2Y12 inhibitor effect
		- Delay oral drug absorption

- Avoid

	- NSAIDs
		- ↑ MACE risk

	- Do not start empirically
		- Additional antiplatelets
		- Anticoagulants
		- Beta blockers
	- Unless ACS confirmed/probable

- Further diagnostic testing

	- Purpose
		- Refine risk
		- Identify CAD
		- Identify alternative diagnoses
		- Assess future ACS risk

- Intermediate-risk patients

	- Require
		- Invasive angiography OR
		- Non-invasive cardiac testing

- Troponin-based approach

	- hs-cTn >99th percentile
		- Inpatient evaluation

	- hs-cTn ≤99th percentile
		- Outpatient testing acceptable
		- Ideally within 30 days

- Low-risk patients

	- Routine non-invasive testing NOT recommended
	- GP follow-up
		- Symptom review
		- Risk-factor assessment
		- Prevention

- CT Coronary Angiography (CTCA)

	- First-line test
		- Intermediate-risk ACS
		- No known CAD
		- No contraindications

	- Benefits
		- Detects obstructive CAD
		- Detects non-obstructive plaque
		- Guides preventive therapy

	- Normal CTCA
		- Excludes ACS reliably
		- Very low ACS risk for 4–5 years

	- Calcium score alone
		- Not sufficient
		- Must not replace CTCA

- Functional testing

	- Preferred if
		- Known CAD
		- Prior stents
		- Heavy coronary calcification

	- Assesses
		- Ischaemia
		- Obstructive disease significance
		- Short-term prognosis

	- Options
		- Stress echo
		- Stress cardiac MRI
		- Stress/rest SPECT
		- Stress/rest PET
		- Exercise ECG

- Stress MRI / Stress Echo advantages

	- LV function assessment
	- Wall motion abnormalities
	- Valve assessment
	- Diagnose alternatives
		- Myopericarditis
		- Takotsubo

- Choosing a test

	- Consider
		- Cardiovascular risk
		- Local expertise
		- Availability
		- Regional/remote access

- Special situations

	- Poor access to follow-up
	- Difficulty re-presenting to hospital
	- First Nations peoples
	→ Consider inpatient testing even if low risk

- Regional/remote considerations

	- CTCA may improve access
	- Telehealth-supported stress testing useful

- High-yield AMC pearls

	- Aspirin
		- 300 mg load → 100 mg daily

	- Oxygen
		- Only if SpO₂ <90%

	- GTN
		- Symptom relief only
		- Avoid in RV infarct + hypotension + PDE5 use

	- NSAIDs contraindicated in ACS

	- Intermediate risk
		- Further cardiac testing required

	- CTCA
		- Preferred first-line test
		- Intermediate-risk patient without known CAD

	- Known CAD/stents
		- Prefer functional testing

- Primary care ACS principles
	- All suspected ACS require best-practice care
	- Early specialist support improves outcomes
	- Telehealth useful
	- Reduces missed STEMI

- ACS assessment requires
	- ECG
	- History + examination
	- Troponin

- Primary care
	- If ACS suspected
			→ Transfer for definitive assessment
	- Do not attempt exclusion without appropriate investigations

- ECG in primary care

	- ECG within 10 min

	- ECG must not delay transfer

	- If ECG unavailable within 10 min
			→ Ambulance transfer

	- First ECG may be interpreted by paramedics

	- If
			- Non-ischaemic ECG
			- Clinical picture not ACS
		→ Continue primary care assessment

- Troponin in primary care

	- If ACS remains possible
			→ Transfer to ED/facility

	- No validated single-test contemporary POC strategy

	- Serial troponins usually required

	- hs-cTn POC
			- Available
			- Limited distribution

- Primary care risk scores

	- Do NOT use to exclude ACS
		- Marburg Heart Score
		- Grijseels rule
		- Bruins Slot rule

	- GP clinical judgement ≈ older risk scores

- Regional/remote ACS

	- Initial assessment unchanged
		- ECG
		- History
		- Examination
		- Troponin

- Transfer decision influenced by
	- Local resources
	- Available investigations
	- Clinical judgement

- Regional/remote ECG

	- If ECG recorded but not interpretable
			→ Telehealth review

	- ACOMI diagnosed
			→ Urgent reperfusion pathway
			→ Transfer for PCI/fibrinolysis

- Regional/remote troponin

	- Often contemporary troponin used

	- Know local assay type

	- Use validated pathway for that assay

	- Normal troponin does NOT exclude UA

	- If ACS suspicion high
			→ Serial troponins 6–8 hr

	- May require
			- ACS treatment
			- Cardiac monitoring
			- Transfer to PCI centre

- Regional/remote risk stratification

	- Do not rely on risk scores alone

	- If contemporary troponin used
			→ Combine with validated risk score

	- Example
			- EDACS + serial POC troponin

- Further testing

	- If unavailable locally
			→ Transfer

	- Tertiary centres should support regional patients

	- Consider
			- Telecardiology
			- Rapid access chest pain clinics

- Discharge criteria

	- Structured assessment completed
	- No admission required
	- Safe discharge determined

- General discharge principles

	- ACS not definitively diagnosed in many
	- Life-threatening causes considered very unlikely
	- Good discharge planning reduces re-presentation
	- Address patient anxiety

- GP communication

	- Prompt discharge summary mandatory

- Low-risk ACS discharge advice

	- Explain diagnosis
	- Explain further investigations if needed
	- Explain:
			- MI excluded
			- UA unlikely

	- Explain
			- CAD NOT excluded

	- GP follow-up required

	- Advise:
			- What to do if symptoms recur
			- Cardiovascular risk education
			- Reliable health information sources
			- Telephone triage contacts

	- Reassure safe discharge

	- Explain:
			- Symptom management
			- When to call ambulance
			- When to return to ED
			- When to contact GP

	- Explain outpatient referral
			- Who
			- Where
			- Timeline
			- Appointment details

	- Explain medications
			- What
			- How
			- When

	- Cardiovascular risk education

- Known CAD / stable CAD discharge

	- Reassure safe discharge

	- Explain:
			- Probable cardiac cause may remain

	- Provide:
			- Follow-up plan
			- Symptom management plan
			- Ambulance/ED return instructions
			- Cardiovascular education

- Key AMC pearls

	- Suspected ACS in primary care
			→ Transfer if troponin required

	- ECG within 10 min

	- Goal

		- Fibrinolysis

	- Choice of reperfusion
		- Most appropriate treatment
			- Primary PCI if achievable within 120 min of first medical contact (FMC)

		- PCI time targets
			- PCI-capable centre
				- Wire crossing ≤60 min
			- Transferred patient
				- Wire crossing ≤90 min

		- If PCI delay >120 min
			- Give fibrinolysis
			- Unless absolute contraindication

		- Assess

			- Comorbidities
		- PCI generally preferred
		- Limited evidence
			- Age >90 years
			- Frailty not assessed in trials

- Fibrinolysis
	- Indications
		- STEMI
		- PCI unavailable within 120 min
		- No absolute contraindications

	- Timing
		- Within 30 min of FMC
		- Preferably pre-hospital

	- Agents
		- Tenecteplase
			- Weight-adjusted IV bolus
			- Preferred agent
		- Reteplase
		- Alteplase

	- Elderly (>75 years)

	- Contraindications
		- Absolute contraindication
			- Transfer for PCI
		- Relative contraindication
			- Individual risk-benefit assessment

- After Fibrinolysis
	- ECG monitoring
		- Until pain free
		- At least 60–90 min post-fibrinolysis

	- Successful reperfusion
			- Relief of chest pain
			- ST-segment resolution

	- Failed reperfusion
		- Ongoing chest pain
		- ≤50% ST recovery at 60–90 min
		- Haemodynamic instability

	- Management of failed fibrinolysis
		- Rescue PCI
			- Most appropriate treatment

	- Routine post-fibrinolysis management
		- Early angiography/PCI
		- PCI ≥2 h after fibrinolysis
		- Reduces reinfarction
		- Improves outcomes compared with fibrinolysis alone

- Primary PCI
	- Access
		- Preferred
			- Radial access
		- Benefits
			- ↓ Mortality
			- ↓ Major bleeding

	- Infarct-related artery (IRA)
		- Routine thrombus aspiration
			- Not recommended
			- ↑ Stroke risk
		- Consider aspiration
			- Large thrombus burden

	- Stenting
		- Preferred
			- Drug-eluting stent (DES)
		- Avoid
			- Routine bare metal stent

	- Deferred stenting
		- Not routinely recommended
		- Consider if
			- Large thrombus burden
			- High risk of no-reflow

	- Occluded IRA >48 h
		- Routine PCI not recommended
		- No survival benefit
		- ↑ Recurrent MI risk

	- If PCI not feasible
		- Consider CABG
			- Left main disease
			- Uncrossable lesion
			- Large myocardium at risk

	- Recognise

		- Lower angiography rates
		- Lower radial access rates
		- Worse outcomes

	- Consider
		- Atypical symptoms
		- SCAD
		- MINOCA

	- Pregnancy
		- Primary PCI preferred
		- Except SCAD-related STEMI
		- Use fetal radiation shielding

	- Establish transfer pathways

		- ECG acquisition
		- Cath lab activation
		- Direct cath lab transfer

- Confirmed NSTEACS
	- Risk stratification
		- Purpose
			- Determine need for invasive management
			- Determine timing of angiography
			- Guide antithrombotic therapy
			- Guide DAPT duration

	- Clinical assessment
		- Very high-risk features
			- Haemodynamic instability
			- Cardiogenic shock
			- Life-threatening arrhythmias
			- Mechanical complications of MI
			- Acute heart failure due to NSTEACS
			- Ongoing ischaemic symptoms with high-risk ECG changes

		- High-risk ECG changes
			- ST depression >1 mm in >6 leads
			- STE in aVR and/or V1
			- Wellens syndrome
			- Recurrent intermittent STE

			- Early invasive strategy required

	- Objective risk assessment
		- Ischaemic risk
			- Best validated score
				- GRACE score
			- Better than
				- TIMI score
				- Clinical judgement alone
			- Strong predictor
				- hs-cTn level

		- Bleeding risk
			- ACTION score
				- Most accurate
			- CRUSADE score
			- ACUITY score
			- ARC-HBR score
				- Practical alternative

	- Key principle
		- If both bleeding and ischaemic risk high
			- Prioritise bleeding risk when determining DAPT duration

- NSTEACS Risk Categories

	- High Risk
		- NSTEMI diagnosis confirmed
		- High-risk hs-cTn pattern
		- Dynamic ST-segment changes
		- Dynamic T-wave changes
		- Transient STE
		- GRACE >140

	- Very High Risk
		- Haemodynamic instability
		- Cardiogenic shock
		- Life-threatening arrhythmias
		- Mechanical complications
		- Acute heart failure
		- Ongoing chest pain with

	- Very High Risk

			- Angiography within 2 hours

	- High Risk
		- Most appropriate management
			- Early invasive strategy
			- Angiography within 24 hours

		- Non-invasive testing first

		- ↓ Death
		- ↓ MI
		- ↓ Rehospitalisation
		- Greatest benefit in high-risk patients

	- Very high risk
		- Immediate
			- <2 hours

	- High risk
		- Early
			- <24 hours

	- Lower risk
		- Delayed/selective

		- Not high risk
		- Not very high risk
		- No known CAD
		- Diagnosis uncertain

		- Excludes CAD
		- Defines coronary anatomy
		- Avoids unnecessary invasive angiography

	- Preferred vascular access

		- Alternative

		- Wait until angiography if angiography performed within recommended timeframe

		- Benefit

				- Clopidogrel

		- Ticagrelor

		- Clopidogrel
			- 5 days
		- Prasugrel
			- 7 days

	- Routine use
			- Not recommended

	- Consider only
			- Bailout therapy
			- High thrombus burden
			- Slow-flow/no-flow

	- Avoid if
			- Platelets <150,000/µL
			- Platelet fall ≥50%

			- Tirofiban/Eptifibatide

			- Abciximab
				- 12 hours

	- Women

		- Less likely to receive

		- Assess

			- Polypharmacy
			- Cognitive dysfunction

				- Bleeding

- DAPT After ACS

	- Standard duration
		- DAPT
			- Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor
			- Usually 12 months

	- Short DAPT
		- 1–3 months
		- Consider if
			- High bleeding risk (HBR)

		- Benefits
			- ↓ Major bleeding
			- Similar MACE outcomes

	- Extended DAPT
		- >12 months

		- Consider if
			- High ischaemic risk
			- Low bleeding risk

		- Benefits
			- ↓ MI
			- ↓ Stroke
			- ↓ Stent thrombosis

		- Risks
			- ↑ Bleeding

	- High bleeding risk (HBR)
		- Assessment tools
			- PRECISE-DAPT
			- ARC-HBR

		- Preferred strategy
			- Short DAPT
				- 1–3 months

	- Older adults (≥70 years)
		- Consider clopidogrel as P2Y12 inhibitor

	- GI bleeding risk
		- Add PPI

- Long-Term Antiplatelet Therapy

	- After DAPT completion
		- SAPT required

	- Preferred SAPT
		- P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy
			- Clopidogrel
			- Ticagrelor

	- Benefits vs aspirin alone
			- ↓ Bleeding
			- ↓ MI

	- PBS note
			- Ticagrelor monotherapy not PBS-subsidised

- ACS + Atrial Fibrillation Requiring Long-Term DOAC

	- Initial period
		- Triple therapy
			- DOAC
			- Aspirin
			- Clopidogrel

	- Duration
		- Up to 1 month if low bleeding risk

	- Then
		- Dual therapy
			- DOAC + Clopidogrel

	- After 12 months
		- DOAC alone

	- High bleeding risk
		- Consider anticoagulation alone after 6 months

	- Triple therapy
		- Add PPI

	- Preferred P2Y12 inhibitor
		- Clopidogrel

- Lipid-Lowering Therapy

	- Initiate during admission
		- Regardless of baseline LDL

	- First-line
		- High-intensity statin

	- Preferred agents
		- Atorvastatin
		- Rosuvastatin

	- LDL reassessment
		- 4–6 weeks

	- If target not achieved
		- Intensify statin
		- Add ezetimibe
		- Add PCSK9 inhibitor

	- PCSK9 inhibitors
		- Alirocumab
		- Evolocumab
		- Inclisiran

	- Young ACS
		- Men <55 years
		- Women <60 years

		- Assess for familial hypercholesterolaemia
			- Dutch Lipid Clinic Network score

	- Elevated triglycerides
		- TG 1.5–5.6 mmol/L
		- LDL 1.0–2.6 mmol/L despite statin

		- Consider
			- Icosapent ethyl

- Beta Blockers

	- Strongest indication
		- MI + LV dysfunction (HFrEF)

	- Benefits
		- ↓ Recurrent MI
		- ↓ Cardiovascular events

	- HFrEF-proven agents
		- Bisoprolol
		- Carvedilol
		- Metoprolol CR/ER
		- Nebivolol

	- Preserved EF
		- No proven long-term mortality benefit
		- Benefit beyond 12 months uncertain

	- Unstable angina + normal EF
		- Little evidence for routine long-term use

	- Caution
		- Cardiogenic shock risk
		- Hypotension
		- Acute decompensated HF

	- IV beta blocker before PCI
		- Not routinely recommended

- ACE Inhibitors / ARBs

	- Post-MI
		- ACE inhibitor recommended

	- Benefits
		- ↓ Mortality
		- ↓ Reinfarction
		- ↓ Stroke

	- If ACEI intolerant
		- ARB

	- LVEF ≥40%
		- Consider ACEI/ARB for survival benefit

	- Hypertension + ACS
		- ACEI/ARB first-line

	- Heart failure + ACS
		- Follow HFrEF guideline-directed therapy

	- ARNI
		- No proven post-MI benefit over ACEI

- Colchicine

	- Rationale
		- Reduces residual inflammation

	- Evidence
		- Possible reduction in
			- Stroke
			- Coronary revascularisation

	- Current status
		- Not routine standard therapy
		- Benefit remains uncertain

- Semaglutide

	- GLP-1 receptor agonist

	- Consider in
		- Established CVD
		- Overweight/obesity

	- Benefits
		- Weight loss
		- ↓ CV death
		- ↓ Non-fatal MI
		- ↓ Non-fatal stroke

	- Useful secondary prevention adjunct
