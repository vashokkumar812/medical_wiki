**Given CT scan left hydronephrosis image.**

- definition
	- dilatation of the renal pelvis and calyces due to obstruction of urine flow
	- may be acute or chronic
	- may be unilateral or bilateral

- causes
	- upper urinary tract
		- ureteric stone
		- pelviureteric junction obstruction
		- ureteric stricture
		- ureteric tumour
		- blood clot
		- sloughed renal papilla
	- external compression
		- pregnancy
		- retroperitoneal fibrosis
		- pelvic malignancy
		- lymphadenopathy
	- lower urinary tract
		- benign prostatic hyperplasia
		- prostate cancer
		- urethral stricture
		- bladder tumour
		- neurogenic bladder
		- posterior urethral valves

- symptoms
	- flank or loin pain
	- renal colic
	- nausea and vomiting
	- haematuria
	- recurrent urinary tract infections
	- reduced urine output if bilateral obstruction
	- fever if infection

- signs
	- costovertebral angle tenderness
	- palpable bladder if urinary retention
	- fever in infected obstruction
	- hypertension in chronic disease

- investigations
	- urinalysis
	- urine culture
	- full blood count
	- urea, electrolytes, creatinine
	- renal ultrasound
		- first-line imaging
	- non-contrast CT KUB
		- best test for ureteric stones
	- MAG3 renogram
		- assesses functional obstruction
	- micturating cystourethrogram
		- if vesicoureteric reflux suspected

- management
	- treat underlying cause
	- analgesia
	- intravenous fluids if required
	- antibiotics if infection
	- ureteric stent or percutaneous nephrostomy if urgent decompression required
	- catheter if bladder outlet obstruction
	- stone removal if indicated
	- surgery for anatomical obstruction

- emergency indications for decompression
	- infected obstructed kidney
	- sepsis
	- bilateral obstruction
	- solitary kidney with obstruction
	- acute kidney injury
	- anuria
	- uncontrolled pain

- complications
	- pyelonephritis
	- pyonephrosis
	- acute kidney injury
	- chronic kidney disease
	- permanent renal damage
	- renal atrophy
	- hypertension

- amc pearls
	- renal ultrasound is the first imaging test for hydronephrosis
	- non-contrast CT KUB is the best test for suspected ureteric stone
	- fever with hydronephrosis indicates an infected obstructed kidney until proven otherwise
	- infected obstruction requires urgent drainage and intravenous antibiotics
	- prolonged obstruction can cause irreversible renal damage
