

- Parotitis
	- Inflammation of the parotid gland

- Causes
	- Acute infective
		- Acute suppurative parotitis
			- *Staphylococcus aureus* (≈80%; most common)
			- Polymicrobial infection
		- Viral
			- Mumps (Paramyxovirus)
			- Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)
			- Influenza
			- Parainfluenza
			- Coxsackievirus
			- Cytomegalovirus (CMV)
			- HIV
	- Chronic infective
		- Tuberculosis
		- Actinomycosis
		- Syphilis
	- Drug-induced
		- Clozapine
		- Anticholinergics
		- Antihistamines
		- Antidepressants
		- Anti-Parkinson medications
		- Sulfamethoxazole
	- Metabolic (sialadenosis)
		- Diabetes mellitus
		- Alcohol use disorder
		- Anorexia nervosa
		- Bulimia nervosa

- Risk factors for acute suppurative parotitis
	- Dehydration
	- Reduced salivary flow
	- Recent major surgery
	- Older age
	- Diabetes mellitus
	- Immunosuppression
	- Hypothyroidism
	- Malnutrition

- History
	- Acute bacterial
		- Rapid unilateral facial swelling
		- Severe pain
		- Fever
		- Dysphagia
		- Trismus
		- Reduced oral intake
		- Reduced neck movement
	- Obstructive
		- Recurrent unilateral swelling
		- Worse during meals
		- Reduced salivary flow
		- History of smoking
		- Gout
	- Viral/systemic
		- Bilateral swelling
		- Fever
		- Malaise
		- Fatigue
		- Myalgia
		- Arthralgia
		- Night sweats
	- Sjögren syndrome
		- Dry eyes
		- Xerostomia
		- Dry mucous membranes
	- Mumps
		- Bilateral parotitis
		- Fever
		- Respiratory symptoms
		- Orchitis
		- Aseptic meningitis

- Examination
	- Diffuse swelling crossing angle of mandible
	- Tenderness
	- Warmth
	- Erythema
	- Induration
	- Examine Stensen's duct
		- Opposite second maxillary molar
		- Erythematous papilla
		- Oedematous papilla
		- Milk gland posterior → anterior
			- Purulent discharge
				- Send for culture
			- Possible stone extrusion
	- Assess trismus
		- <3-finger mouth opening
	- Examine dentition
		- Dental infection
		- Xerostomia-related caries
	- Examine oropharynx
		- Peritonsillar swelling
		- Parapharyngeal involvement
	- Neck examination
		- Ipsilateral cervical lymphadenopathy
		- Bilateral lymphadenopathy suggests viral/systemic disease
	- Facial nerve examination (CN VII)
		- Mandatory
		- New palsy strongly suggests malignancy
		- Urgent ENT referral
	- Differentiate
		- Diffuse gland enlargement
			- Parotitis
		- Focal mass
			- Abscess
			- Lymph node
			- Neoplasm

- Investigations

	- Microbiology
		- Culture pus from Stensen's duct
	- Viral testing
		- Mumps IgM
		- Buccal/oral PCR

- Imaging
	- Not routinely required for uncomplicated acute parotitis
	- Ultrasound
		- First-line imaging
		- Detects
			- Sialolithiasis
			- Abscess
			- Focal masses
		- High sensitivity and specificity
	- CT (contrast preferred if complications suspected)
		- Detects stones
		- Identifies abscess
		- Detects deep neck infection
		- Surgical planning
	- MRI
		- Best for chronic disease
		- Characterises benign vs malignant tumours

- Management
	- General measures
		- Rehydration
		- Analgesia
		- Warm compresses
		- Parotid massage
		- Sialogogues
			- Lemon juice
			- Vitamin C
			- Sour lollies
			- Mints
	- Acute suppurative parotitis
		- Flucloxacillin
		- Consider MRSA risk or penicillin allergy
			- Clindamycin
			- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
		- Reassess after 48–72 hours
			- Poor response → urgent hospital referral
		- Short course prednisolone may help trismus
			- Weigh risks individually
	- Viral parotitis
		- Supportive care
		- Hydration
		- Sialogogues
		- Mumps
			- Isolate for 5 days after onset
			- Notify public health authority

- Red flags requiring emergency referral
	- Fever >38°C
	- Tachycardia
	- Hypotension
	- Altered mental state
	- Severe malaise
	- Moderate or severe dehydration
	- Severe dysphagia
	- Severe trismus
	- Inability to maintain oral intake
	- Severe limitation of neck movement
	- Fluctuant parotid swelling
	- Suspected abscess
	- Facial nerve palsy
	- Cutaneous fistula/sinus
	- Deep neck infection
	- Septic thrombophlebitis (Lemierre syndrome)
	- Imaging suspicious for malignancy

- ENT referral
	- ≥2 episodes of unilateral parotitis
	- Imaging-confirmed sialolithiasis with parotitis
	- Bilateral or recurrent parotitis without obvious cause
	- Incidental parotid mass or tumour
	- Facial nerve palsy
	- Persistent focal parotid mass
