- Cat scratch disease (CSD)
	- Zoonotic infection caused primarily by *Bartonella henselae*

	- Reservoir
		- Domestic cats
			- Especially kittens
	- Human transmission
		- Cat scratch
		- Cat bite
		- Cat saliva contaminating broken skin
		- Rarely flea transmission
		- No human-to-human transmission

- Epidemiology
	- Peak incidence
		- Autumn and winter

- Clinical features
	- Primary lesion
		- Appears 3–10 days after inoculation
		- Papule
		- Vesicle
		- Pustule
		- Crusted lesion
		- Resolves spontaneously
	- Regional lymphadenopathy
		- Occurs 1–3 weeks after scratch
		- Tender
		- Enlarged
		- Mobile
		- Usually unilateral
		- Suppurative in 10–20%
		- Common sites
			- Axillary
			- Cervical
			- Inguinal
	- Constitutional symptoms
		- Mild fever
		- Malaise
		- Fatigue
		- Headache
		- Anorexia
	- Ocular manifestations
		- Parinaud oculoglandular syndrome
			- Unilateral conjunctivitis
			- Preauricular lymphadenopathy
		- Neuroretinitis
			- Optic disc edema
			- Macular star
			- Visual loss
	- Neurological manifestations
		- Encephalopathy
		- Seizures
		- Transverse myelitis
		- Radiculopathy
		- Peripheral neuropathy
	- Musculoskeletal manifestations
		- Osteomyelitis
		- Arthralgia
		- Arthritis
	- Hepatosplenic disease
		- Fever of unknown origin
		- Hepatic abscesses
		- Splenic abscesses
		- Elevated liver enzymes
	- Cardiac manifestations
		- Endocarditis
		- Myocarditis

- Bacillary angiomatosis
	- Occurs in immunocompromised patients
		- HIV/AIDS
		- Organ transplant recipients
	- Features
		- Vascular skin lesions
		- Fever
		- Bone lesions
		- Hepatic involvement
		- Splenic involvement

- Diagnosis
	- Clinical diagnosis
		- History of cat exposure
		- Typical lymphadenopathy
	- Laboratory findings
		- CBC usually normal
		- Mild leukocytosis
		- Elevated ESR
		- Elevated CRP
	- Serology
		- Indirect fluorescent antibody (IFA)
		- Positive IgG supports diagnosis
	- PCR
		- Lymph node aspirate
		- Tissue biopsy
		- Highest specificity
	- Histology
		- Granulomatous inflammation
		- Stellate microabscesses
		- Necrosis
		- Warthin-Starry silver stain
	- Imaging
		- Ultrasound
			- Suppurative lymph nodes
		- CT/MRI
			- Hepatosplenic lesions
			- Osteomyelitis
			- CNS involvement

- Differential diagnosis
	- Infectious
		- Tuberculosis
		- Nontuberculous mycobacteria
		- Epstein-Barr virus
		- Cytomegalovirus
		- Toxoplasmosis
		- Tularemia
		- Brucellosis
		- Plague
		- Syphilis
		- HIV
		- Lymphogranuloma venereum
	- Malignancy
		- Lymphoma
		- Leukemia
		- Metastatic cancer
	- Inflammatory
		- Sarcoidosis
		- Kikuchi disease

- Management
	- Mild uncomplicated disease
		- Usually self-limited
		- Resolution within 2–6 months
		- Supportive care
			- Analgesia
			- Antipyretics
			- Warm compresses
	- Antibiotics
		- Indications
			- Moderate to severe disease
			- Significant lymphadenopathy
			- Disseminated disease
			- Immunocompromised patients
		- First-line
			- Azithromycin
				- 500 mg on day 1
				- 250 mg daily on days 2–5
		- Severe disease
			- Doxycycline
			- Rifampicin
			- Ciprofloxacin
			- Gentamicin
			- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
		- Neuroretinitis
			- Doxycycline + rifampicin
			- 4–6 weeks
		- Bacillary angiomatosis
			- Doxycycline or erythromycin
			- ≥3 months
		- Endocarditis
			- Prolonged combination antibiotics
			- Infectious diseases consultation
			- Possible valve surgery
	- Suppurative lymph nodes
		- Needle aspiration if painful
		- Avoid routine incision and drainage
		- Avoid routine surgical excision

- Complications
	- Persistent lymphadenopathy
	- Suppurative lymphadenitis
	- Neuroretinitis
	- Permanent visual loss
	- Encephalopathy
	- Osteomyelitis
	- Hepatosplenic abscesses
	- Endocarditis
	- Bacillary angiomatosis
	- Peliosis hepatis

- Prognosis
	- Immunocompetent patients
		- Excellent prognosis
		- Complete recovery common
	- Immunocompromised patients
		- Higher morbidity
		- Disseminated disease
		- Prolonged antimicrobial therapy
