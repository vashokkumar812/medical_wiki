# hydrocephalus
- Classification
	- Communicating
		- Impaired CSF absorption
		- CSF flows freely through ventricles
	- Non-communicating (obstructive)
		- Mechanical obstruction within ventricular system
	- Normal-pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)
		- Chronic communicating hydrocephalus
		- Normal CSF opening pressure

- Causes
	- Congenital
		- Aqueductal stenosis
		- Chiari malformation
		- Dandy-Walker malformation
		- Neural tube defects
	- Acquired
		- Brain tumour
		- Intraventricular haemorrhage
		- Subarachnoid haemorrhage
		- Meningitis
		- Head trauma
		- Cerebral abscess
		- Post-neurosurgery
	- Rare
		- Choroid plexus papilloma (↑ CSF production)

- Clinical features
	- Infants
		- Increasing head circumference
		- Bulging fontanelle
		- Separated sutures
		- Sunset eyes
		- Irritability
		- Poor feeding
		- Vomiting
		- Developmental delay
	- Older children/adults
		- Headache
		- Morning vomiting
		- Papilloedema
		- Diplopia (VI nerve palsy)
		- Altered consciousness
		- Gait disturbance
		- Cognitive impairment
		- Urinary incontinence
	- Normal-pressure hydrocephalus
		- Triad
			- Gait disturbance (earliest)
			- Urinary incontinence
			- Cognitive decline ("wet, wobbly, wacky")

- Examination
	- Neurological examination
	- Fundoscopy
		- Papilloedema
	- Cranial nerve examination
	- Head circumference (infants)

- Diagnosis
	- CT brain
		- First-line in acute presentation
		- Ventricular enlargement
		- Identify obstructing lesion
	- MRI brain
		- Better anatomical detail
		- Aqueductal stenosis
		- Posterior fossa lesions
	- Cranial ultrasound
		- Infants with open fontanelle
	- Lumbar puncture
		- Contraindicated if raised ICP suspected
		- NPH
			- Large-volume tap test may predict shunt response

- Management
	- Acute hydrocephalus
		- ABCs
		- Elevate head 30°
		- Neurosurgical emergency
		- External ventricular drain (EVD) if indicated
		- Treat underlying cause
	- Definitive treatment
		- Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt
			- Most common
		- Endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV)
			- Selected obstructive hydrocephalus
		- Tumour resection if causative
		- Antibiotics for meningitis
		- Evacuate haemorrhage when indicated

- Complications
	- Raised intracranial pressure
	- Brain herniation
	- Vision loss
	- Developmental delay
	- Cognitive impairment
	- Shunt complications
		- Infection
		- Obstruction
		- Over-drainage
		- Mechanical failure

- VP shunt complications
	- Fever
	- Headache
	- Vomiting
	- Redness along shunt tract
	- Abdominal pain
	- Altered mental state
	- Return of hydrocephalus symptoms

- High-yield AMC facts
	- Most common cause of congenital hydrocephalus
		- Aqueductal stenosis
	- Most common treatment
		- VP shunt
	- NPH triad
		- Gait disturbance
		- Urinary incontinence
		- Cognitive decline
	- Acute hydrocephalus with reduced consciousness
		- Neurosurgical emergency
	- Lumbar puncture
		- Avoid if raised ICP or obstructive hydrocephalus suspected
