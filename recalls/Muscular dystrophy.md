- AMC One-Liners

	- **DMD**
		- Boy <5 years + Gowers sign + very high CK → **DMD until proven otherwise**

	- **BMD**
		- Similar to DMD but later onset and milder disease

	- **SMA**
		- Floppy infant + tongue fasciculations + absent reflexes + normal sensation → **SMN1 genetic testing**

	- **Myotonic Dystrophy**
		- Grip myotonia + cataracts + cardiac conduction defects → **DMPK genetic test**

	- **CMT**
		- Pes cavus + distal weakness + sensory loss + absent ankle reflexes → **NCS followed by genetic testing**
# Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)
- definition
	- X-linked recessive mutation of dystrophin gene
- epidermiology
	- most common neuromuscular disease
	- male ; mother - asymptomatic carrier
- clinical features
	- 1 year
		- poor head control
	- 2 years
		- subtle hip-girdle weakness
	- 3–6 years
		- Gower sign
		- waddling gait
		- lumbar lordosis
		- calf pseudohypertrophy
		- thigh muscle wasting
	- till 10 years
		- walks independently
	- till 12 years
		- requires orthoses until ~12 years
	- after 12
		- wheelchair dependence followed by rapid scoliosis progression
		- respiratory insufficiency
		- recurrent pulmonary infections
		- pharyngeal weakness
			- aspiration
		- contractures
		- dilated cardiomyopathy
	- neurodevelopment
		- intellectual disability
			- IQ <70 in ~30%

- investigations
	- creatine kinase (CK/CPK)
		- 15,000–35,000 U/L
		- initial screening test
	- molecular genetic testing
		- best initial diagnostic test
		- dystrophin gene mutation
	- muscle biopsy
		- absent or reduced dystrophin
		- if genetic testing is negative or inconclusive

- management
	- multidisciplinary care
	- cardiology follow-up
	- heart failure treatment
		- digoxin
	- aggressive treatment of respiratory infections
	- adequate nutrition
	- calcium supplementation
		- prevent osteoporosis
	- physiotherapy
		- delay contractures
	- orthoses
	- wheelchair support

- prognosis
	- progressive disease
	- death usually around 18 years
		- respiratory failure
		- heart failure
		- pneumonia
		- aspiration
# Myotonic Dystrophy Type 1 (DM1)
- Congenital DM1
	- Severe form
- etiology
	- autosomal dominant
	- Usually inherited from symptomatic mother
	- Present at birth
	- Hypotonia common
![[Pasted image 20260615114327.png]]
- clinical features
	- Myotonia
		- Delayed muscle relaxation after contraction
		- appears after >5 years
		- Difficulty opening fist and releasing handshake
		- Not painful
		- Not a muscle spasm
	- Distal Muscle Weakness and Wasting
		- Characteristic feature
	- Important AMC exception
		- Myopathies usually proximal
		- Neuropathies usually distal
		- DM1 is a myopathy with distal wasting

	- Progression
		- Hands
		- Dorsal forearm muscles
		- Anterior compartment of lower leg
		- Proximal muscles later

	- Functional progression
		- Difficulty climbing stairs
		- Gower sign later

	- Slow progression
		- Childhood → adulthood
		- Most remain ambulant

	- Characteristic Facies
		- Facial wasting
		- Thin cheeks
		- Narrow elongated face
		- Inverted V-shaped upper lip
		- Scalloped temporalis muscles
		- High-arched palate

	- Systemic Manifestations

		- Bulbar
			- Poor speech articulation
			- Slurred speech
			- Dysphagia
			- Aspiration pneumonia

		- Ocular
			- Cataracts
				- Classic association
			- Extraocular muscle weakness

		- Cardiac
			- Cardiac conduction defects
				- Heart block
				- Arrhythmias
			- AMC Pearl
				- Conduction disease typical
				- Cardiomyopathy less prominent than other muscular dystrophies

		- Endocrine
			- Diabetes mellitus
			- Hypogonadism
			- Thyroid dysfunction

		- Gastrointestinal

			- Constipation


		- Obstetric
			- Ineffective uterine contractions
			- Prolonged labour

		- Neurological/Cognitive
			- Intellectual impairment (~50%)

- Diagnosis

	- Best test
		- DNA testing
		- Detects CTG repeat expansion

	- EMG
		- Myotonic discharges

	- CK (CPK)
		- Mildly elevated
		- Usually only hundreds
		- Much lower than Duchenne muscular dystrophy

	- Muscle biopsy
		- Usually not required

- Management
	- Supportive
	- Physiotherapy
	- Speech therapy
	- Cardiac monitoring
	- Respiratory support
	- Endocrine complication management


# spinal muscular atrophy
- Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)
  - Autosomal recessive motor neuron disease
  - SMN1 gene mutation/deletion
  - Degeneration of anterior horn cells
  - Progressive muscle weakness and atrophy
  - Sensation preserved
  - Cognition/intelligence normal

- Classification

  - Type 1 (Werdnig-Hoffmann)
    - Onset <6 months
    - Never sit independently
    - Severe hypotonia
    - Feeding difficulties
    - Respiratory weakness
    - Most severe

  - Type 2
    - Onset 6–18 months
    - Can sit independently
    - Cannot walk independently
    - Scoliosis
    - Contractures
    - Hand tremor

  - Type 3 (Kugelberg-Welander)
    - Onset >18 months
    - Able to walk initially
    - Progressive weakness
    - Fatigue
    - Muscle cramps

- Functional Classification

  - Non-sitter
    - Unable to sit independently

  - Sitter
    - Able to sit independently
    - Unable to walk

  - Walker
    - Able to walk independently

- Clinical Features

  - Hypotonia
    - "Floppy infant"

  - Symmetrical proximal muscle weakness
    - Legs > arms

  - Delayed motor milestones
    - Rolling
    - Sitting
    - Crawling
    - Walking

  - Poor head control

  - Weak cry

  - Weak cough

  - Dysphagia

  - Feeding difficulties

  - Respiratory muscle weakness

  - Recurrent chest infections

  - Muscle wasting

  - Fatigue

- Examination

  - Hypotonia

  - Reduced muscle bulk

  - Symmetrical proximal weakness

  - Reduced or absent deep tendon reflexes

  - Normal sensation

  - Scoliosis

  - Contractures

- Initial Investigations

  - Genetic testing for SMN1 mutation/deletion

  - CK
    - Normal or mildly elevated

- Additional Investigations

  - EMG
    - Denervation pattern

  - Nerve conduction studies
    - Usually normal sensory studies

  - Pulmonary function assessment

  - Swallow assessment

- Most Appropriate Investigation

  - SMN1 genetic testing

- Complications

  - Respiratory failure

  - Recurrent chest infections

  - Aspiration

  - Malnutrition

  - Scoliosis

  - Contractures

- Initial Management

  - Multidisciplinary care

  - Respiratory assessment

  - Nutritional assessment

  - Physiotherapy

  - Swallow assessment

- Most Appropriate Treatment

  - Nusinersen (Spinraza)

- Other Management

  - Respiratory support

  - Chest physiotherapy

  - Antibiotics for respiratory infections

  - Feeding support

  - Gastrostomy if required

  - Physiotherapy

  - Occupational therapy

  - Orthopaedic management

  - Scoliosis surgery if indicated

- Prognosis

  - Variable
  - Depends on disease severity
  - Improved significantly with disease-modifying therapies

# CMT
- Definition
	- Most common HMSN of periphery
- etiology
	- Most common inheritance
		- Autosomal dominant
	- Less common
		- Autosomal recessive
		- X-linked
- Classification
	- Demyelinating neuropathy
		- CMT1
			- Most common subtype
			- better slowed nerve conduction velocities
	- Axonal neuropathy
		- CMT2
			- very slow nerve conduction velocities
			- Reduced amplitudes
	- X-linked neuropathy
		- CMTX
			- GJB1 (connexin 32) mutation
- Clinical features
	- Onset
		- Childhood
		- Adolescence
		- Early adulthood
	- Motor symptoms
		- Distal lower limb weakness
			- Earliest manifestation
			- Difficulty running
			- Difficulty climbing stairs
		- Foot drop
		- Frequent ankle sprains
		- Progressive distal upper limb weakness
			- Hand weakness
			- Poor fine motor skills
		- Muscle wasting
			- Peroneal muscle wasting
			- Distal calf wasting
				- "Stork legs"
				- "Inverted champagne bottle legs"
		- Foot deformities
			- Pes cavus
				- High-arched feet
			- Hammer toes
			- Clawed toes
		- Reflexes
			- Reduced ankle jerks
			- Generalised hyporeflexia
			- Areflexia in advanced disease
	- Sensory symptoms
		- Distal sensory loss
			- Vibration
			- Proprioception
			- Light touch
		- Stocking distribution
	- Gait abnormalities
		- High-stepping gait
			- Due to foot drop
- Investigations
	- Investigation of choice
		- Nerve conduction studies
	- Nerve conduction studies
		- CMT1
			- Markedly reduced conduction velocity
		- CMT2
			- Near-normal conduction velocity
			- Reduced amplitudes
	- Electromyography (EMG)
		- Chronic denervation changes
	- Genetic testing
		- Confirms diagnosis
		- PMP22 duplication most common finding
	- Nerve biopsy
		- Rarely required
- Differential diagnosis
	- Friedreich ataxia
		- Ataxia
		- Dysarthria
		- Cardiomyopathy
		- Diabetes mellitus
		- Extensor plantar responses
	- Muscular dystrophy
		- Proximal weakness
		- Normal sensation
	- Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP)
		- Acquired disorder
		- Progressive weakness
		- No family history
	- Diabetic neuropathy
		- Adult onset
		- Diabetes present
	- Hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies
		- PMP22 deletion
		- Recurrent focal neuropathies
- Management
	- No curative treatment
	- Supportive management
		- Physiotherapy
		- Occupational therapy
		- Stretching exercises
	- Orthotics
		- Ankle-foot orthosis (AFO)
			- For foot drop
	- Orthopaedic management
		- Severe pes cavus
		- Severe foot deformities
	- Pain management
		- Neuropathic pain treatment if required
	- Genetic counselling
		- Recommended for affected families
- Prognosis
	- Slowly progressive
	- Usually normal life expectancy
	- Many patients remain ambulant throughout life
