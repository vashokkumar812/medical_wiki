## Etiology
- Organism
	- Bordetella pertussis
		- Most common cause
		- Notifiable disease in Australia
	- Bordetella parapertussis
		- Uncommon
		- Usually milder disease
		- Not notifiable
- Transmission
	- Respiratory droplets
	- Commonly household spread

## Epidemiology / Prevention
- Immunised children can still develop pertussis
	- Usually milder disease
- Immunity wanes
	- 4–12 years after last vaccine dose
- No lifelong immunity
- Recurrence may occur
	- Especially with superimposed viral infection

## Risk Factors for Severe Disease
- Infants ≤6 months
	- Highest risk group
	- Apnoea
	- Bradycardia
	- Pneumonia
	- Seizures
	- Encephalopathy

## Clinical Course
- Incubation period
	- 4–21 days
	- Usually 7–10 days
- Catarrhal phase
	- Duration
		- 1–2 weeks
	- Coryza
	- Non-productive cough
- Paroxysmal phase
	- Duration
		- 1–2 weeks
	- Paroxysmal cough
	- Inspiratory whoop
	- Post-tussive vomiting
		- More common in unvaccinated patients
	- Infants
		- Apnoea
		- Bradycardia
		- Cyanosis
- Convalescent phase
	- Duration
		- 2–6 weeks
		- May persist up to 3 months
	- Gradual improvement in cough

- Clinical Features
	- Persistent cough
	- Poor feeding
	- Weight loss
	- Sleep disturbance
	- Examination
		- Fever uncommon
		- Often minimal clinical signs

- Infectious Period
	- From symptom onset until earliest of:
		- 21 days after symptom onset
		- 14 days after onset of paroxysmal cough
		- Completion of 5 days of appropriate antibiotics

- Diagnosis
	- Primarily clinical

## Investigations
- PCR
	- Investigation of choice
	- Nasopharyngeal flocked swab
	- May become negative:
		- After 21 days
		- 5–7 days after effective antibiotic therapy
- Culture
	- Recommended in:
		- Catarrhal phase
		- Early paroxysmal phase
		- Severe cases
		- Hospitalised patients
		- Suspected treatment failure
		- Public Health Unit request
- Serology
	- Not clinically useful
- Blood tests
	- Not routinely required
	- Lymphocytosis
		- Correlates with disease severity
	- Thrombocytosis
		- Common
		- Associated with poorer infant prognosis
- Chest X-ray
	- Unwell infants <6 months
	- Assess for:
		- Pertussis pneumonia
		- Pulmonary hypertension

- Complications
	- Neurological
		- Seizures
		- Hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy
	- Respiratory
		- Pneumonia
		- Pneumothorax
		- Pulmonary hypertension
	- ENT
		- Otitis media
	- Musculoskeletal
		- Rib fractures
	- Ophthalmic
		- Scleral haemorrhage
	- Gastrointestinal / Abdominal wall
		- Hernia

- Management
	- Indications for antibiotic treatment
		- Catarrhal phase
		- Early paroxysmal phase
		- Cough <14 days
		- Symptoms <21 days
		- Hospital admission
		- Severe disease
		- Complications
	- Antibiotics
		- Preferred
			- Azithromycin
			- Clarithromycin
		- Alternative
			- Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole
				- If macrolides contraindicated or resistance present
		- Avoid
			- Erythromycin
				- Risk of infant pyloric stenosis
	- Antibiotics reduce:
		- Disease severity
		- Transmission
		- Most effective if given within:
			- 14 days of paroxysmal cough
			- 21 days of symptom onset

- Admission Criteria
	- All infants <6 months
	- Respiratory distress
	- Inability to feed
	- Pneumonia
	- Cyanosis
			- With or without cough
	- Apnoea
			- With or without cough
	- Seizures
	- Droplet precautions required

- Isolation and Exclusion
	- Exclude from:
		- School
		- Childcare
		- Contact with others
			- Especially infants
	- Continue exclusion until earliest of:
		- 21 days after symptom onset
		- 14 days after onset of paroxysmal cough
		- Completion of 5 days of appropriate antibiotics
	- Children no longer infectious after:
		- 21 days of illness
		- 5 days of antibiotics

- Notification
	- Notify all suspected or confirmed cases

- Contact Management
	- Close contacts
		- Exposure during first 21 days of illness
		- Includes:
			- Household contacts
			- Overnight stays
			- Face-to-face contact within 1 metre for >1 hour
				- Shorter threshold for neonates
	- High-risk contacts
		- Infants <6 months
		- Pregnant women in last month of pregnancy
		- People who may transmit infection to infants
			- Parents
			- Childcare staff
			- Healthcare workers
	- Post-exposure treatment
		- Same antibiotic regimens as cases
		- Goal:
			- Prevent transmission to infants <6 months
	- Treat:
		- Within 14 days of first exposure
		- Within 21 days for exposed infants <6 months
		- Any exposed infant <6 months
		- Pregnant women in final month of pregnancy
		- Contacts likely to transmit to infants <6 months
			- Household members of infant <6 months
			- Healthcare staff caring for infants or pregnant women
			- Childcare staff and children caring for infants <6 months
	- Contact exclusion
		- Unimmunised or incompletely immunised children
			- <3 doses
			- <7 years
		- Exclude until:
			- 14 days after last exposure
			- Or completion of 5 days of appropriate antibiotics

- Vaccination
	- Diagnosed but unimmunised or partially immunised children
		- Complete routine schedule
	- Close contacts not up to date
		- Administer DTPa or dTpa
	- Adults without pertussis-containing vaccine within previous 10 years
		- Offer booster

- Consultation Indications
	- Infant ≤6 months
	- Severe disease
		- Apnoea
		- Cyanosis
		- Pneumonia
		- Seizures
		- Encephalopathy

- Transfer Indications
	- Care required beyond local hospital capability
	- Contact retrieval services

- Discharge Criteria
	- Tolerates coughing episodes without:
		- Hypoxia
		- Bradycardia
	- Adequate oral intake
	- Cough may persist after discharge
