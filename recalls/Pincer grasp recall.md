Q19. Pincer grasp cannot be done. fine movt of hand affected. What is the reason?

1. lesion at C8T1
    
2. Injuries at wrist
    

C8-T1 lesion — Controls intrinsic hand muscles (lumbricals, interossei) + long flexors. Loss = no pincer grasp, impaired fine motor. Classic ulnar + median nerve root level deficit.

### Why Pincer Grasp Requires C8-T1 Innervation

Pincer grasp (precision grip—thumb-to-fingertip opposition) depends on intrinsic hand muscles, which are innervated exclusively by the C8-T1 nerve roots (via the ulnar and median nerves).

C8 radiculopathy presents with:

- Finger flexors weakness (motor deficits)
    
- Difficulty with fine motor skills — dropping things, changes in handwriting, difficulty with jewelry clasps
    
- Sensory loss in fingers IV (ulnar half) and V
    

### Intrinsic Hand Muscles Involved

The intrinsic hand muscles responsible for pincer grasp are innervated by:

Ulnar nerve (C8-T1):

- Lumbricals (3rd and 4th)
    
- Interossei (all)
    
- Adductor pollicis (crucial for thumb opposition/pinch)
    
- Hypothenar muscles
    

Median nerve (C8-T1):

- Lumbricals (1st and 2nd)
    
- Thenar muscles (abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis)
    

### Why NOT Wrist Injuries?

Wrist injuries affect:

- Extrinsic forearm muscles (flexor/extensor tendons)
    
- Local soft tissue structures
    
- Not the nerve roots or intrinsic hand innervation
    

- Wrist pathology would preserve gross hand movement; fine motor and pincer grasp would
