

- **Definition**
	- Chronic immune-mediated inflammatory skin disease
	- Characterised by **well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scales**
	- Chronic relapsing course

- **Epidemiology**
	- Any age
	- Peak onset
		- 20–30 years
		- 50–60 years
	- Positive family history common

- **Risk Factors / Triggers**
	- Family history
	- Streptococcal infection
		- Guttate psoriasis
	- Skin trauma (**Koebner phenomenon**)
	- Stress
	- Smoking
	- Obesity
	- Alcohol
	- Medications
		- Lithium
		- Beta-blockers
		- Antimalarials
		- Interferon
		- Withdrawal of corticosteroids

- **Clinical Features**
	- Well-defined erythematous plaques
	- Silvery-white scales
	- Symmetrical distribution
	- Mild itching
	- Chronic relapsing disease

- **Common Sites**
	- Extensor elbows
	- Extensor knees
	- Scalp
	- Lumbosacral region
	- Umbilicus
	- Gluteal cleft

- **Nail Changes (Highly Tested)**
	- Nail pitting
	- Onycholysis
	- Subungual hyperkeratosis
	- Oil-drop (salmon patch) discoloration

- **Types**
	- **Chronic plaque psoriasis (most common)**
	- Guttate
		- Small drop-like lesions
		- After streptococcal pharyngitis
	- Inverse (flexural)
		- Axillae
		- Groin
		- Minimal scale
	- Pustular
		- Sterile pustules
	- Erythrodermic
		- Generalised erythema
		- Medical emergency

- **Associated Conditions**
	- **Psoriatic arthritis**
	- Obesity
	- Metabolic syndrome
	- Type 2 diabetes
	- Hypertension
	- Dyslipidaemia
	- Cardiovascular disease
	- Depression
	- Inflammatory bowel disease

- **Diagnosis**
	- Clinical
	- Skin biopsy
		- Only if diagnosis uncertain

- **Management**
	- Mild disease
		- Emollients
		- **Topical corticosteroids**
		- Vitamin D analogues
			- Calcipotriol
		- Combination steroid + calcipotriol
	- Moderate disease
		- Narrowband UVB phototherapy
	- Severe disease
		- Methotrexate
		- Cyclosporine
		- Acitretin
		- Biologic agents
			- TNF inhibitors
			- IL-17 inhibitors
			- IL-23 inhibitors

- **Complications**
	- Psoriatic arthritis
	- Secondary infection
	- Erythroderma
	- Reduced quality of life
	- Increased cardiovascular risk

- **Important Signs**
	- **Auspitz sign**
		- Pinpoint bleeding after scale removal
	- **Koebner phenomenon**
		- Lesions develop at sites of trauma

- **Differential Diagnosis**
	- Seborrhoeic dermatitis
	- Tinea corporis
	- Atopic dermatitis
	- Lichen planus
	- Pityriasis rosea

- **AMC Pearls**
	- **Most common type = Chronic plaque psoriasis.**
	- **Extensor surfaces + silvery scales = Psoriasis.**
	- **Nail pitting strongly suggests psoriasis.**
	- **Guttate psoriasis follows streptococcal pharyngitis.**
	- **Koebner phenomenon = lesions appear after skin trauma.**
	- **Auspitz sign = pinpoint bleeding when scales are removed.**
	- **Topical corticosteroids + calcipotriol are first-line for mild plaque psoriasis.**
	- **Methotrexate is commonly used for severe psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis.**
	- **Avoid systemic corticosteroids when possible due to risk of rebound and pustular psoriasis.**
