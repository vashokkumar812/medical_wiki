# chicken pox

- varicella (chickenpox)
  - caused by
    - varicella-zoster virus (VZV)

  - incubation
    - 8–21 days
    - 8–28 days
      - if VZIG given

  - infectious period
    - 2 days before rash
    - until all lesions crusted

  - clinical features
    - prodrome
      - fever
      - lethargy
      - anorexia
    - rash
      - papules → vesicles → crusts
      - lesions at different stages simultaneously
      - crusts by ~10 days
    - distribution
      - scalp
      - face
      - trunk
      - mouth
      - conjunctivae
    - surrounding erythema
      - usually normal
      - cellulitis commonly overdiagnosed

  - complications
    - bacterial skin infection
      - GAS
      - S. aureus
    - pneumonia / pneumonitis
    - encephalitis
    - hepatitis
    - arthritis
    - reye syndrome

  - high-risk groups
    - premature neonates
    - neonates <7 days
    - immunocompromised
      - malignancy
      - chemotherapy
      - prednisolone >20 mg/day for ≥2 weeks
      - primary immunodeficiency
    - unimmunised adolescents

  - management
    - immunocompetent child
      - supportive care only
    - avoid
      - aspirin
      - NSAIDs
    - eczema
      - continue usual treatment
        - including topical steroids
    - school exclusion
      - until lesions crusted
    - hospital admission
      - severe disease only
      - contact isolation if admitted
    - pregnant contacts
      - review within 24 hours

  - antiviral therapy
    - IV aciclovir
      - premature neonates
      - term neonates <7 days
      - immunocompromised
      - severe or disseminated disease
      - CNS disease
    - oral aciclovir (consider)
      - unvaccinated >12 years
      - severe eczema
      - chronic lung disease
      - oral steroids
      - long-term salicylates
      - secondary household cases

  - vaccination
    - routine
      - MMR-V at 18 months
    - catch-up ≥14 years
      - 2 doses
      - ≥4 weeks apart
    - breakthrough disease
      - usually mild
    - post-vaccine rash
      - 1–3 weeks

  - post-exposure prophylaxis
    - healthy >1 year
      - varicella vaccine within 5 days
    - uncertain vaccination history
      - repeat vaccine is safe
    - high-risk unable to receive vaccine
      - VZIG
    - age 1 month–1 year
      - usually observe
      - consider VZIG if high risk
    - immunocompromised contacts
      - VZIG
    - susceptible household contacts
      - vaccinate

  - neonatal varicella
    - symptomatic
      - investigate
      - IV aciclovir
    - exposed neonate becomes unwell
      - IV aciclovir
        - even if VZIG previously given
    - hospital care
      - isolate
        - 21 days after exposure
        - 28 days if VZIG given
      - continue breastfeeding
        - cover breast lesions if present

  - VZIG
    - indications
      - maternal varicella
        - 7 days before → 2 days after delivery
      - exposed high-risk neonates
      - immunocompromised contacts
      - susceptible high-risk children unable to vaccinate

    - timing
      - ideally within 96 hours
      - effective up to 10 days
    - alternative
      - normal human immunoglobulin

  - neonatal exposure management
    - maternal antibody unknown
      - assume absent
    - maternal varicella <28 weeks
      - VZIG
    - maternal varicella >28 weeks
      - maternal antibody present
        - observe
      - maternal antibody absent
        - VZIG
    - <1 week exposed from mother
      - VZIG
    - <1 week exposed from non-maternal contact
      - maternal antibody present
        - observe
      - maternal antibody absent
        - VZIG

  - recurrence
    - herpes zoster
      - dermatomal rash
      - milder in children
      - post-herpetic neuralgia rare
